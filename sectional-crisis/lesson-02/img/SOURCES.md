- Stowe, Uncle Tom's Cabin, concluding remarks: quoted from the Project Gutenberg transcription (ebook 203) of the first edition (Boston: John P. Jewett and Company, 1852), fetched from Project Gutenberg during this build and verified against the transcription's own text of Chapter XLV, Concluding Remarks. The passages printed are the chapter's opening answer on authenticity and its jurisprudence passage ending at the inherent injustice sentence. Cuts are marked with ellipses and disclosed in the Source line. The transcription's em dash characters are set as commas in this printing to meet the unit's print standard; the wording is otherwise as the transcription carries it, and the activity's Source line says so.
- The Lincoln greeting Stowe anecdote is stated in the reading, the guided notes key, the deck, and the writing bank only as an anecdote printed decades later and unverifiable, exactly as the unit skeleton directs. No wording of the greeting beyond the commonly repeated short form is quoted as fact anywhere in this lesson.

## Subjects for which no image is used, recorded honestly
- The Stowe and Lincoln meeting: no image is used. The greeting itself is unverifiable, and a picture of the meeting would certify what the lesson teaches students to doubt.
- Thomas Sims, William Henry, and the Christiana house: no verified public domain image with an adequate basis read on its file page was confirmed during this build, so the renditions are taught in text and in the Burns portrait.
- Josiah Henson: no portrait with a verified public domain basis and adequate resolution was confirmed during this build, so Henson is taught in text.
- The Akron convention of 1851: no period image of the convention is used, because none was verified for this build; Truth is shown in her own later photograph, dated on its face wherever it appears.
