## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- The sack of Lawrence and the Pottawatomie killings: no image of either event is used anywhere in this lesson. The violence is stated in text, plainly and briefly, and no image of violence is used as decoration, following the unit spec.
- Lawrence, Kansas, in 1856: no verifiable public domain period print of the town was located and downloaded during this build, so the town is taught in text and in the Sumner and activity records.
- Salmon P. Chase: no verified public domain portrait was downloaded for this build. Chase enters the lesson through the Appeal he led, quoted at Station 2.

## Quoted source log for this lesson
- U.S. Congress, Kansas-Nebraska Act, section 14, 1854 (Avalon Project, Yale Law School). Audited TPSP bank record kansas-nebraska-act-1854, sectional-civil-war.json. Extracted programmatically from the bank file during this build and quoted in the activity, Station 1, and in shortened form in the quick check, Passage A, with cuts marked with ellipses. Both excerpts were verified as exact substrings of the bank record text after extraction.
- John L. Magee, "Forcing Slavery Down the Throat of a Freesoiler," 1856 (Library of Congress; image described). Audited TPSP bank record forcing-slavery-cartoon-1856, sectional-civil-war.json. The bank record's description is quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 3, and the image is reproduced from the bank record's Library of Congress image file.
- S. P. Chase, Charles Sumner, J. R. Giddings, Edward Wade, Gerrit Smith, and Alexander De Witt, Appeal of the Independent Democrats in Congress to the People of the United States, Washington, January 19, 1854 (pamphlet text, as transcribed by the Independence Hall Association at ushistory.org). Fetched from that transcription during this build, cross-checked against the American Presidency Project's transcription of the same pamphlet, and quoted in the activity, Station 2, with cuts marked with ellipses. Passages were chosen from portions where the transcription is clean; garbled passages elsewhere on the page were not used.
- Charles Sumner, The Crime Against Kansas, speech in the Senate of the United States, May 19, 1856 (1856 pamphlet edition; full text from the Internet Archive scan, crimeagainstkans00sumn). Fetched and verified from that scan during this build and quoted in the activity, Station 4, and in the quick check, Passage B, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Station 5 of the activity, the Lecompton record, is a labeled teaching summary built from the public record of the 1857 convention and referendum. It is labeled a summary on its face in the activity, and no part of it is presented as a quotation.
