Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedWhat it claimed about slavery and citizenship
A party built from a repeal, 1854The Kansas-Nebraska Act repealed the Missouri Compromise line, and anti-Nebraska meetings named themselves Republican, gathering ex-Whigs, Free Soilers, anti-Nebraska Democrats, and temperance and nativist voters on one plank: Congress must bar slavery from the federal territories.The plank claimed Congress held the power and the duty to bar slavery from the territories, and claimed nothing against slavery where state law protected it. Barred from spreading, slavery would be put in the course of ultimate extinction.
The creed and the first campaign, 1854 to 1856The party rested on the free labor creed, labor honorable and the laborer free to rise, with Black exclusion laws in states such as Oregon and Indiana standing as its shadow. In 1856 John C. Frémont carried eleven of the sixteen free states, and Buchanan won on Pennsylvania and the South.The campaign proved a wholly northern party could nearly win the presidency with no southern strength at all. The creed's claim was about labor and land, not about Black citizenship, and the exclusion laws show the limit plainly.
Scott's case, 1846 to 1857Dred Scott, enslaved in Missouri, was carried to Illinois and to Fort Snelling, free soil both, married there, and sued for his family's freedom in 1846 on the ground that residence on free soil had made them free. The case took eleven years to reach its answer.Scott's claim was that free soil made free people, under the Missouri Compromise's bar at Fort Snelling and Illinois law. His suit forced the Court to say whether a Black man could be heard in a federal court at all.
The decision, March 6, 1857Chief Justice Roger B. Taney, for a majority of seven, held that Black people could not be citizens, that Scott's residence on free soil had not freed him, and that the Missouri Compromise had been unconstitutional from the start.The decision claimed the Constitution excluded Black people from citizenship forever, protected enslaved people as property, and had never given Congress power over slavery in the territories, voiding the Republican platform itself.
The answers, 1857 to 1858Justices McLean and Curtis dissented, Curtis from the founding record. Douglass answered in speeches and in his letter to Hugh Auld. In June 1858 Lincoln's House Divided speech charged that the repeal, the decision, and the administration's policy were one design to make slavery national.Curtis claimed free Black men had been citizens and voters in five original states at ratification, so the Constitution was not made by and for the white race alone. Lincoln claimed the question would be settled all one way or all the other, not by decree.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers carry the promise and the attempted settlement together. Example: The Republican Party promised that Congress would bar slavery from the federal territories, leaving it untouched in the states and putting it in the course of ultimate extinction. The Supreme Court in Dred Scott tried to settle the question by decree, holding on March 6, 1857 that Black people could not be citizens, that free soil residence freed no one, and that Congress had never had the power the platform claimed. Justice Curtis answered from the ratification record of free Black voters in five states, and Lincoln's House Divided speech of June 1858 charged that the decision belonged to one design to make slavery national. Within three years the voided party had elected a president on the voided platform.
