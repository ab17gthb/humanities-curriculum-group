Writing Task: Purpose or Instrument?

The prompt. By the end of 1863, had emancipation become the Civil War's purpose or its instrument? Answer in a developed essay of about 300 words, argued from the records in The Emancipation File and from the reading. A strong answer does more than celebrate the proclamation. It states emancipation's terms and limits in the proclamation's own words, it follows the Black soldiers and their recruiters through what their service claimed, and it answers the strongest claim on the other side: that a measure drawn for military necessity, which left slavery legal in four loyal states, changed the war's methods without changing its meaning.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The road: from May 1861 enslaved people reached Union lines at Fort Monroe and Butler held them as contraband of war. Congress followed with the First Confiscation Act and compensated emancipation in the District of Columbia in April 1862, freeing the capital's enslaved people and paying loyal owners.

2. The proclamation's terms, January 1, 1863: all persons held as slaves in any state or part of a state then in rebellion are then, thenceforward, and forever free, and the government, including its military and naval authority, will recognize and maintain that freedom and receive such persons into the armed service.

3. The proclamation's limits: it did not name Delaware, Maryland, Kentucky, or Missouri, and it exempted Tennessee and the occupied parts of Louisiana and Virginia. It freed where the Union could not yet enforce and stood aside where it could, the critics' charge, stated in the reading.

4. The recruiters: Douglass and other Black leaders, March 1863, 'Men of Color, To Arms!': if we would be regarded men, if we would forever silence the tongue of calumny, of prejudice and hate, let us rise now and fly to arms.

5. The soldiers: some 180,000 Black men served in the United States Colored Troops. They were paid ten dollars a month against a white soldier's thirteen until equal pay in June 1864. The 54th Massachusetts assaulted Fort Wagner on July 18, 1863, lost Colonel Shaw and nearly half the regiment, and refused unequal pay for eighteen months.

6. The Address, November 19, 1863: the war tests whether a nation dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal can long endure, and the living resolve that the nation shall have a new birth of freedom. Gettysburg cost the two armies some fifty thousand casualties on July 1 to 3, and Vicksburg fell on July 4, splitting the Confederacy.

7. The resistance: New York's draft riots of July 1863 killed about one hundred and twenty people, the mobs lynching Black New Yorkers and burning the Colored Orphan Asylum, before troops from Gettysburg restored order. Robinson's Pictures of Slavery and Anti-Slavery, published in Philadelphia in 1863, defended slavery as an institution imposed by God.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your evidence, answer the other side in full, and end by restating what 1863 settled about the war's purpose and what it left for 1864 and after.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the prompt's either or directly, purpose or instrument, and defines the chosen term against the other, not a claim that emancipation was important or that 1863 was a turning point in general. (Blueprint 13, 14)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, drawn from at least three different records, including the proclamation's own terms (bank 2) and the soldiers' record (bank 5). Dates and figures must be right: January 1, 1863; March 1863; some 180,000 served; ten dollars against thirteen; equal pay June 1864; July 18, 1863; July 1 to 3 and July 4, 1863; November 19, 1863. (Blueprint 13, 14)
  • The proclamation's limits carried in the argument, not footnoted away: the four loyal slave states unnamed, Tennessee and the occupied districts exempted, and the critics' charge answered or conceded with a reason. An essay that writes that the proclamation freed all enslaved people cannot earn full credit under this prompt. (Blueprint 13)
  • The other side answered from the record. The strongest purpose answer grants the instrument facts, military necessity on the document's face and enforcement only where armies advanced, and then shows what the records register beyond method: a government committed to maintain a freedom declared forever, Black men armed and arguing citizenship from the ranks, and a president rededicating the war, at its costliest battlefield, to the proposition of equality. The strongest instrument answer grants the Address and the enlistments and shows the limits surviving them: slavery legal in four states at the year's end, and freedom's reach still exactly the reach of Union bayonets.

Model opening for teacher reference. By the end of 1863 emancipation had become the war's purpose wearing an instrument's clothing. The proclamation was drawn as a war measure and admits it, freeing only where rebellion gave the commander in chief his reach, but the freedom it declared was declared forever, the government bound itself to maintain it, and the men it armed spent the rest of the war making the declaration a fact no peace could bargain away. A full model continues in this pattern, carries the soldiers and the Address through the claim, answers the limits and the riots from the bank, and closes on what 1863 settled and what it deliberately left open.

Common weak answers. A celebration claim (Lincoln freed the slaves and the war was won) never states the limits, the pay fight, or a single date, and cannot use the bank. A cynicism claim (the proclamation freed no one) ignores that the enslaved executed the measure themselves by reaching the lines, that 180,000 men served under it, and that Vicksburg and Gettysburg fell in its first six months. An essay that treats the Gettysburg Address as decoration, without its proposition and its new birth of freedom, has skipped the record in which the war's purpose is restated in so many words.
