Quick Check: Ottawa to Savannah, 1858 to 1861

Directions: Read the two records and the background, then answer the questions.

Background. Lincoln at Ottawa, Illinois, on August 21, 1858, in the first debate with Stephen A. Douglas, quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record lincoln-ottawa-debate-reply-1858 (edition named in that record: Abraham Lincoln, 'Reply at the Ottawa Debate,' 1858 (Political Debates, 1860 first edition; punctuation normalized).). Stephens at Savannah on March 21, 1861, quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record stephens-cornerstone-speech-1861 (edition named in that record: Alexander H. Stephens, 'Cornerstone Speech,' 1861 (American Rhetoric; after Henry Cleveland, Alexander H. Stephens in Public and Private, 1886).). Cuts are marked with ellipses.

"[...] I will say here, while upon this subject, that I have no purpose, directly or indirectly, to interfere with the institution of slavery in the States where it exists. [...] I have no purpose to introduce political and social equality between the white and the black races. [...] I, as well as Judge Douglas, am in favor of the race to which I belong having the superior position. [...] But in the right to eat the bread, without the leave of anybody else, which his own hand earns, he is my equal and the equal of Judge Douglas, and the equal of every living man. [...]"

"Our new government is founded upon exactly the opposite idea; its foundations are laid, its corner-stone rests, upon the great truth that the negro is not equal to the white man; that slavery -- subordination to the superior race -- is his natural and normal condition."

1. Define the Lincoln-Douglas debates as the guided notes state them: how many, where, in what campaign, how long each, and before what crowds. Then state, from the first record, the two things Lincoln says he has no purpose to do.

2. Define the Freeport Doctrine as the guided notes state it. Then explain, using the first record, why Douglas needed Lincoln to be read as preaching equality: what position does Lincoln claim for his own race in the same breath as the bread right?

3. State the facts of the raid in order: the place and date of the seizure, the officer whose marines stormed the engine house, the date of the address Brown gave to the court, and the one design Brown admitted there.

4. Define secession as the guided notes state it. Then state, from the second record, the great truth Stephens says the Confederate cornerstone rests on, in his own words.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Define the debates, and state the two denials. The Lincoln-Douglas debates are the seven open air debates between Abraham Lincoln and Stephen A. Douglas across Illinois in the Senate campaign of 1858, three hours each before crowds in the tens of thousands, which made Lincoln a national figure and fixed the terms of the slavery argument. From the record, Lincoln has no purpose, directly or indirectly, to interfere with the institution of slavery in the states where it exists, and no purpose to introduce political and social equality between the white and the black races. (Blueprint 9)

2. Define the Freeport Doctrine, and explain Douglas's need. The Freeport Doctrine is Douglas's answer at Freeport in August 1858, that a territory could keep slavery out despite the Dred Scott decision by refusing to pass the local police laws slavery needed to survive, the answer that saved his Senate seat and forfeited the South. Douglas needed Lincoln read as an equality preacher because his own ground was racial: in this same record Lincoln claims the superior position for the race to which he belongs while insisting that in the right to eat the bread his own hand earns, the Black man is the equal of every living man. A student who reports only the bread sentence, or only the superior position sentence, has halved the record Douglas was campaigning against. (Blueprint 9)

3. State the facts of the raid in order. John Brown seized the federal arsenal at Harpers Ferry, Virginia, on October 16, 1859. United States Marines under Colonel Robert E. Lee stormed the engine house and captured him two days later, on October 18. Brown addressed the Virginia court at Charles Town on November 2, 1859, and admitted one design only, to free enslaved people, denying any intent of murder, treason, or insurrection. (Blueprint 10)

4. Define secession, and quote the cornerstone. Secession is the doctrine and act by which eleven slaveholding states, beginning with South Carolina on December 20, 1860, declared their union with the United States dissolved and withdrew, holding that the Union was a compact from which a state could withdraw as it had entered. Stephens's words: the Confederate corner-stone rests upon the great truth that the negro is not equal to the white man, and that slavery, subordination to the superior race, is his natural and normal condition. (Blueprint 10)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 predict success on the blueprint 9 half of the unit test, and questions 3 and 4 on the blueprint 10 half. Question 2 is the sourcing diagnostic: students who cannot hold Lincoln's two statements together will misread every later document in the unit that quotes one of them alone.
