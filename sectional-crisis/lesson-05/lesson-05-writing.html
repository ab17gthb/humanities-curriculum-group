Writing Task: Which Turn Closed the Road

The prompt. Answer the question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did the argument of 1858 become the break of 1860 and 1861? Argue which single turn did most to close the road to compromise: the Freeport Doctrine, John Brown's raid and gallows, or the seceding states' own stated grounds for leaving. A strong answer states what each side said its settlement was, follows the turns in order, and answers the strongest evidence for another turn. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, drawn from both halves of the bank, and explain how each piece supports your judgment.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and the Break File activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. At Freeport in August 1858, Douglas answered Lincoln that a territory could keep slavery out despite the Dred Scott decision by refusing to pass the local police laws slavery needed to survive. The Freeport Doctrine saved his Senate seat in Illinois and forfeited the South, whose leaders demanded a federal slave code for the territories. (Lesson reading; Key Terms.)

2. At Ottawa on August 21, 1858, Lincoln denied any purpose to interfere with slavery where it existed or to introduce political and social equality, and favored the superior position for his own race, while insisting that in the right to eat the bread his own hand earns, the Black man is the equal of every living man. Douglas, in the same debate, denied that the Black man was his equal or his brother in any sense. (Audited TPSP records lincoln-ottawa-debate-reply-1858 and douglas-ottawa-debate-1858.)

3. On October 16, 1859, John Brown and twenty one men, five of them Black, seized the federal arsenal at Harpers Ferry. The first man killed was Heyward Shepherd, a free Black railroad porter. Marines under Robert E. Lee stormed the engine house on October 18, and Brown was hanged on December 2 after telling the court that the same act, done for the rich and powerful, would have been deemed worthy of reward. (Lesson reading; Brown's address, named transcription.)

4. In 1860 the Democratic Party split at Charleston and Baltimore over the slave code demand. Douglas ran on popular sovereignty, Breckinridge on the code, Bell on the Union alone, and Lincoln on no extension of slavery. Lincoln won with 39.8 percent of the popular vote, no southern state, and no place on the ballot in ten slave states. (Lesson reading.)

5. South Carolina seceded on December 20, 1860. Its Declaration of the Immediate Causes charged that fourteen northern states had broken the Constitution's fugitive stipulation, denounced slavery's opponents for disturbing the peace and assisting escapes, and treated the election of a president hostile to slavery as the final breach releasing the state from the compact. (Audited TPSP record south-carolina-secession-declaration-1860.)

6. The Crittenden Compromise, proposing to write the Missouri line into the Constitution as an unamendable amendment protecting slavery south of it forever, failed in January 1861 after Lincoln instructed Republicans to refuse compromise on the extension of slavery. (Key Terms.)

7. On March 21, 1861, Alexander H. Stephens, the Confederacy's vice president, said at Savannah that the new constitution had put the slavery question at rest forever, that slavery was the immediate cause of the rupture, and that the government's cornerstone rests on the great truth that the Black man is not equal to the white man and that slavery is his natural and normal condition. (Audited TPSP record stephens-cornerstone-speech-1861.)

Plan Before You Write

My judgment (one sentence that names the turn that did most to close the road, and gives the reason in the same sentence):

My evidence picks (bank numbers, at least one from items 1 to 3 and at least one from items 4 to 7) and what each one proves:

The strongest evidence for another turn, and how I will answer it:

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your judgment, present the turns in order, weigh the turn you chose against the record of the others, answer the strongest evidence for another turn in one or two sentences, and end by restating what the documents show.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A judgment that names one turn, the Freeport Doctrine, Brown's raid and gallows, or the seceding states' stated grounds, and defends the choice rather than narrating the years. (Blueprint 9 and Blueprint 10)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, with at least one from each half. Bank items 1 through 3 carry the argument: the Freeport Doctrine and the slave code demand it produced, the two Ottawa records with Lincoln's two statements held together and Douglas's denial of equality, and the raid, the engine house, and Brown's address and hanging. (Blueprint 9) Bank items 4 through 7 carry the break: the four party election and its figures, South Carolina's declaration and its fugitive clause case, the Crittenden Compromise's failure on Lincoln's instruction, and Stephens's cornerstone. (Blueprint 10)
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the judgment. The strongest essays show the mechanism: how a debate answer became a party split, how a raid became a section's picture of invasion, or how a declaration's own logic left no terms to negotiate, and say why that mechanism closed more roads than the others.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest evidence for another turn, answered from the record rather than dismissed. An essay choosing Freeport must answer Brown's gallows or the declaration's charges. An essay choosing Brown must answer the slave code split that preceded the raid's political harvest. An essay choosing the declarations must answer the compromise that was still on the table in December and why it failed.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Freeport Doctrine did most to close the road, because it made the one party that still crossed the sections choose between its halves. A full model continues in this pattern, carries the Ottawa records and the raid beside the election figures and the two secession documents, answers the strongest opposing turn, and closes on what the record shows.

Common weak answers. A chronology with no mechanism (a list of events with no claim about which turn closed the road) has not answered the lesson question. An essay that quotes Lincoln's bread sentence without his superior position sentence, or the reverse, has halved the Ottawa record. An essay that explains secession from tariffs, railroads, or any ground the seceding states' own documents do not state has replaced the record with a later story, and the declaration and the cornerstone are in the bank precisely to prevent that.
