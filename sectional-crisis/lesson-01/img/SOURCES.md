## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- Stephen A. Douglas and Millard Fillmore: no portrait was downloaded for this lesson. Their sections in the deck use text slides rather than a likeness, and this file records that choice honestly rather than filling the space.
- No image of violence is used as decoration anywhere in the lesson. The one print that depicts violence, the Kaufmann lithograph, is itself Station 5's assigned record, quoted from the audited bank presentation and captioned as an argument, not an illustration.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- Henry Clay, resolutions introduced in the Senate, January 29, 1850. Edition named: Congressional Globe, 31st Congress, 1st Session, pages 244 to 247, in the Teaching American History transcription of the Globe text (teachingamericanhistory.org/document/compromise-of-1850-2/), fetched and verified during this build. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 1, four of the eight resolutions, cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed in the Source line.
- Daniel Webster, speech in the Senate, March 7, 1850 (the Seventh of March speech). Edition named: the text reprinted in The U.S. Constitution, A Reader, published by Hillsdale College (constitutingamerica.org), fetched and verified during this build; the opening was cross checked against the U.S. Capitol Visitor Center's account of the speech. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 2, cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed.
- John C. Calhoun, speech on the admission of California and the general state of the Union, March 4, 1850. Edition named: The Works of John C. Calhoun, edited by Richard K. Crallé. The cords passage was verified during this build against the Works text as reproduced in the Google Books copy of the Crallé edition speeches volume and against a full transcript of the speech; the quotations agree. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 3, cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed.
- Fugitive Slave Act, September 18, 1850, sections 5, 6, 7, and 8. Edition named: the Avalon Project at Yale Law School transcription of the Statutes at Large, fetched and verified during this build through the House Divided (Dickinson College) printing of the Avalon text, which carries the Avalon citation on its face. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 4, and the quick check, cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed.
- Theodor Kaufmann, "Effects of the Fugitive-Slave-Law," 1850. Audited TPSP bank record effects-of-fugitive-slave-law-1850 in sectional-civil-war.json; its text was extracted programmatically from the bank file during this build and quoted in full in the activity, Station 5, with the bank record's sourceNote quoted in the teacher key: Theodor Kaufmann, 'Effects of the Fugitive-Slave-Law,' 1850 (Library of Congress; image described, caption abridged).
- Teaching summaries: none of this lesson's five stations is a labeled teaching summary. All five stations are verbatim texts or the audited bank record, as itemized above. The guided notes summary table, the notebook chart, and the writing evidence bank are teacher and student apparatus written for this lesson from the documents named, not quotations, and none is presented as a period text.
