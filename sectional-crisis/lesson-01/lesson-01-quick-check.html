Quick Check: Ten Dollars and Five

Directions: Read the passage and the background, then answer the questions.

... they are hereby authorized and empowered ... to summon and call to their aid the bystanders, or posse comitatus of the proper county, when necessary ... and all good citizens are hereby commanded to aid and assist in the prompt and efficient execution of this law, whenever their services may be required ... In no trial or hearing under this act shall the testimony of such alleged fugitive be admitted in evidence ... he shall be entitled to a fee of ten dollars in full for his services in each case, upon the delivery of the said certificate to the claimant ... or a fee of five dollars in cases where the proof shall not ... warrant such certificate and delivery ...

Source: Fugitive Slave Act, September 18, 1850, sections 5, 6, and 8, in the Avalon Project at Yale Law School transcription of the Statutes at Large. Cuts are marked with ellipses.

Background. The Act was the fifth measure of the Compromise of 1850, the package passed bill by bill under Stephen A. Douglas in September 1850 after Henry Clay's single omnibus failed. California entered as a free state, and the Act was the South's enforcement gain inside the same settlement. Its hearings ran before federal commissioners rather than judges and juries. Northern states answered within the decade with personal liberty laws granting accused fugitives the jury trials and counsel the federal law withheld.

1. In the passage, what is the alleged fugitive barred from doing at the hearing, in the statute's own sentence?

2. State the commissioner's two fees and the outcome attached to each, exactly as the passage gives them.

3. Using the background, give two facts: one about where this Act sat inside the Compromise of 1850, and one about the northern state answer to it. State what each fact shows about the settlement.

4. A defender argues that the passage is ordinary law enforcement, no different from any arrest law. State the strongest answer to that argument from the passage itself, naming the two features an ordinary criminal hearing of the era would have given the accused.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What is the alleged fugitive barred from doing? Testifying. In the statute's sentence: In no trial or hearing under this act shall the testimony of such alleged fugitive be admitted in evidence. Credit requires the substance and, for full credit, the quoted sentence. (Blueprint 2)

2. State the two fees and their outcomes. Ten dollars in full for his services in each case, upon the delivery of the certificate to the claimant, that is, when the commissioner remands the prisoner; and five dollars in cases where the proof does not warrant the certificate and delivery, that is, when he frees the person. (Blueprint 2)

3. Give two facts, one about the Act's place in the Compromise and one about the state answer. The Compromise fact: the Act was one of the five measures of September 1850, passed bill by bill under Douglas after Clay's omnibus failed, the South's enforcement gain in a settlement whose other headline was California free; it shows the Act was not an accident of the settlement but its price. (Blueprint 1) The state answer: northern states passed personal liberty laws granting accused fugitives the jury trials and counsel federal law withheld; it shows the settlement's enforcement term reopened the contest as a fight between state and federal authority. (Blueprint 2)

4. State the strongest answer from the passage, naming the two missing features. The answer: the passage builds the hearing itself against the accused. The commissioner is paid double for one outcome over the other, and the person whose freedom is at stake is barred from speaking in the proceeding at all. An ordinary criminal hearing of the era would have given the accused the right to testify in the proceeding in some form through counsel and witnesses, and, for a charge carrying loss of liberty, trial by jury; this hearing has neither, and it adds a command that bystanders and all good citizens aid the claimant's side. A defense that addresses only the arrest power and not the fee schedule and the testimony bar earns partial credit. (Blueprint 2)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 test close reading of the statute, the skill Station 4 of the activity grades. Question 3 carries both of the lesson's blueprint entries, the Compromise's terms and passage for Blueprint 1 and the Act's enforcement and its answer for Blueprint 2. Question 4 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires weighing a law's stated purpose against its printed machinery.
