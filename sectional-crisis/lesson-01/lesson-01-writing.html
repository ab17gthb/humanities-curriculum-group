Writing Task: A Settlement, or an Armistice?

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: what did the Compromise of 1850 settle, and what did its Fugitive Slave Act ask the North to do? A strong answer does more than list the five terms. It states how the package passed after the omnibus failed, works through the Act's machinery at the level of the accused, the commissioner, and the bystander, and weighs the newspapers' verdict of a final settlement against the personal liberty laws and the Underground Railroad that answered at once. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading, stations, and documents. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording, except where a bank item quotes a document.

Evidence Bank

1. California, peopled by the gold rush, wrote a constitution prohibiting slavery and applied as a free state against a Senate balanced at fifteen free and fifteen slave states, so its admission would make the count sixteen to fifteen and leave the South a permanent minority in that chamber. (Lesson reading, section A.)

2. Clay's resolutions of January 1850 proposed the whole bargain in one package: California admitted without a restriction, the Mexican lands organized without a restriction, the Texas claim settled, the slave trade prohibited in the District of Columbia, and, in his words, more effectual provision for the restitution and delivery of persons bound to service or labor who escape. (Congressional Globe text, quoted in the activity, Station 1.)

3. The omnibus collapsed in July 1850. Douglas passed the five measures one at a time, each with a different majority, and Fillmore signed them in September 1850 after Taylor's death. The five terms: California free; Utah and New Mexico without a restriction; the Texas boundary settled and its debt assumed; the slave trade abolished in the District of Columbia; and the Fugitive Slave Act. (Lesson reading, sections E and F.)

4. The Act provided that in no trial or hearing under this act shall the testimony of such alleged fugitive be admitted in evidence, paid the commissioner ten dollars upon delivery of the certificate and five dollars where the proof did not warrant it, commanded all good citizens to aid in the law's execution, and fined aiding or harboring a fugitive up to one thousand dollars with six months in prison. (Avalon Project transcription of the Statutes at Large, quoted in the activity, Station 4, and the quick check.)

5. Calhoun, in his last speech, read aloud by Mason on March 4, 1850, warned that the cords binding the Union had snapped one by one under agitation of the slavery question and would finally all snap, leaving nothing to hold the states together except force. Webster, on March 7, spoke as an American for the preservation of the Union, and Whittier answered him in “Ichabod.” (Crallé edition of Calhoun's Works and the Seventh of March text, quoted in the activity, Stations 2 and 3; lesson reading, sections C and D.)

6. Northern legislatures passed personal liberty laws forbidding state officers to help renditions, denying state jails, and granting the jury trials federal law withheld, while the Underground Railroad worked on, Harriet Tubman returning south repeatedly after her own 1849 escape to guide some seventy people to freedom. Theodor Kaufmann's 1850 lithograph pictured the law for northern viewers under quotations from Deuteronomy and the Declaration of Independence. (Lesson reading, section G, and the activity, Station 5, the audited bank record.)

Scaffold. Build the essay in this shape. Open with the claim. A first body paragraph on the settlement: the balance California broke, the bargain Clay framed, and the passage in pieces that made five separate majorities do what one package could not. A second body paragraph on the enforcement: the accused silenced, the commissioner paid ten against five, the bystander commanded, and the aider fined and jailed. A closing move on the verdict word: state whether final settlement or armistice fits the record of the personal liberty laws and the railroad, and defend the word from your evidence.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the question in one judgment, for example that the Compromise settled the territorial and statehood questions of 1850 while its enforcement act conscripted the North into rendition and thereby reopened the question it claimed to close. A claim that lists only the terms, or only condemns the Act, earns partial credit before the evidence is read. (Blueprint 1 and Blueprint 2)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 carries the Senate balance and California's application. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 2 carries Clay's bargain in his own resolution language. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 3 carries the passage in pieces and the five terms. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 4 carries the Act's machinery in the statute's own words, the testimony bar, the fees, the command, and the penalties. (Blueprint 2) Bank item 5 carries the two Senate warnings that frame the verdict question. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 6 carries the northern answer in law, in flight, and in print. (Blueprint 2)
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. The decisive link is passage against enforcement: strong essays argue whether a settlement that required five different majorities and a silenced accused had settled a question or only adjourned it under new management, and the fee schedule and the personal liberty laws are the evidence that decides between those readings.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest fact on the other side, such as the decade the Union held after 1850 answered from bank item 6: a settlement whose enforcement had to be obstructed by state law within the decade was being contested from the year of its passage.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Compromise of 1850 settled every question that was pending in its own year, and its price reopened all of them. California came in free against a Senate balanced fifteen to fifteen, Clay framed the bargain whole in January, and when the omnibus failed, Douglas passed the five measures one at a time for Fillmore to sign: the territories organized, Texas paid, the District trade ended. But the fifth measure asked the North to do the South's rendition work, before commissioners who silenced the accused and were paid ten dollars to remand and five to free, with bystanders commanded to help and neighbors fined and jailed for feeding a fugitive. A full model continues in this pattern, weighing Calhoun's snapping cords against Webster's plea, and closing on the personal liberty laws, which are the record's own verdict on how final the final settlement was.

Common weak answers. The Compromise postponed the Civil War for ten years (a label, not an answer) skips both halves of the question and the machinery the prompt requires. An essay that treats the Fugitive Slave Act as a minor part of the settlement ignores bank item 4, where its fees, command, and penalties occupy the settlement's enforcement whole. An essay that presents the Compromise as a single vote Clay won has misread bank item 3 and should be sent back to Station 1 and the reading, section E.
