Teacher Answer Key

Piece of the storyWhat happenedWho gained or lostThe evidence
California and the balanceCalifornia, peopled by the gold rush, wrote a constitution prohibiting slavery and applied as a free state, threatening the Senate balance of fifteen and fifteen.At risk: the South's Senate veto; gained: a free CaliforniaAdmission as a free state made the count sixteen to fifteen and left the South a permanent minority in the Senate.
The two roadsTaylor would admit California and New Mexico at once and let their constitutions settle slavery. Clay instead laid out his resolutions in January 1850 as a single omnibus bargain giving each section a victory and a defeat in one vote.Gained: a negotiable package; lost: Taylor's direct roadClay had built the compromises of 1820 and 1833 and returned to the Senate in 1849 at seventy two.
Calhoun and WebsterCalhoun, dying, had Mason read his March 4 speech demanding southern equality in the territories and warning that the Union's cords had snapped one by one. Webster's Seventh of March speech pleaded for the Union and the package, and Whittier answered with “Ichabod.”Lost: Webster's antislavery standing; warned: the whole SenateCalhoun: if agitation went on, every cord would snap and nothing would hold the states together except force.
Passage in piecesThe omnibus collapsed in July 1850. Douglas passed the five measures one at a time, each with a different majority, and Fillmore signed them in September after Taylor's death.Gained: the settlement, without any single vote for the wholeNorthern votes carried California's admission and southern votes carried the fugitive slave bill.
The five termsCalifornia free; Utah and New Mexico organized without a slavery restriction; the Texas boundary settled and its debt assumed; the slave trade abolished in the District of Columbia; and the stricter Fugitive Slave Act.Gained and lost on both sides, by designIn the capital the trade ended while slavery itself remained lawful, the bargain stated in one city.
The Act and the answerThe Act tried accused fugitives before commissioners on an affidavit, with no testimony and no jury, ten dollars for remanding against five for freeing, posses, and penalties for aiding. Northern states answered with personal liberty laws, and the Underground Railroad worked on.Lost: the accused, and northerners commanded to help; gained: claimants, a federal machineryHarriet Tubman, escaped from Maryland in 1849, returned south repeatedly to guide some seventy people out.

The settlement in one sentence. Strong answers join the settlement to its enforcement. The Compromise settled the questions of its year, but its Fugitive Slave Act put rendition on northern streets, silenced the accused, commanded the bystander, and called out the personal liberty laws and the railroad in answer, so the settlement carried the next crisis inside it.
