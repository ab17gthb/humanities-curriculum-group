Quick Check: The Woe Due

Directions: Read the two passages and the background, then answer the questions.

Document A. "On the occasion corresponding to this four years ago all thoughts were anxiously directed to an impending civil war. All dreaded it, all sought to avert it. While the inaugural address was being delivered from this place, devoted altogether to saving the Union without war, insurgent agents were in the city seeking to destroy it without war--seeking to dissolve the Union and divide effects by negotiation. Both parties deprecated war, but one of them would make war rather than let the nation survive, and the other would accept war rather than let it perish, and the war came."

Source: Abraham Lincoln, Second Inaugural Address, March 4, 1865 (Abraham Lincoln, 'Second Inaugural Address,' 1865 (Avalon Project, Yale Law School).), as carried in the audited TPSP record.

Document B. "You cannot qualify war in harsher terms than I will. War is cruelty, and you cannot refine it; and those who brought war into our country deserve all the curses and maledictions a people can pour out. I know I had no hand in making this war, and I know I will make more sacrifices to-day than any of you to secure peace. But you cannot have peace and a division of our country. If the United States submits to a division now, it will not stop, but will go on until we reap the fate of Mexico, which is eternal war. You might as well appeal against the thunder-storm as against these terrible hardships of war. They are inevitable, and the only way the people of Atlanta can hope once more to live in peace and quiet at home, is to stop the war, which can only be done by admitting that it began in error and is perpetuated in pride."

Source: William Tecumseh Sherman, letter to James M. Calhoun, Mayor, E. E. Rawson and S. C. Wells, representing the City Council of Atlanta, September 12, 1864, in the Wikisource transcription of the letter, fetched and verified for this lesson. Cuts are marked with ellipses and disclosed here; the paragraphs printed are complete paragraphs of the letter as the transcription carries them.

Background. Grant became general in chief in March 1864 and made the Confederate armies the objective. Sherman took Atlanta in September 1864 and marched to the sea. Lincoln was reelected that November. Congress passed the Thirteenth Amendment on January 31, 1865. Richmond fell on April 3, 1865, and Lee surrendered at Appomattox Court House on April 9. Booth shot Lincoln on April 14, and the president died on April 15.

1. In Document A, state the fraction of the population Lincoln gives for the enslaved, and state what he says all knew about the interest they constituted.

2. In Document A, state the offense Lincoln names, and state to whom he says the war, as the woe due, was given.

3. In Document B, state Sherman's definition of war, and state the only way he gives for Atlanta's people to live in peace and quiet at home again.

4. Set the two documents against each other. State one thing Lincoln and Sherman agree the war was about, and one point on which their explanations of the war differ.

Teacher Answer Key

1. State the fraction, and what all knew. One eighth of the whole population were colored slaves. All knew that this interest was somehow the cause of the war. (Blueprint 15)

2. State the offense, and the woe's recipients. The offense is American slavery. The war is the woe due to those by whom the offense came, given to both North and South together. (Blueprint 15)

3. State the definition, and the one way home. War is cruelty, and you cannot refine it. The only way Atlanta's people can hope to live in peace and quiet at home is to stop the war, by admitting that it began in error and is perpetuated in pride. (Blueprint 15)

4. State the agreement, and the difference. Agreement: both documents hold that the war's cause lay in the slaveholding interest and its bid to divide the Union, and that the Union must be restored whole (Sherman: you cannot have peace and a division of our country; Lincoln: the insurgents would rend the Union to perpetuate slavery). Difference: Sherman explains the war as a political act to be reversed by submission to the laws, begun in error and perpetuated in pride. Lincoln explains it as a judgment, an offense answered by a woe apportioned by God to both sections, in which the North shares guilt and cannot claim its prayers were answered. (Blueprint 15)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 to 3 test close reading of the lesson's two verdicts on the war. Question 4 tests the second half of the blueprint line, the Second Inaugural read against Sherman, the comparison the writing task develops.
