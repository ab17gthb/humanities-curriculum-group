## Image discipline for this lesson, stated on the record
- No photograph of the dead, at Antietam or anywhere else, is downloaded or reproduced in any document, deck, or teacher notes document in this lesson, following the unit's binding image discipline. This lesson uses no battle photograph at all; its war is carried in portraits, in the McLean house photograph, and in the documents.
- No image of violence is used as decoration anywhere in this lesson.

## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- No image of the March to the Sea, of burning Atlanta or Columbia, or of the Richmond fires was used. Period photographs of burned Richmond exist in public collections, but the lesson teaches the destruction through Sherman's letter and the reading's text, and does not print ruins as illustration for a lesson whose documents ask students to judge that destruction.
- No image of Lincoln's assassination or of John Wilkes Booth was used. The assassination is taught in text, and no period print of the shooting was located whose provenance this build could certify to the lesson's standard in the time available.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- Abraham Lincoln, Second Inaugural Address, March 4, 1865. Audited TPSP bank record lincoln-second-inaugural-1865 (sectional-civil-war.json); edition named in the record: Abraham Lincoln, 'Second Inaugural Address,' 1865 (Avalon Project, Yale Law School). Extracted programmatically from the bank file and quoted verbatim from the record in the activity (in full), the quick check (the third paragraph), and the writing evidence bank (a short quoted passage with its cut marked). The record's double hyphen punctuation is carried as the record carries it.
- Annie Davis, letter to Abraham Lincoln, Belair, Maryland, August 25, 1864. Audited TPSP bank record annie-davis-letter-1864 (sectional-civil-war.json); edition named in the record: Annie Davis, 'Letter from Annie Davis to Abraham Lincoln,' 1864 (National Archives; Freedmen and Southern Society Project transcription). Extracted programmatically from the bank file and quoted verbatim, in Davis's own spelling, in the activity.
- Women's Loyal National League, "To the Women of the Republic," 1864. Audited TPSP bank record wlnl-address-1864 (sectional-civil-war.json); edition named in the record: Women's Loyal National League, 'To the Women of the Republic,' 1864 (DocsTeach; punctuation modernized). Extracted programmatically from the bank file and quoted verbatim from the record in the activity, with the record's own ellipses.
- United States Congress, Homestead Act, section 1, May 20, 1862. Audited TPSP bank record homestead-act-1862 (sectional-civil-war.json); edition named in the record: U.S. Congress, 'Homestead Act,' 1862 (transcribed from the enrolled act; Kansas State History transcript). Extracted programmatically from the bank file and quoted verbatim from the record in the activity.
- William Tecumseh Sherman, letter to James M. Calhoun, Mayor, E. E. Rawson and S. C. Wells, representing the City Council of Atlanta, September 12, 1864. No bank record exists for this letter. The three paragraphs printed at Station 5 of the activity and in the quick check are quoted from the Wikisource transcription of the letter (en.wikisource.org, "Letter to James M. Calhoun, et al., September 12, 1864"), fetched and read in full during this build on October 22, 2026, and the printed paragraphs were checked against that transcription during the build. They are complete paragraphs of the letter as the transcription carries them; the Source line in the documents names the transcription. Paragraphs of the letter containing the transcription's em dash character were not selected, in keeping with this unit's print standard. This station is verbatim from a named transcription, not a teaching summary.
- All casualty and signature figures in this lesson are stated as the usual estimates or the campaign's usual count, on the face of the documents: some sixty five thousand Union casualties in the Overland campaign's first six weeks, some sixty two thousand soldiers on the March to the Sea in a corridor some sixty miles wide, and some 400,000 signatures gathered by the Women's Loyal National League petition campaign.
- The Thirteenth Amendment definition used in the guided notes, the deck's speaker notes, and the deck's vocabulary is the unit spec's definition, carried character for character: "the constitutional amendment, passed by Congress on January 31, 1865, and ratified that December, that abolished slavery and involuntary servitude everywhere in the United States, except as punishment for a crime, the first of the three Reconstruction amendments and the only one adopted while the war still ran." It is written for the Reconstruction build to carry forward unchanged, and this lesson alters no word of it.
