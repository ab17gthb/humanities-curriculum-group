# Image and Quotation Sources: Lesson 6, The War Begins: Sumter to Antietam

All images are public domain as stated below, recorded as the holding archive or file page states it. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/, and the master downloads live in img/ beside this file.

## Binding image discipline for this lesson, stated plainly

Alexander Gardner's Antietam photographs of the Confederate dead are taught in this lesson through the audited bank record's described text (antietam-dead-1862) and the documented effect of their New York exhibition in October 1862. The photograph itself was NOT downloaded and is NOT reproduced in any student document, deck slide, or teacher notes document in this unit. The activity's Station 4, the deck's Antietam slides, and the writing task's evidence bank all say so on their faces. No image of violence is used as decoration anywhere in this lesson. A period battle print of Antietam located during the build was deliberately not used, for that reason.

### Sullivan Ballou, Letter to Sarah Ballou, July 14, 1861 (bank: ballou-letter-to-sarah-1861)
- Edition as the bank record's sourceNote names it: Sullivan Ballou, 'Letter to Sarah Ballou,' 1861 (AmericanLiterature.com; authenticity disputed).

### Summary and teaching records, labeled on their faces
- Activity Station 5 (Scott's Anaconda Plan) is a teaching summary written for this lesson from the public record of General Winfield Scott's May 1861 letter to General George B. McClellan, labeled as a summary on its face. No sentence in it is presented as a period quotation. Its facts: the blockade of the southern ports, a column of some eighty thousand men with gunboats proposed for the Mississippi, the cutting off of Texas, Arkansas, and Louisiana, Scott's expectation that pressure would revive Union sentiment inside the Confederacy, the newspapers' anaconda name, and Lincoln's adoption of the plan's parts in substance. Item 1 of the writing task evidence bank and item 6 (Bull Run and the campaigns of 1862) restate lesson facts and are labeled in summary in the bank.
- The reading, guided notes, notebook, and deck prose are original lesson writing and present no period text inside quotation marks except the bank quotations logged above.

## Figures stated with their precision
- Antietam casualties: some 22,700, stated everywhere as the usual reckoning or the usual estimates, because the figure is an estimate assembled from the two armies' returns.
- Shiloh casualties: some twenty four thousand, stated as the usual estimates.
- Populations: the Union about 22 million and the Confederacy about 9 million, more than a third of the Confederate figure enslaved, stated as the approximate census based figures the era's record supports.
- Blockade fleet size: some six hundred vessels by the war's later years, as the unit vocabulary's blockade definition states it.

## Gaps recorded honestly
- No photograph of Sullivan Ballou verified as a public domain file was used. Ballou is taught from his letter's text, and no image in this lesson is captioned as Ballou.
- No image of Fort Sumter is used. No period Sumter image was verified as a public domain file under the names tried during this build, so Sumter is taught in text and the deck's Section A carries Lincoln's portrait.
- Gardner's Antietam photographs are a deliberate non-use, not a gap: see the discipline statement above.
