Writing Task: Who Gained, Who Paid in the West?

The prompt: How did railroads and federal land policy remake the West? Write a paragraph that answers the lesson question and takes a position on who gained and who paid. Use the scaffold below to build the paragraph before you write the final version.

Teacher Answer Key and Rubric

Sample paragraph. Railroads and federal land policy remade the West by turning public land into the money that built a commercial region, and the clearest example is the land grant checkerboard. The government gave railroads alternating sections along the line, and the companies sold those sections and borrowed against them to pay for construction, so a line could be built before it had customers. The Homestead Act worked the same land from the other side: a claimant could earn title to 160 acres, but only after paying a filing fee and living on and improving the claim for five years, which many families could not survive through drought and debt. The same policies cost Native nations the most, because the reservations they were pressed onto were far smaller than the land they had held under treaty and were reduced again when miners found gold or silver inside them. Railroads, companies, and the settlers who lasted gained a connected West; Native nations and failed claimants paid for it with land.

Scoring rubric. Full credit should be awarded if the student:

  • Answers the lesson question directly, naming both railroads and federal land policy as causes, not just describing western life.
  • Uses at least two specific facts from the lesson, drawn from at least two rows of the evidence chart (for example the alternating section land grant and the five year homestead conditions, or barbed wire ending the drives and reservations reduced after a strike).
  • States who gained and who paid as part of the argument, with a mechanism for each, rather than listing them without explanation.
  • Handles at least one accuracy point correctly: a homestead required a filing fee, five years of residence, and improvement; land grants came in alternating sections; or cattle drives declined because railroads reached Texas and barbed wire closed the range.

Common weak responses. A paragraph that calls homestead land free with no conditions has missed the central condition of the policy and should be sent back for that correction. A paragraph that treats the West as empty land has missed the reservation evidence entirely. A paragraph with gainers but no payers, or payers but no gainers, answers only half of the unit question.
