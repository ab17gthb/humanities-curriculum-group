Guided Notes: The New Immigration

Lesson question: Who were the new immigrants, and how did the United States decide who belonged?

Unit question: How did industrial growth transform American life, and who gained, who paid, and who resisted?

Key Terms

immigrant: a person who comes to live permanently in a new country

emigrant: a person who leaves a country to live elsewhere

nativism: a policy or attitude favoring native-born residents over immigrants

Ellis Island: the federal immigration station in New York Harbor that processed most European arrivals after 1892

Angel Island: the immigration station in San Francisco Bay that processed and often detained many Asian arrivals

Chinese Exclusion Act: the 1882 federal law that suspended Chinese labor immigration and barred Chinese residents from naturalized citizenship

assimilation: the process by which a person or group adopts the customs and language of another culture

F restriction steps: 1882, the Chinese Exclusion Act suspended Chinese labor immigration and barred Chinese residents from naturalized citizenship. 1885, contract laborers recruited abroad were barred. 1917, a literacy test for adults and a barred zone ending most immigration from Asia. 1921 and 1924, quota laws cut immigration sharply, with the smallest quotas for southern and eastern Europeans.

Station answer: inspectors held the chalk, the manifest check, and the power to exclude or detain, and Angel Island questioning decided individual cases for months.
