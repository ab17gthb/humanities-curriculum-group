Guided Notes: Native Americans and the Closing Frontier

Lesson question: Why did the United States pursue assimilation, and what did Native peoples lose, resist, and preserve?

Unit question: How did industrial growth transform American life, and who gained, who paid, and who resisted?

Key Terms

assimilation: the process by which a person or group is made to adopt the customs and language of another culture

allotment: the division of tribal land into individual parcels under federal policy

Dawes Act: the 1887 federal law that divided reservation land into individual allotments and sold surplus land to non-Native buyers

reservation system: federal policy that confined Native nations to defined areas of land under federal supervision

boarding school: a school that removed Native children from their families to suppress Native languages and cultures

Ghost Dance: a religious movement that promised renewal for Native peoples and spread across western reservations in 1889 and 1890

Wounded Knee: the 1890 killing of Lakota men, women, and children by United States troops in South Dakota

At a Glance, reservation system: Goal: keep Native peoples in fixed places under federal control while opening the West to settlement. Outcome: nations confined to a fraction of their homelands, dependent on rations and agents.

At a Glance, boarding schools: Goal: remake Native children as English speaking farmers and Christians. Outcome: children separated from families, languages punished, some children died; some graduates later defended their nations.

At a Glance, Dawes Act: Goal: teach private property and farming and end tribal landholding. Outcome: about 90 million acres lost by 1934 and the tribal land base broken up.

At a Glance, Wounded Knee: Goal: disarm Big Foot's band and stop the Ghost Dance. Outcome: about 250 to 300 Lakota killed after surrendering.

Exit ticket, question 3: stated goal, to remake Native people as farmers, Christians, and English speakers and end tribal cultures; accept any one documented effect (land loss, family separation, language suppression).
