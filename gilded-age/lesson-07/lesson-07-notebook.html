Teacher Answer Key

Policy or eventWhat the government said it would doWhat it actually didHow Native peoples responded
Reservation systemProtect Native nations on defined land while the West was settled in an orderly way.Confined nations to a fraction of their homelands, often poor land, under agents who controlled rations and movement. Treaties were broken when settlers wanted the land, as with the Black Hills in 1877.Armed defense by the Lakota, Cheyenne, Nez Perce, and Apache into the 1880s; after surrender, treaty claims pressed in courts and petitions.
Boarding schoolsEducate Native children and prepare them to join American society as farmers and Christians.Removed children hundreds of miles from home, cut their hair, required uniforms and English, and punished Native languages. Some children died of disease at school.Families taught language and ceremony at home. Some graduates, and writers such as Zitkala-Sa, used English and print to defend Native nations and expose school conditions.
Dawes Act and allotmentGive each family private property, teach farming and independence, and end tribal landholding.Sold surplus land to non-Native buyers and broke up the tribal land base. About 90 million acres passed out of Native hands between 1887 and 1934.Leaders protested allotment, challenged losses in court, and worked to hold remaining land together despite parcels being divided among many heirs.
Ghost Dance and Wounded KneeKeep order on reservations and disarm groups officials considered dangerous.Troops surrounded Big Foot's band at Wounded Knee Creek on December 29, 1890, and killed about 250 to 300 Lakota men, women, and children after the band had surrendered.The Ghost Dance itself was a form of hope and renewal under loss. Lakota families preserved memory of Wounded Knee and their language and ceremonies through the years that followed.

The pattern. Strong answers name the shared structure: each policy was announced in the language of protection, order, or help, and each one in practice took land, children, food, or life while weakening tribal self government. A second shared point deserves credit: in every row, Native peoples found a way to respond, and tribal nations, languages, and ceremonies survived.
