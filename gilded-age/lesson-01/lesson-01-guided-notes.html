Guided Notes: The Engines of Industry

Lesson question: Why did railroads become the engine of a national industrial economy?

Unit question: How did industrial growth transform American life, and who gained, who paid, and who resisted?

Key Terms

transcontinental railroad: a railroad line connecting the eastern rail network with the Pacific coast

Bessemer process: a method for making steel quickly and cheaply by blowing air through molten iron

refinery: a plant where crude oil is separated into useful products such as kerosene

corporation: a business owned by shareholders and treated by law as distinct from its owners

capital: money or property used to start or expand a business

telegraph: a system for sending coded messages over wires

standard time: a system of uniform time zones that replaced local solar time

Exit ticket 1. The 1869 line was the start of a network. Mileage grew from about 30,000 miles in 1860 to about 193,000 by 1900, and the network is what created one national market.

Exit ticket 2. Standard gauge solved the unloading and reloading of freight where lines with different gauges met. Standard time replaced local solar time with four uniform zones, which made schedules and connections across lines possible.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that ties the railroads to at least two system facts. The strongest answers argue that the railroads created the national market, set its standards, created the demand for cheap steel, carried oil in both directions, coordinated business by telegraph, and spread the corporation as the way to raise capital.
