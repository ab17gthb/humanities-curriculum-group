Guided Notes: Race, Politics, and Protest

Lesson question: How did Jim Crow and economic protest shape the politics that Progressives inherited?

Unit question: How did industrial growth transform American life, and who gained, who paid, and who resisted?

Key Terms

Jim Crow: the system of state and local laws and customs that enforced racial segregation in the South

segregation: the separation of people by race in public facilities, schools, and transportation

disfranchisement: the denial of the right to vote through law or practice

Plessy v. Ferguson: the 1896 Supreme Court decision that upheld segregation under the doctrine of separate but equal

Atlanta Compromise: Booker T. Washington's 1895 speech proposing that Black southerners accept segregation for the present and build economic strength first

Populist Party: the People's Party, founded in 1892 to represent farmers and laborers against banks, railroads, and corporations

free silver: the proposal to coin silver without limit at a ratio of 16 to 1 with gold, expanding the money supply

Exit ticket 1. Plessy made segregation constitutional while disfranchisement removed the Black vote that might have challenged segregationist governments.

Exit ticket 2. Wells demanded direct public confrontation of lynching and defense of civil and political rights; Washington publicly accepted segregation for the present in exchange for economic opportunity.

Exit ticket 3. Bryan's defeat and the Populist endorsement of a Democratic nominee dissolved the party as an organization, but its platform demands were later adopted in part by Progressives and enacted into law.
