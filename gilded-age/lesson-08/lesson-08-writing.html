Writing Task: Two Fronts, One Inheritance

The prompt. The 1890s produced two protest fronts: the struggle against Jim Crow and the farm protest that became Populism. Write a short essay arguing which front left the deeper mark on the politics Progressives inherited after 1900. Keep the two fronts distinct in your evidence, use at least three of the evidence cards below, and answer the strongest argument for the front you rank second.

The cards are summaries drawn from the lesson reading. Write the history in your own sentences and quote facts, not the cards.

Card 1: Plessy and Disfranchisement

On May 18, 1896, the Supreme Court upheld Louisiana railway segregation in Plessy v. Ferguson under separate but equal. Southern states paired segregation with poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses. Black voter registration fell sharply, and segregation stood as constitutional law for decades.

Card 2: Wells Against Lynching

Ida B. Wells documented lynching in Southern Horrors (1892) with names, dates, and newspaper records, showing that many victims had committed no crime. She campaigned in the North and in Britain after a mob destroyed her Memphis newspaper office. No federal anti-lynching law passed in her era.

Card 3: Washington's Bargain

In the Atlanta Compromise (1895), Booker T. Washington proposed that Black southerners accept segregation for the present and build economic strength first. White leaders praised the speech. Black critics argued that without the vote, economic gains had no protection.

Card 4: The Populist Program

The Omaha Platform (1892) demanded free silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads, and direct election of senators. The Populist Party collapsed after William McKinley's victory over William Jennings Bryan in 1896, but Progressives later enacted versions of several platform demands.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A clear ranking of one front as the deeper inheritance, with a reason that can be tested against evidence, such as duration in law or adoption into later reform programs. A both mattered equally answer does not meet the prompt.
  • At least three cards used accurately, with the fronts kept distinct: Cards 1 to 3 belong to the Jim Crow front and Cards 4 to the farm front, and a strong essay uses cards from both sides of its ranking.
  • Accurate anchor facts: Plessy decided May 18, 1896, on Louisiana railway segregation; Southern Horrors in 1892; the Atlanta Compromise in 1895; the Omaha Platform in 1892; McKinley, not Bryan, won in 1896.
  • A real counterargument answered, not dismissed. The strongest counter to a Jim Crow ranking is Card 4: the Populist program was literally enacted later. The strongest counter to a Populist ranking is Card 1: Plessy governed American law for generations and Progressives mostly left it standing.
  • For the Jim Crow ranking, credit answers that note the inheritance was a limit as much as a legacy: Progressive reform advanced while segregation and disfranchisement remained in place.

Sample claim. The Jim Crow front left the deeper mark because its settlement was written into constitutional law in 1896 and survived the whole Progressive Era, while the Populist program, though widely adopted, was absorbed and completed within a generation.
