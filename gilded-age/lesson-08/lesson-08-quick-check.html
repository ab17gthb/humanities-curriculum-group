Quick Check: Race, Politics, and Protest

Directions: Study the photograph, then answer all five questions. Use facts from the lesson.

1. What Jim Crow practice does the photograph show, and what doctrine did the Supreme Court use in Plessy v. Ferguson to uphold laws of this kind?

2. Name two devices southern states used to disfranchise Black voters, and explain how one of them worked.

3. In one sentence each, state the public strategy of Ida B. Wells and the public strategy of Booker T. Washington.

4. A farmer owes $1,000. Crop prices fall by half, but the debt stays $1,000. Why is the debt harder to pay, and what is this process called?

5. What was free silver, which candidate championed it in 1896, and who won that election?

Teacher Answer Key

1. [Blueprint 12] Segregation: separation by race in a public facility, here drinking water in a transportation terminal. In Plessy v. Ferguson (May 18, 1896), the Court upheld Louisiana railway segregation under the doctrine of separate but equal.

2. [Blueprint 12] Any two of poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses. Poll tax: a fee required before voting, which priced out poor Black voters. Literacy test: a reading test graded unfairly by white officials. Grandfather clause: an exemption for men whose grandfathers voted before the Civil War, which protected white voters from the other barriers.

3. [Blueprint 13] Wells: direct public confrontation, documenting lynching with evidence in Southern Horrors (1892) and campaigning for federal action and public opinion against it. Washington: in the Atlanta Compromise (1895), accept segregation for the present and build economic strength first through education, farming, trades, and business.

4. [Blueprint 14] The farmer must sell roughly twice as many crops at the lower price to raise the same $1,000, so the real burden of the fixed debt rises. The process is deflation. This grievance drove the Granger laws and Populist organizing.

5. [Blueprint 15] Free silver: the proposal to coin silver without limit at a ratio of 16 to 1 with gold, expanding the money supply. William Jennings Bryan championed it in 1896, with Populist support. William McKinley won the election.

Teacher note. This check is formative. Items are tagged to blueprint entries 12 to 15 in the key. The common weak answer on question 1 names segregation without the separate but equal doctrine; the common weak answer on question 4 says the farmer simply earns less, without connecting the fixed debt to deflation.
