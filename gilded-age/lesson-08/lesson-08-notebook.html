The pattern. What did Progressives inherit from each of the two protest fronts? Answer in two or three sentences, naming one inheritance from each front.

Teacher Answer Key

SubjectWhat happenedWho acted, and howResult by 1896
Jim Crow systemSouthern states required segregation in schools, facilities, and transportation and disfranchised Black voters with poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses. Lynching enforced the system.White Democratic legislatures and state conventions wrote the laws; the Supreme Court upheld segregation in Plessy v. Ferguson on May 18, 1896, under separate but equal.Segregation and disfranchisement stood as settled law. Black registration had fallen sharply and Black southerners had little political protection.
Black protestLynching and the loss of rights provoked organized Black responses with different public strategies.Ida B. Wells documented lynching in Southern Horrors (1892) and campaigned against it. Booker T. Washington, in the Atlanta Compromise (1895), urged economic strength first while accepting segregation for the present; critics answered that rights could not wait.The argument over strategy continued. No federal anti-lynching law passed, and the protest tradition passed to the next century.
Farm protestFalling prices, debt under deflation, and railroad rates squeezed farmers in the South and West.Farmers organized in the Grange, won Granger laws regulating railroad rates, built Farmers' Alliances, and founded the Populist Party on the Omaha Platform in 1892.The Populists won state and congressional seats and forced monetary and regulation questions into national politics.
Election of 1896Free silver became the central issue. Bryan won the Democratic nomination with his cross of gold speech; the Populists endorsed him.William Jennings Bryan campaigned nationally for free silver. William McKinley ran on sound money with far larger campaign funds.McKinley won. The Populist Party collapsed, but many platform demands later became Progressive reforms.

The pattern. Strong answers state both inheritances: from farm protest, a reform program against concentrated economic power that Progressives later enacted in part; from the struggle against Jim Crow, a legal order of segregation and disfranchisement that most white Progressives left in place, so the work of Wells and other Black protestors remained unfinished.
