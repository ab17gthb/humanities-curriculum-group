Writing Task: Built It, Captured It, or Both?

Directions: Answer the lesson question in one well-built paragraph of eight to twelve sentences. Use the scaffold below to plan, then write your paragraph on the lines of space provided. Your paragraph will be graded on the rubric in the teacher key.

Prompt. Did big business build modern America, capture it, or do both at once?

Step 1: Gather Evidence on Both Sides

Fill in the chart from the lesson reading and the trust hearing. You need at least two facts in each column before you choose a claim. A both-at-once claim uses facts from both columns. A one-sided claim uses one column and must answer the other.

Evidence that big business built modern AmericaEvidence that big business captured modern America

Step 2: Build the Paragraph

Use this structure. The sentence starters are optional; the moves are not.

  • Claim. State your answer in the first sentence. Starter: Big business did both at once: it built modern America and, in building it, captured...
  • Evidence 1. Give a specific fact, with the company or method named. Starter: Carnegie built... by vertical integration, which meant...
  • Explanation 1. Say what that fact proves about your claim.
  • Evidence 2. Give a second specific fact, from the other column if your claim is both at once. Starter: At the same time, Rockefeller's trust...
  • Explanation 2. Say what that fact proves about your claim.
  • Counterargument and answer. Name the strongest argument against your claim and answer it. Starter: A defender would answer that..., but that argument leaves out...
  • Conclusion. Restate the claim in one sentence that a reader could argue with.

Words you must use accurately: trust, monopoly, and one of vertical integration, horizontal integration, laissez-faire, Social Darwinism, or Sherman Antitrust Act, each with its meaning shown by how you use it.

trust: a combination of corporations that limits competition within an industry

monopoly: control of an industry by one company or combination, with no effective competition

Teacher Key and Rubric

A Strong Model Paragraph (for teacher reference)

Big business did both at once: it built modern America and, by the same methods, captured much of it. Carnegie built a steel industry of national scale through vertical integration, owning the mines, transport, and mills, which cut costs and supplied the rails and beams the country ran on. Rockefeller's trust shows the capture inside the building: Standard Oil combined rival refiners under trustees until it held a near monopoly in refining, and it held that position in part through secret railroad rebates that lowered its costs below any rival's. A defender would answer, in the language of laissez-faire, that government should not regulate business and that low kerosene prices prove the system served the public. Prices did fall, but a price set by a single seller can rise again once competition is gone, which is what consumers testified when rivals closed. Congress recognized the problem in the Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890, even if weak courts kept the law from working for a decade. The building was real, and so was the capture; they were the same process seen from two sides.

Rubric

Full credit (10 points) should be awarded as follows:

  • Claim (2 points). The first sentence answers the prompt directly with one of the three positions, stated so a reader could disagree with it.
  • Evidence (3 points). At least two specific facts from the lesson, with companies, methods, or the act named accurately (for example, Carnegie and vertical integration; the 1882 trust and trustees; secret rebates; about 90 percent of refining; the 1895 sugar decision).
  • Explanation (2 points). Each fact is connected to the claim in the student's own words, not left as a list.
  • Counterargument (2 points). The strongest opposing argument is stated fairly (efficiency and low prices, laissez-faire, or Social Darwinism as fitness) and answered with evidence.
  • Vocabulary (1 point). Trust and monopoly are used accurately, plus one further term from the required list used in a way that shows its meaning.

Common deductions. A paragraph that praises or condemns big business without a named method or fact earns no evidence points. Use of Social Darwinism as a doctrine of Charles Darwin is a content error; it was a period argument made by figures such as William Graham Sumner and popularizers of Herbert Spencer. A both-at-once claim with evidence from only one column earns at most half of the evidence points.
