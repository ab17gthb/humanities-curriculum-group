Guided Notes: The Workers' World

Lesson question: Why did workers organize, and why did collective action so often end in defeat?

Unit question: How did industrial growth transform American life, and who gained, who paid, and who resisted?

Key Terms

union: an organization of workers formed to bargain with employers as a group

strike: a work stoppage meant to pressure an employer to meet workers' demands

collective bargaining: negotiation between an employer and workers organized as a group

injunction: a court order that requires or forbids a specific action

Knights of Labor: a national labor organization of the 1870s and 1880s that sought broad reform and organized many kinds of workers

American Federation of Labor: a federation of craft unions founded in 1886 that focused on wages, hours, and working conditions

child labor: paid work by children, often in factories, mines, mills, and street trades

At a glance, Haymarket: wanted the eight-hour day; defeated because the bomb linked labor with violence in public opinion, leaders were tried and hanged, and the Knights were blamed and collapsed.

At a glance, Homestead: wanted to keep their union and stop a wage cut; defeated because state militia protected the reopened plant and replacement workers until the strike collapsed.

At a glance, Pullman: wanted wages restored or rents lowered and union recognition; defeated because a federal injunction and federal troops broke the boycott and Debs was jailed.

Exit ticket 1: accept any two of low wages that could not support a family, long hours and machine-set pace, dangerous work with no compensation, and the fact that a single worker could be replaced at will. Exit ticket 2: Haymarket, public opinion and the courts; Homestead, the state militia; Pullman, the federal injunction, federal troops, or the courts. Exit ticket 3: the AFL organized narrower craft unions, focused on wages, hours, and conditions, and kept dues and strike funds, so it did not overextend the way the Knights did.
