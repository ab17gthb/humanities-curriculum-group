Teacher Answer Key

StrikeWhat the workers wantedWhat the other side didHow it ended, and what it proved
Haymarket 1886The eight-hour day, the goal of the national labor campaign of May 1886. The Haymarket meeting itself protested police killings of strikers the day before.Police moved to disperse the meeting. After the bomb, the state tried eight anarchist leaders for murder on the theory that their speeches encouraged the act, although the bomb thrower was never identified.Four leaders were hanged. Labor was linked with violence in public opinion, the Knights of Labor were blamed although they had not called the meeting, and Knights membership collapsed within a few years.
Homestead 1892To keep the Amalgamated Association as their union at the Carnegie Steel works and to stop Frick's wage cut.Frick locked the workers out and brought in about 300 armed Pinkerton guards by barge. After the Pinkertons surrendered, Pennsylvania sent about 8,000 state militia.The plant reopened with replacement workers under military protection and the strike collapsed by November 1892. The union was destroyed and steel stayed largely unorganized for a generation.
Pullman 1894Restoration of cut wages, or lower rents in the company town, and recognition of their union. The American Railway Union backed them with a boycott of Pullman cars.The company refused to negotiate. A federal court issued an injunction against the boycott, and President Cleveland sent federal troops to Chicago over the governor's objection.The boycott was broken, Debs was jailed for violating the injunction, and the American Railway Union was destroyed. The strike ended in complete defeat.

The pattern. Strong answers name the combination: employers supplied private guards, replacement workers, and lawyers, while governments supplied the militia, troops, and court orders, and newspapers turned public opinion against the strikers. No union of the period could defeat all three at once.
