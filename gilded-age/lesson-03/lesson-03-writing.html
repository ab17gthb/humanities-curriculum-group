Writing Task: Why Did Collective Action Fail?

The prompt. Between 1886 and 1894, three of the largest labor actions in American history ended in complete defeat. Write an essay arguing which single cause best explains why collective action so often failed in this period: the force employers could hire, the power of government, or the unions' own weaknesses. Use evidence from at least two of the three evidence cards below, and answer the strongest argument for a cause you did not choose.

The cards summarize the three defeats from the lesson reading. Write the history in your own sentences and quote facts, not the cards.

Card 1: Haymarket, 1886

A bomb was thrown at police dispersing a Chicago labor meeting during the eight-hour campaign. The thrower was never identified. Eight anarchist leaders were tried for murder on the theory that their speeches encouraged the act, and four were hanged. The Knights of Labor had not called the meeting, but the public linked labor with violence, and Knights membership collapsed within a few years.

Card 2: Homestead, 1892

Henry Clay Frick, running Carnegie Steel while Carnegie was in Scotland, locked out the steelworkers and brought in about 300 armed Pinkerton guards by barge. Workers defeated the Pinkertons in a day-long battle on July 6 and forced their surrender. Pennsylvania then sent about 8,000 state militia. The plant reopened with replacement workers under military protection, and the strike collapsed by November.

Card 3: Pullman, 1894

Pullman cut wages but not rents in his company town. The American Railway Union boycotted trains carrying Pullman cars and tied up rail traffic across much of the country. A federal court issued an injunction against the boycott, President Cleveland sent federal troops to Chicago over the governor's objection, and Eugene V. Debs was jailed for violating the injunction. The union was destroyed.

Write two to three paragraphs, about 300 to 400 words. Open with your claim, support it from two cards, and close by answering the counterargument you planned above.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that names one cause and ranks it above the others. An essay that lists three causes without ranking them does not answer the prompt.
  • Accurate evidence from at least two cards. Government power: the Haymarket murder trials, the Pennsylvania militia at Homestead, the federal injunction and troops at Pullman. Employer force: the Pinkerton barges, the lockout, replacement workers. Union weakness: the Knights blamed for an act they did not organize, the American Railway Union destroyed by a boycott it could not sustain.
  • A real counterargument, answered rather than dismissed. The strongest answers usually concede a second cause and show it depended on the first: Pinkertons lost their battle, and the strike still failed once the militia arrived, which is evidence for government power over employer force.
  • Accuracy discipline: no claim that a Haymarket defendant threw the bomb (the thrower was never identified), no claim that Carnegie directed Homestead in person (Frick ran the works; Carnegie was in Scotland), and no claim that Debs was jailed for leading a strike (he was jailed for violating the injunction).

Scoring guide. 4: ranked claim, two cards used accurately, counterargument answered. 3: ranked claim and accurate evidence, counterargument named but weakly answered. 2: evidence present but no ranking, or ranking with only one card. 1: summary of the strikes without an argument. Blueprint 6.
