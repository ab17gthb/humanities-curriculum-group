The shape of the year

Every unit in the course is built to the same measure: eight lessons, a review day, and a 50-point unit test built to a published blueprint, with every test item tagged in the key. Taught one lesson per school day, a unit runs about ten school days, so the fifteen teaching units come to roughly 150 school days before review. Three review units sit alongside the sequence for the end of the course or the end of a part: an early review over the first six units, a modern review over the last nine, and a full course review over all fifteen.

Those counts are the building blocks, not a fixed calendar. Schools start and end on different dates, lose different days, and test on different schedules. The year planner takes your first and last days, your days off, and your state test or AP exam date, then lays the sequence across the days you actually have and tells you plainly whether it fits.

Two shapes: the survey and the two-part course

A survey course runs all fifteen units in order across the year. A two-part course often divides around 1877: the first part ends with Reconstruction, and the second runs from the Gilded Age to the present. A modern course can also open with one earlier unit of the teacher's choosing, Reconstruction, for example, before starting the modern sequence. The year planner supports all three shapes, and this guide's sequence is the same in each of them; only the starting point and the calendar change.

Fitting the guide to your calendar

In the year planner, you enter your term dates and any state test or AP exam date, and the planner proposes a pacing timeline for each unit across your school days. Days off move the work around them; nothing is scheduled on a day school is out. Inside a unit, the unit planner lays your chosen materials onto the unit's days: a day off pushes the remaining work later, a sub day holds its date and the test day while teaching nothing new, and anything that cannot fit before the test lands in a named overflow instead of disappearing. As you pick materials, a coverage check keeps score against the unit test's blueprint, so you can see before the test arrives whether your picks cover everything on it. Plans print, export to a calendar file, and, when you sign in, save to your account and follow you between devices.

The Progressive Era unit is free, planning included, so you can run this whole flow on a real unit before deciding anything: read the unit, pace it in the unit planner, and lay it into your year.
