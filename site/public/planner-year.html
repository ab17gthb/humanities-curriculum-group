How the counts work

Unit lengths are lesson-days, where one lesson-day is one lesson in a period of about 45 minutes. A block of about 90 minutes fits about two lessons per meeting, so a unit of 18 lesson-days takes about 9 block meetings. On an A/B rotation this course meets every other school day, and units land on meeting days only.

US History can be organized as the full survey in one year or in two parts. Many two-part courses divide around 1877, near the end of Reconstruction, but starting points vary. Pick the shape closest to the course you teach and the unit list, calendar, totals, and exports follow it. A Part 2 plan can also open with one Part 1 unit. Your day counts for units carry across shapes, so you can switch back without losing work.

Grading periods

A period is a named deadline. The planner marks the end date on the calendar and counts the instructional days in each period.

Days off and PD days

Holidays, breaks, PD days, and testing windows. None of these count as instructional days, and every unit reflows around them. The examples below are editable; delete any that do not match your school.

The year at a glance

Each unit is a color. The last day of a unit band is its test day. Days off are hatched and labeled in the list above; grading period ends carry a dark top border; the exam date is ringed in rust. Drag a unit's band onto a new date to pin that unit's start there; the units before it keep their lengths and any gap stays open as buffer. Unpin from the unit list.
