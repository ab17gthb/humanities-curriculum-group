# The Founding — Unit Manifest

**Essential question:** Why did the colonies break with Britain, and what did Americans build in the place of the empire they rejected?

**Course position:** The second unit of the US History course (courseId u2), built eleventh in the build order. It opens with the imperial crisis of 1763 to 1765, exactly where the Colonial America unit closed (the Treaty of Paris, Pontiac's Rebellion, and the Proclamation of 1763 are its premise, not its content), and closes at December 1791 with the new government constituted and the Bill of Rights ratified. The Early Republic unit owns Washington's administration and the politics of the 1790s, and this unit does not open them: the Farewell Address and the Alien and Sedition Acts are named only as a closing pointer in Lesson 8. The Revolution is taught with its peoples in the frame throughout, Black Americans, women, and Native nations acting on the record for reasons of their own, and slavery stands where the founding documents themselves put it: in the Declaration's deleted passage and closing charges (Lesson 3), in Dunmore's Proclamation and the freedom petitions (Lesson 5), in Washington's 1786 letter (Lesson 6), and in the Convention's arithmetic (Lesson 7).

**Built:** 2026-10-22. 51 documents, 248 printed pages. 8 slide decks, 226 slides, 34,378 words of speaker notes, 8 printable teacher notes documents (81 pages).

**Topics (6):** 1 The empire after 1763: revenue, reform, and resistance · 2 The road to revolution: protest becomes rebellion, 1773 to 1776 · 3 Independence declared: the ideas and the decision of 1776 · 4 The war for independence: campaigns, allies, and home fronts · 5 The Confederation tested: governing the new republic in the 1780s · 6 The Constitution: framing, ratifying, and the Bill of Rights.

**Test blueprint:** 15 entries, two per lesson except Lesson 8 (one). Entry-to-topic map and per-lesson item assignments are in the skeleton: ~/workspace/curriculum-planner/the-founding-skeleton.md.

**TPSP source plan:** The bank HAS a founding era file: ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/founding.json, era "Revolution and the Founding (1763-1800)", 27 audited records, and this unit quotes from it wherever it carries the text. The premium catalog was checked directly in Firestore (collection psp_premium) for this build, because the Colonial build's skeleton had wrongly reported its era's catalog. Two premium DBQs belong to this unit and are verified: DBQ 1, "Why Did the Colonies Break with Britain?" (document dbq1-breaking-britain, catalog code DBQ176, era "Revolution and the Founding"), 7 documents, every docId confirmed present in founding.json; and DBQ 2, "Whose Voices Counted in the Founding?" (dbq2-whose-voices-counted, DBQ177), 7 documents, every docId likewise confirmed. DBQ 7 (DBQ182) straddles the boundary into the Early Republic and is not a lead here. When the planner integration is built, the picker leads with DBQ 1 (DBQ176), with DBQ 2 (DBQ177) alongside it. This unit's quotations are drawn from the bank's verified records wherever the bank carries the text, and otherwise from the named editions in each lesson's img/SOURCES.md.

**Copyright accounting:** No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this unit. Every quotation is a public domain period text quoted from a named edition: the audited bank records, the Avalon Project at Yale, the National Archives' Charters of Freedom transcriptions, Founders Online, the Wikisource transcription of Dickinson's 1768 collected edition, the Pennsylvania Magazine of History and Biography's Waldo diary edition, and the Gilder Lehrman and Norton transcriptions of the Massachusetts Archives petition copy. Where an edition modernizes spelling, the Source line says so, following the bank records' own practice.

**Definition overlap check:** The other built units' specs and guided notes were searched before the vocabulary was fixed. One carry was owed and was made character for character: "boycott," defined in the Civil Rights unit, whose definition this unit's spec carries unchanged and flags. Terms the Colonial America unit owns (Enlightenment, salutary neglect, Great Awakening, among others) are named in this unit's prose only and are nowhere redefined with different wording; the check and its rule are recorded in the build spec so the Early Republic build can run the same check against this unit.

## Lesson 1 — The Imperial Crisis: Taxation and Resistance

Unit opener. The revenue program through the Massacre and the quiet years. Lesson question: "Why did Britain tax the colonies after 1763, and why did the colonists resist taxes they had never been asked to pay before?"

- Reading: 898 words, 4 pages. Seven sections matching the skeleton's scope: the empire's bill and Grenville's program, the Stamp Act, resistance in argument, combination, and crowd, repeal with the Declaratory Act, the Townshend round and the purse, troops and the Massacre with both accounts open, and the quiet years closing on the committees of correspondence.
- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 7 pages, five stations. Notebook page: 4 pages. Quick check: 3 pages, tagged to blueprint entries 1 and 2 (see the coordinator repair note in Verification). Writing task: 5 pages.
- Deck: 28 slides, speaker notes 139 to 171 words a slide, three giant stat slides (10,000 soldiers; 1766; 5 killed), teacher notes document 10 pages. Images: Revere's Massacre engraving (Library of Congress), the Colonies Reduced cartoon, the 1765 stamp proof sheet (British Library Philatelic Collection), and the 1766 Repeal funeral cartoon (British Museum), all public domain per their file pages.
- Sources: the Stamp Act Congress's Declaration of Rights and Grievances and the Declaratory Act quoted verbatim from the Avalon Project transcriptions; Preston's account and Drowne's testimony from the audited bank records.
- Honest flags: Dickinson's Letters from a Farmer, Letter II, is quoted from the Wikisource transcription of the collected 1768 edition, because the Avalon Project does not carry the full text of the Letters; the substitution is recorded on the station's Source line and in SOURCES.md. Drowne excerpts were chosen from passages whose punctuation carries no em dash, so the verbatim text complies with the unit's zero-dash rule; the choice is disclosed in SOURCES.md. No period image of the Stamp Act Congress exists and none was invented.

- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 9 pages, five stations, all verbatim documents from named editions; no teaching summaries were needed. Notebook page: 2 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, built on the paired Lexington records, tagged to entries 3 and 4. Writing task: 3 pages.
- Deck: 27 slides, speaker notes 140 to 188 words a slide, teacher notes document 10 pages. Images: the Currier Boston Tea Party lithograph of 1846 (captioned as a later imagining everywhere), the Bostonians Paying the Excise-Man print of 1774 (described from the bank record), Doolittle's 1775 Lexington and Concord engravings, and Romans's Charlestown view of about 1775 (Met Open Access).
- Honest flags: no period map of Boston with a public domain basis read on its file page was verified, so Boston geography is taught in text; no Tea Party image from 1773 exists, and the 1846 lithograph and Hewes's text stand in its place. Casualty figures are carried as the standard counts (273 British, about 95 American).

## Lesson 3 — Declaring Independence

Paine, the drafting, and the argument read closely. Lesson question: "What did the Declaration argue, and why did Americans need an argument at all?"

- Reading: 897 words, 4 pages. The closing charges are quoted in the document's own words, including "merciless Indian Savages" and the domestic insurrections charge, and taught plainly against the preamble's equality claim.
- Guided notes: 4 pages. Activity: 10 pages, five stations (longer than the model's because its five quoted records are longer; every page is substantially full). Notebook page: 2 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, tagged to entries 5 and 6. Writing task: 3 pages.
- Deck: 27 slides, speaker notes 129 to 186 words a slide, three giant stat slides, teacher notes document 10 pages. Images: the Dunlap broadside of July 4, 1776 (Beinecke Library), Trumbull's Declaration painting of 1819 captioned as a later commemoration, and the 1791 Paine engraving (Smithsonian, CC0).
- Honest flags: no Common Sense title page image is used; no verified public domain file of the 1776 title page was obtained (two Commons fetches were denied during the build), so the pamphlet is carried by the Paine engraving and by text and stat slides. The bank's Declaration record carries the preamble; the grievances and conclusion in the activity are quoted from the National Archives transcription of the engrossed parchment, the edition named in the document. Paine's sales and the German auxiliaries are labeled as estimates and standard counts as the skeleton directs.

- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 8 pages, five stations, all quoting period texts; no teaching summary was needed. Notebook page: 3 pages. Quick check: 3 pages, tagged to entries 7 and 8. Writing task: 4 pages.
- Deck: 29 slides, speaker notes 124 to 174 words a slide, teacher notes document 10 pages. ## Lesson 7 — The Constitution and the Great Compromises

Philadelphia, the plans, the deadlock, and slavery's arithmetic. Lesson question: "How did fifty five men in one room design a government strong enough to act and limited enough to trust?"

- Reading: 897 words, 5 pages. The electoral college is stated with its tangled reasons and the three-fifths advantage named as an effect of the design, with the deck notes recording scholarly argument over its weight rather than settling it.
- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 9 pages, five stations; the annotated draft station is a labeled description quoting the bank record's text, with the record's own correction (the Convention met in the Pennsylvania State House, not Carpenters' Hall) stated on its face. Notebook page: 2 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, built on the Preamble, tagged to entries 13 and 14. Writing task: 3 pages.
- Deck: 28 slides, speaker notes 125 to 183 words a slide, four giant stat slides (55; 5 to 4; 3/5; 39), teacher notes document 10 pages. Images: the engrossed Constitution's first page (National Archives), Stuart's Washington (Met Open Access), Vanderlyn's Madison of 1816, Birch's State House view of 1798 to 1800, and the Dunlap and Claypoole printing's first page (Library of Congress).
- Honest flags: no image of the Convention in session exists from 1787, and Christy's 1940 canvas, a later imagining, was deliberately not used anywhere. No Franklin portrait was downloaded; Franklin is carried in his own words. One coordinator repair touched this lesson's deck and is recorded under Verification below.
