Guided Notes: The Constitution and the Great Compromises

Lesson question: How did fifty five men in one room design a government strong enough to act and limited enough to trust?

Unit question: Why did the colonies break with Britain, and what did Americans build in the place of the empire they rejected?

Key Terms

Constitutional Convention: the meeting at Philadelphia from May to September 1787, called to revise the Articles of Confederation, which instead wrote the Constitution of the United States

Virginia Plan: the plan proposed at the Convention by Virginia's delegation, drafted chiefly by James Madison, for a strong national government with a legislature apportioned by population

New Jersey Plan: the plan proposed at the Convention by the small states, which kept a one house legislature with equal votes for every state under a strengthened Articles of Confederation

Great Compromise: the Convention's settlement, also called the Connecticut Compromise, which apportioned the House of Representatives by population and gave every state two senators in the Senate

Three-Fifths Compromise: the Convention's settlement that counted each enslaved person as three fifths of a person when apportioning representatives and direct taxes among the states

federalism: the division of power between a national government and state governments, each supreme in its own sphere, which the Constitution established in place of the Confederation

separation of powers: the division of the federal government into legislative, executive, and judicial branches, each with its own powers and the means to check the others, so no single branch could rule alone

Summary table. The room: fifty five delegates from twelve states met at Philadelphia from May 25, 1787 under a secrecy rule, Washington presiding in silence and Madison recording every night; Rhode Island sent no one, and the Convention's orders ran only to revising the Articles. The deadlock: the Virginia Plan apportioned both houses by population and the New Jersey Plan kept one vote per state, and on July 2 the vote on equal representation in the second house tied. The settlement: on July 16, by five states to four, the Great Compromise apportioned the House by population and gave every state two senators, so the union carries a people's house and a states' house in one frame. The count: each enslaved person counted as three fifths of a person for representation and direct taxes, the slave trade protected from prohibition until 1808 and escapees made returnable on claim, with slavery named nowhere in the document. The close: the Convention refused a bill of rights on September 12 with no state in favor, opened the document with We the People, and thirty nine delegates signed on September 17 after Franklin asked each man to doubt a little of his own infallibility.

Exit ticket 1. The Great Compromise apportioned the House of Representatives by population and gave every state two senators in the Senate.

Exit ticket 2. 240,000 + (100,000 x 3/5) = 240,000 + 60,000 = 300,000. The sixty thousand added by the fraction were enslaved people who could not vote, hold office, or leave, and whose count raised the political weight of the states that held them.
