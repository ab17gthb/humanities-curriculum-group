Writing Task: Strong Enough to Act, Limited Enough to Trust

The prompt. How did fifty five men in one room design a government strong enough to act and limited enough to trust? Answer in a developed essay of about 300 words, argued from the records in Philadelphia in Five Records and from the reading. A strong answer does more than list the design's parts. It states where the government's strength came from, it follows one compromise through both its settlement and its price, and it answers the strongest claim on the other side: that the Convention simply built the strongest government it could and called the result limited afterward.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The mandate and the room, 1787: Congress called the Convention to revise the Articles of Confederation, for that sole and express purpose. Fifty five delegates from twelve states met at Philadelphia from May 25 under a rule of secrecy, Washington presiding in silence, Madison recording the debates each night.

2. The Virginia Plan: drafted chiefly by Madison and presented by Edmund Randolph, it built a legislature of two houses apportioned by population or wealth, empowered to strike down conflicting state laws, choosing the executive and the judiciary.

3. The deadlock and the Great Compromise: on July 2 the vote on equal representation in the second house tied. On July 16 the Convention adopted the Connecticut settlement by five states to four, the House apportioned by population and elected by the people, the Senate holding two members from every state.

4. The Three-Fifths Compromise: each enslaved person counted as three fifths of a person for representation and direct taxes alike. A state holding one hundred thousand enslaved people gained sixty thousand in its count, though none of those counted could vote, and the advantage ran through the House count into the electoral college.

5. The design: power divided by federalism between nation and states, and by the separation of powers among legislative, executive, and judicial branches, laced with a veto and its override, Senate consent, impeachment, and judges holding office during good behavior. Amendments require two thirds of Congress and three fourths of the states.

6. The refusal and the name: on September 12 the Convention refused George Mason's motion for a bill of rights with no state voting in favor. The Committee of Style opened the document with We the People of the United States, and the Atlantic slave trade stood protected from prohibition until 1808.

7. Franklin's close, September 17: Franklin confessed that parts of the Constitution did not have his approval, asked every member to doubt a little of his own infallibility and sign, and judged the sun on Washington's chair to be rising. Thirty nine delegates signed. Mason, Gerry, and Randolph declined.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your evidence, answer the other side in full, and end by restating what the design was built to do and what it cost.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that names both the design's strength and its limit or price, not a claim that the framers were wise or that the Constitution is balanced in general. (Blueprint 13, 14)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, drawn from at least three different records, including one compromise (bank 3 or 4) and the close (bank 6 or 7). Names, dates, and counts must be right: May 25; July 2 tied; July 16 by five states to four; September 12; sixty thousand per one hundred thousand held; thirty nine signing. (Blueprint 13, 14)
  • One compromise followed through both halves of its record: what it settled and what it cost. An essay that treats the three fifths count as a curiosity, or the Great Compromise as a courtesy between large and small states, cannot earn full credit under this prompt. (Blueprint 14)
  • The other side answered from the record. The strongest form of the answer grants what is true in the claim, that the Convention exceeded its mandate in the first week and wrote no bill of rights at all, and then shows the limits the record contains as deliberate construction: a Senate the large states did not want, an executive fenced from Congress and from direct election alike, powers enumerated rather than general, and an amendment rule that made the document hard for any single generation to move.

Model opening for teacher reference. The strength came first and it came from the Virginia Plan: a national legislature apportioned by population, empowered against the states, choosing the executive and the judiciary. The limits were built in the same summer and by the same hands, each time the strength met a price it had to pay. On July 16 the large states accepted a Senate in which Delaware counted as Virginia did, because the alternative on July 2 had been dissolution. A full model continues in this pattern, carries one compromise through its settlement and its cost, answers the claim that the limits were decoration with the Senate, the enumeration, and the amendment rule, and closes on the price the documents themselves record.

Common weak answers. A balance claim (the framers balanced power carefully) never names a vote, a date, or a count, and cannot use the bank. A greatness claim (the Constitution was a miracle of wisdom) ignores Franklin's own closing words, in which he approves parts of it not at all. An essay that prices no compromise, or that prices the three fifths count without its arithmetic, has described the design without judging it, and the prompt asks for a judgment.
