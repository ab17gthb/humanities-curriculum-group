## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- No period image of a ratifying convention in session was downloaded for this lesson. The conventions left no period print of their debates, and the deck uses the Federal Pillars score cartoon and text and stat slides for Section E rather than a later artist imagining of the Richmond or Poughkeepsie debates.
- No portrait of Madison, Hamilton, or the writer called Brutus was used. Brutus identity is an attribution, not a settled fact, and no likeness of him exists from the period. The deck uses the title page of The Federalist for the Federalist sections instead.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- James Madison, Federalist No. 10, 1787. Audited TPSP bank record federalist-10; edition named in the record: Congress.gov, spelling modernized as the record states. Quoted verbatim, with the record own ellipses.
- James Madison (attributed), Federalist No. 51, 1788. Audited TPSP bank record federalist-51; edition named in the record: Congress.gov. Quoted verbatim, with the record own ellipses. Note on completeness: the bank excerpt closes before the essay final passage on justice as the end of government. The lesson names that closing judgment in paraphrase only and does not quote it, since the audited record does not carry the lines.
- Brutus (attributed to Robert Yates), Brutus No. 1, 1787. Audited TPSP bank record brutus-no-1-1787; edition named in the record: Constitution.org. Spelling is as the edition carries it, including controul and uncontroulable. Quoted verbatim, with the record own ellipses.
- Amos Doolittle (attributed), The Looking Glass for 1787, 1787. Audited TPSP bank record looking-glass-1787; the record text field is the Library of Congress image description, used in the activity as a description and labeled there as the record text, not as a quotation.
- First Congress, Bill of Rights, 1791. Audited TPSP bank record bill-of-rights; edition named in the record: National Archives. The record carries excerpts: Amendments I, II, IV, and X. The activity quotes only those four amendments and says on its face that the record carries excerpts. The Ninth Amendment is treated in the reading, guided notes, and deck notes as paraphrase of its standard text and is not placed in quotation marks anywhere in the lesson.
