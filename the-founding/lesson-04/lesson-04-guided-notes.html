Guided Notes: The War for Independence

Lesson question: How did the United States survive a war against the world's strongest empire, and win it?

Unit question: Why did the colonies break with Britain, and what did Americans build in the place of the empire they rejected?

Key Terms

Continental Army: the army created by the Second Continental Congress in June 1775, commanded by George Washington, drawn from all thirteen colonies and enlisted for terms beyond the militia's short service

militia: the local armed force of a colony or state, made of citizen soldiers who served part time near home and could be called out in emergencies

George Washington: the Virginia planter and former militia colonel chosen in 1775 to command the Continental Army, whose strategy of keeping the army in being carried the cause through eight years of war to Yorktown

Valley Forge: the Pennsylvania camp where the Continental Army wintered in 1777 and 1778, enduring hunger and disease while Baron von Steuben drilled it into a professional force

Battle of Saratoga: the American victory in the fall of 1777 in which a British army under General Burgoyne surrendered in upper New York, the win that persuaded France to ally openly with the United States

Treaty of Alliance: the treaty of February 1778 by which France recognized American independence and joined the war against Britain, bringing French money, ships, and soldiers to the American cause

Battle of Yorktown: the last major battle of the war, in the fall of 1781, in which Washington's army and a French fleet trapped General Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia, and forced his surrender

G. The trap and the surrender: Washington and Rochambeau marched their armies from New York to Virginia in the summer of 1781 while the French fleet under de Grasse sealed the Chesapeake against rescue or escape. Some seventeen thousand American and French troops closed around Cornwallis's eight thousand, the figures stated as the standard counts. The bombardment opened on October 9, and Cornwallis surrendered his army on October 19, 1781. The tune story: tradition holds that the surrendering band played The World Turned Upside Down, and the reading labels the story tradition, not record. The peace: the Treaty of Paris of September 3, 1783, by which Britain recognized the United States as free, sovereign, and independent, set its boundary at the Mississippi River and the Great Lakes, and granted fishing rights off Newfoundland, with terms on debts and Loyalist property the states would honor unevenly.

The Winning at a Glance. The army survives: Long Island, New York, and Fort Washington lost in 1776, the army kept in being, Trenton on Christmas night 1776 and Princeton on January 3, 1777, and Valley Forge endured in the winter of 1777 and 1778. Proof and alliance: Burgoyne's surrender at Saratoga on October 17, 1777, some 5,800 men by the standard count, and the Treaty of Alliance of February 6, 1778, bringing French money, ships, and soldiers, with Spain following in 1779. The South answers: Charleston lost in May 1780 and a second army destroyed at Camden that August, then King's Mountain in October 1780, Cowpens in January 1781, and Greene's campaign of retreat through Guilford Courthouse. Yorktown and the peace: de Grasse's fleet sealing the Chesapeake, some seventeen thousand allied troops around Cornwallis's eight thousand (standard counts), the surrender of October 19, 1781, and the Treaty of Paris of September 3, 1783.

Exit ticket 1. The Continental Army is the army created by the Second Continental Congress in June 1775, commanded by George Washington, drawn from all thirteen colonies and enlisted for terms beyond the militia's short service. The militia is the local armed force of a colony or state, made of citizen soldiers who served part time near home and could be called out in emergencies. The Continental served for longer terms and campaigned away from home; the militia served part time near home.

Exit ticket 2. On October 17, 1777, Burgoyne surrendered his army at Saratoga, some 5,800 men by the standard count, an American army beating a British army in the field and taking it whole. That proof persuaded France to sign the Treaty of Alliance on February 6, 1778, recognize the United States, and enter the war with money, ships, and soldiers.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that joins the survival to the winning. Example: The United States survived because Washington kept an army in being through the defeats of 1776 and the Valley Forge winter, and Saratoga turned that survival into a French alliance. At Yorktown the French fleet sealed the Chesapeake while some seventeen thousand American and French troops (standard counts) closed around Cornwallis's eight thousand, and he surrendered on October 19, 1781. The Treaty of Paris of September 3, 1783 closed the war on the terms the army and the alliance had won.
