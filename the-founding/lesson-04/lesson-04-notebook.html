Teacher Answer Key

Turning pointWhat happenedWhat it changed
Trenton and Princeton, December 1776 to January 1777After losing Long Island, New York City, and Fort Washington, the army retreated across New Jersey with enlistments expiring. On Christmas night 1776 Washington recrossed the Delaware through floating ice with some 2,400 men and took the Hessian garrison at Trenton at dawn. Slipping around Cornwallis by night, he won again at Princeton on January 3, 1777.The map barely changed, and the army did not dissolve. Belief, recruits, supplies, and credit flowed back to a cause that had been weeks from dissolving, and Washington's strategy of keeping an army in being had its proof that it could work.
Saratoga, October 1777Burgoyne marched south from Canada to seize the Hudson valley while Howe sailed instead to take Philadelphia. Burgoyne's supply line stretched, his army was cut up at Bennington and Freeman's Farm, and on October 17, 1777 he surrendered at Saratoga, some 5,800 men by the standard count.An American army had beaten a British army in the field and taken it whole. The victory was the war's diplomatic instrument: it gave France the proof it had been waiting for that the Americans could win a campaign.
The alliance and Valley Forge, winter 1777 to 1778On February 6, 1778 France signed the Treaty of Alliance, recognized the United States, and entered the war with money, ships, and soldiers; Spain followed in 1779. The same winter, at Valley Forge, some two thousand men died of disease, hunger, and exposure, the figure stated as an estimate, while Steuben drilled the survivors into a professional force.The war widened past what Britain could hold, to the Caribbean, Gibraltar, and India, and the army that survived the winter fought the British to a standstill at Monmouth in June 1778. Survival and alliance arrived in the same season and made each other usable.
The southern turn, 1780 to 1781Charleston surrendered in May 1780 with an entire American army, the worst American defeat of the war, and a second army was destroyed at Camden that August. Militia destroyed a Loyalist force at King's Mountain in October 1780, Morgan wrecked Tarleton's legion at Cowpens in January 1781, and Greene wore Cornwallis down through Guilford Courthouse and a campaign of retreat.The British southern strategy, built on Loyalist strength, lost the forces it rested on and the men Britain could not replace. Cornwallis abandoned the Carolinas and marched into Virginia, to the coastal position at Yorktown where a fleet could trap him.
Yorktown and the peace, 1781 to 1783Washington and Rochambeau marched from New York to Virginia while de Grasse's French fleet sealed the Chesapeake. Some seventeen thousand American and French troops closed around Cornwallis's eight thousand, the figures stated as the standard counts, and Cornwallis surrendered on October 19, 1781. Preliminary articles followed in November 1782.The Treaty of Paris of September 3, 1783 converted the victory into a settlement: Britain recognized the United States as free, sovereign, and independent, with a boundary at the Mississippi and the Great Lakes and fishing rights off Newfoundland. Its terms on debts and Loyalist property would be honored unevenly.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers join the survival to the winning and carry a labeled figure. Example: The United States survived because Washington kept an army in being after the disasters of 1776, restocked the cause at Trenton and Princeton, and held the army together through Valley Forge, where some two thousand men died, the figure stated as an estimate. It won because Saratoga, where Burgoyne surrendered some 5,800 men by the standard count on October 17, 1777, brought the French alliance of February 6, 1778, and because the southern turn of 1780 and 1781 put Cornwallis at Yorktown, where a French fleet and some seventeen thousand allied troops (standard counts) forced his surrender on October 19, 1781. The Treaty of Paris of September 3, 1783 wrote the result into law.
