# Image Sources for The Founding Lesson 4, The War for Independence

All images are public domain works. Downloads were made on October 22, 2026, from the Wikimedia Commons 1600 pixel renditions of the files cataloged below, after each file page's public domain tagging was verified through the Wikimedia Commons API during this build. Every downloaded file was opened and verified with PIL before use. Document copies are stored in the lesson's assets folder and img folder; deck versions are cropped to portrait (1046 by 1500) or to 16:9 for the title slide and stored in slides-build/img; no other alteration.

## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Address of Congress, activity Station 1 and quick check: Continental Congress, Address of the Congress to the Inhabitants of the United States, May 9, 1778, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record address-congress-inhabitants-1778 in founding.json, whose sourceNote names the Gilder Lehrman Institute. The passages beginning "FRIENDS AND COUNTRYMEN," "WE were then quite defenceless," and "However unequal this contest" are quoted verbatim from the bank record's text field, including its spelling (defenceless, cloathing, thro'). The bank record's own cuts, marked [...] in the record, fall outside the passages quoted in the activity; in the quick check, one cut between quoted passages is marked with an ellipsis and disclosed in the source line. The broadside and church reading circumstances stated in the activity and in the quick check background come from the bank record's headnote.
- Treaty of Alliance, activity Station 2: Treaty of Alliance between the United States and France, February 6, 1778, Articles 1, 2, and 8, as transcribed by the Avalon Project, Yale Law School (avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fr1788-2.asp), whose source line names Treaties and Other International Acts of the United States of America, edited by Hunter Miller, Government Printing Office, 1931. Article 2 and Article 8 are quoted entire. Article 1 is quoted from its operative clause, with the cut marked by an ellipsis; the Avalon transcription's spelling is preserved in the quoted text (independance, Gouvernement, obtain'd). The omitted opening of Article 1 contains the Avalon file's misprints of the word between, which is one reason the quotation begins at the operative clause, with the cut disclosed.
- Valley Forge record, activity Station 3: Albigence Waldo, diary entries of December 14 and December 21, 1777, from Valley Forge, 1777-1778. Diary of Surgeon Albigence Waldo, of the Connecticut Line, as printed from the manuscript in the Pennsylvania Magazine of History and Biography (the edition's headnote states the manuscript was contributed by Amos Perry of the Rhode Island Historical Society), read in the JSTOR Early Journal Content full text reproduced at the Internet Archive (archive.org/stream/jstor-20085750). The December 14 passage on the army growing sickly and the December 21 passage on provisions and the cry of "No Meat! No Meat!" are quoted verbatim from that text, with spelling preserved (Alacrity, hutts, thro'). Cuts inside the December 21 entry are marked with ellipses. Two passages of the diary that contain the edition's em dashes are not quoted, because this unit's documents carry no em dashes; the quoted passages were chosen from parts of the same entries that the edition prints without them, and no wording was altered to achieve that.
- Southern campaign record, activity Station 4: Daniel Morgan to Nathanael Greene, January 19, 1781, as transcribed by Founders Online, National Archives, from the copy enclosed in Greene to George Washington, January 24, 1781 (George Washington Papers, founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/03-30-02-0222). The opening report of the victory, the losses on both sides, the list of what fell into American hands, and the closing comparison of numbers are quoted verbatim from that transcription, spelling preserved (compleat, Tarlton as the edition prints it in the letter copy, Musquets). The National Park Service transcription of Morgan's letter, checked during this build, reads 27 commissioned officers taken where the Founders Online copy enclosed to Washington reads 29; this lesson quotes the Founders Online copy and its figure, and the difference between the copies is recorded here rather than smoothed over. The list's entry counting seventy Black people among the captured property is quoted as written and addressed in the station question and the teacher key as the period paperwork's treatment of people as spoils.
- Treaty of Paris, activity Station 5: The Definitive Treaty of Peace, Paris, September 3, 1783, Articles 1, 4, 7, and 8, as transcribed by the Avalon Project, Yale Law School (avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_Century/paris.asp), whose source line names Treaties and Other International Acts of the United States of America, edited by Hunter Miller, Government Printing Office, 1931. Articles 1, 4, and 8 are quoted entire. Article 7 is quoted through the withdrawal clause, with the cut at its end marked by an ellipsis. Spelling follows the edition (Brittanic).
- No teaching summary is used at any station in this lesson. Both records the skeleton allowed to fall back to a labeled summary, the Valley Forge record and the southern campaign record, were verified verbatim from the named editions above, so all five stations quote period texts.

## Figures stated as standard counts or estimates

- Trenton crossing force, Christmas night 1776: some 2,400 men, as the reading states it.
- Saratoga surrender, October 17, 1777: some 5,800 men, stated everywhere in this lesson as the standard count.
- Valley Forge deaths, winter 1777 and 1778: some two thousand men, stated everywhere in this lesson as an estimate.
- Yorktown forces, October 1781: some seventeen thousand American and French troops around Cornwallis's eight thousand, stated everywhere in this lesson as the standard counts.
- Cowpens losses, January 17, 1781: stated only as Morgan reported them in his letter of January 19, 1781, quoted at Station 4, and attributed to him wherever they are used.
- Tradition labeled as tradition: the story that the surrendering British band at Yorktown played The World Turned Upside Down is labeled tradition, not record, in the reading, the guided notes key, and the deck notes, every time it appears.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- The Delaware crossing and Trenton: no period image of the crossing exists; the lesson uses Leutze's 1851 painting, captioned with its date and its commemorative character, in the reading and the deck.
- Cowpens and King's Mountain: no verified public domain period image of either battle was located in this build, and no later painting of either was substituted. Both battles are taught in text, in Morgan's own report, and in the lesson's maps of other campaigns.
- Saratoga battlefield mapping: the Faden map of Burgoyne's campaign (1780, Library of Congress) is used in the quick check and the deck rather than a modern diagram, and Trumbull's 1821 commemoration carries the surrender itself in the deck, captioned as a later work in the notes.
- Cornwallis's portrait and the Yorktown surrender ceremony: Trumbull's 1820 Rotunda painting is used in the deck with its commemorative character stated in the notes, including that Cornwallis, pleading illness, sent General O'Hara to the ceremony the painting stages.
- No catalog number is stated anywhere in this lesson for any image beyond the Library of Congress item numbers recorded above, which are taken from the Library's own item records as carried on the Wikimedia Commons file pages.
