Guided Notes: The Imperial Crisis: Taxation and Resistance

Lesson question: Why did Britain tax the colonies after 1763, and why did the colonists resist taxes they had never been asked to pay before?

Unit question: Why did the colonies break with Britain, and what did Americans build in the place of the empire they rejected?

Key Terms

Stamp Act: the British law of 1765 that taxed printed paper in the colonies, including newspapers, legal documents, and playing cards, collected through stamps sold by royal distributors

repeal: the canceling of a law by the authority that made it, as Parliament canceled the Stamp Act in 1766 under pressure from colonial resistance and British merchants

Declaratory Act: the British law of 1766, passed the same day as the Stamp Act's repeal, declaring that Parliament had power to make laws for the colonies in all cases whatsoever

Townshend Acts: the British laws of 1767 that taxed imports into the colonies, including tea, glass, lead, paint, and paper, and used the revenue to pay royal governors and judges

boycott: a refusal to buy goods or use services as a form of protest, meant to pressure an institution by withdrawing the money and cooperation it depends on

virtual representation: the British argument that members of Parliament represented all British subjects, including the colonists, whether or not the colonists had voted for them

Boston Massacre: the clash of March 5, 1770, in which British soldiers fired into a Boston crowd that had surrounded and taunted them, killing five colonists

Summary table. The revenue program: a Grenville ministry program of 1764 and 1765 to make the colonies help pay for a standing army of some ten thousand soldiers after a war that had nearly doubled the national debt. The Stamp Act and the answer: the act of March 1765 taxed printed paper directly inside the colonies through stamped paper sold by royal distributors, and the colonies answered with argument in the Virginia Resolves and the Stamp Act Congress, with merchant boycotts and homespun, and with crowd action that drove every distributor to resign. Repeal and the Declaratory Act: Parliament repealed the Stamp Act in March 1766 after British merchants petitioned and Franklin testified, and on the same day claimed in the Declaratory Act full power to make laws binding the colonies in all cases whatsoever. Townshend to the Massacre: the Townshend Acts of 1767 taxed imports and paid royal governors and judges from the revenue, Dickinson answered that a revenue duty was a tax in any form, troops came to Boston in 1768, and on March 5, 1770 soldiers fired into a crowd and killed five men.

Exit ticket 1. The Stamp Act taxed printed paper in the colonies, including newspapers, legal documents, and playing cards, and the tax was collected through stamped paper sold by royal distributors.

Exit ticket 2. Repeal canceled one tax while the Declaratory Act, passed the same day in March 1766, declared that Parliament had power to make laws for the colonies in all cases whatsoever. The colonists had won the Stamp Act and lost the principle, so the next revenue law could reopen the whole quarrel, as the Townshend Acts did in 1767.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names the war debt and the standing army as the reason Britain taxed, the absence of colonial representatives in Parliament and the break with assembly taxation as the reason the colonists resisted, and one later event, such as the Townshend Acts, the posting of troops to Boston, the killings of March 5, 1770, or the keeping of the tea duty in 1770.
