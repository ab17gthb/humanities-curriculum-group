Teacher Answer Key

Part of the argumentWhat it claimsThe words or evidence it usesWhat it settles or leaves open
The truthsAll men are created equal and hold rights by their Creator's gift, not by any government's grant.Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness, named among certain unalienable Rights in the preamble.It settles the standard every later claim is measured against. It leaves open who counts among the men the sentence names.
The contractGovernments are instituted to secure those rights and draw their just powers from the consent of the governed. A government destructive of those ends may be altered or abolished.The social contract stated in one sentence of the preamble, paired with the caution against changing long established governments for light and transient causes.It settles the rule for a lawful break. It leaves the proof to the charges, which must show a long train of abuses, not a single injury.
The chargesGeorge III alone broke the contract, by misgovernment at home and then by war on the colonies.Refused assent to laws, dissolved assemblies, taxes without consent, coasts plundered, towns burned, mercenaries transported, insurrections excited, and Native nations named in the document's words as merciless Indian Savages.It settles the defendant: every charge names the king, because the bond being dissolved ran to the Crown. It leaves Parliament, the target of a decade of protest, nearly unmentioned.
The deleted chargeJefferson's draft charged the king with waging cruel war against human nature itself in the slave trade and with blocking colonial laws meant to end it.A market where MEN should be bought and sold, in the draft's capitals, and the king exciting enslaved people to rise and purchase the liberty he had deprived them of.Congress struck it before adoption, at the insistence of South Carolina and Georgia, with northern delegates tied to the trade unwilling to press it.
The conclusionThe colonies are free and independent states, absolved from all allegiance, with full power to levy war, conclude peace, and contract alliances.The signers' mutual pledge of their lives, their fortunes, and their sacred honor, made by men most of whom signed on August 2, 1776.It settles the legal claim in the document's own terms. In British law the same words were a confession of treason, which is what the pledge answers.
The standardThe Declaration stated a measure for every American government to come: rule by consent for the protection of equal rights.Abigail Adams had asked that March that the new laws remember the ladies, warning that women would not hold themselves bound by laws in which they had no voice. Her husband laughed.It freed no one by itself and settled nothing about who the people were. The words stayed on the record, wide enough for the excluded to quote back.

The argument in one sentence. Strong answers name the rule and the audience. The Declaration argued from natural rights and consent that the king's long train of abuses had dissolved the bond, and it had to argue in public because the resistance had spent a decade claiming British liberties, the delegates acted on their colonies' instructions, foreign powers would not aid an unexplained rebellion, and the signers were publishing what British law called treason.
