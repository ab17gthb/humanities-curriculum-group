# Image Sources: Lesson 3, Declaring Independence

All images are public domain or CC0 as stated on their source pages, as recorded below. Downloads were made on October 22, 2026, and each file was verified as a valid image with PIL. Original downloads live in img/. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/.

## Dunlap broadside of the Declaration of Independence (dunlap-broadside.jpg; deck crops title-169.jpg and broadside-port.jpg; document copy in assets/)
- Title: Yale Dunlap Broadside, the Dunlap broadside copy of the Declaration of Independence
- Creator: Printed by John Dunlap (1747-1812), Philadelphia, the night of July 4, 1776
- Date: 1776, as stated on the file page
- Holder: Yale University Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library, per the file page description
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yale_Dunlap_Broadside.jpg (file page description and licensing read via the Wikimedia Commons file page as mirrored at en.wikibooks.org)
- Public domain basis: The file page marks the image as a faithful photographic reproduction of a two-dimensional public domain work of art, and states that the work of art itself is in the public domain and that the reproduction is therefore also considered to be in the public domain in the United States. Original file 1,224 by 1,536 pixels, as stated on the file page. Used as the deck title image (16:9 crop of the top of the sheet), as a portrait crop in the deck, and in the activity, Station 3.

## Declaration of Independence, by John Trumbull (trumbull-declaration.jpg; deck crop trumbull-port.jpg)
- Title: Declaration of Independence
- Creator: John Trumbull (1756-1843), as stated on the Commons file page
- Date: 1819, as stated on the Commons file page
- Holder: United States Capitol, per the Commons file page (collection: United States Capitol; source/photographer: US Capitol)
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Declaration_of_Independence_(1819),_by_John_Trumbull.jpg
- Public domain basis: A painting of 1819 by an artist who died in 1843, photographed by the US Capitol and hosted on Wikimedia Commons; copyright in the work has long expired. Original file 3,000 by 1,970 pixels, per the file page's file history. The file page description states plainly that the painting does not represent a real ceremony and that the characters portrayed were never in the same room at the same time. The lesson captions it wherever it appears as a later commemoration, painted 1819, and the deck notes repeat that dating. Used in the deck only, as the section D divider image, in a portrait crop centered on the committee presenting the draft.

## Thomas Paine, engraving after Charles Willson Peale (paine-engraving.jpg; deck crop paine-port.jpg; document copy in assets/)
- Title: Thomas Paine
- Creator: Unidentified artist, copy after Charles Willson Peale (1741-1827), as stated on the Smithsonian object page
- Date: 1791, as stated on the Smithsonian object page
- Holder: National Portrait Gallery, Smithsonian Institution, object number NPG.78.24, per the object page credit line
- Source: https://npg.si.edu/object/npg_NPG.78.24 (image delivered by the Smithsonian at https://ids.si.edu/ids/deliveryService?id=NPG-7800043B_1)
- Public domain basis: The object page's Restrictions and Rights field states CC0, the Smithsonian Open Access public domain dedication. Medium: engraving and etching on paper. Used as the section B divider image in the deck and in the activity, Station 1.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- The title page of Common Sense (1776): no verified public domain file of the 1776 title page was obtained for this build. The pamphlet is represented by the Paine engraving above and by text and stat slides, and no title page image is used or implied.
- The engrossed Declaration parchment: a candidate Wikimedia Commons file fetch was denied during this build, so no photograph of the engrossed copy is used. The lesson uses the Dunlap broadside, the Declaration's first printing, for its document imagery instead.
- King George III: no portrait is used. The king's side of the argument is taught through his Proclamation of Rebellion, quoted at Station 4 of the activity.
- The signing of the Declaration: no image is used, because the familiar signing scene exists only in later artists' imaginings. Trumbull's 1819 painting, used above, shows the committee presenting the draft, and it is captioned as a commemoration with its date.

## Quoted source log for this lesson
- Thomas Paine, Common Sense, 1776 (Project Gutenberg; from the February 14, 1776 Philadelphia edition). Audited TPSP bank record paine-common-sense-1776, founding.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 1, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Thomas Jefferson, Jefferson's Rough Draft of the Declaration, 1776 (H. A. Washington, ed., The Writings of Thomas Jefferson, 1853; via Teaching American History). Audited TPSP bank record jefferson-rough-draft-1776, founding.json. The bank record presents selected grievances from the draft, including the deleted anti-slavery passage, with its cuts marked, and the activity presents them as the bank record presents them, at Station 2.
- Thomas Jefferson (for the Continental Congress), Declaration of Independence, 1776 (National Archives). Audited TPSP bank record declaration-of-independence, founding.json. The preamble passage is quoted from the bank record in the activity, Station 3, and in the quick check, Passage A, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Declaration of Independence, 1776, grievances and conclusion, from the National Archives transcription of the engrossed parchment (a transcription of the Stone Engraving of the parchment on display at the National Archives Museum, spelling and punctuation reflecting the original), https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript. Quoted in the activity, Station 3, and in the quick check, Passage B, with cuts marked with ellipses. The bank record carries the preamble only, so the grievances and conclusion are quoted from this named National Archives transcription, the same edition the bank record names.
- King George III, Proclamation Suppressing Rebellion and Sedition, 1775 (Encyclopedia Virginia; from the National Archives). Audited TPSP bank record king-proclamation-rebellion-1775, founding.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 4, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Second Continental Congress, Olive Branch Petition, 1775 (National Humanities Center; spelling modernized, as the bank record's sourceNote states). Audited TPSP bank record olive-branch-petition-1775, founding.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 5, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- No teaching summaries are used in this lesson's activity. Every station quotes a named edition.
