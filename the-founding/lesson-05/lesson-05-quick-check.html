Quick Check: Dunmore's Proclamation, 1775

Directions: Read the proclamation excerpt and the background, then answer the questions.

Background. By November 1775, Virginia's royal governor, Lord Dunmore, had been driven from Williamsburg and was operating from a British warship off Norfolk with only a few hundred men. On November 7, 1775, he proclaimed martial law in the colony and made the offer printed below. The text is quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record dunmore-proclamation-1775; the edition named in that record is the Pennsylvania Journal printing of December 6, 1775, by way of the Gilder Lehrman Institute. Cuts are marked with ellipses.

"And I do hereby farther declare all indented servants, Negroes, or others (appertaining to rebels) free, that are able and willing to bear arms, they joining his Majesty's troops, as soon as may be, for the more speedily reducing this Colony to a proper sense of their duty, to his Majesty's crown and dignity. . . ."

"Given on board the ship William, off Norfolk, the 7th of Nov."

1. State the offer in the proclamation's own terms. Who is declared free, what two things must they be able and willing to do, and what limit is carried in the parenthesis, appertaining to rebels?

2. Dunmore signed from a ship with a few hundred men. Explain what he needed, and state what happened to most of the men who reached his lines and joined the Ethiopian Regiment.

3. Define Dunmore's Proclamation as the guided notes state it, and then set the American record beside it: how did American policy on Black soldiers change, and how many Black men served in American forces, by the standard estimate?

4. The war ended for some three thousand Black Loyalists in the Book of Negroes. State what that book recorded, where and when it was made, and why the record was kept.

Teacher Answer Key

1. State the offer and its limit. Dunmore declares free "all indented servants, Negroes, or others (appertaining to rebels)" who are "able and willing to bear arms," joining his Majesty's troops. The conditions are capacity and enlistment: the freedom is for those who can fight and do fight. The parenthesis limits the offer to people held by rebels. Enslaved people and servants of Loyalists are not freed, and people unable to bear arms are not reached by the offer at all. Credit requires the offer, both conditions, and the limit. (Blueprint 9)

2. What did Dunmore need, and what happened to his recruits? Dunmore had been driven from Williamsburg to a warship off Norfolk with only a few hundred men. He needed soldiers, and the offer was his recruiting instrument and a blow at the rebels' labor force in one act. Some hundreds of enslaved Virginians reached his lines and served in an Ethiopian Regiment, and most of them died of smallpox and fever in the camps. Both halves are required: the need explains the offer, and the camps answer it. (Blueprint 9)

3. Define the proclamation and set the American record beside it. The definition must match the guided notes: the order of November 1775 by Virginia's royal governor, Lord Dunmore, offering freedom to enslaved people and indentured servants held by rebels if they left their masters and bore arms for the king. The American side at first barred Black men from the Continental Army on slaveholders' protests, then enlisted them as manpower ran short. Some five thousand Black men served in American forces, the standard estimate, among them men of the First Rhode Island Regiment of 1778, raised substantially from enslaved men who earned freedom by enlisting. (Blueprint 9)

4. State what the Book of Negroes recorded and why. At New York in 1783, as the British evacuated, the Book of Negroes recorded the names, ages, and former owners of some three thousand Black Loyalists leaving with the British. It was kept so their freedom would hold against American owners' claims under the peace treaty: the written record was the proof that each named person had come into British lines under wartime offers and was free. Many of those families later moved on to Sierra Leone in 1792. (Blueprint 10)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the close reading diagnostic: students who miss the parenthesis have read a general emancipation into a limited military offer. Questions 2 and 3 predict success on the blueprint 9 half of the unit test, and question 4 on the blueprint 10 half, because each requires the offer, its conditions, and the records at the evacuation in the student's own words.
