# Image Sources: Lesson 5, Winning and Losing: The War's Home Fronts and Its Peoples

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages or by the holding institution. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/ and img/.

## A Society of Patriotic Ladies, at Edenton in North Carolina (edenton-tea-party.jpg; deck crops edenton-port.jpg and title-169.jpg)
- Title: A Society of Patriotic Ladies, at Edenton in North Carolina
- Creator: Mezzotint attributed to Philip Dawe; printed in London for R. Sayer and J. Bennett
- Date: March 25, 1775
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division; digital ID cph.3a15070 (loc.gov/resource/cph.3a15070/)
- Source: Wikimedia Commons file "Edenton-North-Carolina-women-Tea-boycott-1775.jpg", downloaded from commons.wikimedia.org via Special:FilePath and verified as a readable JPEG of 1280 by 1696 pixels
- Public domain basis: A work first published in London in 1775. The Wikimedia Commons file page records the print as public domain in the United States, as a work published before January 1, 1929, and public domain in its country of origin and other countries and areas where the copyright term is the author's life plus 100 years or fewer.
- Use rule for this lesson: the print is a British satire of the Edenton women's boycott resolve of October 1774, and it is captioned as a satire wherever it appears, on the title slide, in Section C of the deck, and on the notebook page. It is not presented as a likeness of the meeting.

## Deborah Sampson (deborah-sampson.jpg; deck crop sampson-port.jpg)
- Title: Deborah Sampson
- Creator: Engraving by George Graham, from a drawing by William Beastall, after a painting by Joseph Stone; published by H. Mann
- Date: 1797, the frontispiece of Herman Mann, The Female Review: Life of Deborah Sampson, the Female Soldier in the War of Revolution (Dedham, Massachusetts, 1797)
- Holder: Library of Congress, LCCN2002725275; a copy of the biography is held by the Massachusetts Historical Society
- Source: Wikimedia Commons file "Deborah Sampson LCCN2002725275.jpg", downloaded from commons.wikimedia.org via Special:FilePath and verified as a readable JPEG of 979 by 1536 pixels
- Public domain basis: An engraving published in 1797; copyright expired, and the file is recorded on Wikimedia Commons as public domain, by way of the Library of Congress.
- Use rule for this lesson: the portrait appears at Station 4 of the activity and on one deck slide, captioned with its 1797 publication. The station's text beside it is a labeled teaching summary, and the portrait is not used as decoration anywhere else in the lesson.

## Texts quoted in this lesson, with their editions
- Lord Dunmore's Proclamation, November 7, 1775: quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record dunmore-proclamation-1775 in founding.json; the edition named in that record is the Pennsylvania Journal printing of December 6, 1775, by way of the Gilder Lehrman Institute. Cuts are marked with ellipses in the activity and the quick check.
- Abigail Adams to John Adams, March 31, 1776: quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record abigail-adams-remember-the-ladies in founding.json; the edition named in that record is the Massachusetts Historical Society, Adams Family Papers. Original spelling preserved. John Adams's reply of April 14, 1776 is described in the activity key in paraphrase: he wrote that he could not but laugh at her extraordinary code of laws (Adams Family Papers, Massachusetts Historical Society).
- Petition for freedom to the Massachusetts Council and House of Representatives, January 13, 1777: quoted verbatim from the transcription of the copy in the Massachusetts Archives Collection, volume 212 (Revolution Resolves, 1777), as published by the Gilder Lehrman Institute of American History. A manuscript copy of the petition is held and transcribed by the Massachusetts Historical Society (Collections Online, item 557). The Massachusetts Historical Society viewer page could not be loaded during this build, so every quoted passage was checked against two published transcriptions of the same Archives copy, the Gilder Lehrman Institute's and the one printed in Jill Lepore, These Truths, source notes (W. W. Norton), which agree in the wording of every passage quoted here. The eight signers are named in the activity with the spellings carried in the Library of America transcription of the same Archives copy: Lancaster Hill, Peter Bess, Brister Slenser, Prince Hall, Jack Pierpont, Nero Funelo, Newport Sumner, and Job Look. The Gilder Lehrman transcription spells several of the names differently, as manuscript copies of the petition differ. Cuts are marked with ellipses.
- Deborah Sampson's service (Station 4 of the activity) is a teaching summary written for this lesson from the public record and is labeled as a teaching summary on its face. The enlistment in May 1782 as Robert Shurtliff, the light infantry company of the 4th Massachusetts Regiment, the honorable discharge in October 1783, and the later Massachusetts pension rest on the army's and the state's records. The detail of the musket ball in the thigh treated by Sampson herself rests on Herman Mann's 1797 biography and is labeled in the summary as her biographer's account.
- The Haudenosaunee decision and the Sullivan expedition (Station 5 of the activity) is a teaching summary written for this lesson from the public record and is labeled as a teaching summary on its face. No period text is quoted in that station.
- Figures labeled as estimates wherever they appear, as the unit skeleton directs: some five thousand Black men in American service (the standard estimate), perhaps a fifth of the white population Loyalist (the conventional estimate), some sixty thousand or more Loyalists leaving at the peace (an estimate), some three thousand Black Loyalists recorded in the Book of Negroes, about forty towns burned in 1779, and some four thousand men on the Sullivan expedition.
- The Molly Pitcher story is taught in the reading, the guided notes, and the deck with the seam between record and legend showing, as the unit skeleton directs: Mary Ludwig Hays carrying water at Monmouth in 1778 is the documented record, and the name Molly Pitcher is later tradition.

## Images searched for and not used
- A page of the Book of Negroes (1783) was searched for at the Library of Congress, the National Archives, and Wikimedia Commons. No verifiable public domain copy was reached and downloaded in this build, so the ledger is taught in words and in the 60,000 stat slide, and no catalog number is claimed for it.
- A period map of the Sullivan campaign country and a portrait of Joseph Brant were searched for in the same collections. No file for either subject was verified as public domain and downloaded for this lesson in this build, so Section E of the deck is taught with text and stat slides rather than with an unverified image.
- No image of violence is used anywhere in this lesson, and no later artist's imagining of Molly Pitcher, Margaret Corbin, or the Sullivan expedition is used. The war's peoples are shown only in the two period prints listed above, each captioned with its maker, its date, and its purpose.
