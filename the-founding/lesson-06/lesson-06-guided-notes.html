Guided Notes: The Confederation and the Road to Philadelphia

Lesson question: Why did the government the Revolution built first fail to hold the union together?

Unit question: Why did the colonies break with Britain, and what did Americans build in the place of the empire they rejected?

Key Terms

Articles of Confederation: the first constitution of the United States, adopted by Congress in 1777 and ratified in 1781, which joined the thirteen states in a league that left most power with the states

confederation: a league of independent states that keep their sovereignty and join for limited common purposes, the form of union the Articles of Confederation created

requisition: a request by Congress under the Articles of Confederation asking a state to supply money or troops, which the state could lawfully refuse or ignore

Land Ordinance of 1785: the Confederation Congress's law for surveying the western lands into townships and lots and selling them to raise revenue, which set the grid still visible across the Midwest

Northwest Ordinance: the Confederation Congress's law of 1787 for governing the territory north of the Ohio River, which barred slavery there and promised its territories statehood on equal terms with the original states

Shays's Rebellion: the uprising of 1786 and 1787 in western Massachusetts, led in part by the former captain Daniel Shays, in which indebted farmers closed courts to stop foreclosures before the state militia put them down

Annapolis Convention: the meeting of September 1786, attended by five states, called to discuss trade among the states, which instead called for a convention at Philadelphia to repair the federal government

Summary table. The design: a firm league of friendship adopted in 1777 and ratified in 1781, in which sovereign states held one vote each and Congress could make war and treaties but could not tax, regulate commerce, or compel a state. The empty treasury: revenue by requisition produced a small fraction of what Congress asked by the middle of the 1780s, the army went unpaid, and Washington's persuasion, not any power of the government, settled the officers at Newburgh in 1783. The ordinances: the land cessions created a national domain, the Land Ordinance of 1785 set its survey grid and school sections, the Northwest Ordinance of 1787 promised equal statehood and barred slavery north of the Ohio, and Virginia's Statute of 1786 disestablished a church, while the Ordinance's territory rested on land its Native owners had never sold. The road to Philadelphia: Mount Vernon in 1785, Annapolis in September 1786 calling for a Philadelphia convention, Shays's Rebellion that winter, Congress's endorsement in February 1787 for the sole and express purpose of revising the Articles, and fifty five delegates from twelve states.

Exit ticket 1. The Articles kept sovereignty in the states and gave Congress no power to tax or compel, because the generation that wrote them had fought a distant legislature's taxes and meant to build a center too weak to tax them the same way.

Exit ticket 2. Achieved: the Land Ordinance of 1785 and the Northwest Ordinance of 1787, which surveyed a national domain and set the path from territory to equal statehood while barring slavery north of the Ohio (Virginia's Statute for Religious Freedom, 1786, also earns credit as the era's work). Could not do: collect its own revenue, as the failed requisitions and the Newburgh crisis of 1783 show, or enforce the peace treaty, as Britain's held posts and Spain's closure of the Mississippi in 1784 show.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that joins the design to the record and uses two dates. The strongest answers run the league adopted in 1777 and ratified in 1781, revenue by requisition failing through the 1780s, the treaty unenforced and the Mississippi closed in 1784, set against the ordinances of 1785 and 1787 as proof the failure was one of powers rather than of talent, and close on Annapolis in 1786 and Congress's February 1787 endorsement of a convention to revise the Articles.
