## Quotation log

### Articles of Confederation (audited bank record articles-of-confederation-1777; edition: Second Continental Congress, Articles of Confederation, 1777, Road to the Civil War, as the record's sourceNote names it)
- Quoted in the activity, Station 1: Article II entire; Article III entire; Article VIII in two passages (the common treasury supplied by the several states, and the taxes laid and levied by the state legislatures), with the cut between them marked by ellipses; Article XIII's amendment clause, opening cut marked by ellipses. Quoted in the quick check background: Article II entire, and short passages of Articles VIII and XIII, presented as the record carries them.
- The reading and guided notes paraphrase the Articles and name their terms (sovereignty, one vote, nine states for major measures) without quotation marks around paraphrase.

### Northwest Ordinance, July 13, 1787 (Avalon Project, Yale Law School)
- Edition used: the Avalon Project transcription, avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/nworder.asp, whose stated source is Documents Illustrative of the Formation of the Union of the American States, Government Printing Office, 1927, House Document No. 398, selected, arranged and indexed by Charles C. Tansill. The builder read the full transcription.
- Quoted in the activity, Station 2: Article 1 entire; Article 3 in its schools passage and the opening of its good faith passage, cut marked by ellipses; Article 5's statehood passage, opening and closing cuts marked by ellipses; Article 6 entire, including its proviso. Quoted in the writing task evidence bank: Article 5's equal footing phrase, Article 6's slavery bar, both as the transcription carries them.

### Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom (audited bank record virginia-statute-religious-freedom-1786; edition: Virginia General Assembly, Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom, January 16, 1786, Code of Virginia, as the record's sourceNote names it)
- Quoted in the activity, Station 3: the compelled contributions passage and the civil rights passage, each a continuous excerpt of the record's text with the record's own bracketed cuts shown as ellipses, and the enacted clause entire. Quoted in the writing task evidence bank: the enacted clause's opening and the civil capacities phrase.

### Washington to John Francis Mercer (audited bank record washington-mercer-slavery-1786; edition: George Washington, letter to John Francis Mercer, September 9, 1786, Founders Online, National Archives, as the record's sourceNote names it)
- Quoted in the activity, Station 4: the refusal passage, a continuous excerpt of the record's text ("I never mean ... to possess another slave by purchase; it being among my first wishes to see some plan adopted, by the legislature by which slavery in this Country may be abolished by slow, sure, & imperceptable degrees"), spelling as the record carries it, including "imperceptable"; and the debt sentence from later in the same letter ("I must sell Land or Negroes to discharge"), a continuous excerpt.

### Summary and teaching records, labeled on their faces
- Activity Station 5 (Shays's Rebellion) is a teaching summary written for this lesson from the public record, labeled as a summary on its face. No sentence in it is presented as a period quotation. Its facts: the Massachusetts hard money tax policy, the court closings at Northampton, Worcester, and Springfield from August 1786, Daniel Shays's service at Bunker Hill and Saratoga, the privately subscribed militia, the four deaths at the Springfield arsenal on January 25, 1787, and the eased taxes and Governor Bowdoin's defeat that followed.
- No Washington letter of fall 1786 on the disorders is quoted anywhere in this lesson. The reading paraphrases the urgency Shays's Rebellion gave the reform movement without putting words in Washington's mouth, because no verified Founders Online text of those letters was fetched for this build.

## Figures stated with their precision
- Requisitions: the states by the middle of the 1780s sent a small fraction of what Congress asked. No percentage is stated anywhere in this lesson, because the surviving state by state accounts do not support one honest figure.
- Delegates: fifty five delegates from twelve states attended the Philadelphia convention; Rhode Island sent none. Standard counts.
- Shays's Rebellion deaths at the Springfield arsenal: four men, January 25, 1787. Standard count.
- Statehood threshold in the Northwest Ordinance: sixty thousand free inhabitants, from the ordinance's own text, Article 5.
- Survey figures (townships of six miles square, thirty six sections of one square mile, one section reserved for schools) are from the Land Ordinance of 1785 as the reading states them.

## Gaps recorded honestly
- No public domain period image of the Newburgh camp, of the Annapolis Convention, or of the Mount Vernon Conference was verified in this build, so Sections B and G of the deck use text and stat slides for those subjects rather than a later artist's imagining.
- No public domain period image of the Springfield arsenal or of the court closings was verified; Station 5 and Section F use the 1787 Bickerstaff's Boston Almanack woodcut of Shays and Shattuck instead, captioned as an almanac print.
- No portrait of Daniel Shays from life exists; the almanac woodcut is a printed likeness, and SOURCES.md records it as such.
