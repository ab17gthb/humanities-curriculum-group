Massachusetts, 1786 to 1787Watch. Congress could neither supply troops nor pay for a force.Act in a domestic crisis inside a state.The crisis was settled by the state: a privately financed militia, four dead at Springfield, then eased taxes and a new governor. Its national achievement was the lesson it taught, which sent fifty five delegates to Philadelphia.

Both sides of the decade in one paragraph. Strong answers hold the two columns together. The weakness was structural and dated: a Congress built in 1777 without a tax power was, by the middle of the 1780s, receiving a small fraction of its requisitions, unable to pay the army whose officers Washington calmed at Newburgh in 1783, and unable to open the Mississippi Spain closed in 1784. The achievement was legislative and just as real: the same Congress surveyed a continent in 1785 and, on July 13, 1787, wrote the Northwest Ordinance, promising equal statehood and barring slavery north of the Ohio, while Virginia's legislature passed Jefferson's Statute in 1786. Full credit requires the student to say why both are true at once: the Articles withheld the powers of compulsion while leaving the powers of legislation for a domain and leaving the states free to legislate like founders, so the decade failed at governing and succeeded at lawmaking in the same years.
