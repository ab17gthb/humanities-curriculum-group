## The Bostonians Paying the Excise-Man, 1774 (exciseman-1774.jpg; deck crop exciseman-port.jpg; used in the activity)
- Title: The Bostonian's Paying the Excise-Man, or Tarring and Feathering
- Creator: Philip Dawe (attributed)
- Date: published at London, October 31, 1774
- Holder: Gilder Lehrman Collection (GLC04961.01), as recorded in the audited TPSP bank record bostonians-paying-exciseman-1774; image file as carried by Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Philip_Dawe_%28attributed%29%2C_The_Bostonians_Paying_the_Excise-man%2C_or_Tarring_and_Feathering_%281774%29_-_02.jpg
- Public domain basis: Public domain (work published in 1774; creator died in the eighteenth century). The bank record's text field, which describes the print, is quoted in the activity caption: Patriots hold customs official John Malcolm down while tea is poured into his mouth and feathers are stuck to his tarred body, beneath a Liberty Tree with the Stamp Act nailed to it upside down, with colonists dumping tea into Boston Harbor in the background. The print compresses two events into one scene, Malcolm's tarring in January 1774 and the Boston Tea Party of December 1773.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- A period map of Boston: no map file with a public domain basis read on its file page or holding record was verified for this build, so no map is used. Boston's geography is taught in text.
- The Boston Tea Party itself has no image from 1773; no artist recorded it that night. The lesson uses the 1846 Currier lithograph, captioned as an 1846 imagining, and Hewes's written recollection instead.
- Portraits of George Hewes, Thomas Hutchinson, and John Parker: the familiar likenesses are later imaginings with no verified public domain basis relied on for this build. All three men are taught in text.
