Teacher Answer Key

StepWho acted, and what they didWhy the next step became harder to avoid
The Tea Act, May 1773Parliament rescued the East India Company by letting it sell tea directly in the colonies through its own agents, cutting out colonial merchants, at a lower price with the Townshend duty still on the tea.Cheap tea became a test of the taxing power itself. Buying it conceded Parliament's right to tax, so resistance organized around stopping the tea from landing at all.
The Tea Party, December 16, 1773When Governor Hutchinson refused the Dartmouth clearance to leave, some one hundred Boston men disguised as Mohawks emptied 342 chests into the harbor in about three hours, harming nothing else.Destroyed property gave Parliament a price to demand and a crime to punish. Hewes's disciplined account and London's mob print fought over what the night meant, but neither side could treat it as a petition any longer.
The Intolerable Acts, spring 1774Parliament closed Boston's port until the tea was paid for, rewrote the Massachusetts charter, allowed officials to be tried outside the colony, widened quartering, and was read as adding the Quebec Act as a fifth blow. Gage arrived with regiments to enforce the acts.A whole town was punished without trial, and a charter was rewritten by a legislature the colonists did not elect. Other colonies read their own charters in Boston's, sent supplies, and prepared to answer together.
Congress and the Association, fall 1774Fifty six delegates from twelve colonies met at Philadelphia, declared their rights in the Declaration and Resolves, and adopted the Continental Association, a boycott enforced by elected committees in every county and town.The committees exercised government power beside the royal government, and Massachusetts armed and drilled under the Suffolk Resolves. Two authorities now claimed the same ground, and both began gathering powder.
Lexington and Concord, April 19, 1775Gage sent some seven hundred regulars to seize the Concord stores. At Lexington the regulars and Parker's company met at dawn; eight Americans died. The column fought back to Boston under fire, with standard counts of some 273 British and about 95 American casualties, and Boston was besieged by nightfall.Blood made the quarrel a war before any declaration. Congress raised the Continental Army under Washington, the militia stood at Bunker Hill, and the king answered the Olive Branch Petition with a Proclamation of Rebellion.

The step that closed the door. Strong answers name the row, state the change in one sentence, and follow it forward. On the Port Act, the change is punishment without trial, and its consequence is continental: the colonies unite because every charter now looks revocable. On Congress and the Association, the change is a second government in fact, and its consequence is the winter of powder, because two authorities cannot both hold the magazines. On Lexington, the change is blood with two irreconcilable records of it, and its consequence is an army and a siege that neither side could simply vote away.
