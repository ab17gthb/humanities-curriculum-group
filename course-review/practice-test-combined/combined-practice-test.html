Part 3: Sources

A1. (3 points) Government is instituted by the people to secure rights they already hold by nature, and it derives its just powers only from their consent. When a government becomes destructive of those ends, the people retain the right to alter or abolish it and institute a new one. All three elements are required: pre-existing rights, consent as the sole source of just power, and the reserved right of revolution.

A2. (2 points) John Locke, whose Second Treatise argued government by consent and a right of resistance to tyranny. Accept any one of: Paine's Common Sense, which carried the argument to a mass readership; the state constitutions and declarations of rights written in 1776 and after; the Declaration's own grievance list, which is the theory applied as an indictment; or the ratification debates, where consent of the governed was the ground both sides claimed.

B1. (3 points) He is asking for the voting rights bill, federal power to strike down the tests and devices by which southern registrars disfranchised Black citizens, eight days after Bloody Sunday at Selma. The framing serves it by denying the opposition its ground: if the problem is not southern but American, then federal intervention is not sectional punishment but the nation honoring its own promise, and members from every region are addressed as owners of the failure and the remedy alike.

B2. (2 points) It claims that the movement's cause has become the nation's official business: a president adopting the protesters' song, in their words, before Congress, announces that the federal government now stands inside the demand rather than refereeing it from outside. The borrowing is the Selma strategy's receipt, the crisis manufactured in Alabama collected as legislation introduced by the president himself.

C1. (3 points) The operative words are 'he determines.' The authorization reaches nations, organizations, or persons the president determines planned, authorized, committed, aided, or harbored in connection with the September 11 attacks. Congress named the category and handed the identification of its members to the executive, which is why the same sentence could later be applied to organizations that did not exist in 2001, in countries far from the attacks, on a president's determination alone.

C2. (2 points) No country named: the war it authorized was never confined to Afghanistan or to al Qaeda's 2001 membership, and was applied across administrations to associated forces in Yemen, Somalia, Syria, and elsewhere. No end date: the authorization remained in force for decades without renewal, so a vote taken in a single week of September 2001 became the standing legal basis of an era's wars, and ending them required no one's permission lapsing but Congress's own repeal, which it did not pass.
