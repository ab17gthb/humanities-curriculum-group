Band 2 Review Packet: A Working Republic and Its Discontents

Directions: This packet compresses two full units, Early Republic and Expansion and Jackson and Reform, into one review. Read the two arguments, study the timeline, and keep the packet beside you for the term sprint and the practice set. Both of those name the lesson to reopen when an answer will not come.

The Band in Brief

The Constitution of 1788 was a design on paper. The generation after Washington had to invent the working government inside it: a treasury and a bank, parties that had not been planned for, courts that claimed the power to void laws, and a presidency that doubled the national domain while preaching small government. The republic survived its second war with Britain, entered a nationalist season of banks, tariffs, and canals, and twice settled the question of expansion, outward with the Monroe Doctrine and inward with the Missouri Compromise, in each case postponing more than it closed. Then it doubled again, by annexation and conquest, and the Wilmot Proviso asked the question the Missouri line could no longer answer.

Jackson's America widened the political nation and narrowed its boundaries at the same time. Property tests fell, parties organized the mass electorate, and the common white man governed in his own name, while Native nations were removed at bayonet point, slavery hardened, and women and free Black men stood outside the widening circle. The era tested the Union twice, over nullification and over the Bank, and built a new world of factories, canals, wires, and revivals underneath the politics. Out of the revival came the reformers, and out of reform came the two movements that would outlast the era: abolition demanding immediacy, and women's rights demanding the Declaration be read as written.

Early Republic and Expansion asked: How did the United States turn its Constitution into a working government, and what did continental expansion do to the republic that government served?

Jackson and Reform asked: What did democracy mean in Jackson's America, who was it for, and what did the era's reformers claim it still owed?

The Argument of Early Republic and Expansion

Inventing the government

Washington's administration had to make every office real. Hamilton's program, assumption of the state debts, a funded national debt, an excise, and the Bank of the United States, was nation building by finance: it gave the government's creditors a stake in the government's survival. It also created the first parties, Federalist and Republican, organizing around his system and its Virginia opposition, and the Whiskey Rebellion gave the young government its test of enforcement, which Washington passed by marching at the head of the militia and then pardoning the convicted. His Farewell warned the republic against the two forces already visible, party spirit at home and permanent entanglement abroad, and the warning's career through the next thirty years is the unit's throughline.

The opposition governs

The Federalists' answer to opposition, in the war scare of 1798, was the Alien and Sedition Acts, and the prosecutions that followed made "sedition" the era's fighting word. Jefferson and Madison's Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions answered with the compact theory, that the states, as parties to the constitutional compact, could judge federal overreach, the seed of the nullification doctrine a generation later. The election of 1800 then did the remarkable thing: the Federalists lost, and handed over power. Jefferson unwound what he could, taxes, the military establishment, the judiciary's new seats, yet governed through the powers Hamilton had claimed: the midnight judges fight produced Marbury and judicial review, and the Louisiana Purchase doubled the nation on a treaty power the strict constructionist president doubted he had. The Corps of Discovery then walked the purchase, and its journals' West was not an empty continent but a world of nations, Shoshone, Mandan, Nez Perce, whose diplomacy and provisions, and Sacagawea's and York's labor, carried the expedition through.

War, and the nationalist turn

Impressment and the Orders in Council made neutral rights the republic's unsolved problem. Jefferson's embargo, peaceful coercion by self-blockade, failed so completely that it discredited its own theory, and the War Hawks' Congress declared war in 1812 on Britain at its most distracted. The war itself was a ledger of disasters and salvations, a burned capital, a defended Baltimore, Tecumseh's confederacy broken with his death, the Hartford Convention's grievances overtaken by peace, and a treaty at Ghent that settled none of the declared causes because the European war that had caused them was over. What the war settled was psychological and political: a second independence, felt. The nationalist season followed, the Second Bank, Clay's American System of tariff and internal improvements, the Panic of 1819 as the new economy's first general lesson, and Marshall's Court building the Constitution's federal frame in McCulloch and Gibbons.

Settlements that postponed, and expansion's return

The settlements of the early 1820s faced both directions. Outward, the Monroe Doctrine declared the hemisphere closed to new colonization, with John Quincy Adams's hand in it and Britain's fleet, for its own reasons, behind it. Inward, Missouri's application forced the slavery question into Congress for the first time since the founding, and the Compromise answered with a pair of states and a geographic line, 36°30′, through the Louisiana lands. Sectionalism was named in the unit's own terms because the settlement's authors knew what they had done: Jefferson heard in it a fire bell in the night. A generation later Manifest Destiny gave expansion its theology, Texas was annexed, Oregon divided, and the war with Mexico took the Southwest and California. The Wilmot Proviso, proposing that slavery be barred from the cession, passed the House and died in the Senate, and there the unit leaves the question, standing open at the band's edge.

The Argument of Jackson and Reform

Democracy widened, and for whom

The electorate of 1828 was new: property qualifications fallen in most states, parties organized to mobilize ordinary voters, and a campaign run as a public spectacle. Jacksonian democracy meant real things, the common white man's political equality, rotation in office against a permanent officeholding class, suspicion of chartered privilege and paper wealth. Its boundaries were as definite as its promises. Suffrage widened for white men while free Black men lost ground in state after state, women stood outside citizenship's political half under coverture, and the Native nations of the Southeast faced a president who counted their land as the democracy's inheritance.

Removal, and the limits of law

The Cherokee had done everything the republic's civilization program asked: farms, a written language, a constitution, a supreme court case. Worcester v. Georgia held that Georgia's laws had no force in Cherokee country, and the holding protected nothing, because enforcement belonged to the president who favored removal. The Treaty of New Echota, signed by a minority the Cherokee Nation repudiated, supplied the legal paper, and in 1838 and 1839 the Army marched some sixteen thousand Cherokee west; about four thousand died. The unit teaches removal as the era's central test of law against power, and of democracy's definition of who counted as the people.

The Union tested twice

Nullification took the compact theory of 1798 to its conclusion: Calhoun's South Carolina, ground down by the Tariff of Abominations, claimed a state could void a federal law inside its borders. The Webster-Hayne debate argued the theory in the Senate; Jackson answered the Ordinance with a proclamation that called nullification treason's cousin and a Force Bill to collect the tariff at gunpoint; and Clay's graduated compromise tariff let South Carolina repeal its ordinance claiming victory, having lost the doctrine and won the rates. The Bank War was a separation-of-powers fight of the same size. Jackson read McCulloch's bank as a monopoly that made the rich richer, vetoed its recharter, removed the deposits to pet banks, and broke it; the Whigs formed in opposition to King Andrew; and the loose money, the Specie Circular, and London's credit together produced the Panic of 1837, which the Democracy owned for a generation.

The reforming decades

Underneath the politics, the market revolution rebuilt daily life: canals and then railroads collapsing distance and price, the factory system gathering work under one roof and one clock, Lowell's mill girls living a designed experiment in industrial womanhood and writing their own record of it, interchangeable parts and the telegraph remaking manufacture and news, and trade unions testing how far combination could go. The Second Great Awakening, Finney's revivals in the burned-over district above all, taught that salvation was a choice and society therefore a project: temperance, common schools under Mann, Dix's asylums, and the perfectionist confidence that institutions could be remade. Two movements took the era's premises past its boundaries. Abolition turned immediate: Walker's Appeal, Turner's revolt and the South's closing answer, Garrison's demand for emancipation now, the gag rule in the House, and Douglass's testimony made slavery a question that would not be tabled. And at Seneca Falls in 1848, women trained in the abolitionist movement, excluded at London in 1840 for their sex, rewrote the Declaration as the Declaration of Sentiments and asked, first of all, for the vote.

People of the Band

Alexander Hamilton built the financial system that made the national government creditable, and died in a duel with the vice president his party politics had made. John Marshall spent thirty-four years making the federal judiciary a coequal branch and the Constitution a nationalist charter. Sacagawea interpreted, provisioned, and steadied the Corps of Discovery while carrying her infant son across the continent. Andrew Jackson made the presidency the people's tribune, broke the Bank, removed the Cherokee, and faced down nullification, a record no single verdict holds. John C. Calhoun argued the Union's most dangerous theory with its most disciplined mind, in defense of slavery's expansion and, at the end, its protection. Henry Clay compromised at every crisis for thirty years and called himself the Great Pacificator. Charles Grandison Finney professionalized revival and aimed it at society. Frederick Douglass escaped slavery, and made his own literacy and testimony abolition's strongest evidence. Elizabeth Cady Stanton drafted the Declaration of Sentiments and insisted, against some of her allies, on naming the vote.
