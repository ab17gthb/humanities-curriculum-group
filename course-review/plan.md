# Course Review unit — plan

Status: SHAPE APPROVED by Alex 2026-10-23. Band 3 template day BUILT 2026-10-23 (course-review/band-3/, manifest there) and awaiting Alex's review before the remaining bands are built.
Placement: a sixteenth unit after Modern America, taken in the last weeks of the
course before the final exam or state test. Additive only: nothing about the
fifteen teaching units changes.

## What it is

The fifteen units teach the course. This one runs the course back, fast, in the
order students will meet it on a cumulative test: era by era, with the terms,
the timelines, and the argument of each unit compressed into review work, and a
full practice test at the end built to the same standard as every unit test.

It also gives the year planner's existing review days a content. The year
planner already reserves review days before the exam date (default five). Today
those days are blank. This unit is what fills them: a teacher who plans the
review unit is planning those days.

## Who it serves

- A teacher whose state test or final is cumulative and who currently builds
  review week out of spare handouts.
- A teacher on any of the three course shapes. Alex directed on 2026-10-23
  that all three get their review: the combined full-course review, an early
  review for the course that ends in 1877, and a modern review for the
  course that begins in 1877. The three variants share the same eight band
  days (see below); what differs is each variant's blueprint slice, its
  closing pieces, and its length.

## The three variants

The band days are shared. The variants are three registry units built over
them:

- **Course Review (combined)**, registry "rv": all eight bands, the
  fifteen-entry blueprint, ten-day core (eight band days, a flex day, the
  practice test on day ten). For the full-year shape.
- **Early Course Review**, registry "re": bands 1 to 3, a six-entry
  blueprint (entries 1 to 6), a five-day core (three band days, a flex day,
  the practice test). For the early shape ending at 1877. Band 3, the
  template day, is its closing band as well as the combined review's third.
- **Modern Course Review**, registry "rm": bands 4 to 8, a nine-entry
  blueprint (entries 7 to 15), a seven-day core (five band days, a flex day,
  the practice test). For the modern shape from 1877.

Each variant gets its own closing pieces: a study guide covering exactly
its units, a crossword at four answers per unit (early 24, modern 36,
combined 60), and a fifty-point practice test over its own blueprint with
every entry assessed at least twice. Each also gets its own combined Rycal
deck merged from its units' decks (early 336 cards, modern 504, combined
840). The DBQ capstone picker works the same way in each variant, scoped to
the DBQs standing on its units.

## Structure: eight review days, grouped by era

The course's fifteen units group into eight era bands. Each band is one review
day. The bands follow the course's own arcs, so a day never straddles a break
the course itself respects.

1. Colonies and the Founding (Colonial America, The Founding)
2. A Working Republic and Its Discontents (Early Republic and Expansion,
   Jackson and Reform)
3. Slavery, War, and the Unfinished Settlement (Sectional Crisis and Civil
   War, Reconstruction)
4. Industry and Reform (The Gilded Age, Progressive Era)
5. Power Abroad, Turmoil at Home (Imperialism and World War I, The Twenties)
6. Depression, Recovery, and Total War (Depression and New Deal, World War II)
7. Cold War Abroad, Rights at Home (The Cold War, Civil Rights)
8. Modern America and the Long Arc (Modern America, plus the cross-era
   threads: federal power, rights, migration, war powers)

## The plate per review day

Four pieces per day, all print-first to the standing standard:

- **Review packet** (the day's core document): a two-to-three-page condensed
  run of the band: a dated timeline drawn from the units' own materials, the
  band's essential questions restated, and the through-line argument in short
  prose. Built from the units' verified readings and notes, not new research.
- **Term sprint**: the band's key terms, drawn verbatim from the units' guided
  notes (a band holds two units, so up to 112 terms; the sprint works a
  selected set of about 40 per band in matching and short-answer form, with a
  key).
- **Practice set**: fifteen to twenty questions in the unit tests' style and
  difficulty, each printed with its blueprint tag, plus a key that names the
  unit and lesson each question reaches back to, so a miss points at the exact
  reteach target.
- **One skills task**, rotating by day: chronology (order and date a mixed
  set), causation (which cause mattered most, argued from the packet),
  comparison (two eras on one question), continuity and change (one thread
  across the course). Day 8's task is a document exercise built from the
  unit's own sources.

## Closing pieces

- **Course study guide**: the variant's whole span on a few pages per band:
  timeline, the essential questions of its units, and the term lists by
  unit, formatted like the unit study guides. Three versions, one per
  variant.
- **Course crossword**: four answers per unit (early 24, modern 36,
  combined 60), drawn from the printed term lists, built with the same
  generator as the unit crosswords.
- **Practice test**: fifty points per variant, built exactly like a unit
  test: every item printed with its tag, a full key, and coverage of every
  entry in the variant's blueprint at least twice (see below). One sitting,
  mixed across the variant's units.

Document count: 8 days × 4 pieces = 32, plus study guide, crossword, practice
test, and its key materials in the teacher file: about 36 documents. Lighter
than a teaching unit's 51, because review condenses verified material instead
of introducing new material.

## Blueprint and coverage

The review unit's blueprint has fifteen entries, one per teaching unit, named
by unit and span ("Entry 9 · The Twenties, 1919–1929" style). Coverage in the
planner then answers the review question directly: which units of the course
has this class actually reviewed, and which has it not reached yet.

- Every practice set item and every practice test item carries a tag for the
  unit it reviews; skills tasks tag to both units in their band where the
  task genuinely works both.
- The practice test meets the standing rule: every one of the fifteen entries
  assessed at least twice, recomputed from the printed tags.
- Topics: the unit's six topic labels are the course's big threads (federal
  power and the Constitution; expansion and its costs; slavery, race, and
  rights; industrial capitalism and labor; America in the world; reform
  movements). The bpTopic mapping is editorial, as in every unit.

## What it reuses, what is new

Reused verbatim or by condensation: every source excerpt (already audited in
the units or the TPSP bank; the 90-word copyright cap and edition rules
already hold for the originals and are inherited, never re-derived), every
term and definition, every timeline date, the essential questions, the test
style and tagging discipline, the crossword generator, the Rycal card data.

New writing: the packets' connective prose, the practice questions (original,
same standard as the unit tests' questions), the skills tasks, the study
guide's compression. No new source auditing is needed because no new source
enters the course.
