Band 8 Skills Task: The Long Arc in Four Documents

Directions: The review's last skill is the first one the course taught: reading documents closely. Four short texts, spanning 225 years, each state a position on American power or American rights. Part 1 reads each one. Part 2 sets them arguing with each other. Part 3 asks which of them a citizen now most needs to reread.

Part 1: Read Each Document

Part 2: Set Them Arguing

Documents A and C take opposite sides of the same question, a century and a half apart. In three or four sentences: what happened between them to move the country's answer, and which of the two was the country following in the year of Document D?

Document B borrows its sentences from a document of 1776. In two or three sentences: what does the borrowing itself argue, before any of its grievances are read?

Part 3: The Rereading

Choose the one document of the four that a citizen of the present most needs to reread, and defend the choice in a paragraph. Your defense must state the document's claim accurately, name the present argument it speaks to, and answer the strongest objection: why not one of the others?
