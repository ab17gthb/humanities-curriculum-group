Band 3 Skills Task: Two Settlements, Two Failures

Directions: Twice in this band, the United States wrote a settlement meant to close the slavery question for good: the Compromise of 1850, and the Reconstruction settlement built on three amendments between 1865 and 1870. Both settlements failed within a generation. Your work is causal, not narrative: complete both organizers from the review packet, then argue which failure was more avoidable.

Part 1: The Organizers

Settlement One: The Compromise of 1850 (failed by 1861)

Possible causes to weigh: the fugitive law required northern hands to enforce it, and northern hands refused; the bargain covered the territories already organized and left every future territory an open fight; both national parties needed northern and southern wings at once; the Supreme Court tried to take the question out of politics and put it beyond compromise instead.

Settlement Two: The Reconstruction Amendments (failed by 1877)

Possible causes to weigh: the settlement protected rights on paper but left freedpeople without land, and so economically dependent on the men who had opposed it; protection required a standing federal presence that northern voters tired of paying for; the Supreme Court narrowed the amendments' reach within a decade; organized terror raised the price of voting and governing year after year until the price was refused.

Part 2: The Verdict

Prompt: Both settlements tried to close the question. Argue which failure was more avoidable. Build the argument on at least three pieces of evidence for each settlement, drawn from the packet, and answer the strongest point that can be made for the other side before you finish.

Before you write: state your claim in one sentence. Avoidable means a real choice existed, in the hands of people at the time, that could have gone the other way. A cause nobody could have moved is not a failure of choice, and your argument has to separate the two.
