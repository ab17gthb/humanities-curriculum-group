## Image

- Title: Washington Reviewing the Western Army at Fort Cumberland, Maryland
- What it actually is: Frederick Kemmelmeyer's oil painting of George Washington, on the white horse at center, reviewing the militia army assembled at Fort Cumberland, Maryland, in October 1794 for the suppression of the Whiskey Rebellion, the scene this unit's Lesson 1 teaches as Washington's precedent of federal enforcement. The painting ships in the unit itself, in Lesson 1 (img/washington-western-army-kemmelmeyer.jpg), where the lesson's SOURCES.md carries its record and the deck's title image derives from it.
- Creator: Frederick Kemmelmeyer (American)
- Date: after 1795
- Repository: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
- Identifiers: Metropolitan Museum of Art Open Access object 11302, accession number 63.201.2, credit line "Gift of Edgar William and Bernice Chrysler Garbisch, 1963," medium oil on canvas, object page https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/11302
- Public domain basis: the Metropolitan Museum Open Access API record for object 11302 marks the work isPublicDomain true; the painting is dated after 1795, long out of copyright. The record was opened directly through the Met's Open Access API for this cover (object 11302), and its primary image URL is the file the unit ships. The unit's Lesson 1 SOURCES.md records the same basis.
- The copy held locally is covers/early-republic-washington-western-army.jpg (3811 x 2428), the same file the unit ships in Lesson 1. The cover frame crop (object-fit: cover, object-position center 45%) holds Washington on the white horse, the mounted officers, and the militia line at left; visually checked.
