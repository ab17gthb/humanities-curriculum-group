## Image

- Title: Map showing the distribution of the slave population of the southern states of the United States. Compiled from the census of 1860
- What it actually is: the U.S. Coast Survey's choropleth map of the enslaved population by county, drawn under A. D. Bache from the 1860 census and published in Washington in September 1861, sold for the benefit of the sick and wounded soldiers of the U.S. Army, as the sheet itself states. It is the map Lincoln kept and the map this unit's Lesson 6 teaches as a document of the rival strategies. No photograph of the dead, no ruins, and no assassination imagery appears on the cover, following the unit's stated image discipline; the cover image is a data map, the war's cause drawn county by county.
- Creator: U.S. Coast Survey (compiled from the census of 1860; drawn under A. D. Bache; published by Henry S. Graham)
- Date: 1861
- Repository: Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division
- Identifiers: LOC item 99447026, call number G3861.E9 1861 .U5, digital id g3861e cw0013200, record https://www.loc.gov/item/99447026/
- Public domain basis: a work of the United States federal government (the Coast Survey), published in 1861 and long out of copyright on any reading; the Library of Congress record for the item carries no rights advisory. The record was verified directly through the Library of Congress's own item page and JSON record for this integration.
- The copy held locally is covers/slave-population-map-1861.jpg (1600 x 1290), the same file the unit ships in Lesson 6 (lesson-06/img/slave-population-map-1861.jpg), where the lesson's SOURCES.md records it as the Digital Public Library of America copy of this same Coast Survey map, also the activity document of the audited TPSP bank record slave-population-map-1861. The cover frame crop (object-fit: cover, object-position center 38%) holds nearly the whole sheet, title block and Florida included; visually checked.
