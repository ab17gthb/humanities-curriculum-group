Source file: cover-cold-war.html. Outputs: cold-war-cover.png (1632 x 2112) and cold-war-cover.pdf (printable, US Letter).

## Photograph

- Title: "Crowds at the Brandenburg Gate, Berlin, December 1989" (the audited TPSP bank record's title); the Commons file page image title reads: Crowds throng around the Brandenburg Gate following the structure's official opening on December 22nd
- What it actually is: Crowds thronging around the Brandenburg Gate in Berlin on December 1, 1989. The Commons description states the gate is already freely accessible from the East Berlin side, and the crossing to the Western side at the gate opened officially on December 22, 1989. The same photograph ships in the unit itself, in Lesson 8 (reading figure, activity Station 3, and quick check figure; TPSP record corkran-berlin-wall-1989).
- Creator: SSgt. F. Lee Corkran, United States Air Force
- Date: December 1, 1989 (per the Commons file page and the bank record's sourceNote)
- Repository: United States Department of Defense, via Wikimedia Commons
- Identifiers: DoD short title DF-ST-91-03559 (per the Commons file page metadata); Commons file "BrandenburgerTorDezember1989.jpg"
- Source URLs: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BrandenburgerTorDezember1989.jpg
- Public domain basis: The photograph is a work of a United States Air Force photographer made in the course of his official duties, held by the Department of Defense; the Commons file page marks it as a DoD photo (USA), and the audited Primary Source Project record carries the same photograph, attribution, and December 1, 1989 date.
- The full-resolution original is held locally as covers/cold-war-brandenburg-1989.jpg (2780 x 1870, the copy the unit itself ships in Lesson 8). The cover HTML renders from a downscaled copy (covers/cold-war-brandenburg-1989-cover.jpg, 2000 x 1345) so the printable cover PDF stays near the other units' size; there is no visual difference at cover size. The cover frame crop (object-fit: cover, object-position center 35%) holds the gate and the crowds around it.
- Date honesty, carried from the unit itself: no verified public-domain photograph of the night of November 9, 1989 exists in the build, so the cover, like the lesson, uses the December 1, 1989 Department of Defense photograph with its true date in the caption rather than implying it shows the night the Wall opened.
