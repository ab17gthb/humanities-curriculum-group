# World War II cover notes

Built 2026-10-22, following the construction of the Depression and New Deal cover (cover-depression-new-deal.html): navy background, cream wordmark, rust rule, one period photograph, contents lines, Knoweth footer. Source file: cover-world-war-ii.html. Outputs: world-war-ii-cover.png (1632 x 2112) and world-war-ii-cover.pdf (printable, US Letter).

## Photograph

- Title: "Into the Jaws of Death" (also circulated as "Taxis to Hell, and Back")
- What it actually is: Troops of Company E, 16th Infantry, 1st Infantry Division, wading ashore from an LCVP off the Coast Guard-crewed USS Samuel Chase onto the Easy Red sector of Omaha Beach during the Normandy landings, at 7:40 a.m. local time. The same photograph ships in the unit itself, in Lesson 4 and the unit test (TPSP record sargent-into-the-jaws-of-death-1944).
- Creator: Chief Photographer's Mate Robert F. Sargent, United States Coast Guard
- Date: June 6, 1944 (per the National Archives catalog record)
- Repository: National Archives and Records Administration; original prints also held by the United States Coast Guard Historian's Office
- Identifiers: U.S. Coast Guard photograph 26-G-2343; the Wikimedia Commons file used by the unit is "Into the Jaws of Death 23-0455M edit.jpg"
- Source URLs: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Into_the_Jaws_of_Death_23-0455M_edit.jpg
- Public domain basis: The photograph is a work of a United States Coast Guard photographer made in the course of his official duties; the Commons file page marks it Public domain, and the audited Primary Source Project record carries the same photograph and attribution.
- The full-resolution original is held locally as covers/jaws-of-death-1944.jpg (2963 x 2385, the copy the unit itself ships in Lesson 4). The cover HTML renders from a downscaled copy (covers/jaws-of-death-1944-cover.jpg, 2000 x 1610) so the printable cover PDF stays near the other units' size; there is no visual difference at cover size. The cover frame crop (object-fit: cover, object-position center 32%) holds the open ramp, the troops in the water, and the beach ahead.
- One discrepancy carried in the unit's own materials, flagged at integration: the unit test's printed caption places the photograph at Utah Beach, while the catalog record, the Lesson 4 image record, and the Lesson 4 notebook caption place it at Omaha Beach. The cover follows the catalog record. Correcting the test caption is unit-content work, outside this integration pass.
