Guided Notes: The Movement Turns North and Inward

Lesson question: Why did the movement's methods stop working as it moved north, and how did its own people argue about what freedom required next?

Unit question: How did the civil rights movement change American law and American life, and what did it leave unfinished?

Key Terms

de facto segregation: segregation in fact rather than in law, produced by housing patterns, economics, and custom, the form segregation took in the cities of the North

Chicago Freedom Movement: Martin Luther King Jr.'s campaign of 1966 against housing and school segregation in Chicago, met by violent mobs on its open-housing marches and ended by an agreement the city did not enforce

Watts uprising: the six days of violence in the Watts neighborhood of Los Angeles in August 1965, beginning five days after the Voting Rights Act was signed, in which thirty four people died

Black Power: the call raised by Stokely Carmichael in Mississippi in June 1966 for Black Americans to build their own political and economic power and to define freedom for themselves rather than rely on white goodwill

Black Panther Party: the organization founded in Oakland, California in 1966 by Huey Newton and Bobby Seale, which patrolled the police, carried arms in self-defense, and ran free breakfast and health programs in Black neighborhoods

Poor People's Campaign: Martin Luther King Jr.'s planned campaign of 1968 to bring an interracial army of the poor to Washington to demand federal action on poverty, carried on after his assassination as Resurrection City

Memphis sanitation strike: the strike of 1968 by Black sanitation workers in Memphis, Tennessee, whose signs read I Am a Man, and which brought Martin Luther King Jr. to the city where he was assassinated

A. Five Days After the Vote: Watts

The Voting Rights Act was signed on August 6, 1965. Five days later the (1) ____________ neighborhood of Los Angeles rose in six days of violence that left (2) ____________ people dead. No movement organization directed the rising. The grievances residents named afterward were police treatment, poor housing, low pay, and weak schools.

B. The Northern Problem

In the North, segregation rested not on statutes but on (3) ____________ segregation, segregation in fact rather than in law. Restrictive (4) ____________ barred sales to Black buyers in whole neighborhoods. Lenders practiced (5) ____________, treating Black neighborhoods as bad risks and steering mortgage money to white suburbs. School boards drew attendance lines around neighborhoods already sorted by race.

C. Chicago: The Movement Marches North

In 1966 King took the movement north in the (6) ____________ Movement, renting a tenement flat on Chicago's West Side and leading open housing marches. At (7) ____________ Park the marchers were met by mobs carrying swastikas and throwing bottles and bricks, and a brick struck King. The campaign ended in a (8) ____________ Agreement promising steps toward open housing, which the city did not enforce.

D. The Argument Inside: Black Power

In June 1966 James Meredith was shot on his March Against Fear, and the movement organizations continued the march together. At Greenwood, (9) ____________, chairman of SNCC, was arrested for the twenty seventh time and came from jail calling for (10) ____________. SNCC and CORE moved toward Black leadership and away from the interracial liberal coalition. King refused the slogan, fearing it would be heard as a call to violence, while understanding its source in a decade of nonviolence answered with bombs and acquittals.

E. Malcolm X and the Panthers

Malcolm X, assassinated in February (11) ____________ after breaking with the Nation of Islam, had carried a nationalist case drawn from his father, an organizer for Marcus (12) ____________. In October 1966 Huey Newton and Bobby Seale founded the (13) ____________ Party in Oakland, California. Its members patrolled police stops, and the party ran free (14) ____________ programs and health clinics while the FBI worked to destroy it.

F. The Turn to Economics

King planned the (15) ____________ Campaign to bring an interracial army of the poor to Washington to demand federal action on poverty. Its demands were jobs, income, and housing, stated in an economic (16) ____________ of rights. The plan drew little support from the coalition that had passed the acts of 1964 and 1965, and King went ahead anyway.

Summary table. The northern turn, 1965 to 1966: the movement faced de facto segregation built by covenants, redlining, white flight, and school lines, with no statute at its center to strike down, and Watts showed the grievances five days after the Voting Rights Act. It tried the Chicago Freedom Movement, open housing marches met by mobs at Marquette Park and a Summit Agreement the city did not enforce. The argument inside, 1966: on the Meredith March, Carmichael raised Black Power after his twenty seventh arrest, SNCC and CORE moved toward Black leadership, Malcolm X's nationalism stood behind the call, and the Panthers organized in Oakland around self-defense and community programs, while King refused the slogan and understood its source. The economic turn, 1967 to 1968: King planned the Poor People's Campaign, an interracial army of the poor to demand jobs, income, and housing from the federal government. Memphis, 1968: the sanitation workers struck for union recognition and dignity under the sign I Am a Man, King came to support them, and he was assassinated on April 4, 1968, with uprisings following in more than a hundred cities.

Exit ticket 1. De facto segregation is segregation in fact rather than in law, produced by housing patterns, economics, and custom. Any two of the following earn credit: restrictive covenants barring sales to Black buyers, redlining by lenders, white flight, and school attendance lines drawn around sorted neighborhoods.

Exit ticket 2. The signs read I Am a Man, a claim that the dispute concerned dignity before wages. The larger campaign was the Poor People's Campaign, King's plan to bring an interracial army of the poor to Washington to demand federal action on poverty.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that covers both halves with facts from the notes. The strongest answers state that the southern methods had aimed at statutes and officials who could be forced to enforce court orders, while the northern problem was built from housing, lending, and custom with no single law to strike; and that the argument inside the movement ran between King's interracial nonviolent coalition turned toward economics, Carmichael's call for Black political and economic power, Malcolm X's nationalism, and the Panthers' program of self-defense and community service.
