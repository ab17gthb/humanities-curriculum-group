## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, stated honestly
- Chicago Freedom Movement marches and the Marquette Park mobs, 1966: the known photographs are press photographs (Chicago Tribune, Chicago Sun-Times, and the Bernard Kleina collection, the last circulated under a Creative Commons noncommercial license), not public domain. No image is used. The lesson teaches Chicago through text slides and the reading.
- Watts, August 1965: the known photographs are press and wire photographs (Associated Press, Getty, Los Angeles Public Library collections) under copyright. No image is used. The lesson teaches Watts through a text slide and a stat slide.
- The Lorraine Motel and King's assassination, April 4, 1968: the known photographs are press photographs under copyright. No image is used, and no image of the assassination is used as decoration or otherwise.
- The Memphis sanitation strike and the I Am a Man posters, 1968: the iconic strike photographs are by Ernest C. Withers and are held under the Withers Family Trust copyright. No photograph is reproduced. The posters are taught from the audited TPSP bank record, civil-rights-posters-memphis-1968, with the slogans transcribed in the activity, exactly as the bank carries them.
- Black Panther Party, Oakland, 1966: no Commons file page stating a public domain basis for a party photograph was verified for this build. No image is used; the party is taught through its Ten Point Program text and text slides.
- The Poor People's Campaign and Resurrection City, 1968: the located photographs are held under photographer copyright (for example the Robert Houston photographs at the National Museum of African American History and Culture, marked permission required). No image is used.

## Quoted sources, editions, and exact counts

### The Ballot or the Bullet (copyrighted; this lesson is the unit's only quotation of it)
- Edition: Malcolm X, "The Ballot or the Bullet," April 12, 1964, Bill of Rights Institute transcription, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record ballot-or-the-bullet-1964.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 1 only. Nowhere else in this lesson's files is the speech quoted; other files paraphrase it or name its title.
- Passage 1 (33 words): "I'm not a Democrat. I'm not a Republican, and I don't [...] consider myself an American. [...] I'm not an American. I'm one of the 22 million black people who are the victims of Americanism."
- Passage 2 (49 words): "Just as it took nationalism to remove colonialism from Asia and Africa, it'll take black nationalism today to remove colonialism from the backs and the minds of twenty-two million Afro-Americans here in this country. And 1964 looks like it might be the year of the ballot or the bullet."
- Total quoted from the speech across all files in this lesson: 82 words, within the unit's 90 word cap. Cuts are marked with ellipses in the text and disclosed in the station's Source line. Word counts exclude the bracketed ellipses.

### The Autobiography of Malcolm X (copyrighted)
- Edition: Malcolm X, "The Autobiography of Malcolm X," 1964, published text, Penguin Random House, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record malcolm-x-autobiography-garvey-1964.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-reading.html, Section E only, one passage of 13 words: "freedom, independence and self-respect could never be achieved by the Negro in America"
- Total quoted from the Autobiography across all files in this lesson: 13 words, within the 60 word limit set for this lesson. Everything beyond that passage is paraphrased, and the deck's speaker notes paraphrase it as well and do not repeat the quotation.

### Martin Luther King Jr.
- No words of King's are quoted anywhere in this lesson's files: 0 quoted words. The Marquette Park remark, the Mason Temple address of April 3, 1968 (the Mountaintop speech), and all other King language are paraphrased, and the documents say on their face that the words are paraphrased. Letter from Birmingham Jail and I Have a Dream are not quoted.

### Black Power at Greenwood
- Source: Stokely Carmichael, remarks at Greenwood, Mississippi, June 16, 1966, as carried in the public record at BlackPast.org, "Stokely Carmichael's Black Power Speech (1966)." Quoted in the activity, Station 2 only.

### Black Panther Party Ten Point Program
- Edition named in the Source line: The Black Panther Party for Self-Defense, Ten Point Program, "What We Want Now!", written by Huey Newton and Bobby Seale in October 1966 and published in The Black Panther, May 15, 1967. Points 1, 2, 4, and 7 are printed in their short form in the activity, Station 3. Points 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, and 10 are not printed, and the omissions are marked with ellipses in the text and disclosed in the Source line.

### Memphis posters
- Edition: Various (Memphis sanitation workers' strike), "Civil Rights Posters, 1968," Gilder Lehrman Institute, poster slogans transcribed, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record civil-rights-posters-memphis-1968. Slogans transcribed in the activity, Station 4: "I AM A MAN" (AM underlined) and "HONOR KING: END RACISM!"

### Kerner Commission
- Edition: Report of the National Advisory Commission on Civil Disorders, Introduction, March 1, 1968, Teaching American History, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record kerner-commission-report-1968. This is a public government document. Quoted in the activity, Station 5, and in the quick check, with cuts marked. The passages quoted are the commission's basic conclusion (the two societies sentence) and the passage assigning responsibility to white institutions (created, maintain, condones).
