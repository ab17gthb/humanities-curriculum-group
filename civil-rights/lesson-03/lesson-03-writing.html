Writing Task: The Methods and the Federal Answer

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did a bus boycott and nine schoolchildren teach the movement its methods, and force the federal government to act? A strong answer separates the two halves of the record. It names the methods Montgomery and the Till record taught, economic pressure held together by an organization of the movement's own, and witness, before it explains how a governor's defiance at Little Rock left the president with a choice he could not decline. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Jo Ann Robinson's letter of May 21, 1954 warned Mayor Gayle that three-fourths of the riders were Black, that the buses could not operate without their fares, and that more than twenty-five organizations were already talking of a citywide boycott.

2. Bayard Rustin's diary of February 24, 1956 records 42,000 Black residents off the buses since December 5, police harassment and arrests of taxi drivers, and a car pool with 23 dispatch centers built to replace them, with men walking 7 and 14 miles a day.

3. The boycott held for 381 days through indictments of its leaders and the bombing of King's house, and ended when the Supreme Court ruled in Browder v. Gayle that bus segregation was unconstitutional.

4. After an all-white jury acquitted the men charged with murdering Emmett Till, Mamie Till-Mobley held an open-casket funeral and let Jet publish David Jackson's photographs. The bank record states that the images shocked the nation and helped galvanize the civil rights movement. The photographs are copyrighted magazine images and are described in this unit rather than shown.

5. Governor Faubus posted the Arkansas National Guard to bar the nine students from Central High in September 1957. Elizabeth Eckford walked alone through a shouting crowd, and the students who entered on September 23 were withdrawn within hours.

6. On September 24, 1957 Eisenhower signed Executive Order 10730, federalizing the Arkansas Guard and authorizing federal troops. The 101st Airborne escorted the nine students into Central High on September 25, and Faubus later closed the high schools for the Lost Year.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the prompt, that Montgomery and the Till record taught the movement methods it could run itself, and that Little Rock forced the federal government to act when a governor defied a court order with his own soldiers. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 supports the prepared boycott and its economic lever. (Blueprint 5) Bank items 2 and 3 support the organization and endurance of the campaign and its court ending. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 4 supports witness as a method, with the acquittal stated before the publication. (Blueprint 6) Bank items 5 and 6 support the defiance and the federal answer, including the Lost Year as the resistance's reply. (Blueprint 6) A full credit essay draws on at least two groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. An essay that lists arrests and troop movements without saying which method each fact teaches has assembled the lesson's events without its argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that keeps the two judgments separate, as the lesson does: Executive Order 10730 settled who commanded the Arkansas Guard and whether the court's order would be enforced at Central High, and it left open the larger question of enforcement elsewhere, which Faubus's Lost Year closings immediately posed again.

Model opening for teacher reference. The movement learned its methods in two classrooms. In Montgomery the lesson was endurance organized: a boycott planned a year and a half before the arrest that triggered it, run by an association the riders built themselves, kept on the street by a car pool of 23 dispatch centers when the city arrested its taxi drivers. At Little Rock the lesson ran the other way. Nine students and a court order could not open a school against a governor's Guard, so the confrontation itself became the instrument, forcing a president who had never endorsed Brown to federalize that Guard and send the 101st Airborne. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the Till record as the education in witness between the two, then closing on the Lost Year.

Common weak answers. A spontaneous boycott answer (one woman's tired feet started a movement) ignores Robinson's 1954 letter, the Women's Political Council, and the leaflets printed the night of the arrest. A presidential rescue answer (Eisenhower integrated Little Rock because he believed in the cause) ignores the ground Eisenhower himself claimed, the duty to uphold the courts against mob rule, and his refusal to endorse Brown. Credit requires both records held at once: methods the movement built and ran itself, and a federal intervention compelled by defiance rather than offered from conviction.
