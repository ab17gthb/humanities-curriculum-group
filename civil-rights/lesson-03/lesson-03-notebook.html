Teacher Answer Key

Part of the lessonWhat happenedThe tactic or power shownThe result
The warning letterJo Ann Robinson of the Women's Political Council wrote to Mayor Gayle on May 21, 1954 with three requests on seating, boarding, and bus stops, a year and a half before the Parks arrest.A boycott threatened in advance, resting on the fact that three-fourths of the riders were Black and the buses could not operate without their fares.The city did not grant the requests. The letter shows the boycott was prepared, not spontaneous, and the council's network was ready when the arrest came.
The boycottAfter Rosa Parks's arrest on December 1, 1955, some 42,000 Black residents stayed off the buses for 381 days. The Montgomery Improvement Association ran a car pool with 23 dispatch centers, and weekly mass meetings sustained the effort through indictments and the bombing of King's house.A boycott sustained by nonviolent direct action and by an organization of the movement's own.The Supreme Court ruled in Browder v. Gayle that bus segregation was unconstitutional, and the boycott ended on December 21, 1956 with the buses desegregated.
The Till recordEmmett Till, fourteen, was murdered in Mississippi in August 1955. An all-white jury acquitted the men charged, who then confessed to a magazine. Mamie Till-Mobley held an open-casket funeral and let Jet publish David Jackson's photographs. The photographs are copyrighted magazine images, described in this lesson rather than shown.Witness: publishing the evidence of a killing so the nation had to look at what a jury had excused.Tens of thousands filed past the casket, and the bank record states that the images shocked the nation and helped galvanize the civil rights movement.
The barred doorIn September 1957 Governor Orval Faubus posted the Arkansas National Guard at Central High School and barred the nine Black students a federal court had ordered admitted. Elizabeth Eckford walked alone through a shouting crowd, and the students who entered on September 23 were withdrawn within hours.Defiance: a governor using his state's soldiers to stop a federal court order.The confrontation passed to the president. A court order alone had not opened the school, and only federal power remained.
The federal answerOn September 24, 1957 Eisenhower addressed the nation and signed Executive Order 10730, federalizing the Arkansas Guard and authorizing federal troops. The 101st Airborne escorted the nine students into Central High on September 25. Faubus answered with his occupied territory statement and later closed the high schools for the Lost Year.A court order enforced by troops: federal authority used, on the stated ground that mob rule cannot override the decisions of the courts.The Little Rock Nine finished the school year under military protection. The resistance's answer was the Lost Year closings of 1958 and 1959.

The methods in one sentence. Strong answers name both the methods and the compulsion. The boycott taught the movement that withdrawing the money an institution depended on, through an organization of its own that could hold out for 381 days, could defeat a segregation practice, and the Till record taught it the power of witness. The nine students taught it the federal lever: when a governor defied a court order with his own soldiers, the defiance itself forced the president to choose, and Eisenhower chose enforcement, with Executive Order 10730 and the 101st Airborne.
