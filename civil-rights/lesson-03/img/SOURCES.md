# Image Sources: Lesson 3, Montgomery and Little Rock: The Movement Finds Its Tactics

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages. Downloads were made from Wikimedia Commons on October 22, 2026, and each file was verified as a valid image with PIL. Original downloads live in assets/. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here.

The Emmett Till photographs are not reproduced in this lesson, in any document or in the deck, by the unit rule. The bank record (cold-war.json, id emmett-till-jet-photographs-1955) describes them without reproducing them, and the student materials state plainly that the photographs are copyrighted magazine images described rather than shown, and that Mamie Till-Mobley chose to publish them in 1955. The famous photograph of Elizabeth Eckford walking alone, by Will Counts, is copyrighted and is not used anywhere in this lesson.

## Soldiers of the 101st Airborne escort students to Central High School (littlerock-escort.jpg; deck crops littlerock-port.jpg and title-169.jpg)
- Title: Operation Arkansas, Little Rock Nine
- Creator: The U.S. Army
- Date: September 1957, as stated on the Commons file page
- Holder: Wikimedia Commons file, courtesy of the National Archives per the file page description
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Operation_Arkansas,_Little_Rock_Nine.jpg
- Public domain basis: Commons file page marks the image Public domain. File page description records soldiers from the 101st Airborne Division escorting African-American students to Central High School in Little Rock in September 1957, after the governor of Arkansas tried to enforce segregation. Used in the Lesson 3 activity, Station 4, in the quick check, and in the deck.

## Rosa Parks being fingerprinted by Deputy Sheriff D. H. Lackey, February 22, 1956 (parks-fingerprint.jpg; deck crop parks-port.jpg)
- Title: Rosa Parks being fingerprinted by Deputy Sheriff D.H. Lackey after being arrested on February 22, 1956, during the Montgomery bus boycott
- Creator: Gene Herrick for the Associated Press; restored by Adam Cuerden, as stated on the Commons file page
- Date: February 22, 1956, as stated on the Commons file page
- Holder: Wikimedia Commons file, from the Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery record named in the file page source line
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rosa_Parks_being_fingerprinted_by_Deputy_Sheriff_D.H._Lackey_after_being_arrested_on_February_22%2C_1956%2C_during_the_Montgomery_bus_boycott.jpg
- Public domain basis: Commons file page marks the image Public domain, stating that the work is in the public domain in the United States because it was published in the United States between 1931 and 1977, inclusive, without a copyright notice, and that the distributed copy carried no copyright notice attached. File page description records Parks being fingerprinted as one of the people indicted as leaders of the Montgomery bus boycott. Used in the Lesson 3 activity, Station 2, and in the deck. Note the exact date: this photograph shows the February 1956 indictments, not the December 1, 1955 arrest, and the documents caption it that way.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- The Jet magazine photographs of Emmett Till (David Jackson, September 1955) are copyrighted magazine images. Not reproduced anywhere in this unit, by rule; taught from the bank record's description.
- The photograph of Elizabeth Eckford walking alone on September 4, 1957, by Will Counts, is copyrighted. Not used. The lesson uses the United States Army escort photograph instead.
- No public domain photograph of Jo Ann Robinson, the Women's Political Council, or the Montgomery car pool dispatch centers was verified on a Commons file page for this build, so the Montgomery sections of the documents and deck are text led, with the Parks fingerprint photograph as the single Montgomery image.

## Quoted source log for this lesson
- Jo Ann Robinson, Letter to the Mayor, May 21, 1954 (Historical Thinking Matters classroom edition). Audited TPSP bank record jo-ann-robinson-letter-mayor-1954, civil-rights.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 1, with cuts marked.
- Bayard Rustin, Montgomery Diary, February 24, 1956 (Reading Like a Historian classroom edition). Audited TPSP bank record bayard-rustin-montgomery-diary-1956, civil-rights.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 2, at the length the bank record carries.
- Rosa Parks, Reflections on Her Arrest, ca. 1956 to 1958 (American Yawp transcription; original in the Library of Congress). Audited TPSP bank record rosa-parks-reflections-arrest-1956, civil-rights.json. One sentence of 19 words quoted in the reading, section B.
- Rosa Parks fingerprint card, December 1, 1955 (National Archives; card described). Audited TPSP bank record rosa-parks-fingerprint-card-1955, civil-rights.json. Described, not reproduced; the charge wording in the reading key follows the record's text.
- Martin Luther King Jr., Speech at the MIA Mass Meeting, March 22, 1956, as reported by Anna Holden (Reading Like a Historian classroom edition). Audited TPSP bank record mlk-mia-mass-meeting-1956, civil-rights.json. Quoted once in the reading, section C, for exactly 27 words: "Freedom doesn't come on a silver platter. With every great movement toward freedom there will inevitably be trials. Somebody will have to have the courage to sacrifice." The speech is a reporter's account, not a transcript, and the reading introduces it that way. No other King words are quoted anywhere in this lesson; King's other statements in the deck notes are paraphrased.
- Emmett Till photograph record (Jet magazine; image described, no image reproduced). Audited TPSP bank record emmett-till-jet-photographs-1955, cold-war.json. The activity, Station 3, quotes the bank record's descriptive text in full (49 words) and states on its face that the photographs are not reproduced.
- Dwight D. Eisenhower, Radio and Television Address to the American People on the Situation in Little Rock, September 24, 1957 (American Presidency Project). Audited TPSP bank record eisenhower-little-rock-address-1957, cold-war.json. Quoted in the reading, section F (the mob rule sentence) and in the activity, Station 4, with cuts marked. Public document.
- Orval Faubus, Statement on the Little Rock crisis, September 26, 1957 (Eyes on the Prize transcript, WGBH). Audited TPSP bank record faubus-little-rock-statement-1957, cold-war.json. Quoted in full at the length the bank record carries in the activity, Station 5, and in part in the reading, section G.
- Executive Order 10730, September 24, 1957 (Wikisource). Audited TPSP bank record executive-order-10730-1957, cold-war.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 5, with cuts marked. Public document.
