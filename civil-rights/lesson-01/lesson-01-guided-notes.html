Guided Notes: Foundations: The Movement Before the Movement

Lesson question: What had Black Americans already built, and already won, before the movement's famous decade began?

Unit question: How did the civil rights movement change American law and American life, and what did it leave unfinished?

Key Terms

Jim Crow: the system of state and local laws and customs that enforced racial segregation in the South

segregation: the separation of people by race in public facilities, schools, and transportation

disfranchisement: the denial of the right to vote through law or practice

NAACP: the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, founded in 1909, which fought segregation through the courts, through lobbying, and through its magazine The Crisis

Double V campaign: the campaign led by Black newspapers from 1942 for victory over fascism abroad and victory over racism at home

To Secure These Rights: the 1947 report of President Truman's Committee on Civil Rights, which called for federal laws against lynching and disfranchisement and for the end of segregation in American life

Executive Order 9981: President Truman's order of July 26, 1948 directing equality of treatment and opportunity for all persons in the armed forces, the order that desegregated the United States military

A. The System in Place

Across the South, Jim Crow governed daily life by law and by custom. Statutes required separate schools, railroad cars, waiting rooms, and drinking fountains. In Plessy v. Ferguson in (1) ____________ the Supreme Court upheld the arrangement under the doctrine called (2) ____________ but equal. The facilities provided were separate and plainly unequal, in school funding most of all.

B. Shut Out of the Ballot Box

Southern states disfranchised Black voters by the (3) ____________ tax, a fee for voting, and by the (4) ____________ test, administered by white registrars. The (5) ____________ primary closed the one election that counted in the one party South. Violence stood behind the paperwork. In 1940 only about (6) ____________ in every hundred Black adults in the South were registered to vote.

C. The Institutions That Held

The Black (7) ____________ was an institution Black Americans owned outright, with buildings, budgets, and a weekly audience no white official controlled. Black colleges trained the community's teachers, lawyers, and doctors, and the Black press spoke to a national audience. The NAACP, founded in (8) ____________, lobbied Congress, published its magazine (9) ____________, and built a legal program that carried segregation into the courts case by case.

D. Victories in Court Before 1950

In Smith v. Allwright in 1944 the Supreme Court struck down the white primary, holding that a primary run under state law was state action under the (10) ____________ Amendment. In Shelley v. Kraemer in 1948 the Court held that state courts could not enforce (11) ____________ covenants, the private agreements barring sales of homes to Black buyers.

E. The War and the Moving North

About (12) ____________ million Black Americans served in the Second World War, in segregated units. The Black press answered with the Double V campaign, demanding victory over fascism abroad and victory over (13) ____________ at home. In 1941 A. Philip Randolph threatened a march on Washington, and President Roosevelt answered with Executive Order (14) ____________, barring discrimination in defense industries. A second wave of the Great Migration carried Black southerners to northern and western war industry cities, where their votes counted in national elections.

F. A Committee, a Report, and a Split Party

In 1946 President Truman created a Committee on Civil Rights. Its 1947 report, To Secure These Rights, named lynching, disfranchisement, and segregation as federal business. In February 1948 Truman sent Congress a civil rights program built on the report. Southern delegates answered by walking out of the Democratic convention and running their own ticket, the (15) ____________.

G. The Order of July 26, 1948

In 1948 Randolph told Congress he would lead resistance to a segregated draft. On (16) ____________, Truman signed Executive Order 9981, declaring equality of treatment and opportunity in the armed forces without regard to race. The order was carried out through the Korean War, until the military became the most integrated institution in American life.

Summary table. The system: Jim Crow enforced segregation by statute and by custom across the South, blessed by Plessy v. Ferguson in 1896 under separate but equal, and guarded the ballot by the poll tax, the literacy test, and the white primary, with violence behind all three, so that about three Black adults in a hundred in the South were registered in 1940. The institutions: the Black church, owned outright and beyond white control; Black colleges, training the community's professionals; the Black press, speaking nationally; and the NAACP, founded in 1909, lobbying, publishing The Crisis, and litigating on dues from branches nationwide. The court victories: Smith v. Allwright in 1944 ended the white primary as state action under the Fifteenth Amendment, argued by Thurgood Marshall, and Shelley v. Kraemer in 1948 made restrictive covenants unenforceable in state courts under the Fourteenth Amendment. The war and the first federal steps: about a million Black servicemen, the Double V campaign, Executive Order 8802 in 1941 after Randolph's threatened march, the second Great Migration creating a northern electorate presidents had to court, To Secure These Rights in 1947, and Executive Order 9981 on July 26, 1948 desegregating the armed forces.

Exit ticket 1. The NAACP is the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, founded in 1909. It fought on three fronts: the courts, through its case by case legal program; lobbying, pressing Congress directly; and publication, through its magazine The Crisis.

Exit ticket 2. The war put about a million Black Americans in uniform in a segregated military, and the Black press answered with the Double V campaign, demanding victory over fascism abroad and victory over racism at home, which stated the contradiction in a form the whole country could repeat. At the same time the second wave of the Great Migration moved Black voters into northern and western cities where no registrar could turn them away, so their ballots began to count in the national elections that presidents had to win.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names one thing built and one thing won from the lesson. The strongest answers pair them: institutions built across forty years, the church, the colleges, the press, and the NAACP with its legal staff, and victories already on the record before 1950, the white primary struck down, restrictive covenants made unenforceable, a presidential report naming segregation federal business, and an executive order desegregating the armed forces.
