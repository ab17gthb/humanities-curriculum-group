Quick Check: The Order and the Argument

Directions: Read the passage and the background, then answer the questions.

The passage. It is hereby declared to be the policy of the President that there shall be equality of treatment and opportunity for all persons in the armed services without regard to race, color, religion or national origin. This policy shall be put into effect as rapidly as possible, having due regard to the time required to effectuate any necessary changes without impairing efficiency or morale.

Source: Harry S. Truman, Executive Order 9981, July 26, 1948 (Harry S. Truman Library), as carried in the audited TPSP record.

Background. In 1941 A. Philip Randolph's threatened march on Washington won Executive Order 8802 against discrimination in defense work. In 1947 the report To Secure These Rights called for an end to segregation in American life, including the armed forces. In the spring of 1948 Randolph told Congress he would lead resistance to a segregated draft. About a million Black Americans had served in the Second World War, and the second Great Migration had made Black voters a constituency in the northern states whose electoral votes decided presidential elections.

1. In the passage, what policy does Truman declare, and for what institution? Answer in your own words, using one phrase from the passage as evidence.

2. The passage orders the policy put into effect as rapidly as possible and then qualifies the speed. Quote the qualifying phrase and explain what work it does.

3. Truman acted by executive order rather than asking Congress for a law. Using the background, give two reasons the armed forces were the place a president could act alone in 1948.

4. A critic in 1948 argues that the order proves the movement's pressure, not the president's conviction, did the work. State the critic's strongest evidence from the background.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What policy does Truman declare, and for what institution? He declares equality of treatment and opportunity for all persons in the armed services without regard to race, color, religion or national origin, the definition of Executive Order 9981 in the lesson vocabulary. Credit requires the policy and the institution, with a quoted phrase such as equality of treatment and opportunity or without regard to race. (Blueprint 2)

2. Quote the qualifying phrase, and what work does it do? Having due regard to the time required to effectuate any necessary changes without impairing efficiency or morale. The phrase promises speed and licenses delay in the same sentence: officials who opposed desegregation could plead efficiency and morale, and the services moved slowly until Korean War manpower needs made integration a practical necessity. (Blueprint 2)

3. Why could a president act alone on the armed forces? First, the president is commander in chief, so the military's internal rules run through his chain of command rather than through a congressional statute, unlike schools or voting, which Congress and the states controlled. Second, the pressure was aimed at the military specifically: Randolph had threatened resistance to a segregated draft in 1948, and the 1947 report had already named military segregation as federal business. Students may also credit the electoral reason, that Black voters in northern states now counted in presidential elections, as long as one answer rests on the president's direct authority. (Blueprint 2)

4. State the critic's strongest evidence. The sequence in the background is pressure first, action second, twice over. In 1941 a threatened march produced Executive Order 8802 within weeks. In 1948 a threatened campaign of resistance to a segregated draft preceded Executive Order 9981 by a single season, and the same man, A. Philip Randolph, delivered both threats. Add the migration's electoral arithmetic and the critic's case is that presidents moved when organized Black pressure raised the cost of standing still. Answers must work from the background's sequence rather than from later events. (Blueprint 1 and Blueprint 2)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 2 is the hinge diagnostic: students who can hold the order's promise and its brake in one reading have the close reading the unit test will ask for. Question 4 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires the groundwork read as pressure with a record, not as a gift.
