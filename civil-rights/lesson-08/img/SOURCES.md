# Image Sources for Civil Rights Lesson 8, Victory, Backlash, and the Unfinished Agenda

All images are real public domain photographs held by the Lyndon B. Johnson Presidential Library and distributed through its online photo collection. The image was downloaded on October 22, 2026. The download was opened and verified with PIL (3000 by 2052 JPEG). Document copies are stored in the lesson's assets folder and img folder; deck versions are cropped to portrait (1046 by 1500) or to 16:9 for the title slide and stored in slides-build/img; no other alteration.

## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Fair Housing Act passage, activity Station 1: Civil Rights Act of 1968, Title VIII, Sec. 804, 82 Stat. 83 (Statutes at Large, via GovInfo), quoted as enacted in 1968, which is why the protected classes in the passage are race, color, religion, and national origin only; later amendments added further classes. Cuts marked with ellipses.
- Kerner Commission passage, activity Station 2: National Advisory Commission on Civil Disorders, Report, Introduction, 1968 (Teaching American History), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record kerner-commission-report-1968. Cuts marked with ellipses.
- Swann passage, activity Station 3: Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 (1971), opinion of the Court, as transcribed from United States Reports by Justia. The quoted sentences were verified against two published quotations of the opinion.
- Milliken passage, activity Station 3: Milliken v. Bradley, 418 U.S. 717 (1974), opinion of the Court, United States Reports, via the Library of Congress (U.S. Reports PDF, tile.loc.gov). The quoted sentence was verified against the Constitution Annotated summary of the holding published by the Library of Congress.
- Bakke passages, activity Station 4: Regents of the University of California v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265 (1978), syllabus and opinion of Powell, J., United States Reports, via the Library of Congress (U.S. Reports PDF, tile.loc.gov). Cuts marked with ellipses.
- Howard University passage, activity Station 5: Lyndon B. Johnson, Howard University Commencement Address, June 4, 1965 (American Rhetoric), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record lbj-howard-university-address-1965. Cuts marked with ellipses.
- Delano records, reading section F and the deck: Robert F. Kennedy, Statement on Cesar Chavez, March 10, 1968 (Senate press release typescript, Catholic University of America), audited TPSP bank record rfk-delano-statement-1968, and the United Farm Workers account of the same day, audited TPSP bank record rfk-delano-chavez-1968. Both are paraphrased in the reading and deck, not quoted at length; the single shared sentence about respect for one of the heroic figures of our time is the bank records' own wording.
- Loving v. Virginia, available in the bank for this lesson, was not used: the lesson's five stations are fixed by the unit skeleton, and the marriage decision belongs to the era of the great statutes rather than to the reckoning this lesson teaches. Recorded here so the omission is a decision, not an oversight.
- No words of Martin Luther King Jr.'s Letter from Birmingham Jail, I Have a Dream, or Malcolm X's The Ballot or the Bullet are quoted anywhere in this lesson, in any document, the deck, or the notes. The unit's allowances for those works are spent in Lessons 5 and 7, and this lesson honors that accounting.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- Delano, March 10, 1968: the famous photographs of Robert F. Kennedy with Cesar Chavez at the end of the fast were taken by John Kouns for the farmworker movement and circulate through the Farmworker Movement Collection at California State University, Northridge. No public domain basis for them was verified on a holding archive page in this build, so the Delano day is taught in text and through the two bank records, with no photograph.
- Detroit and Newark, 1967: the widely reproduced photographs of the uprisings are press photographs (United Press International and newspaper staff) under copyright, and the Detroit Historical Society holdings carry request-image terms rather than a public domain statement. No public domain photograph of the 1967 uprisings was verified in this build, so the uprisings and the Kerner verdict are taught in text and stat form only.
- Kerner Commission: a photograph of President Johnson with members of the commission in the Cabinet Room circulates on the commission's reference pages, but this build did not open a holding archive file page stating its public domain basis and catalog data line by line, so it is not used. The lesson's one verified photograph is the signing image above.
- School busing: the well known photographs of busing in Boston, Louisville, and Charlotte are press photographs under copyright. No public domain busing photograph was verified in this build, so Swann and Milliken are taught through their texts.
- George Wallace and the 1968 campaign: campaign photographs in circulation are press and wire photographs under copyright, and no federal public domain portrait was verified in this build. Wallace is taught in text and in the stat slide recording his five states and nearly ten million votes.
- Bakke: no photograph is used for the 1978 decision. The case is taught from the syllabus and the Powell opinion only.
- No catalog number is stated anywhere in this lesson for any image beyond the Johnson Library number recorded above, which is taken from the library's own photo page.
