## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- The Greensboro sit-in itself (February 1960): the contemporary photographs of the Woolworth counter are newspaper press photographs. No Commons file page stating a public domain basis for a photograph of the February 1960 Greensboro sit-in was found, so the activity's Station 1 carries no image and says so on its face. Modern photographs of the preserved counter section are Creative Commons licensed, not public domain, and were not used.
- The burning Freedom Ride bus at Anniston (May 14, 1961): the contemporary photographs are press photographs under copyright. No public domain image is used; the violence is carried in plain text, following the unit's documentary sobriety rule.
- The Nashville sit-in attacks of February 27, 1960 and the Albany marches: no image whose Commons file page states a public domain basis was verified for these subjects, so none is used.
- Checked and rejected: the Commons file "Joan Trumpauer Mulholland 1961.jpg" (Jackson police photograph, June 8, 1961) is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 on its file page, not public domain, so it was downloaded for inspection and deleted unused. The lesson set uses the Farmer, Reagon, and Moskowitz mug shots, whose file pages state public domain, in its place.

## Quoted texts and their editions
- Boynton v. Virginia, 364 U.S. 454 (1960): quoted from the opinion of the Court as printed in the United States Reports, reproduced by GovInfo (USREPORTS-364-454). Quoted in the reading (with one cut, marked by ellipses) and at Station 3 of the activity (with cuts, marked by ellipses).
- Interstate Commerce Commission regulations of September 22, 1961 (effective November 1, 1961): quoted at Station 4 of the activity from the regulations as codified at Title 49, Code of Federal Regulations, Part 374 (sections 374.101 and 374.103), via GovInfo and the United States Department of Transportation, with one cut marked by ellipses. The ticket notice text is quoted in full.
- Jesús Colón, "Little Things are Big" (1961): quoted in the writing task's evidence bank from the audited Primary Source Project bank record (civil-rights.json, id jesus-colon-little-things-are-big-1961), whose sourceNote names the edition as Jesús Colón, "Little Things are Big," 1961 (Facing History & Ourselves). The quotation is cut at its opening, marked by ellipses, in part so that no em dash from the bank text enters the student documents.
- Labeled summaries, stated on their faces: activity Station 1 (Greensboro record), Station 2 (Nashville workshop record), the ride record table at Station 3, and Station 5 (Albany record) are summaries prepared for this lesson from the standard historical record, not transcriptions. No contemporaneous document is quoted from memory anywhere in the lesson.
- Copyrighted King and Malcolm X texts: none is quoted in this lesson, so the unit's 90-word caps for the Letter from Birmingham Jail, I Have a Dream, and The Ballot or the Bullet are untouched by Lesson 4 (0 words used).
