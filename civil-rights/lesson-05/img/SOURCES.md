## Copyrighted photographs NOT used, recorded on purpose
- The famous Birmingham photographs of fire hoses and police dogs from May 1963, by press photographers including Bill Hudson (Associated Press) and Charles Moore, are copyrighted press photographs. They are not reproduced anywhere in this lesson. The lesson teaches those days through the text records in the reading and the activity, where the activity's Station 3 describes the photographs and states on its face that they are copyrighted and not reproduced.
- No photograph of the 16th Street Baptist Church bombing damage is reproduced. Contemporary photographs of the damage are press and local archive photographs, and this lesson states the bombing's facts in text, following the unit's rule that no atrocity image is used as decoration. A modern photograph of the church was attempted under a guessed file name and the download returned an error page rather than an image file, so no church photograph is used. Section G of the deck uses text and stat treatment instead.

## Quotation log (copyright discipline, binding on this lesson)
The Letter from Birmingham Jail and I Have a Dream are under copyright. Each may be quoted at no more than 90 words in total across the whole unit. This lesson is the only one in the unit that quotes either work. Every quotation is listed here with its exact count.

### Letter from Birmingham Jail (audited bank record mlk-birmingham-jail; edition basis: the published letter text, longer excerpt supplied from a classroom edition, headnote and footnotes not reproduced)
- Activity, Station 1, passage 1: the sentence beginning "I am in Birmingham" and closing "injustice is here" (identified here by incipit so this log does not add quoted words to the unit total). 8 words.
- Activity, Station 1, passage 2: the sentence beginning "Injustice anywhere" and closing "justice everywhere" (identified by incipit). 8 words.
- TOTAL quoted from the Letter across all files in this lesson: 16 words. No other file in this lesson quotes the letter. The reading, guided notes, notebook, quick check, writing task, deck, and teacher notes all paraphrase its argument and say so where the paraphrase appears.

### I Have a Dream (audited bank record mlk-i-have-a-dream; edition: Martin Luther King Jr., I Have a Dream, 1963, King Papers Project, King Institute)
- Reading, Section F, one passage: the passage beginning "I have a dream that my four little children" and closing "content of their character" (identified by incipit so this log does not add quoted words to the unit total). 35 words.
- TOTAL quoted from I Have a Dream across all files in this lesson: 35 words. No other file in this lesson quotes the address. The activity, writing task, deck, and teacher notes refer to the passage by its content in paraphrase and state its word count without reproducing it.

## Gaps recorded honestly
- No public domain photograph of Birmingham in 1963 is used in this lesson, because the defining photographs of the campaign are copyrighted press photographs. Birmingham is taught through text, the chain of pressure ledger, and stat slides.
- No photograph of President Kennedy delivering the June 11 address is used. No verified public domain file was downloaded for it in this build, so Section D of the deck uses text slides rather than a substituted image under a guessed credit.
