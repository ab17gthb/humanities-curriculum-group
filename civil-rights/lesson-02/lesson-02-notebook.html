Teacher Answer Key

Move in the argumentDateWhat the record shows
What the Court claimedMay 17, 1954In Brown v. Board of Education, a unanimous Court held that separate educational facilities are inherently unequal and that segregation in public schools denies Black children the equal protection of the laws under the Fourteenth Amendment.
What it declined to orderMay 31, 1955Brown II set no date. It required a prompt and reasonable start, placed the burden of proving delay on the school boards, and ordered admission with all deliberate speed, a phrase southern officials read as permission to move slowly.
What the resistance built1956 to 1959Massive resistance: Virginia closing laws, pupil placement laws, and tuition grants; the Southern Manifesto of 1956 signed by 101 members of Congress; and in Prince Edward County the closing of the public schools from 1959 to 1964, leaving about 1,700 Black children without formal schooling.
What the Court answeredSeptember 1958In Cooper v. Aaron, arising from Little Rock, a unanimous Court held that its interpretation of the Fourteenth Amendment in Brown is the supreme law of the land under Article VI, binding on state officials by oath, and that Brown rights can be nullified neither openly nor indirectly.
What was still true in 1964Autumn 1964Ten years after Brown, barely one Black child in a hundred in the Deep South attended school with white children. Prince Edward County reopened its schools in 1964 only after Griffin ordered the taxes levied.

The move that carried the most weight. Strong answers name the move, its date, and its consequence for actual schools. On Brown II, the absence of a date turned a national holding into thousands of local negotiations, which is why a right declared in 1954 was still mostly unexercised in 1964. On Cooper, the Court closed every legal theory of nullification in 1958 and the schools still did not move. On Prince Edward, the closing shows the resistance at its limit, and it took a second Supreme Court order, in Griffin, to reopen the schools.
