# The Planner — Design Brief

_Written 2026-10-20 after Alex's direction: "Our big selling point is the planner. We really need to put work and thought into making it phenomenal." This brief replaces the build order in planner-v1.md. The shipped prototype stays live as a demo of the coverage idea; it is not the product._

## The one-sentence edge

Planboard and Common Curriculum are blank planners: the teacher brings the content, builds the calendar by hand, and the software tracks standards generically. Ours is the only planner where the curriculum is already inside it, every assignment carrying a duration and the test-blueprint entries it teaches, so pacing is proposed automatically and coverage is checked against the actual test the students will take.

## What teachers actually love in the incumbents (researched Oct 2026)

- **Bump.** One action shifts every later lesson forward when a snow day, assembly, or fire drill eats a day (Common Curriculum's signature feature; Planboard has shift/copy too).
- **Drag between days.** A lesson that needs more time moves; everything after it reflows.
- **Zoom.** One planbook from the single day out to the whole year; nothing is entered twice.
- **Reuse.** Last year's plan copies into this year and re-lands on the new calendar.
- **Sub plans.** A shareable link or printout of the day that a substitute can run (a paid-tier driver for Planboard).
- **Rotations and multiple sections.** A/B block schedules and the same course taught to several sections at different paces.
- **Autosave you can trust.** The loudest complaint in Planboard reviews is plans lost to flaky autosave. Reliability is a feature.

## Design principles

1. **The calendar is the database.** Year, unit, week, and day are views of one plan, never separate tools. Enter a fact once (a PD day) and every view and every number downstream updates.
2. **Reflow, never wreck.** Any disruption (day off added, lesson dragged, unit extended) pushes later work forward and shows the teacher exactly what moved and what got squeezed, before they commit to it.
3. **Coverage is a conscience, not a report.** The blueprint meter is always on screen at year and unit level. A change that uncovers a test entry warns at the moment of the change, with the cheapest fix attached.
4. **Setup in minutes.** The year setup is one short pass: first and last day, grading period ends, days off. Preset packs for federal holidays; paste or pick dates individually. No timetable construction project before value appears.
5. **Fast on a school Chromebook.** Static and local first, instant interactions, works offline in the classroom.

Course structure (Alex, Oct 21, 2026): US History is taught three ways and the year planner supports all three as a per-plan setting: the full survey (founding to modern day in one year), or two parts split at 1877, the end of Reconstruction — Part 1 "early" ends with Reconstruction; Part 2 "modern" opens with the Gilded Age. Unit day-count overrides are stored per unit id and carry across shapes, so switching loses nothing. The course map page marks the 1877 split explicitly.

## The year layer (answers Alex's Oct 20 question directly)

Setup, in one screen:
- School year start and end dates.
- Grading periods: name and end date, added one at a time (quarters, trimesters, semesters all work, since a period is just a named boundary). Period ends render on the calendar and in every summary, because teachers plan against them ("finish the unit before the quarter ends" is a real constraint the planner should check).
- Days off, each with a date or range, a label, and a kind: holiday, PD day, break, testing window, other. Kinds matter because they behave differently: a PD day and a holiday are both non-instructional, but a testing window may still allow some classes to meet. Version 2 treats all as non-instructional; the kind is stored for later behavior.
- State test or AP exam date, which the pacing engine reserves review days in front of.

The year calendar:
- Month grids for the whole year. Weekends shaded, days off labeled, grading period ends marked, exam date marked.
- Units laid across instructional days in course order as continuous bands. Each unit band shows its start date, test date, and instructional day count.
- A unit's length is the sum of its plate picks from the unit planner, not a guess. Change the picks, the band resizes, and every later unit moves.
- Year totals always visible: instructional days in the year, days committed, buffer remaining. Running out of year is shown as a number in red long before it becomes a surprise in April.

## The unit layer (the prototype, rebuilt to product standard)

- Keeps the four moves that already work: dates in, pacing proposed from blueprint weights, plate picks, coverage meter with named gaps and one-click fills.
- Adds day types inside the unit: a day can be marked assembly, sub, field trip, or testing, and its lesson bumps forward instead of vanishing.
- Adds pinning: a lesson or the test can be pinned to a date so reflow moves other work around it.
- Adds split: an activity can be split across two days.
- The schedule exports a printable week view and a sub-plan view (the day's materials as a checklist with the PDFs attached).

## Persistence and accounts (phasing decision)

- v2 keeps plans in the browser (localStorage), autosaved on every change, with export and import of the plan as a file. This matches the Rycal guest model and the no-student-data posture: plans are teacher data about dates and picks, nothing about students.
- Accounts and sync arrive when a second course ships or when teachers ask to move plans between school and home computers. Building accounts before the planner earns it would slow the part that sells.

## Explicitly not now

Student-facing anything, grades, attendance, parent communication. The incumbents bundle those to justify district contracts; our wedge is the individual teacher, and focus is the feature.

## Class schedule support (built and deployed 2026-10-21)
Content is measured in lesson-days (a 45-minute lesson). Year plans carry schedule {period: 45|90, pattern: "daily"|"ab"}; unit sections carry their own period (pattern = the section's rotation field). At 90 minutes a meeting fits about two lesson-days: the year planner converts with meetings = ceil(lessonDays/2) and walks meeting days (every other instructional day under A/B, anchored on the year's first instructional day); the unit planner packs up to 2 entries per meeting date, test always alone. totalDays keeps its lesson-days meaning (the teacher home page reads it); saved state also carries totalMeetings. 45/daily behavior is regression-identical to the pre-schedule build (40/40 checks on the planners, 19/19 on the home page, whose copied math was updated to mirror both). Exam-review reserve counts only this course's meeting days under A/B; a pinned start whose date is not a meeting day notes the actual first meeting.

## Calendar additions (built and deployed 2026-10-21)
Teachers can add items that are not on the plate. Unit-plan state carries customItems [{id "x…", title, days 0|0.5|1|2, kind "own"|"tpsp", link, note, bp[], tpspId?}]; additions join the shared order, are picked by default, and behave exactly like plate items (packing, splits, pins, print, sub plan, ICS, share, sync). Coverage that comes only from additions is dagger-marked with the footnote "† Covered by an item you added and tagged yourself." A 2-day addition in a 45-minute section spans consecutive meetings ("Lesson continues"). The TPSP picker (window.HCG_TPSP_PE, 51 entries) is generated from the real TPSP era file; TPSP has no per-source deep link, so source links open tpsp.web.app/library?q=<exact title>; the premium DBQ links to the premium tab and defaults to 2 days. 23 of 50 sources carry conservative blueprint pre-tags; teachers can retag via Edit. The teacher home page renders additions ("TPSP:" linked, or "Your resource:").
