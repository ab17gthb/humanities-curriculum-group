## Text editions used in this lesson, recorded here as the spec directs
- Jackson's message on Indian removal, December 6, 1830, and Worcester v. Georgia, 1832: audited TPSP bank records jackson-indian-removal-message-1830 and worcester-v-georgia-1832 in ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/jackson-reform.json. The message record's sourceNote names the Yale Avalon Project, via Re-Imagining Migration; the Worcester record's sourceNote names Justia. Quoted verbatim from the bank text fields in the activity, with cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines.
- Indian Removal Act, May 28, 1830: quoted from the Avalon Project transcription of the Statutes at Large (4 Stat. 411), as the unit spec assigns. During this build the quoted sections (the title, Section 1, the Section 7 treaty proviso, and the Section 8 appropriation of five hundred thousand dollars) were checked word for word against the National Archives DocsTeach transcription of the enrolled act and against two further Statutes at Large transcriptions, all in agreement.
- Treaty of New Echota, December 29, 1835: quoted from the Avalon Project transcription (text as printed in the Statutes at Large, 7 Stat. 478), as the unit spec assigns. During this build Article 1 and the preamble passage quoted were checked against the Statutes at Large text as carried by Wikisource and against the Digital Public Library of America's transcription of the treaty document, in agreement on the quoted wording.
- Cherokee memorial and protest, 1836: the Memorial and Protest of the Cherokee Nation (Executive Document 286, 24th Congress) and John Ross's letter to the Senate and House of Representatives of September 28, 1836. The passages quoted in Station 4 were taken from the National Park Service transcription of Ross's protest letter and checked against the memorial text as carried by Digital History (University of Houston) and the American Yawp Reader, both printing Executive Document 286, in agreement on the quoted wording. The delegation's records are held by the National Archives among the records of the United States Senate. The unit spec names a National Archives transcription for this station; the transcription actually read and verified during this build was the National Park Service's, of the same Senate documents, and the Source line in the activity says so on its face rather than claiming the Archives' own transcription was read.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- Cherokee Phoenix syllabary chart (modern): a modern syllabary chart found on Wikimedia Commons during this build (File:Cherokee Syllabary Chart.png) is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (2021, own work), not public domain, so it was not used. The period chart of Sequoyah's original order above is used instead.
- Samuel Worcester: no portrait with a public domain basis read on its file page was verified for this build, so Worcester is taught in text.
- Major Ridge and Elias Boudinot portraits exist in the McKenney and Hall series, but no download with a verified public domain basis and adequate resolution was confirmed during this build (one Boudinot file verified at only 285 by 350 pixels), so the Treaty Party is taught in text and in the Ross lithograph's counterpoint.
- The removal itself: no image of the march is used anywhere in this lesson. Period imagery of the removal as an event does not exist in any verified public domain form for this build, and later artists' imaginings were not used as decoration, in keeping with the unit spec.
