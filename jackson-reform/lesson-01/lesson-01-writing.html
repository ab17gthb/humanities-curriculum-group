Writing Task: The Birth of Democratic Politics?

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: what changed when the common white man got the vote, and who was still left outside it? A strong answer does more than list changes. It states what the vote had required before and what it required by 1828, works through the widening and the exclusion record together, and weighs Margaret Bayard Smith's closing judgment that the people would rule against the people the new democracy refused to count. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading, stations, and documents. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording, except where a bank item quotes a document.

Evidence Bank

1. In the 1810s and 1820s the states struck down property and taxpaying limits on voting, New York removing its property test for white men in 1821, until by 1828 nearly every white man could vote and turnout ran near three times the turnout of 1824. New western states entered the union with broad white manhood suffrage in their constitutions. (Lesson reading, section A, and the activity, Station 3, a labeled teaching summary from the state constitutions.)

2. New Jersey's law of 1807 limited the vote to free white male citizens after some women and free Black men had voted under the constitution of 1776 for thirty years, and Pennsylvania's constitution of 1838 wrote white before freemen, taking the vote from free Black men in the state. (Lesson reading, section B, and the activity, Station 5, a labeled teaching summary from the laws named.)

3. In 1824 Jackson led the popular and electoral votes without a majority, Clay backed Adams in the House, Adams won on February 9, 1825, and named Clay secretary of state. Jackson's supporters charged a corrupt bargain. Both men denied it, and no evidence of a bargain has ever been produced. (Lesson reading, section C, and the activity, Station 4, a labeled teaching summary from the public record.)

4. Margaret Bayard Smith, describing March 4, 1829, set a crowd silent, orderly and tranquil at the Capitol against a rabble, a mob in the White House, and closed: But it was the People's day, and the People's President and the People would rule. (The First Forty Years of Washington Society, edited by Gaillard Hunt, 1906, quoted in the activity, Station 2.)

5. Jackson's First Annual Message of December 8, 1829 held that office is considered as a species of property, that the duties of public officers are so plain and simple that men of intelligence may readily qualify themselves for their performance, and that more is lost by long continuance in office than is gained by experience. His administration removed some nine hundred of nine thousand civil officers, defended as rotation and attacked as the spoils system. (Richardson's Messages and Papers of the Presidents, quoted in the activity, Station 1, and lesson reading, section F.)

6. The Democratic Party formed around Jackson out of the election of 1828, joining southern planters, western farmers, and northern workingmen. Jackson himself held enslaved people at the Hermitage, and Native nations stood outside the polity entirely while free Black men were losing the vote. (Lesson reading, sections B and G.)

Plan Before You Write

My claim (one sentence that answers the question's two halves at once):

My three evidence picks (bank numbers) and what each one proves:

The strongest fact on the other side of my claim, and how I will answer it:

Scaffold. Build the essay in this shape. Open with the claim. A first body paragraph on the widening: before 1828 the vote asked a white man for property, and by 1828 it asked him for almost nothing, and parties and turnout changed to match. A second body paragraph on the boundary: the same decades closed the vote where it had stood open, in the two states your evidence names. A closing move on Smith: state whether her closing sentence describes the whole people or names the part of it the new democracy counted, and defend the reading from your evidence.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your three pieces of evidence in the order of the lesson, answer the strongest fact on the other side in one or two sentences, and end on Smith's closing judgment.

Essay, continued. Keep your evidence in the order of the lesson, and end on Smith's closing judgment.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the question in one judgment, for example that 1828 made white men political equals at the ballot box while it defined the people by race and sex, or that the change was real and the boundary was its price. A claim that answers only the widening, or only the exclusions, earns partial credit before the evidence is read. (Blueprint 1)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 carries the legal change and the turnout that proves it. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 2 carries the exclusion record with dates, 1807 and 1838. (Blueprint 2) Bank item 3 carries the grievance that organized the new electorate, with the charge labeled unproven. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 4 is Smith's paired scenes and her closing sentence. (Blueprint 2) Bank item 5 is the doctrine of rotation in Jackson's own words and the removal count that tests it. (Blueprint 1 and Blueprint 2) Bank item 6 supplies the party and the president's own household as the boundary in one item. (Blueprint 2)
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. The decisive link is sequence against coincidence: strong essays argue whether the exclusions were leftovers the widening had not yet reached or acts the widening's own generation passed on purpose, and the dates in bank item 2 are the evidence that decides between those readings.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest fact on the other side, such as the turnout of 1828 answered from bank item 2: a tripled turnout of white men does not answer a closing that took ballots from people who had cast them.

Model opening for teacher reference. What changed in 1828 was the definition of a voter, and who was left outside was decided by the same hands. A white man no longer needed an acre to cast a ballot: the states had struck the property tests down through the 1820s, New York in 1821 among them, and turnout near tripled as committees and newspapers organized the new electorate. But the people being enlarged were named as they were enlarged. New Jersey had already closed its vote to white men only in 1807, ending thirty years in which some women and free Black men had voted there, and Pennsylvania would write white into its constitution in 1838. A full model continues in this pattern, weighing the corrupt bargain as the grievance that organized the widening, and closing on Smith's sentence, which is true of the Capitol crowd she watched and incomplete as a description of the country that crowd lived in.

Common weak answers. Jackson was the people's president (a label, not an answer) skips both halves of the question. An essay that treats the exclusions as a later problem the era had not reached ignores the dates in bank item 2, 1807 and 1838, which sit inside the widening itself. An essay that presents the corrupt bargain as a proven deal has misread bank item 3 and should be sent back to Station 4.
