## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Bank Veto Message, activity Station 1 and quick check: Andrew Jackson, Bank Veto Message, July 10, 1832, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record jackson-bank-veto-1832 in jackson-reform.json, whose sourceNote names the Yale Avalon Project, from Richardson's Messages and Papers of the Presidents, 1897, with punctuation modernized as the record discloses. Passages are quoted verbatim from the bank record's text field. The farmers, mechanics, and laborers appositive in the record's text is carried inside a marked ellipsis cut in Station 1 so that the lesson's documents contain no dash punctuation from the passage; the cut is marked and disclosed in the source line, and the reading and keys name the group in prose.
- King Andrew the First, activity Station 2: the audited TPSP bank record king-andrew-first-1833 in jackson-reform.json, whose sourceNote names the Library of Congress. The record's image description is quoted in the station, and the print itself is reproduced from the Library of Congress impression (see image 1 above).
- Biddle's answer, activity Station 3: a labeled teaching summary, labeled on its face, built from the record of the Bank's operations in 1833 and 1834 (the contraction of loans, the pressure on state banks, the relief petitions to Congress, and Biddle's stated design in his correspondence with the Bank's friends). No sentence in Station 3 is presented as a quotation, and no Biddle letter is quoted, because no verbatim Biddle text from a named edition was verified during this build. The spec permits the labeled summary in exactly this case.
- Jackson's Farewell Address, activity Station 4: Andrew Jackson, Farewell Address, March 4, 1837, as carried in the Wikisource transcription of the address (en.wikisource.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson's_Farewell_Address), fetched in full during this build. The paper currency passages are quoted from that transcription with its spelling preserved. Honest flag: the unit spec assigns the Avalon Project for this text; the Avalon copy could not be fetched and verified during this build (the Avalon Jackson addresses did not resolve), so the lesson quotes the Wikisource transcription actually used and names it in the station's Source line and here, rather than attaching Avalon's name to a text not taken from it.
- Specie Circular, activity Station 5 and quick check: Circular from Levi Woodbury, Secretary of the Treasury, to receivers of public money and the deposit banks, July 11, 1836, as transcribed by the American Presidency Project (presidency.ucsb.edu), fetched in full during this build. The operative payment clause and the settler proviso are quoted from that transcription. This is the named Treasury transcription the spec permits for this station.

## Figures stated as counts or estimates

- The Second Bank's charter: granted in 1816 for twenty years, expiring in 1836, as stated in the reading and the deck.
- The recharter bill: passed by Congress in the summer of 1832 and vetoed on July 10, 1832, as stated in the bank record's headnote and the reading.
- The settler exception in the Specie Circular: 320 acres, to December 15, 1836, quoted from the circular's own text at Station 5.
- The suspension: May 1837, banks of New York first and the country's banks following, as stated in the Specie Circular's and the Panic of 1837's definitions in the unit spec, carried verbatim in the guided notes.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- Second Bank building: no period print of the Second Bank's Chestnut Street building was verified public domain during this build. The Birch views located on Wikimedia Commons show the First Bank on Third Street, a different building, and were not used, since captioning them as the Second Bank would mislabel the record. The Bank is pictured in this lesson by its 1829 note and by its president's portrait instead.
- Nicholas Biddle's letters: no verbatim text from a named edition (the McGrane Correspondence of Nicholas Biddle, 1919, was identified but its text was not fetched and verified during this build), so Station 3 is a labeled teaching summary, as recorded above.
- The Avalon Project Farewell Address: not fetched, as recorded above; the Wikisource transcription is the edition quoted and named.
- No catalog number is stated anywhere in this lesson for any image beyond the Library of Congress item number for the King Andrew print, the Library of Congress digital ID for the Van Buren photograph, and the Baker Library urn identifier recorded above, which are taken from the bank record and the Wikimedia Commons file pages as cataloged there.
