Teacher Answer Key

EpisodeThe claim madeThe power claimed or usedWhat it settled or left open
The tariff grievanceProtective tariffs taxed the planting states to shelter northern factories and threatened the cotton trade that paid for their imports.Congress used its power to lay duties on imports, under a reading that let it protect industry as well as raise revenue.It settled nothing. It gave a permanent minority its grievance and set South Carolina looking for a remedy outside ordinary legislation.
The doctrineThe Constitution is a compact of sovereign states, so a state convention may nullify a federal law inside its borders until three fourths of the states amend the Constitution over its head.A reserved state power to judge infractions of the compact, claimed from the Kentucky Resolutions of 1798.It settled the theory on paper. It left open whether any state would act on it and what the Union would do if one did.
The Senate answerThe Constitution is the people's government, made by the people and answerable to the people, so no state may judge federal law for itself.National authority resting on the whole people, with constitutional change by amendment and judgment by the courts the Constitution names.It settled the argument in northern opinion and in print. It settled nothing in South Carolina, where the nullifiers held the state.
The ordinanceThe tariffs of 1828 and 1832 are null and void in South Carolina, collection is forbidden after February 1, 1833, and collection by force ends the state's place in the Union.The doctrine of nullification put into state law, backed by a militia and a deadline.It settled that the doctrine would be tested. It left the test itself to the president and Congress.
The proclamationNullification is incompatible with the Union, the Constitution forms a government and not a league, and disunion by armed force is treason.The president's duty to execute the laws, stated to the state's people directly, with forts and cutters reinforced around Charleston.It settled that the federal government would not concede the doctrine. It left open whether duties could be collected without war.
The settlementBoth sides claimed victory. The state pointed to falling duties; the Union pointed to a repealed ordinance and an unbroken authority.The Force Bill's grant of military power to collect the duties, paired with the compromise tariff's staged reductions to 1816 levels by 1842.It settled the crisis of 1833 without a shot. It left the doctrine written, argued, and ready for a later generation to carry further.

The lesson in one sentence. Strong answers join the two halves of the settlement: South Carolina stood alone, Jackson held both a proclamation and a Force Bill, and the compromise tariff gave the state falling duties it could call a prize, so repeal cost less than resistance. Credit answers that name isolation, force, or compromise alone only when the sentence explains how that cause worked on the convention's choice.
