Writing Task: What Made South Carolina Back Down?

The prompt

Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: Could a state veto the Union, and what made South Carolina back down? Take a position on what did the decisive work in the winter of 1833. Was it the threat of federal force, the offer of lower duties, the state's isolation, or some combination you rank in order? A strong answer states the doctrine first from its own documents, then states what the president and Congress did, and judges which of those actions moved the convention to repeal. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim. The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The Exposition and Protest, December 1828: the Constitution is a compact of sovereign states, and a state convention may nullify a federal law inside its borders until three fourths of the states amend the Constitution to grant the power plainly.

2. Webster's second reply to Hayne, January 1830: the Constitution is the people's government, made by the people and answerable to the people, and liberty and union are one and inseparable.

3. The Ordinance of Nullification, November 24, 1832: the tariffs of 1828 and 1832 declared null and void in the state, collection forbidden after February 1, 1833, and secession threatened if the duties were collected by force.

4. Jackson's proclamation, December 10, 1832: the power to annul a federal law is incompatible with the existence of the Union, the Constitution forms a government and not a league, and disunion by armed force is treason.

5. The settlement of March 1833: the Force Bill authorized Jackson to use the army and navy to collect the duties, the compromise tariff lowered duties by stages to the levels of 1816 by 1842, no other state joined South Carolina, and its convention repealed the ordinance and then nullified the Force Bill.

6. Calhoun's Fort Hill letter, July 26, 1831: nullification, under any name, is the fundamental principle of the system, and its utmost reach is to compel the federal government to submit the disputed question to the states that created it.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that ranks the causes of the repeal and answers the lesson question with the ranking: the veto claim stated, and the backing down explained, in one argument. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 supports the doctrine and its amendment test. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 2 supports the national answer in the Senate. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 3 supports what the ordinance ordered and threatened. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 4 supports the president's refusal and its constitutional ground. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 5 supports the paired settlement and the state's isolation. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 6 supports what Calhoun himself said the doctrine could and could not do. (Blueprint 5)
  • A full credit essay must use item 3 or item 5; a verdict on the repeal without the ordinance's own terms or the settlement's own terms is an opinion without its documents.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the ranking. An essay that lists the proclamation, the Force Bill, and the compromise without judging which moved the convention has assembled the winter's events without its argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that keeps the rehearsal in view: the doctrine survived its defeat, in print and in memory, for the generation of 1861.

Model opening for teacher reference. South Carolina did not back down because its doctrine was answered. Webster and Jackson answered it completely, and the nullifiers held their state anyway. It backed down because the answer arrived with an army behind it and a staircase beside it. The Force Bill made resistance a war the state would fight alone, and the compromise tariff made retreat a prize the convention could carry home, duties falling by stages for nine years. Ranked in order, isolation did the first work, force the second, and compromise the last and kindest.
