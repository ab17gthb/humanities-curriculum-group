## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- Charleston harbor and the tariff era waterfront: no verifiable public domain period print of Charleston was located and downloaded during this build, so no Charleston image is used or implied. The port is taught in text.
- The Webster-Hayne debate in the Senate chamber: the well known painting of Webster replying to Hayne is a later imagining held by a school collection, and its public domain basis was not verified on a file page during this build, so it is not used. The debate is represented by the Alexander portrait of Webster.
- Henry Clay: no verified public domain portrait was downloaded for this build. Clay enters the lesson through the compromise tariff in text and in the stat slide for 1842.

## Quoted source log for this lesson
- South Carolina Legislature (written by John C. Calhoun), South Carolina Exposition and Protest, 1828 (Wikisource). Audited TPSP bank record south-carolina-exposition-and-protest-1828, jackson-reform.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 1, with cuts marked with ellipses as the bank record marks them.
- South Carolina Convention, Ordinance of Nullification, 1832 (Paul Leicester Ford, ed., The Federalist, 1898; via the Yale Avalon Project; capitalization modernized, as the bank record's sourceNote states). Audited TPSP bank record south-carolina-nullification-1832, jackson-reform.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 2, and in the quick check, Passage A, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Andrew Jackson, Proclamation Regarding Nullification, December 10, 1832 (Avalon Project, Yale Law School; from James D. Richardson, A Compilation of the Messages and Papers of the Presidents). Fetched from the Avalon Project text during this build and quoted in the activity, Station 3, and in the quick check, Passage B, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- Daniel Webster, Second Reply to Hayne, January 26 and 27, 1830 (The Papers of Daniel Webster: Speeches and Formal Writings, vol. 1, ed. Charles M. Wiltse, University Press of New England, 1986). Two passages quoted in the activity, Station 4: the people's Constitution passage and the closing sentence, both as the published version of the speech carries them.
- John C. Calhoun, Fort Hill Address, July 26, 1831 (The Works of John C. Calhoun: Reports and Public Letters, ed. Richard K. Crallé, D. Appleton, 1856; transcription by Teaching American History). Fetched from that transcription during this build and quoted in the activity, Station 5, with cuts marked with ellipses.
- No station in this lesson is a teaching summary. All five stations quote period texts from the editions named above.
