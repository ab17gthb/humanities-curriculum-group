The turn in one paragraph. Write a paragraph answering the lesson question. State what changed when abolitionists stopped asking gradually, name the two answers the country gave, in law and in the streets, and end with the change in slavery's defense, from necessary evil to positive good.

Teacher Answer Key

The recordWhat was demanded, or doneWho was addressedHow the country answered
Walker, 1829His Appeal judged American slavery worse than the bondage of Egypt or Rome and called on enslaved people to judge their condition and resist it, claiming no master but Jesus Christ.Black readers directly, the colored citizens of the world, carried south sewn into sailors' clothing.Southern states passed laws against the pamphlet's circulation and against teaching enslaved people to read. Georgia's governor asked Boston to suppress it.
Garrison, 1831In The Liberator's first issue he recanted gradual abolition as timidity, injustice, and absurdity, and contended for the immediate enfranchisement of the slave population. The American Anti-Slavery Society followed in December 1833.The public of the free states first, in a Boston newspaper meant to be argued with and reprinted.Georgia's legislature offered a reward for his arrest in 1831, and in October 1835 a Boston mob dragged him through the streets with a rope.
Southampton, 1831Nat Turner, an enslaved preacher who believed signs had appointed him, led the rising of August 21 and 22, 1831, in which about sixty white people were killed.The rising addressed Southampton County itself. Turner's account survives only through Thomas Gray's Confessions, which Gray took down, published, and sold.Retaliation killed many more Black people. Virginia debated ending slavery openly for the last time that winter, voted it down, and the southern states tightened their slave codes.
The petitions, 1835 to 1844The Society flooded the mails with print in 1835 and sent petitions by the hundreds of thousands, many signed by women, demanding Congress act against slavery where it lawfully could.Congress, through the constitutional right of petition.Southern postmasters seized and burned the mail sacks with the Postmaster General's consent. The House adopted the gag rule in May 1836 and tabled petitions unread until John Quincy Adams won repeal in December 1844.
The witnesses, 1836 and 1845Grimké's Appeal asked southern women to read, pray, speak, and act. Douglass's Narrative, chapter VII, showed an enslaved boy teaching himself to read and judged that slavery had corrupted his mistress as it held him.Grimké addressed southern women from inside the slaveholding household. Douglass addressed the reading public under his own name.Grimké's name was reviled in Charleston and her former life closed to her. Douglass, a fugitive still legally enslavable, had to sail for Britain after publication while supporters raised the price of his freedom.

The turn in one paragraph. Strong answers hold the demand and both answers together. What changed was the tense of abolition: Walker in 1829 and Garrison in 1831 withdrew the gradual request and demanded slavery's immediate end as a sin, without stages, payment, or removal, and the Society, the petitions, and the witnesses, Grimké in 1836 and Douglass in 1845, carried the demand into households the old antislavery had never reached. The country answered in law with tightened slave codes, seized mails, and the gag rule of 1836 to 1844, and in the streets with mobs that dragged Garrison in 1835 and killed Lovejoy in 1837. And slavery's defense changed its ground to match: Calhoun's Senate speech of 1837 called slavery a positive good, a defense that no longer promised any ending at all.
