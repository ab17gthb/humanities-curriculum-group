# Jackson and Reform — Unit Manifest

**Essential question:** What did democracy mean in Jackson's America, who was it for, and what did the era's reformers claim it still owed?

**Course position:** The fourth unit of the US History course (courseId u4), built thirteenth in the build order. It opens with the election of 1828, ten years before the Early Republic unit's closing date, and closes at the end of 1848 with the Seneca Falls Convention. Texas annexation and the war with Mexico are prior knowledge here, taught as the Early Republic unit's final lesson and nowhere retaught; the Missouri Compromise is likewise prior knowledge, named in prose and nowhere redefined or retaught as core; and the Compromise of 1850 is not taught here at all, because it opens the Sectional Crisis and Civil War unit. The era is taught with its peoples in the frame throughout, Cherokee constitutionalists and the signers of a minority treaty, mill women writing for their own newspaper, enslaved rebels and the free Black writers who addressed them, revival converts organizing societies, and the women who had run abolition's local work for a decade before they demanded its logic for themselves.

**Built:** 2026-10-22. 51 documents, 248 printed pages. 8 slide decks, 219 slides, 31,349 words of speaker notes, 8 printable teacher notes documents (75 pages).

**Topics (6):** 1 Democracy widened: suffrage, parties, and the people's president · 2 Removal: the republic and the Native nations of the Southeast · 3 The Union tested: nullification, the bank, and the limits of federal power · 4 The market remade: factories, transport, and communication · 5 Revival and reform: awakening, temperance, schools, and asylums · 6 Freedom's claims widened: abolition and women's rights.

**Test blueprint:** 15 entries, two per lesson except Lesson 8 (one). Entry-to-topic map and per-lesson item assignments are in the skeleton: ~/workspace/curriculum-planner/jackson-reform-skeleton.md.

**TPSP source plan:** The bank HAS a Jackson and reform era file: ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/jackson-reform.json, era "Age of Jackson and Reform (1828-1860)", 22 audited records, and this unit quotes from it wherever it carries the text: 12 of its records sit inside this unit's 1828 to 1848 ground and are quoted in Lessons 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, and 8. The premium catalog was checked directly in Firestore (collection psp_premium) for this build on 2026-10-22, the check the Early Republic build's procedure requires. TWO premium DBQs sit on this unit's ground, both verified with every docId located in a bank era file. DBQ 8, "Did Andrew Jackson Expand Democracy or Abuse Power?" (document dbq8-jackson-democracy-power, catalog code DBQ183, era "Jacksonian America"), holds 7 documents, every one inside this unit and every one taught in its pages: the Bank Veto Message, Jackson's Indian removal message, Worcester v. Georgia, the King Andrew the First cartoon, the South Carolina Exposition and Protest, the Ordinance of Nullification, and the Declaration of Sentiments. DBQ 14, "What Strategies Did Reformers Use to Force Change?" When the planner integration is built, the picker leads with DBQ 8 (DBQ183), whose question is the unit's question for Lessons 1 through 4, with DBQ 14 (DBQ189) alongside it.

**Copyright accounting:** No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this unit. Every quotation is a public domain period text quoted from a named edition: the audited bank records, Richardson's Messages and Papers of the Presidents, the Avalon Project at Yale, the Statutes at Large transcriptions, the Papers of Daniel Webster (Wiltse edition), the Works of John C. Calhoun (Crallé edition), Documenting the American South at the University of North Carolina, Project Gutenberg texts of the 1835, 1836, and 1838 originals, the Life and Works of Horace Mann (1891), and the session laws of New York. The 90-quoted-word copyright cap is therefore satisfied trivially, with no ledger entries. Where an edition modernizes spelling or punctuation, the Source line says so, following the bank records' own practice. Where no verbatim text from a named edition could be verified, the document carries a labeled teaching summary instead, labeled on its face: the suffrage, corrupt bargain, and exclusion records (Lesson 1), Biddle's answer (Lesson 4), the railroad, factory rules, and turn out records (Lesson 5), the temperance pledge and camp meeting records (Lesson 6), part of the Grimké Letters argument and the London exclusion record (Lesson 8).

The nullification definition (Lesson 3) is the Early Republic unit's Lesson 2 definition character for character, as that build's spec directed this build to take it unchanged, and the coordinator's verification confirms it is unaltered in the guided notes, the deck notes, and the study guide.
