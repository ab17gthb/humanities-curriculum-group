## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- No image of the Wesleyan Chapel was used. The chapel photographs located are modern photographs under Creative Commons licenses rather than public domain, and no public domain period view of the building that could be verified on its file page was secured. The lesson teaches the meeting in text and in the deck's stat slides rather than printing a modern photograph as if it were a period view. Period views of the chapel interior dated circa 1858 are described in National Park Service materials but no verifiable public domain file was obtained for this build.
- No portrait of Angelina Grimké was used in print. The one public domain portrait located on Wikimedia Commons (Massachusetts Historical Society collection, from before her death in 1879) is 250 by 325 pixels, too small to print at the sizes this lesson uses, so Sarah Grimké's engraving carries the sisters' stations and Angelina is taught in text.
- No image of Sarah or Angelina Grimké lecturing, of the London convention of 1840, or of the Seneca Falls meeting itself was used. No verifiable public domain image of those scenes was secured, and later artists' imaginings of the convention were not used as decoration.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- Elizabeth Cady Stanton and the Seneca Falls Convention, Declaration of Sentiments, July 19, 1848. Audited TPSP bank record seneca-falls-declaration-1848 (jackson-reform.json); edition named in the record: National Park Service; punctuation normalized, as the record states. Quoted verbatim from the record in the activity and quick check, with the record's own ellipses. Passages containing the record's em dash characters were not selected, in keeping with this unit's print standard; the grievance passages printed are complete sentences as the record carries them.
- Elizabeth Cady Stanton, Report of the Woman's Rights Convention, Seneca Falls, July 19 and 20, 1848. Audited TPSP bank record seneca-falls-report-1848 (jackson-reform.json); edition named in the record: National Park Service; the 1848 pamphlet held by the Library of Congress. Quoted verbatim from the record in the activity, with the record's own ellipses and hyphens.
- Sarah Grimké, Letters on the Equality of the Sexes and the Condition of Woman, Letter I, dated July 11, 1837 (Boston: Isaac Knapp, 1838). The passage printed at Station 3 is the Letter I passage on Adam and Eve, quoted from that edition's text of Letter I as attested in the quotation record for the letter. The rest of Station 3 is a labeled teaching summary of Letter I's argument and says so on its face.
- Laws of the State of New York, chapter 200, passed April 7, 1848, "An Act for the more effectual protection of the property of married women," sections 1 and 3, quoted from the transcription of the session laws consulted for this lesson (the Laws of New York, chap. 200 (1848) transcription at HouseofRussell.com, Thomas D. Russell). Section 2 is described, not quoted, in the activity, and the Source line says so.
- The exclusion of the women delegates at the World's Anti-Slavery Convention, London, June 1840. No verbatim text is quoted. Station 5 of the activity is a labeled teaching summary built from the public record of the convention (the vote to refuse the women delegates, the curtained gallery seating, Garrison's refusal of his floor seat, and the Mott and Stanton agreement), and it is labeled a summary on its face wherever it appears.
