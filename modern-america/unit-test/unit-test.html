Part 3: Sources (15 points)

Answer each question in complete sentences, citing the sources by letter.

Source A

Jimmy Carter, Crisis of Confidence Address, July 15, 1979. From Jimmy Carter, 'Crisis of Confidence Address,' July 15, 1979 (Miller Center). Cuts are marked with ellipses in brackets.

“The threat is nearly invisible in ordinary ways. It is a crisis of confidence. It is a crisis that strikes at the very heart and soul and spirit of our national will. We can see this crisis in the growing doubt about the meaning of our own lives and in the loss of a unity of purpose for our Nation. [...] In a nation that was proud of hard work, strong families, close-knit communities, and our faith in God, too many of us now tend to worship self-indulgence and consumption. Human identity is no longer defined by what one does, but by what one owns. But we've discovered that owning things and consuming things does not satisfy our longing for meaning. [...] For the first time in the history of our country a majority of our people believe that the next 5 years will be worse than the past 5 years. Two-thirds of our people do not even vote. [...] So, the solution of our energy crisis can also help us to conquer the crisis of the spirit in our country. It can rekindle our sense of unity, our confidence in the future, and give our Nation and all of us individually a new sense of purpose.”

A1. (2 points) According to President Carter, what crisis strikes at the heart and soul and spirit of the national will, and what two losses does he say the country can see in it?

A2. (3 points) State how Carter says human identity had changed, give the evidence he offers that a majority of Americans expected the next five years to be worse than the past five, and state how he connects the solution of the energy crisis to the crisis of the spirit.

Source B

Ronald Reagan, First Inaugural Address, January 20, 1981. From Ronald Reagan, 'First Inaugural Address,' January 20, 1981 (Gilder Lehrman Institute). Cuts are marked with ellipses in brackets.

“We suffer from the longest and one of the worst sustained inflations in our national history. It distorts our economic decisions, penalizes thrift, and crushes the struggling young and the fixed-income elderly alike. It threatens to shatter the lives of millions of our people. [...] Idle industries have cast workers into unemployment, human misery, and personal indignity. Those who do work are denied a fair return for their labor by a tax system which penalizes successful achievement and keeps us from maintaining full productivity. [...] But great as our tax burden is, it has not kept pace with public spending. For decades we have piled deficit upon deficit, mortgaging our future and our children's future for the temporary convenience of the present. [...] In this present crisis, government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem.”

B1. (2 points) According to President Reagan, what does sustained inflation penalize and whom does it crush, naming both groups he lists?

B2. (3 points) State Reagan's conclusion about government in the present crisis, and explain the reasoning he gives for it from the tax burden, public spending, and the deficits piled up over decades.

Source C

National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, The 9/11 Commission Report: Executive Summary, 2004. From National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, 'The 9/11 Commission Report: Executive Summary,' 2004 (the Commission's own edition). Cuts are marked with ellipses in brackets.

“We present the narrative of this report and the recommendations that flow from it to the President of the United States, the United States Congress, and the American people for their consideration. Ten Commissioners, five Republicans and five Democrats chosen by elected leaders from our nation's capital at a time of great partisan division, have come together to present this report without dissent. [...] September 11, 2001, was a day of unprecedented shock and suffering in the history of the United States. The nation was unprepared. [...] What we can say with confidence is that none of the measures adopted by the U.S. government from 1998 to 2001 disturbed or even delayed the progress of the al Qaeda plot. Across the government, there were failures of imagination, policy, capabilities, and management. [...] The most important failure was one of imagination. We do not believe leaders understood the gravity of the threat. The terrorist danger from Bin Ladin and al Qaeda was not a major topic for policy debate among the public, the media, or in the Congress.”

C1. (2 points) Who produced this report, how were its ten commissioners divided between the two parties, and to whom is the report presented, as the source states its own making?

C2. (3 points) Name the four failures the Commission found across the government, state which failure it calls the most important, and state its finding about the measures the government adopted from 1998 to 2001.

Part 3: Sources

A1. Carter names a crisis of confidence. He says it strikes at the very heart and soul and spirit of the national will. The country can see it, he says, in the growing doubt about the meaning of our own lives and in the loss of a unity of purpose for the nation. One point for the crisis named in the source's words and one point for both losses. (Blueprint 1)

A2. Carter says human identity is no longer defined by what one does, but by what one owns, in a nation where too many people tend to worship self-indulgence and consumption, and he adds that owning and consuming things does not satisfy the longing for meaning. His evidence is that for the first time in the country's history a majority of the people believe the next five years will be worse than the past five years. He connects the two crises directly, stating that the solution of the energy crisis can also help conquer the crisis of the spirit by rekindling a sense of unity and confidence in the future. Full credit requires the identity claim, the majority evidence, and that connection. (Blueprint 1)

B1. Reagan says sustained inflation distorts economic decisions and penalizes thrift. It crushes the struggling young and the fixed-income elderly alike. One point for thrift and one point for both groups named. (Blueprint 3)

B2. Reagan concludes that in this present crisis, government is not the solution to the problem; government is the problem. His reasoning in the passage is that the tax burden, great as it is, has not kept pace with public spending, and that for decades the country has piled deficit upon deficit, mortgaging the future and children's future for the temporary convenience of the present. The tax system in the passage also penalizes successful achievement and keeps the country from maintaining full productivity. Full credit requires the conclusion in the source's words and at least two parts of that reasoning. (Blueprint 3)

C1. The report was produced by the National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States. Its ten commissioners were five Republicans and five Democrats, chosen by elected leaders, and the source states that they came together to present the report without dissent. The source states that it presents its narrative and recommendations to the President of the United States, the United States Congress, and the American people. One point for the commission and the five and five division and one point for the presentation without dissent. (Blueprint 11)

C2. The Commission found failures of imagination, policy, capabilities, and management across the government. It calls the failure of imagination the most important failure, stating that leaders did not understand the gravity of the threat. Its finding on the measures adopted from 1998 to 2001 is that none of them disturbed or even delayed the progress of the al Qaeda plot. Full credit requires all four failures, the most important one named, and that finding stated. (Blueprint 11)
