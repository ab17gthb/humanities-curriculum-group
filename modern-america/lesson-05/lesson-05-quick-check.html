Quick Check: The Table and the Two Cities

Directions: Study the data record and the background, then answer the questions.

Data record. United States Census Bureau figures, decennial censuses and reapportionment records, rounded as the bureau reports them in summary tables.

RecordFirst figureSecond figure
Foreign born population1970: about 9.6 million, 4.7 percent2010: about 40 million, about 13 percent
Detroit, Michigan1950: about 1.85 million2010: about 714,000
Phoenix, Arizona1950: about 107,0002010: about 1.45 million

Background. The Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 ended the national origins quotas and built a preference system on family reunification and needed skills. The Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 granted legal status to about three million people already in the country and banned the knowing hire of unauthorized workers. In every reapportionment from 1970 onward, the South and West gained House seats.

1. From the data record, state the change in the foreign born population between 1970 and 2010 in both size and share, and name the law whose preference system the reading credits with the change.

2. State the two halves of the 1986 act, and give the date before which a person had to have arrived to qualify for legal status under it.

3. Using the two city lines, explain how deindustrialization and white flight on one side and the Sun Belt's growth on the other describe one movement of Americans, naming each city's figures.

4. Explain how the movements in Questions 1 and 3 became political weight: what did the census move after the movers every ten years, and which regions gained in every reapportionment from 1970 onward?

Teacher Answer Key

1. The foreign born change and the law. The foreign born population rose from about 9.6 million and 4.7 percent of the population in 1970 to about 40 million and about 13 percent in 2010 (Census Bureau). The reading credits the preference system of the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, built on family reunification and needed skills after the act ended the national origins quotas. (Blueprint 9)

2. The two halves of the 1986 act and the date. The Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 granted legal status to about three million unauthorized immigrants already in the country and made it illegal for employers knowingly to hire unauthorized workers. Legalization covered those who had arrived before January 1, 1982 and resided continuously in the country from that date. (Blueprint 9)

3. The two cities as one movement. Detroit fell from about 1.85 million people in 1950 to about 714,000 in 2010 as deindustrialization emptied its factory districts and white flight carried families and tax base to the suburbs. Phoenix rose from about 107,000 in 1950 to about 1.45 million in 2010 as the Sun Belt drew movers with air conditioning, defense and technology work, and lower costs. The same decades and the same moving population stand behind both lines: people and jobs left the older industrial North and Midwest and gathered in the South and West. (Blueprint 10)

4. Population movement as political weight. Every ten years the census reapportioned the House of Representatives, moving seats and electoral votes to the states that had gained people and away from the states that had lost them. The South and West gained House seats in every reapportionment from 1970 onward. Accept answers that connect both the immigration of Question 1 and the internal migration of Question 3 to representation, since newcomers and movers are counted in the same census. (Blueprint 10)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the diagnostic for the lesson's first claim, that a preference system, not an open door without rules, produced the transformation of immigration after 1965. Students who describe the 1965 act as removing all limits have missed Station 1 of the activity and should be sent back to its ceilings and preferences. Question 3 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires the two ends of the internal movement held in one explanation with the figures attached.
