Teacher Answer Key

Region or streamWhat changedThe evidence
Immigration from EuropeEurope stopped supplying most newcomers. The national origins quotas that had favored northern and western Europe ended in 1965, and Europe's share of immigration fell across the following decades.In 1960 the largest immigrant groups came from Europe and Canada. By the 2000s none of the five largest source countries was European.
Immigration from Latin America and AsiaLatin America and Asia became the chief sources of immigration under the family reunification and skills preferences of the 1965 act.By the 2000s the largest groups came from Mexico, the Philippines, India, China, and Vietnam. The foreign born rose from about 9.6 million and 4.7 percent in 1970 to about 40 million and about 13 percent in 2010 (Census Bureau).
The unauthorized population and the 1986 settlementThe Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 legalized the unauthorized population already present and banned the knowing hire of unauthorized workers. Critics later said the first half was carried out and the second was not.About three million people gained legal status, covering those who had arrived before 1982. The hiring ban turned on the word knowingly in Section 101 of the act.
The South and WestThe Sun Belt grew decade after decade on air conditioning, defense and technology work, and lower costs, and its political weight grew with its population.Phoenix grew from about 107,000 people in 1950 to about 1.45 million in 2010 (Census Bureau). The South and West gained House seats in every reapportionment from 1970 onward.
The older industrial citiesDeindustrialization emptied factory districts, and white flight carried families and tax base to the suburbs, hollowing the older industrial cities of the North and Midwest.Detroit fell from about 1.85 million people in 1950 to about 714,000 in 2010 (Census Bureau), a loss of more than half.
The strongest city centersFrom the 1990s, wealthier residents and new investment returned to neighborhoods near the centers of the strongest cities, rebuilding them while raising prices and rents.Gentrification rebuilt housing and storefronts in parts of New York and Washington and pushed poorer residents out of the same neighborhoods. Many older industrial cities saw little of the revival.

Two movements, one map, in one paragraph. Strong answers join the two movements rather than listing them. The law of 1965 changed who could come: its family and skills preferences, working through sponsorship over decades, raised the foreign born population from about 9.6 million and 4.7 percent in 1970 to about 40 million and about 13 percent in 2010, and shifted the largest sources from Europe toward Latin America and Asia. The country's own movement changed where Americans lived: jobs, air conditioning, and lower costs drew people to the South and West, House seats followed them in every reapportionment, and the cities they left, Detroit falling from about 1.85 million in 1950 to about 714,000 in 2010, lost people, factories, and tax base together. A paragraph earns full credit when it holds both movements in one account and carries at least the required figures accurately.
