Quick Check: The Machine, the Network, and the News

Directions: Study the table and the statute line, then answer the questions.

YearHouseholds with Internet access at home (Census Bureau)
1997about 18 percent
2000about 42 percent
2001about half of households

Statute line, Telecommunications Act of 1996, Section 230: "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider."

1. The photograph shows the era before the personal computer. State what changed about who owned and ran a computer between this era and the 1990s, and give the Census figure for households with a computer in 2000.

2. Read the table. By how much did household Internet access grow between 1997 and 2000, in percentage points, and in what year did access reach about half of households?

3. State in your own words what the statute line protects, and name one kind of service that later grew behind that protection.

4. Put the fractured news world in sequence. Give CNN's founding date, the year the Fairness Doctrine was repealed, and the development of the middle 2000s that put publication in every pocket.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What changed about ownership and operation, and the 2000 computer figure? In the era of the photograph, computers filled rooms, belonged to government agencies, universities, and large companies, and were run by specialists. The personal computer put a machine on one person's desk and then in the home, run by its owner, and office work changed task by task. The Census Bureau counted about 51 percent of households with a computer in 2000, up from about 8 percent in 1984. (Blueprint 7)

2. Growth 1997 to 2000, and the year of about half. Household Internet access grew from about 18 percent in 1997 to about 42 percent in 2000, a rise of about 24 percentage points in three years. Access reached about half of households in 2001. (Blueprint 7)

3. What does the statute line protect, and what grew behind it? Section 230 protects a provider or user of an interactive computer service from being treated as the publisher or speaker of information provided by another content provider, in plain terms shielding the service from a publisher's legal responsibility for its users' words. Social media platforms are the clearest later growth behind the shield, and comment, review, auction, and search services also rest on it. Accept any one accurately named service. (Blueprint 7, 8)

4. The news sequence. CNN was founded on June 1, 1980, opening cable news around the clock. The Federal Communications Commission repealed the Fairness Doctrine in 1987, and national talk radio grew in the space that opened. From the middle 2000s, social media put publication in every pocket, joined to the person by the smartphone after 2007. The digital divide ran beneath the whole sequence, following income, education, age, and place. (Blueprint 8)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 carry blueprint entry 7 and separate students who can read a federal data table from students who hold the era as a story. Questions 3 and 4 carry blueprint entry 8, with question 3 also resting on entry 7's statute. A student who gives the Section 230 protection as censorship, or who dates CNN to the Internet era, has the sequence wrong in a way the review day should repair.
