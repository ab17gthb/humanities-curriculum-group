Writing Task: Which Change Reached Furthest?

The prompt. Which change, the machine, the network, or the fractured news world, reached furthest into ordinary life by 2010? Answer in a developed essay of about 300 words, argued from the records in The Transformation File. A strong answer does more than praise a technology. It measures reach in daily practice, at work, in commerce, and in the news, it uses at least two stations as evidence, and it answers the strongest claim for one of the other two changes, including the evidence of the digital divide, which limits every claim of reach.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The machine: households with a computer rose from about 8 percent in 1984 to about 51 percent in 2000, per the Census Bureau, and office work changed task by task as typing pools and drafting tables gave way to screens.

2. The network at home: household Internet access rose from about 18 percent in 1997 to about 42 percent in 2000 and about half of households by 2001, per the Census Bureau's Current Population Survey supplements.

3. The law: Section 230 of the Telecommunications Act of 1996 provided that no provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider, the shield behind the platforms.

4. The boom and the bust: e-commerce grew from almost nothing in the middle 1990s into a central part of American retail, while the NASDAQ composite peaked at 5,048 on March 10, 2000 and fell about 78 percent by October 2002, and the network the speculation paid to build remained in use.

5. The news world: CNN was founded on June 1, 1980; the Fairness Doctrine was repealed in 1987 and national talk radio grew; rival cable channels launched in 1996; newspapers lost advertising to online listings; and social media from the middle 2000s, joined by the smartphone after 2007, put publication in every pocket.

6. The limit: the NTIA Falling Through the Net surveys found household Internet access rising from 26.2 percent in December 1998 to 41.5 percent in August 2000, while households earning $75,000 or more stood about twenty times as likely to be online as the lowest income households in the 1998 data, and access followed income, education, age, and place.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your evidence, answer the rival in full, and end by restating how far your change reached by 2010 and where its reach stopped.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that names one change, the machine, the network, or the fractured news world, and measures its reach in daily practice rather than in fame or novelty. (Blueprint 7, 8)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, including at least one Census or NTIA figure with its date. Figures must be right: about 8 percent of households with a computer in 1984 and about 51 percent in 2000; Internet access about 18 percent in 1997, about 42 percent in 2000; NASDAQ peak 5,048 on March 10, 2000. (Blueprint 7)
  • The digital divide (bank 6) used as the limit on the claim, with the income ratio or the lines of income, education, age, and place stated, not mentioned in passing. (Blueprint 8)
  • A rival change answered from the record. The strongest machine claim rests on the workplace rewriting and the long 1984 to 2000 arc. The strongest network claim rests on the speed of adoption, 1997 to 2001. The strongest news claim rests on reach among Americans who never owned a computer at all. A full credit essay states one of these fairly before answering it.

Model opening for teacher reference. The network reached furthest, because it changed what the machine was for. A personal computer in 1990 drafted letters faster; a connected computer in 2001 was also a store, a post office, a newspaper, and a public square, and the Census Bureau counted that change moving through half the country's households in about four years. A full model continues in this pattern, carries the divide as the measured limit of the claim, answers the machine's longer arc, and closes on reach and its boundary rather than on praise.

Common weak answers. A novelty claim (the smartphone was new, so it reached furthest) never measures how many people a change touched or what practice it altered. A crash claim (the bust proves the transformation was hollow) ignores bank 4's record that commerce and the network itself survived the fall in prices. An essay that claims universal reach without the divide cannot earn full credit under this prompt, because the federal surveys in bank 6 are part of the record the prompt requires students to argue from.
