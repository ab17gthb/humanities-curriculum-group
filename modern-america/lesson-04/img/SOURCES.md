## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- Personal computers in homes and offices in the 1980s and 1990s, Internet cafes, dot-com offices, and the smartphone: the familiar photographs are corporate product photography and press photography under copyright. No Commons file page stating a public domain basis was verified for these subjects, so the deck carries the personal computer, commerce, and smartphone sections on text and stat slides, and the computing photographs used are the federal institutional photographs above, captioned for exactly what they show.
- CNN's founding, cable news studios, and talk radio studios: press and corporate property. No public domain image is used; the news sections are carried in text, tables, and the labeled summary at Station 4 of the activity.
- Magazine covers and newspaper front pages of the dot-com boom: copyrighted. Not used. The boom is taught from the index record at Station 3.

## Quoted texts and their editions
- Telecommunications Act of 1996, Public Law 104-104, Section 230 (47 U.S.C. Section 230): the operative sentence, "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider," is quoted in the reading, the activity (Station 1), the guided notes key, and the quick check, from the U.S. Code text as printed by GovInfo and the Cornell Legal Information Institute. 26 quoted words. The text is a work of the federal government and is not copyrighted.
- Copyrighted quotations in this lesson: none. 0 words of any copyrighted work are quoted anywhere in the lesson, so the unit's 90-word cap for copyrighted works is untouched by Lesson 4.

## Data records and their sources
- Census Bureau figures (households with a computer about 8 percent in 1984, about 42 percent in 1998, about 51 percent in 2000; household Internet access about 18 percent in 1997, about 26 percent in 1998, about 42 percent in 2000, about half in 2001): U.S. Census Bureau, Home Computers and Internet Use in the United States: August 2000, Current Population Reports P23-207 (September 2001), Current Population Survey supplements.
- NTIA figures (household Internet access 26.2 percent in December 1998 and 41.5 percent in August 2000; computer ownership 42.1 percent in December 1998 and more than half in August 2000; households at $75,000 or more about twenty times as likely to have Internet access and about nine times as likely to have a computer as the lowest income households, 1998 data): National Telecommunications and Information Administration, Falling Through the Net surveys, 1999 and 2000 editions, archived at ntia.gov.
- NASDAQ Composite values (first close above 1,000 on July 17, 1995; closing peak 5,048 on March 10, 2000; close of about 1,114 on October 9, 2002; a fall of about 78 percent peak to October 2002): market record, index values as carried in the Federal Reserve's FRED series NASDAQCOM (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis) and contemporaneous reporting of index values. Taught at Station 3 as a stated market record, quoting no company or journalist.

## Stations taught as labeled summaries, and why
- Activity Station 4 (cable news record): taught as a labeled summary from the public record. CNN's founding on June 1, 1980, the FCC's repeal of the Fairness Doctrine in 1987, the growth of national talk radio from 1988, and the 1996 launch of MSNBC and Fox News are public facts, but no single public document carries them together, and no verifiable public document text is quoted at this station rather than risk quoting from memory.
- Activity Station 5 (digital divide record): taught as a labeled summary of the NTIA Falling Through the Net surveys' published findings. The overall and ratio figures stated are the surveys' own published figures as recorded above. The education gap is stated in the surveys' comparative terms without a single percentage figure, because a precise education percentage from the archived reports was not verified line by line during this build; the lesson states the staircase the surveys found rather than inventing a number.
- Activity Station 1's ownership passage, Station 2's narrative lines, and Station 3's closing sentences are labeled summaries or stated records on their faces, as marked in each Source line. The only verbatim quotation in the activity is the Section 230 sentence at Station 1.
