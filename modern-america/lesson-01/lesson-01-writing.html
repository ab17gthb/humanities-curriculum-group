Writing Task: Which Diagnosis Did 1980 Reward?

The prompt. Answer the closing question of the lesson in a developed essay of about 300 words: By 1979 Americans had heard two diagnoses of what had gone wrong, President Carter's crisis of confidence and the tax revolt's claim that government itself had become the problem. Which diagnosis did the politics of 1980 reward, and what evidence best proves it? Work through the breakdown in order, weigh the machinery behind the winning diagnosis against the president who stated the losing one, and use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, explaining how each one supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading, stations, and documents. Write the history in your own sentences, except where a bank item quotes a document.

Evidence Bank

1. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted inflation at 11.0 percent in 1974, 11.3 percent in 1979, and 13.5 percent in 1980, and unemployment at 8.5 percent in 1975 and 7.2 percent in 1980. Prices and joblessness stood high together across the decade, the combination named stagflation. (Bureau of Labor Statistics annual averages, as carried in the activity.)

2. The oil shocks made the breakdown a daily experience. Arab oil producing states cut exports in 1973 and prices quadrupled; revolution in Iran halted production in 1979 and the gasoline lines returned. The country's dependence on imported oil was a stated fact of both shocks. (Lesson reading, sections B and C.)

3. Trust had broken before the economics bit. Nixon resigned on August 9, 1974; Ford's Proclamation 4311 then granted him a full, free, and absolute pardon, arguing that a trial would cost the country its restored tranquility. Saigon fell in April 1975, and New York City came within hours of default that autumn. (Ford Presidential Library text, as carried in the audited TPSP record.)

4. In June 1978 California voters passed Proposition 13 by a wide margin. Article XIII A of the state constitution capped the property tax at one percent of full cash value and limited growth in assessed values, and property tax bills fell by more than half on average. The tax revolt spread across the country from that vote. (California Constitution, Article XIII A, Section 1(a), California Legislative Counsel.)

5. On July 15, 1979, Carter told the country that the fundamental threat was a crisis of confidence that strikes at the very heart and soul and spirit of our national will, and that too many Americans now tend to worship self-indulgence and consumption. A majority, he said, believed the next five years would be worse than the past five. (Miller Center text, as carried in the audited TPSP record.)

6. The winning diagnosis had machinery behind it. The New Right had built direct mail lists, single issue groups, and research institutes pressing lower taxes, a stronger military, and resistance to the social changes of the 1960s, and in 1979 the Reverend Jerry Falwell founded the Moral Majority to mobilize evangelical voters. The coalition joined white southern voters courted by the southern strategy to suburban taxpayers in the West and North. (Lesson reading, section G.)

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your three pieces of evidence in the order of the decade, answer the other side in one or two sentences, and end by restating what the election of 1980 settled about the argument of 1979 and what it left open.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the question directly and holds the two diagnoses of 1979 up against the result of 1980. (Blueprint 1 and Blueprint 2)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 and 2 establish the breakdown the diagnoses competed to explain: stagflation in Bureau of Labor Statistics figures and the two oil shocks in daily life. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 3 establishes the trust crisis that preceded both diagnoses. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 4 supports the tax revolt reading at its strongest, because the remedy was enacted by voters a year before Carter spoke. (Blueprint 2) Bank item 5 states Carter's diagnosis in his own words, which a strong essay quotes rather than caricatures. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 6 supports the organization reading: the winning diagnosis arrived with direct mail, single issue groups, research institutes, and the Moral Majority already built. (Blueprint 2) A full credit essay draws on at least two of these groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. The decisive link is sequence: the grievance counted in the figures, the remedy proven at the ballot box in June 1978, and the machinery assembled through the decade all stand before the election of November 1980, and students should argue about what that order of events proves.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest argument on the other side, such as the point that Carter's diagnosis named something real, a recorded loss of faith in the future that no tax cut answered, or the point that a diagnosis without organization behind it could not win an election however accurate it was, answered from the record rather than dismissed.

Model opening for teacher reference. The politics of 1980 rewarded the tax revolt's diagnosis, and the reward can be dated. Government as the problem was not a slogan invented for a campaign. It had already been enacted by California voters in June 1978, who cut and capped the property tax in their constitution, and it had already been organized by a New Right that raised money by direct mail and by a Moral Majority founded the year Carter spoke. Carter's competing diagnosis asked the country to examine its spirit while prices rose at 13.5 percent. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the stagflation and trust evidence in decade order, answering the crisis of confidence argument from Carter's own figures, and closing on what 1980 settled and what it left open.

Common weak answers. Biography only (Carter was unpopular, or the winner was a better speaker) asserts a cause without working the documents. Figures only (inflation was high) skips the question of which diagnosis the result rewarded, and slogan only (government is the problem) skips the machinery that carried the claim. A claim with no other side stated earns partial credit, because the prompt asks for a weighing, not a verdict alone. Grade the use of evidence, never the side a student takes.
