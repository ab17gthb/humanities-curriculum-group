Guided Notes: The Great Recession and Its Long Aftermath

Lesson question: How did the financial system nearly fail in 2008, what did the government do, and why did the recovery leave so many Americans unconvinced?

Unit question: How did the United States remake itself after 1980, in its economy, its population, and its place in the world, and what did the remaking settle and what did it leave open?

Key Terms

subprime mortgage: a home loan made to a borrower with weak credit at a higher interest rate, issued in large numbers during the housing boom of the 2000s and central to the financial crisis when house prices fell

Great Recession: the economic collapse of 2007 to 2009, the deepest since the Great Depression, in which the financial system nearly failed, unemployment reached ten percent, and millions of families lost their homes

Troubled Asset Relief Program: the program of October 2008, known as TARP, in which the federal government was authorized to spend up to seven hundred billion dollars to steady the banking system by buying troubled assets and investing in banks

stimulus: government spending and tax cuts meant to restart a stalled economy by putting money in consumers' hands, as in the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009, at about eight hundred billion dollars

Affordable Care Act: the health care law of 2010 that required most Americans to carry health insurance, barred insurers from denying coverage for prior illness, and expanded Medicaid, the largest change to American health policy since the 1960s

Tea Party movement: the conservative protest movement that rose in 2009 against the bank bailouts, the stimulus, and the health care law, demanding smaller government and lower taxes, and that reshaped Republican politics

Occupy Wall Street: the protest movement that began in New York in September 2011 and spread to cities across the country, camping in financial districts to protest economic inequality in the name of the ninety nine percent against the one percent

A. The Boom That Built the Collapse

For a decade house prices rose and credit grew easy. Lenders issued (1) ____________ mortgages in large numbers, home loans made to borrowers with weak credit at higher interest rates. Banks bundled thousands of mortgages into (2) ____________ and sold them to investors around the world, and rating agencies graded many of the bundles as (3) ____________. Underneath the structure stood one assumption, that house prices would not (4) ____________ across the whole country at once.

B. The Failure in Sequence

House prices began falling in (5) ____________, and borrowers who owed more than their houses were worth stopped paying. In September 2008 the government took over Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, and on September 15 the investment bank (6) ____________ failed outright. Within days short term credit froze, and officials told Congress that the financial system itself stood days from (7) ____________.

C. The Rescue and Its Terms

Congress answered with the Troubled Asset Relief Program, authorized at up to (8) ____________ dollars to steady the banking system. The Federal Reserve cut its main interest rate nearly to (9) ____________ and lent on a new scale. In February 2009 the president signed the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act, a (10) ____________ of about eight hundred billion dollars in spending and tax cuts.

D. The Bill as Households Paid It

Unemployment reached (11) ____________ percent in October 2009, and about (12) ____________ jobs were lost across the downturn, by the Bureau of Labor Statistics count. Millions of families lost their homes to (13) ____________, and household wealth fell by trillions of dollars in housing and retirement accounts.

E. The First Bill Signed

The Supreme Court ruled in 2007 that Lilly Ledbetter's pay discrimination claim was filed (14) ____________, because the clock had run from the original pay decision. The Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009 answered by restarting the filing clock with every (15) ____________ paid under a discriminatory decision.

F. Health Coverage Remade

The Affordable Care Act of 2010 barred insurers from denying coverage because of a (16) ____________ illness and expanded (17) ____________ for people with low incomes. By 2016 about (18) ____________ more people held health coverage than before the act, by the Department of Health and Human Services count.

G. Two Answers and a Slow Recovery

The (19) ____________ movement rose in 2009 against the bailouts, the stimulus, and the health care law, demanding smaller government and lower taxes. Occupy Wall Street began in 2011 in the name of the ninety nine percent against the (20) ____________. Output and jobs returned, while household wealth and trust did not return on the same schedule.

Summary table. The boom and the failure, 2006 to 2008: subprime mortgages were bundled into securities graded as safe on the assumption that house prices would not fall nationwide; prices fell from 2006, the securities soured, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were taken over, Lehman Brothers failed on September 15, 2008, and credit froze. The rescue, 2008 to 2009: TARP was authorized at up to seven hundred billion dollars, the Federal Reserve cut rates nearly to zero and lent widely, the automobile industry was carried through managed bankruptcies, and the stimulus of February 2009 added about eight hundred billion dollars in spending and tax cuts; supporters said the alternative was a second Great Depression and critics charged that the firms whose lending built the collapse were saved. The household bill, 2007 to 2010: unemployment reached 10.0 percent in October 2009, about 8.7 million jobs were lost, millions of homes were foreclosed, and household wealth fell by trillions. The aftermath argued, 2009 to 2016: the Ledbetter Act restarted the pay discrimination clock with every paycheck, the Affordable Care Act expanded coverage to about twenty million more people by 2016 and passed without a vote from the other party, the Tea Party demanded smaller government, and Occupy named the one percent.

Exit ticket 1. A subprime mortgage is a home loan made to a borrower with weak credit at a higher interest rate, issued in large numbers during the housing boom. The assumption was that house prices would not fall across the whole country at once.

Exit ticket 2. The Troubled Asset Relief Program, known as TARP, authorized in October 2008 at up to seven hundred billion dollars to buy troubled assets and invest in banks. Critics charged that the rescue saved the firms whose lending had built the collapse while their executives kept their pay.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that covers both halves with facts from the notes. The strongest answers state that the government steadied the banks through TARP and Federal Reserve lending and restarted spending through the stimulus, while households paid in jobs, homes, and wealth; that the Tea Party read the same record as a government grown too large; and that Occupy read it as concentrated wealth and the financial industry holding the power, so the recovery's return of output and jobs did not settle the argument for either side.
