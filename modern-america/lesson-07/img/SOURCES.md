- Title: Barack Obama signs Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009 1-29-09.jpg (Wikimedia Commons file name)
- Creator: Joyce N. Boghosian, White House photographer, as stated on the Commons file page
- Date: Taken on 29 January 2009, as stated on the Commons file page
- Holder: The White House, via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Barack_Obama_signs_Lilly_Ledbetter_Fair_Pay_Act_of_2009_1-29-09.jpg (file page read and verified for this build; image downloaded through the Commons file path service)
- Public domain basis: The Commons file page states, per whitehouse.gov/copyright, that government produced materials appearing on the site are not copyright protected. The photograph is an official White House photograph by a federal employee, a work of the federal government.
- Size and use: The Commons file is 624 by 351 pixels. It is used at small size in the reading and on the notebook page, and as the deck title image (title-169.jpg, upscaled to 1600 by 900; the softness of the upscale is accepted and recorded here). No portrait crop was made, because a portrait crop from a 624 pixel wide landscape frame would not hold up; the deck's remaining slides are text and stat slides by design.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, stated honestly
- The stimulus signing in Denver, February 17, 2009: a Commons file page for the White House photograph could not be verified for this build (one file page request was refused by the server and was not retried). No image is used; the signing is taught in text.
- The Affordable Care Act signing, March 23, 2010: official White House photographs by Pete Souza and Chuck Kennedy exist and are federal works, but no Commons file page stating the basis was verified for this build. No image is used.
- Occupy Wall Street and Tea Party rallies, 2009 to 2011: the Commons photographs located are held under Creative Commons licenses, which do not qualify under this unit's public domain only rule. No image is used, rather than stretching the rule. Both movements are taught from their documents and stated demands.
- Foreclosure era housing and Lehman Brothers, 2008: the known photographs are press and wire photographs under copyright, or Federal Emergency Management Agency material that could not be verified to a file page for this build. No image is used.

## Quoted sources, editions, and exact counts

All quoted texts in this lesson are public documents or a movement's own published document. No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this lesson, so no quotation counts against the unit's 90 word cap for copyrighted works. Counts below were measured from the activity file as built.

### Financial Crisis Inquiry Report (public government document)
- Edition: Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission, The Financial Crisis Inquiry Report, Conclusions, January 2011, Government Printing Office edition; the conclusions and the Commission's published summary of them were read and verified against the Commission's release as preserved in the Yale Program on Financial Stability document archive and the FRASER archive of the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 1; the verdict sentence also appears in the quick check background and the writing evidence bank in paraphrase and short form.
- Quoted: the sentence "We conclude this financial crisis was avoidable" (7 words) and the Commission's five causes list (87 words). Total quoted in the activity: 94 words. The dissenting views filed in the same report are named in the station's Source line and in the key.

### Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008 (public law)
- Edition: Public Law 110-343, Sections 2 and 115, Statutes at Large via GovInfo; the text was read and verified word for word against the act as printed in the Congressional Record (Bound Edition), Volume 154 (2008), Part 17, GovInfo.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 2, and lesson-07-quick-check.html (Section 2).
- Quoted: Section 2 purposes (92 words) and the two Section 115 limit phrases (16 words). Total quoted in the activity: 108 words. Cuts inside the station's presentation are structural only; the purposes passage is complete as printed.

### Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009 (public law, audited bank record)
- Edition: U.S. Congress, Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009, 2009 (GovInfo), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record lilly-ledbetter-fair-pay-act-2009.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 3, following the bank record's text, including its marked cut between the findings and the rule.
- Quoted: Section 2 finding (72 words) and the Section 3 paycheck rule passage (78 words, excluding the bracketed ellipsis that marks the bank record's cut). Total quoted: 150 words.

### Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (public law)
- Edition: Public Law 111-148, Section 1501, Statutes at Large via GovInfo, as codified at 42 U.S.C. 18091; the findings text was read and verified against the codified section as carried in the Congressional Research Service report on the individual mandate.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 4.
- Quoted: Section 1501(a)(1) (20 words, excluding the bracketed ellipsis) and Section 1501(a)(2)(A) (30 words). Total quoted: 50 words. The station's account of the operative requirement and of the Section 1001 protections is a summary, labeled a summary on its face, because the full operative text runs through the Internal Revenue Code amendment and is restated rather than quoted.

### Declaration of the Occupation of New York City (movement document)
- Edition: New York City General Assembly, Declaration of the Occupation of New York City, accepted September 29, 2011 and published by the movement; the text was read and verified against the movement's published declaration as preserved in the Tamiment Library exhibit at New York University, with two further published reproductions agreeing.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 5 only.
- Quoted: the opening address to allies (31 words) and two charges from the declaration's list (31 words). Total quoted: 62 words. The document is a movement's own publication, taught as a movement document and labeled as one on its face.

### The Tea Party (labeled summary, no quotation)
- No Tea Party text is quoted anywhere in this lesson. The movement published no single authoritative document through an identifiable organization that could be verified for this build, so Station 5 teaches it as a labeled summary resting on the public record of the movement's stated demands at its rallies and in its organizations' published statements, and the station says so on its face. The summary is presented symmetrically with the Occupy document, each movement in its own stated terms.

## Figures used, with their sources as named in the documents
- Unemployment 10.0 percent in October 2009: Bureau of Labor Statistics.
- About 8.7 million jobs lost in the downturn: Bureau of Labor Statistics (the unit skeleton states the same figure as about nine million).
- TARP authorization of up to $700 billion, released in stages from $250 billion: Emergency Economic Stabilization Act, Sections 101 and 115.
- Stimulus at about $800 billion: American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009, as stated in the unit vocabulary.
- House prices falling by roughly a quarter to a third from the 2006 peak: the Case Shiller measure, named in the documents.
- About 20 million more people with health coverage by 2016: Department of Health and Human Services.
- Household wealth falling by trillions of dollars: Federal Reserve accounting, as stated in the reading.
