The Rescue File

Directions: Work through the five stations in order. Each station carries a record of the crisis or of the argument it started: a commission's findings, a rescue statute, two laws of the recovery, and the two protest movements that answered each other. At each station, answer both questions. At the end, enter each record in the ledger and judge which of the era's two protest answers the following decade's politics rewarded.

Part 1: Five Stations

Station 1: The Commission's Verdict, 2011

Source: Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission, The Financial Crisis Inquiry Report, Conclusions, January 2011, Government Printing Office edition, the Commission's own published conclusions. This is a public government document. Commissioners who dissented filed separate views in the same report; one dissent blamed government housing policy as the main cause of the crisis.

"We conclude this financial crisis was avoidable."

The Commission concluded that the crisis was caused by "widespread failures in financial regulation, including the Federal Reserve's failure to stem the tide of toxic mortgages; dramatic breakdowns in corporate governance including too many financial firms acting recklessly and taking on too much risk; an explosive mix of excessive borrowing and risk by households and Wall Street that put the financial system on a collision course with crisis; key policy makers ill prepared for the crisis, lacking a full understanding of the financial system they oversaw; and systemic breaches in accountability and ethics at all levels."

The first sentence makes a claim about whether the crisis had to happen. State the claim, and name the five causes the Commission lists in the second passage.

The Commission assigns the first cause to a named institution and a named failure. State both, and explain what it means to say a regulator failed to stem a tide rather than that it caused the lending itself.

Station 2: The Rescue Statute, October 2008

Source: Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008, Public Law 110-343, Sections 2 and 115, Statutes at Large via GovInfo, with the act's text as printed in the Congressional Record of October 2008. This is a public law. Cuts are marked.

"The purposes of this Act are (1) to immediately provide authority and facilities that the Secretary of the Treasury can use to restore liquidity and stability to the financial system of the United States; and (2) to ensure that such authority and such facilities are used in a manner that (A) protects home values, college funds, retirement accounts, and life savings; (B) preserves homeownership and promotes jobs and economic growth; (C) maximizes overall returns to the taxpayers of the United States; and (D) provides public accountability for the exercise of such authority."

Section 115 released the money in stages. At enactment the authority was "limited to $250,000,000,000 outstanding at any one time," rising by stages until it was "limited to $700,000,000,000 outstanding at any one time."

Section 2 states two purposes. The first names what the Treasury may restore. The second names four things the rescue must do for the public. List the four, in the statute's own terms.

Section 115 did not hand over the full seven hundred billion dollars at once. State the starting limit and the final limit, and explain what the staged release required the government to do before the largest sums could be used.

Station 3: The First Law of the Recovery, January 2009

Source: Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009, Sections 2 and 3, printed from the audited TPSP bank record. Edition named in the bank record: U.S. Congress, GovInfo. This is a public law. Cuts in the bank record are marked in the record and the passage below follows it.

"The Supreme Court in Ledbetter v. Goodyear Tire & Rubber Co., 550 U.S. 618 (2007), significantly impairs statutory protections against discrimination in compensation that Congress established and that have been bedrock principles of American law for decades. The Ledbetter decision undermines those statutory protections by unduly restricting the time period in which victims of discrimination can challenge and recover for discriminatory compensation decisions or other practices, contrary to the intent of Congress."

"[...] an unlawful employment practice occurs, with respect to discrimination in compensation in violation of this title, when a discriminatory compensation decision or other practice is adopted, when an individual becomes subject to a discriminatory compensation decision or other practice, or when an individual is affected by application of a discriminatory compensation decision or other practice, including each time wages, benefits, or other compensation is paid, resulting in whole or in part from such a decision or other practice."

Congress states two things the Supreme Court's 2007 decision did to the law. State both in Congress's own terms.

The rule in the second passage lists three moments when an unlawful practice occurs. State the third one, and explain why it changes the position of a worker who discovers a pay gap years after it began.

Station 4: The Health Law, March 2010

Source: Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, Public Law 111-148, Section 1501, Statutes at Large via GovInfo, as codified at 42 U.S.C. 18091. The two passages in quotation marks are the statute's own findings. The paragraph after them is a summary of the law's requirement and of the protections in its Section 1001, and it is labeled a summary because it restates the statute rather than quoting it.

"The individual responsibility requirement provided for in this section [...] is commercial and economic in nature, and substantially affects interstate commerce."

"The requirement regulates activity that is commercial and economic in nature: economic and financial decisions about how and when health care is paid for, and when health insurance is purchased."

Summary of the requirement and the protections. Section 1501 required most Americans to maintain minimum essential health coverage for each month beginning in 2014, with a payment owed by those who did not and did not qualify for an exemption. Section 1001 of the same law barred health insurers from denying coverage to children because of a prior illness, a bar extended to adults in 2014, barred lifetime dollar limits on coverage, and let young adults remain on a parent's plan until age twenty six.

Congress placed findings about commerce at the head of the requirement. State what the findings claim the requirement regulates, and explain why a Congress writing a national insurance requirement would begin with the commerce power.

From the summary, state the requirement the law placed on most Americans and name two protections Section 1001 placed on insurers.

Station 5: Two Answers, 2009 and 2011

Source: Two protest movements, each presented in its own stated terms. The Tea Party text is a summary, labeled as a summary, resting on the public record of the movement's stated demands at its rallies and in its organizations' published statements, because the movement published no single authoritative document. The Occupy text is a movement document, quoted from the Declaration of the Occupation of New York City, accepted by the New York City General Assembly on September 29, 2011 and published by the movement; the text below follows the movement's published declaration as preserved in the Tamiment Library exhibit at New York University.

The Tea Party, summary. The movement rose in the first months of 2009, weeks after the stimulus was signed, and held rallies across the country on April 15, 2009, the day federal income taxes fall due. Its stated demands, repeated at its rallies and in its organizations' statements, were smaller government, lower taxes, and reduced federal spending and debt. It opposed the bank bailouts begun under President George W. Bush, the stimulus of 2009, and the health care law as parts of one pattern, a federal government grown beyond its proper size. Its name invoked the Boston Tea Party of 1773, a protest against a tax imposed by a distant government.

Occupy Wall Street, movement document. "As we gather together in solidarity to express a feeling of mass injustice, we must not lose sight of what brought us together. We write so that all people who feel wronged by the corporate forces of the world can know that we are your allies."

"They have taken our houses through an illegal foreclosure process, despite not having the original mortgage. They have taken bailouts from taxpayers with impunity, and continue to give executives exorbitant bonuses."

Each movement names a different holder of the power it protests. From the two texts, state who holds the power in the Tea Party's account and who holds it in Occupy's account.

Both movements began from the bailouts. State what each one concluded from them, using one stated demand from the summary and one quoted charge from the declaration.

Teacher Answer Key

Station 1: The Commission's Verdict

State the claim, and name the five causes. The claim is that the crisis was avoidable, a result of human actions and failures rather than an accident no one could have prevented. The five causes are widespread failures in financial regulation, dramatic breakdowns in corporate governance, an explosive mix of excessive borrowing and risk by households and Wall Street, key policy makers ill prepared for the crisis, and systemic breaches in accountability and ethics at all levels. The report carried dissenting views, including one that blamed government housing policy; a student who raises the dissent as a limit on the majority's verdict has read the Source line closely and earns credit for it.

State the institution and the failure, and explain the wording. The institution is the Federal Reserve, and the failure is its failure to stem the tide of toxic mortgages. To say the Fed failed to stem a tide assigns it the duty and the power to limit lending it did not itself make. The charge is omission by the one body the Commission held empowered to set prudent mortgage lending standards, not origination of the loans.

Station 2: The Rescue Statute

List the four public purposes in Section 2(2). Protects home values, college funds, retirement accounts, and life savings; preserves homeownership and promotes jobs and economic growth; maximizes overall returns to the taxpayers of the United States; and provides public accountability for the exercise of such authority.

State the starting and final limits, and what the stages required. The authority began limited to $250,000,000,000 outstanding at any one time and rose by stages to $700,000,000,000. The middle stage required a written presidential certification to Congress. The final stage required a written report detailing the Secretary's plan, and took effect only if Congress did not enact a joint resolution of disapproval within fifteen days.

Station 3: The First Law of the Recovery

State the two things Congress says the 2007 decision did. In Congress's terms, the decision significantly impairs statutory protections against discrimination in compensation that Congress established, and it undermines those protections by unduly restricting the time period in which victims can challenge and recover for discriminatory decisions, contrary to the intent of Congress.

State the third moment, and explain its effect. An unlawful practice occurs when an individual is affected by application of a discriminatory decision, including each time wages, benefits, or other compensation is paid under it. A worker who discovers a pay gap years later is therefore not confined to the date of the original decision. Every paycheck paid under the decision is a new unlawful practice, and the filing clock runs from the paychecks she can still reach.

Station 4: The Health Law

State what the findings claim, and why Congress begins there. The findings claim the requirement is commercial and economic in nature and substantially affects interstate commerce, because it regulates decisions about how and when health care is paid for and when insurance is purchased. A Congress writing a national requirement begins with the commerce power because that power is the constitutional ground on which Congress regulates national markets. The Supreme Court upheld the requirement in 2012 on taxing power grounds, a fact a strong answer may add.

State the requirement and two protections. Most Americans must maintain minimum essential coverage for each month beginning in 2014, with a payment owed by those who did not and did not qualify for an exemption. The Section 1001 protections include the bar on denying coverage to children for a prior illness, extended to adults in 2014, the bar on lifetime dollar limits, and coverage on a parent's plan to age twenty six. Any two earn credit.

Station 5: Two Answers

State who holds the power in each account. In the Tea Party's account the power sits in the federal government, grown beyond its proper size through the bailouts, the stimulus, and the health care law. In Occupy's account the power sits in corporations and the financial industry, which the declaration says place profit over people and run the governments that are supposed to answer to voters. The two accounts disagree about where the power lies, and every later argument in the lesson follows from that disagreement.

State what each concluded from the bailouts. The Tea Party concluded the bailouts belonged to a pattern of oversized government; its stated demands were smaller government, lower taxes, and reduced federal spending and debt. Occupy concluded the bailouts proved corporate power over the public purse, in the declaration's charge that they have taken bailouts from taxpayers with impunity and continue to give executives exorbitant bonuses. Credit requires one demand from the summary and the quoted charge, each in its own movement's terms.

Part 2: Ledger and Verdict (Grading Guide)

Ledger entries earn credit for accuracy to the station texts. Station 1: the claim is that the crisis was avoidable, on the Commission's five causes, dissents noted. Station 2: the failure is the freezing system of September 2008; the answer is Treasury authority bounded by the four public purposes and Section 115's staged limits. Station 3: the failure is a filing rule that barred a worker who could not have known of the discrimination in time; the answer is the paycheck rule. Station 4: the claim is that coverage is a national economic question Congress may regulate, on the terms of Sections 1501 and 1001. Station 5: government power against corporate power, each resting on the bailouts read its own way.

Closing verdicts earn full credit when they argue from the file and answer the other movement's strongest charge. The Tea Party case rests on Stations 2, 4, and 5: its demands entered one party's program within two election cycles, and it reshaped Republican politics. The Occupy case rests on Stations 1 and 5: its language of the ninety nine percent and the one percent entered ordinary political speech, and the Commission's findings echoed its charges on bailouts and bonuses. The rubric credits the evidence used and the counterclaim answered, not the movement chosen.
