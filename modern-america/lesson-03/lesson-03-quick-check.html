Quick Check: The Signing and the Figures

Directions: Read the passage and the data line, then answer the questions.

Passage. President George H. W. Bush, Statement on Signing the Americans with Disabilities Act, July 26, 1990 (National Archives). Audited TPSP bank record, bush-ada-signing-statement-1990. A cut is marked with an ellipsis.

Today, I am signing S. 933, the "Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990." [...] The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) is such legislation. It promises to open up all aspects of American life to individuals with disabilities, employment opportunities, government services, public accommodations, transportation, and telecommunications. [...] It signals the end to the unjustified segregation and exclusion of persons with disabilities from the mainstream of American life.

Data line. Manufacturing employment in the United States was about 17.3 million in 1990 and about 11.5 million by 2010. Bureau of Labor Statistics.

1. Source the passage. Who issued it, on what date, and on what occasion?

2. State the promise the president makes for the law, and name three areas of life he lists.

3. The president says the law signals an end to segregation and exclusion. State, from the lesson, one finding Congress wrote into the act that shows why he could describe the problem that way.

4. Use the data line. How many manufacturing jobs were lost between 1990 and 2010, and why does that loss belong in the same lesson as a budget surplus and 3.9 percent unemployment in 2000?

Teacher Answer Key

1. Source the passage. President George H. W. Bush issued the statement on July 26, 1990, on signing the Americans with Disabilities Act, S. 933, into law on the South Lawn of the White House. (Blueprint 5)

2. State the promise and three areas. The president says the act promises to open up all aspects of American life to individuals with disabilities. Any three of employment opportunities, government services, public accommodations, transportation, and telecommunications earn credit. (Blueprint 5)

3. State a supporting finding. Congress found that society had historically tended to isolate and segregate individuals with disabilities and that such discrimination continued as a serious and pervasive social problem, persisting in employment, housing, public accommodations, education, and transportation, and that individuals with disabilities had often had no legal recourse against it. Any one finding stated accurately earns credit. (Blueprint 5)

4. Use the data line. About 5.8 million manufacturing jobs were lost, from about 17.3 million to about 11.5 million, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The loss belongs in the same lesson because it ran through the boom itself. The economy at the top produced surpluses from 1998 to 2001 and unemployment of 3.9 percent in 2000 while the factory economy shrank, which is the distributional argument of the lesson in one pairing of figures. (Blueprint 6)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 to 3 test Blueprint 5, the Cold War ending at home, and Question 4 tests Blueprint 6, globalization and the new economy. A student who computes the loss but cannot say why it sits beside a surplus has the arithmetic without the argument, and should redo sections E and F of the guided notes before the writing task.
