# Modern America — Unit Manifest

**Essential question:** How did the United States remake itself after 1980, in its economy, its population, and its place in the world, and what did the remaking settle and what did it leave open?

**Course position:** The fifteenth and final unit of the US History course. It opens in June 1978, at the same hinge where the Civil Rights unit closes (Proposition 13 passed days before the Supreme Court decided Bakke), and runs to the present. The Cold War unit owns foreign policy through 1991; this unit teaches the Cold War's end only in its domestic register and keeps the 1990s and after at home as its center of gravity. Contested and recent events are taught the way the whole course teaches everything: documents, statutes, court opinions, data, and speeches from more than one side, every contested claim attached to the named person or body that made it, and the official record stated as the record where one exists. The final lesson weighs continuities with the earlier units rather than declaring a verdict on the present.

**Built:** 2026-10-22. 51 documents, 228 printed pages. 8 slide decks, 220 slides, 34,634 words of speaker notes, 8 printable teacher notes documents (80 pages).

**Topics (6):** 1 The conservative turn · 2 A new economy: globalization, technology, and the service age · 3 A changing people · 4 America in the world after the Cold War · 5 Crisis and response · 6 The unsettled present.

**Test blueprint:** 15 entries, two per lesson except Lesson 8 (one). Entry-to-topic map and per-lesson item assignments are in the skeleton: ~/workspace/curriculum-planner/modern-america-skeleton.md.

**TPSP source plan:** The bank HAS a modern era file: ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/modern.json, era "Modern America (1968-present)", 31 records. Picker lead for the later integration pass: premium DBQ 23, "Evaluate the extent to which Americans' debate since 1968 over what the country should be has been about its moral condition rather than its policies" (key at ~/workspace/primary-source/exemplar-trims/dbq23-modern-america-debate.answerKey.md), whose seven documents are all bank records. This unit's quotations are drawn from the bank's verified records wherever the bank carries the text, and otherwise from the named editions in each lesson's SOURCES.md.

**Copyright accounting:** No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this unit. Every quotation is a federal public document: presidential addresses and statements, statutes, court opinions and syllabi, a commission report, or federal statistics. The one movement document quoted, the Occupy Wall Street Declaration (Lesson 7, Station 5, 62 words), is the movement's own published text and is labeled as such on its face; the Tea Party is taught as a labeled summary because it published no single authoritative text. Barbara Jordan's 1976 keynote is copyrighted and is not quoted; it appears only as one of the seven documents of premium DBQ 23, which Lesson 8's writing task names but does not reproduce. Bill Moyers's published Herblock record and David M. Kennedy's UFW poster record are described in the skeleton and not used. Melba Pattillo Beals's Warriors Don't Cry and John Fussell's essay were not used.

## Lesson 1 — The Conservative Turn: Stagflation and the Tax Revolt

Unit opener. Proposition 13 (June 6, 1978) through Carter's signing statement on the Ethics in Government Act (October 26, 1978). Lesson question: "Why did voters who had lived under the New Deal order for two generations turn against it in the 1970s?"

- Reading: 900 words, 4 pages. Seven sections (A A Price That Will Not Sit Still, B The Oil Weapon, C The President Names It, D California Votes, E The Other States Listen, F What Government Owes, G A Coalition Assembles). Sources: BLS CPI-U annual averages (1973 8.7%, 1974 12.3%, 1979 13.3%, 1980 13.5%; gasoline 36 cents in 1972 to $1.19 in 1980) and unemployment (1975 9.0%, 1982 10.8%); Stagflation and Crisis of Confidence from the bank records; Carter's Inaugural Address (sourced July 15, 1976, bank record) for the limits passage; the Arab oil embargo record (bank); Proposition 13 text (bank); ERA Sections 1 and 2 verbatim (bank); Ford's October 10, 1974 address (bank). Images: Leffler's 1979 gas-line photograph (Library of Congress, p. 7), Carter's 1977 official portrait (Carter Library via NARA), Ford's 1974 official portrait by David Hume Kennerly (federal work).
- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 7 pages, five stations (Carter's Crisis of Confidence, July 15, 1979; Proposition 13 with the ballot pamphlet summary; Ford's WIN address, October 8, 1974; a BLS data record of prices, pay, and jobs, 1965 to 1982, compiled from BLS, the Federal Reserve's FRED series, and the Census Bureau; the ERA with Ratner's account of Schlafly and the counter-ERA amendment). Notebook page: 3 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, tagged to blueprint entries 1 and 2. Writing task: 4 pages, a four-source DBQ-style set (Carter's energy address, Ford's WIN address, the Prop 13 ballot summary, the Arab oil embargo record) with Document 4, Ford's Address Before a Joint Session of the Congress, April 28, 1975, drawn from the bank's premium DBQ 10, named in the key and not reproduced.
- Deck: 26 slides, 3,893 words of speaker notes (123 to 186 words a slide), teacher notes document 9 pages.
- Honest flags: Proposition 13's assessment rules are taught as a labeled summary. Section 1(a) is quoted verbatim from the bank record; the constitution text at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov could not be fetched directly, and the wording was verified against the Legislative Counsel text as reproduced in search results and two independent reproductions of Article XIII A. The Howard Jarvis portrait on Wikimedia Commons is CC BY 4.0 and was not used; no public-domain photograph of Jarvis, of an ERA march, or of Carter delivering the 1979 address was verified, so the tax revolt and the ERA fight run in text and in the documents themselves.
