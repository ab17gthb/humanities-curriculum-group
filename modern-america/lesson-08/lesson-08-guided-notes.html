Guided Notes: A Polarized Nation

Lesson question: What divided Americans most deeply in the first decades of the twenty first century, and which of the course's oldest arguments were they still having?

Unit question: How did the United States remake itself after 1980, in its economy, its population, and its place in the world, and what did the remaking settle and what did it leave open?

Key Terms

political polarization: the sorting of Americans into two hostile political camps with fewer voters in the middle, visible from the 1990s onward in voting records, media habits, and measures of party identity

Bush v. Gore: the Supreme Court decision of December 2000 that stopped the recount of Florida's presidential ballots and settled the election of 2000 for George W. Bush, decided by five votes to four

Citizens United v. Federal Election Commission: the Supreme Court decision of 2010 holding that the government may not limit independent political spending by corporations and unions, which opened the way to super PACs and large outside spending in elections

District of Columbia v. Heller: the Supreme Court decision of 2008 holding that the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to keep a handgun in the home for self-defense

Obergefell v. Hodges: the Supreme Court decision of 2015 holding that the Fourteenth Amendment requires every state to license marriages between two people of the same sex and to recognize such marriages licensed in other states

affirmative action: government policies that take race into account in hiring, contracting, and college admissions in order to remedy the effects of past discrimination, ordered for federal contractors in the revised Philadelphia Plan of 1969

COVID-19 pandemic: the worldwide spread of the coronavirus disease first identified in 2019, which reached the United States in 2020, killed more than a million Americans, and closed schools, businesses, and public life for months

G. The official record and January 6, 2021: on the official record, Joseph Biden won the Electoral College and the popular vote in 2020, state officials of both parties certified their results, and challenges to the count were rejected in court after court; President Trump and many of his supporters claimed the election had been stolen, a claim the official record does not support; on January 6, 2021 a crowd attacked the Capitol while Congress counted the electoral votes, the count was completed that night, and hundreds of participants were later prosecuted and convicted. The four subjects still argued: the size of government, the reach of rights, the terms of membership in the nation, and the country's place in the world.

The Unsettled Present at a Glance. The sorting: parties voting as blocs in Congress, county landslides, split ticket voting shrinking, and the 537 vote Florida margin of 2000. Decided by courts: Bush v. Gore in 2000, Heller in 2008, Citizens United in 2010, Obergefell in 2015, and Students for Fair Admissions in 2023 closing the Bakke settlement of 1978. Tested by crisis: more than a million American deaths in the pandemic by CDC counts, the CARES Act of March 27, 2020 at more than two trillion dollars, and vaccines authorized in under a year. Still unsettled: the size of government, the reach of rights, the terms of membership in the nation, and the country's place in the world, the arguments the course met in 1776, 1861, 1933, 1954, and 1968.
