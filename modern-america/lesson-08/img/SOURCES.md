## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Bush v. Gore passages, activity Station 1 and quick check: Bush v. Gore, 531 U.S. 98 (2000), per curiam opinion (Cornell Legal Information Institute), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record bush-v-gore-2000. Quoted verbatim from the bank record text, cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines. About 140 quoted words across the two documents.
- Heller passage, activity Station 2: District of Columbia v. Heller, 554 U.S. 570 (2008), opinion of the Court (Justia transcription), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record dc-v-heller-2008. Quoted verbatim from the bank record text, cuts marked with ellipses. About 75 quoted words.
- Obergefell passage, activity Station 3: Obergefell v. Hodges, 576 U.S. 644 (2015), opinion of the Court (Supreme Court slip opinion), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record obergefell-v-hodges-2015. Quoted verbatim from the bank record text, cuts marked with ellipses. About 80 quoted words.
- Bakke passage, activity Station 4: Regents of the University of California v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265 (1978), opinion of Powell, J. (Cornell Legal Information Institute), as carried in the audited TPSP bank record regents-uc-v-bakke-1978. Quoted verbatim from the bank record text, cuts marked with ellipses. About 55 quoted words.
- Students for Fair Admissions passage, activity Station 4: Students for Fair Admissions, Inc. v. President and Fellows of Harvard College, 600 U.S. 181 (2023), syllabus of the slip opinion (Supreme Court of the United States, supremecourt.gov), with the syllabus text also read in the preliminary print of volume 600 of the United States Reports. The quoted syllabus sentences state the holding: race based admissions programs must comply with strict scrutiny, may never use race as a stereotype or negative, and must at some point end, and the respondents' systems fail each criterion and must be invalidated under the Equal Protection Clause. About 55 quoted words. All Supreme Court opinions and syllabi are works of the federal government and public documents.
- CARES Act, activity Station 5: Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act, Public Law 116-136, Sec. 1 (Statutes at Large, via GovInfo). The short title sentence is quoted verbatim from the enrolled text as carried on GovInfo. The act's main provisions are given at the station as a labeled summary of the enrolled text, not as a quotation, and the station says so on its face. One quoted sentence, seven words in the citation clause plus the act's names.
- CDC record, activity Station 5: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, COVID-19 Surveillance After Expiration of the Public Health Emergency Declaration, United States, May 11, 2023, Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report 2023, volume 72. The quoted sentence reports that by April 26, 2023, more than 104 million United States COVID-19 cases, 6 million related hospitalizations, and 1.1 million COVID-19-associated deaths were reported to CDC. About 35 quoted words. A work of the federal government.
- Citizens United v. Federal Election Commission, 558 U.S. 310 (2010): the holding is stated in the reading, guided notes, and deck in the words of the lesson's canonical vocabulary definition, which matches the slip opinion's holding that the government may not restrict independent expenditures for communications by corporations and unions and that the prohibition in the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act violated the First Amendment. The case is not quoted at length in the student documents; no quotation is carried, so no word count applies.

## Copyright accounting

Every quotation in this lesson is from a work of the United States federal government or a federal court opinion: court opinions, a federal statute, and a Centers for Disease Control and Prevention report. No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this lesson, in any document, the deck, or the notes. The 90 word cap for copyrighted works is therefore not engaged by this lesson, and the total for copyrighted quotation in this lesson is zero words.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- January 6, 2021: the widely reproduced photographs of the attack on the Capitol are press photographs or Capitol Police material whose Commons file pages do not state a clear public domain basis that this build could verify line by line. No photograph of January 6 is used. The events of that day are taught in text only, from the public record as the reading and deck state it.
- The election of 2000 recount: the famous Florida recount room photographs are press and wire photographs under copyright. No public domain recount photograph was verified in this build, so Bush v. Gore is taught through its text and the 537 vote record, with the Supreme Court Building photograph standing for the institution.
- COVID-19 illness and loss: no photograph of patients, hospitals under strain, or funerals is used. The pandemic is taught through the CDC count, the CARES Act text, the vaccination site photograph above, and stat slides.
- Barack Obama era and White House photography for Citizens United and Obergefell: rather than add further portraits, the lesson uses the 2010 Court portrait that decided Citizens United, recorded above. No additional White House photograph was needed for the pairings the lesson teaches.
- No catalog number is stated anywhere in this lesson for any image beyond the file page data recorded above, all of it taken from the Commons file pages themselves.
