Writing Task: The Closing Verdict

The prompt. This is the closing writing task of the course. Write a developed essay of about 300 words stating the strongest single claim the whole year's documents support about what changed in America and what did not. Your verdict must run the full length of the course: support it with at least three pieces of evidence from the bank below, and with at least one document from an earlier unit, named by its date. State the strongest argument against your own verdict, at full strength, and answer it. Use the evidence rather than copying the bank's wording, and keep contested claims attributed to their makers wherever you use them.

Students who continue from here: the linked extension for this task is TPSP premium DBQ number 23, "Evaluate the extent to which Americans' debate since 1968 over what the country should be has been about its moral condition rather than its policies." It is named here as the course's closing extension and is not reproduced in this task.

Evidence Bank

1. In Bush v. Gore, decided five to four on December 12, 2000, the Supreme Court held that Florida's recount violated the Equal Protection Clause for want of a uniform standard, stopped the recount on a certified margin of 537 votes, and stated that its consideration was limited to the present circumstances.

2. In District of Columbia v. Heller in 2008, the Court held that the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to keep a handgun in the home for self-defense, writing that the enshrinement of constitutional rights necessarily takes certain policy choices off the table. In Citizens United in 2010, it held that the government may not limit independent political spending by corporations and unions.

3. In 2003 no state licensed marriages between two people of the same sex, and Massachusetts became the first in 2004. In Obergefell v. Hodges in 2015, the Court held that the Fourteenth Amendment requires every state to license such marriages and to recognize such marriages licensed in other states, closing with the petitioners' plea for equal dignity in the eyes of the law.

4. In Bakke in 1978, Justice Powell's opinion struck down a set aside of seats by race and allowed race to be deemed a plus in a particular applicant's file. In Students for Fair Admissions in 2023, the Court held that the admissions programs before it failed strict scrutiny, used race as a stereotype or negative, lacked a logical end point, and were invalid under the Equal Protection Clause.

5. The COVID-19 pandemic killed more than a million Americans by the count of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The CARES Act of March 27, 2020 committed more than two trillion dollars in relief. By April 26, 2023, the CDC record counted more than 104 million cases and 1.1 million COVID-19-associated deaths, and vaccines had been developed and authorized in under a year.

6. On the official record, Joseph Biden won the election of 2020 in the Electoral College and the popular vote, state officials of both parties certified their results, and challenges to the count were rejected in court after court. President Trump and many of his supporters claimed the election had been stolen. On January 6, 2021, a crowd attacked the Capitol while Congress counted the electoral votes; the count was completed that night, and hundreds of participants were later prosecuted and convicted.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A verdict that states a single claim about what changed in America and what did not, and holds both halves in one argument. Any verdict can earn full credit if the evidence carries it. An essay that lists changes without a claim over them has summarized the course rather than judged it. (Blueprint 15)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, and at least one document from an earlier unit named by its date, such as the founding amendments of 1791, the Fourteenth Amendment of 1868, the New Deal legislation of 1933 and after, the decisions of 1954, or the Howard University address of 1965. A verdict built on this lesson alone cannot judge the whole year. (Blueprint 15)
  • The strongest argument against the verdict stated at full strength and answered. Against a continuity verdict: the Court in these years settled a presidential election, protected an individual handgun right in a holding of this scope for the first time, required marriage licensing no state had offered in 2003, and ended the admissions settlement of 1978, so the record reads as rapid change. Against a transformation verdict: each of those decisions was argued from texts of 1791, 1868, and 1965, the pandemic was answered with machinery built in 1933, and the disputed election of 2020 was argued in the founding's vocabulary, so the record reads as old arguments under new facts. (Blueprint 15)
  • Holdings stated at the scope their texts claim, including the self limiting sentence in Bush v. Gore and both halves of the Bakke and Students for Fair Admissions pair wherever either is used. (Blueprint 15)
  • Contested claims attributed to their makers and the official record stated as the record, above all in any use of bank 6. An essay that asserts a claim about the election of 2020 without its attribution, in either direction, has failed the lesson's central discipline. (Blueprint 15)

Model opening for teacher reference. What changed in America across this course is who holds rights and who staffs the state; what did not change is the arguments by which those questions are settled. The founding generation wrote the amendments, the Civil War generation rewrote one of them, and the courts of 2000 to 2023 settled a presidential election, a handgun ban, a marriage question, and a forty five year admissions argument by reading those same texts, while the one emergency no court could settle, the pandemic of 2020, was answered with the machinery and the scale the New Deal built in 1933. A full model continues by answering the transformation claim at full strength, that no state licensed a same sex marriage in 2003 and every state was required to by 2015, and closes on the verdict restated.

Common weak answers. A verdict built on one decade cannot carry a course length claim; require the earlier unit document to do work in the argument, not decorate it. A continuity verdict that omits bank 3 cannot explain the distance between 2003 and 2015. A transformation verdict that omits the ages of the texts being read, 1791 and 1868, mistakes new answers for new questions. Grade evidence, pairing, and attribution only, never the side a student takes.
