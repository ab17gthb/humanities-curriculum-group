Teacher Answer Key

The modern recordThe older argument it continuesThe evidence for the pairing
Bush v. Gore, 2000: a court stops a recount and settles an electionThe founding argument over how the people's choice is counted and who referees the count, the argument the Constitution answered with the Electoral College and state control of election machinery.The opinion grounds the right in the legislature vesting the vote in its people, requires equal weight accorded to each vote and equal dignity owed to each voter, and stops the recount for want of uniform standards. Florida's certified margin was 537 votes, and the decision was five to four on December 12, 2000.
Heller, 2008, and Citizens United, 2010: the amendment as refereeThe founding argument over what the Bill of Rights removes from ordinary politics, and who enforces the removal, the legislature or a court reading the amendment.Heller holds the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to keep a handgun in the home for self-defense and states that the enshrinement of constitutional rights takes certain policy choices off the table. Citizens United holds under the First Amendment that the government may not limit independent political spending by corporations and unions.
Obergefell, 2015: marriage licensed in every stateThe Civil War era's equality claim, written into the Fourteenth Amendment in 1868 and pressed again through the movement years, over who holds the rights the amendment promises.The Court holds the Fourteenth Amendment requires every state to license marriages between two people of the same sex and to recognize such marriages licensed in other states, closing with the petitioners' plea for equal dignity in the eyes of the law. In 2003 no state licensed such marriages; Massachusetts was the first in 2004.
Bakke, 1978, to Students for Fair Admissions, 2023: the remedy argumentThe movement years argument over remedy, posed at Howard University in 1965 as equality as a fact and equality as a result, and carried into policy by the revised Philadelphia Plan of 1969.Bakke struck down a set aside of seats by race while deeming race a plus in a particular applicant's file. Forty five years later the Students for Fair Admissions syllabus states that permitted programs must comply with strict scrutiny, may never use race as a stereotype or negative, and must at some point end, and holds that the programs before it failed each criterion.
The pandemic, 2020 to 2023: crisis and the federal answerThe New Deal argument over the federal government's work in a national collapse, first built in 1933, over how far Washington reaches when the whole country's economy and public life stop at once.The CARES Act of March 27, 2020 committed more than two trillion dollars, including direct payments, expanded unemployment assistance, and payroll loans. By April 26, 2023 the CDC record counted more than 104 million cases and 1.1 million COVID-19-associated deaths in the United States, and vaccines had been authorized in under a year.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers pair the modern record with the older arguments and date the pairings. Example: The deepest division in these years ran through the courts, because the sorting had left Congress and the electorate too evenly split to settle the largest questions. A court settled the election of 2000 on a 537 vote margin in Florida. Courts then read the founding amendments to settle the handgun question in Heller in 2008 and the campaign spending question in Citizens United in 2010, read the Fourteenth Amendment of 1868 to require marriage licensing in every state in Obergefell in 2015, and closed the movement years argument over remedy in Students for Fair Admissions in 2023. The one crisis the courts did not answer, the pandemic, was answered in the New Deal's manner, with a statute of March 27, 2020 moving more than two trillion dollars. What divided the country had changed its subjects. The arguments it used had not changed their age.
