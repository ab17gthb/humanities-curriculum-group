Quick Check: The Promise and the Ledger

Directions: Read the passage and the background, then answer the questions.

Source: Ronald Reagan, First Inaugural Address, January 20, 1981 (Gilder Lehrman Institute text), as recorded in the audited TPSP record.

But great as our tax burden is, it has not kept pace with public spending. For decades we have piled deficit upon deficit, mortgaging our future and our children's future for the temporary convenience of the present. To continue this long trend is to guarantee tremendous social, cultural, political, and economic upheavals.

In this present crisis, government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem.

Background. The program built on this claim cut income tax rates in the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981, extended deregulation, and accepted Paul Volcker's high interest rates at the Federal Reserve, which broke inflation through the recession of 1981 to 1982. In August 1981 the president fired more than 11,000 striking air traffic controllers. Across the same years the national debt rose from about 900 billion dollars in 1981 to about 2.9 trillion dollars by 1989, while Social Security and Medicare were left in place.

1. In the passage, what two burdens does the speaker lay at government's door, and what does he say continuing the trend will guarantee?

2. State the sentence that carries the speech's whole claim, and name the 1981 act that turned the claim's economic half into law, with the change it made to the top tax rate.

3. The passage warns against deficit piled upon deficit. Set the background's debt figures beside that warning and state, in one sentence, what the record shows about the warning's fate.

4. A classmate says the Reagan administration shrank the federal government across the board. Correct the statement using two facts from the background, one about what was cut or fired and one about what stood or grew.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What two burdens, and what guarantee? A tax burden that the speech says penalizes successful achievement, and public spending that has outrun even that burden, piling deficit upon deficit and mortgaging the future of the country and its children for the temporary convenience of the present. To continue the trend, the speaker says, is to guarantee tremendous social, cultural, political, and economic upheavals. (Blueprint 3)

2. The claim sentence, the act, and the top rate. The sentence is: "In this present crisis, government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem." The act is the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981, which cut individual income tax rates by about a quarter over three years and brought the top rate down from 70 percent to 50 percent. (Blueprint 3)

3. The warning's fate in the record. The national debt rose from about 900 billion dollars in 1981 to about 2.9 trillion dollars by 1989, more than tripling during the administration of the president who had warned against deficit piled upon deficit, as tax cuts, the military buildup, and the recession reduced revenue faster than spending was cut. (Blueprint 4)

4. Correct the statement. The administration cut in some places and stood pat or grew in others. Cut or fired: income tax rates were cut under the 1981 act, domestic programs were cut in the companion budget act, and more than 11,000 striking air traffic controllers were fired in August 1981. Stood or grew: Social Security and Medicare were left in place, military spending rose, and the national debt grew from about 900 billion dollars to about 2.9 trillion dollars. A government whose debt tripled while its two largest entitlement programs stood untouched was redirected and reprioritized, not shrunk across the board. (Blueprint 3 and Blueprint 4)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 3 is the entry diagnostic: students who cannot set the inaugural's warning beside the debt figures will read the limits section of the lesson as an opinion rather than as the record answering the speech. Question 4 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires the change and the survival in a single corrected sentence.
