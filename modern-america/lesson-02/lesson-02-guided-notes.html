Guided Notes: The Reagan Revolution

Lesson question: What did the Reagan administration change about what the federal government did, and what did it leave in place?

Unit question: How did the United States remake itself after 1980, in its economy, its population, and its place in the world, and what did the remaking settle and what did it leave open?

Key Terms

supply-side economics: the economic theory behind the Reagan program, holding that cutting tax rates and reducing regulation would free investment and production and so grow the whole economy

deregulation: the removal or loosening of government rules on an industry, pursued from the late 1970s onward in airlines, trucking, banking, and communications

Reagan Democrat: a traditionally Democratic voter, often a white working class or union voter, who voted for Ronald Reagan in 1980 and 1984 while often remaining a Democrat

PATCO strike: the strike of August 1981 in which President Reagan fired more than eleven thousand federal air traffic controllers who had struck illegally, a blow unions took as a signal of the era's terms

federal deficit: the amount by which a year's federal spending exceeds the year's revenue, added each year to the national debt

Iran-Contra affair: the scandal of 1985 to 1987 in which officials of the Reagan administration secretly sold arms to Iran and diverted the proceeds to the Contra rebels in Nicaragua, against stated policy and a congressional ban

War on Drugs: the campaign of intensified drug enforcement and mandatory sentences pursued by the federal government from the 1970s onward and greatly expanded in the 1980s, which raised prison populations across the country

Summary table. The mandate: the first inaugural of January 20, 1981 declared that government is not the solution to the problem, government is the problem, and promised to curb the size and influence of the federal establishment. The program: supply-side tax cuts in the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981, the top rate falling from 70 percent to 50 percent, deregulation extended, and Paul Volcker's Federal Reserve breaking inflation at the cost of a recession with unemployment at 10.8 percent. The terms: the PATCO strike of August 1981 ended with more than 11,000 controllers fired, read by unions and employers alike as the era's terms for strikes. The coalition: Reagan Democrats crossed over in large numbers, and Reagan was reelected in 1984 carrying 49 states. The limits and the breach: the debt rose from about 900 billion dollars to about 2.9 trillion, Social Security and Medicare stood untouched, a 1982 tax increase reclaimed part of the 1981 cut, and the Iran-Contra affair showed a foreign policy run in secret against stated policy and a congressional ban.

Exit ticket 1. The claim: "In this present crisis, government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem." The act is the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981.

Exit ticket 2. Accept any accurate pairing with figures. Changed, for example: the top income tax rate, from 70 percent to 50 percent under the 1981 act; or the terms of labor conflict, more than 11,000 controllers fired in August 1981. Left in place, for example: Social Security and Medicare, the entitlement core; and the size of the federal establishment itself, with the national debt rising from about 900 billion dollars in 1981 to about 2.9 trillion dollars by 1989.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that holds a change and a survival together with two supporting facts. The strongest answers pair the tax and labor changes of 1981 with the untouched entitlements and the growing debt, and treat the judicial appointments as the change built to outlast the rest.
