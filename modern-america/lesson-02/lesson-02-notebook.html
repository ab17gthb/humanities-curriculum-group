Teacher Answer Key

Move in the recordDateWhat the record shows
The claim statedJanuary 20, 1981In his first inaugural address, Ronald Reagan declared that government is not the solution to the problem, government is the problem, and promised to curb the size and influence of the federal establishment.
The program enactedAugust 1981, and afterThe Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 cut individual income tax rates by about a quarter over three years and brought the top rate from 70 percent to 50 percent. Deregulation was extended in banking, communications, and energy, and Paul Volcker's Federal Reserve broke inflation with interest rates above 19 percent, at the cost of a recession in which unemployment reached 10.8 percent.
The terms setAugust 3 to 5, 1981When about 13,000 air traffic controllers struck in defiance of federal law and their sworn oath, Reagan gave them 48 hours to return and fired the more than 11,000 who did not. PATCO was decertified that fall, and the episode was read by unions and employers alike as the era's terms for strikes.
The coalition rewardedNovember 1984Reagan Democrats, traditionally Democratic and often white working class or union voters, crossed over in both elections, and in 1984 Reagan was reelected carrying 49 states.
The limits recorded1981 to 1989The national debt rose from about 900 billion dollars in 1981 to about 2.9 trillion dollars by 1989. Social Security and Medicare were left in place, a 1982 tax increase reclaimed part of the 1981 cut, and the revolution's most durable changes were its judicial appointments.
The breach exposed1985 to 1987Administration officials secretly sold arms to Iran and diverted the proceeds to the Contra rebels in Nicaragua, against stated policy and a congressional ban. The Tower Commission reported in February 1987 that established procedures had been ignored and Congress never notified.

The row that carries the most weight. Strong answers name the row, its date, and its consequence for what the government did. On the program enacted, the tax act is the one entry in the chart that rewrote a standing obligation of every taxpayer, which is why later arguments about the era usually begin there. On the terms set, the PATCO firings changed conduct far beyond the federal workforce, because private employers drew the lesson that permanent replacements were usable. On the limits recorded, the debt figures beside the untouched entitlements are the chart's own answer to any claim that the federal establishment shrank. On the breach exposed, the Tower findings show the revolution's governing style meeting its limit in foreign policy run off the books.
