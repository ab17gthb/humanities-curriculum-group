## Quoted texts

1. **The 9/11 Commission Report, executive summary (2004)** — quoted in the reading, guided notes (short phrases), activity Station 1, quick check background concepts, and writing evidence bank item 5. Verbatim from the audited TPSP bank record `911-commission-report-exec-summary-2004`; edition named in the record: National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, "The 9/11 Commission Report: Executive Summary," 2004 (the Commission's own edition). Public document (federal commission report). Cuts marked with ellipses in the bank record and in the activity. Longest single quotation used: the executive summary passage at activity Station 1 (about 95 words across three quoted blocks).

2. **President George W. Bush, Address to the Nation on the September 11 Attacks (September 11, 2001)** — quoted in the reading, activity Station 2, quick check, and writing evidence bank item 1. Verbatim from the audited TPSP bank record `bush-911-address-2001`; edition named in the record: archived White House press release. Public document (presidential address). Longest single quotation used: about 60 words at activity Station 2.

3. **Authorization for Use of Military Force, Public Law 107-40, Section 2 (September 18, 2001)** — quoted in the activity Station 3, quick check key paraphrase, and writing evidence bank item 2. Verbatim from the audited TPSP bank record `authorization-use-military-force-2001`; edition named in the record: U.S. Congress, "Authorization for Use of Military Force," 2001 (Office of the Law Revision Counsel). Public document (federal statute). Quoted: Section 2(a) in full (about 75 words) and Section 2(b)(2), reproduced exactly as the bank record carries them, including the statute's spelling "supercedes."

4. **USA PATRIOT Act, Section 102 (October 26, 2001)** — quoted in the reading (one sentence), activity Station 4, and writing evidence bank item 3 (paraphrase with one short quotation). Verbatim from the audited TPSP bank record `patriot-act-section-102-2001`; edition named in the record: U.S. Congress, "USA PATRIOT Act, Section 102," 2001 (GovInfo). Public document (federal statute). Cuts marked with ellipses.

5. **Comprehensive Report of the Special Advisor to the DCI on Iraq's WMD (the Duelfer Report), Key Findings (September 30, 2004)** — quoted in the activity Station 5, the writing evidence bank item 6, and the deck (slide 17 notes, paraphrased with short phrases in the notes and the findings quoted in the activity). Verbatim from the report's Key Findings as published by the Central Intelligence Agency and archived by the Government Publishing Office (permanent.access.gpo.gov, Comp_Report_Key_Findings.pdf), downloaded and checked word for word in October 2026. Public document (federal report). Two passages quoted: the nuclear finding (29 words) and the chemical weapons finding (42 words). Nothing in Station 5 is a labeled summary; both passages are direct quotations. The report's second finding, Saddam's intent to rebuild after sanctions, is stated from the same Key Findings.

6. **President George W. Bush, address to a joint session of Congress (September 20, 2001)** — named and paraphrased in the reading, guided notes, and deck; NOT quoted as text, following the unit's handling of the bank's audio record `bush-address-after-911-audio-2001`.

## Statistics used, with sources

- 2,977 killed: the toll as stated in the 9/11 Commission's accounting and carried in the unit vocabulary.
- AUMF votes (Senate 98 to 0, House 420 to 1; 518 to 1 combined): the congressional record of September 2001, as carried in the bank record's history and the statute's passage.
- PATRIOT Act Senate vote 98 to 1; signed October 26, 2001: the public legislative record.
- More than 7,000 American service members killed in the Afghanistan and Iraq wars combined, and costs in the trillions: stated in the reading and deck at the precision of the era's public accounting (Department of Defense casualty records as summarized in the public record; cost stated as "sums measured in the trillions," attributed in the deck notes to the accounting the era's documents use).
- 22 agencies merged into the Department of Homeland Security (2002): the Homeland Security Act's reorganization, as carried in the unit vocabulary.
