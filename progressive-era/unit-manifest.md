The pilot originally printed 43 key terms (L1 7, L2 5, L3 6, L4 5, L5 5, L6 5, L7 5, L8 5), short of the 56-term standard (7 per lesson) every later unit follows, and the Rycal companion deck matched it at 43. On Alex's rule that HCG and Rycal carry the identical term set, the unit was brought to 56 and the Rycal deck (hcg-progressive-era) extended to the same 56 in the same step. Thirteen terms were added, each a concept, law, person, or organization the lesson already taught but had never formally defined; definitions are written in the house style of the existing 43, and no existing definition was changed.

- Lesson 3 (+1) Wisconsin Idea: the reading gives it its own section and guided notes Section C is built on it.
- Lesson 4 (+2) Sherman Antitrust Act: the reading's Northern Securities account turns on the Act; guided notes Section B blanks it.
- Lesson 4 (+2) Pure Food and Drug Act (1906): named in the reading's consumer section and blanked in guided notes Section D.
- Lesson 5 (+2) Mann-Elkins Act (1910): named in the reading's opening account of Taft's record and blanked in guided notes Section A.
- Lesson 5 (+2) Eugene V. Debs: the reading's four-candidates section gives Debs and the Socialist argument full treatment; guided notes Section D requires him.
- Lesson 6 (+2) triple wall of privilege: Wilson's framing for the whole lesson; guided notes Section A opens with it.
- Lesson 6 (+2) Adamson Act (1916): named in the reading's closing account of Wilson's later program and blanked in guided notes Section E.
- Lesson 7 (+2) Seneca Falls (1848): the reading opens the movement's story there and guided notes Section A begins with it.
- Lesson 7 (+2) Night of Terror (1917): named in the reading's final-push section; guided notes Section E blanks the event.
- Lesson 8 (+2) Jim Crow: the reading's first section describes the southern legal structure by this name; it frames the whole lesson.
