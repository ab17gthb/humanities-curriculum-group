Teacher Answer Key

ProblemWhat it looked likeWhy it happenedWho cared, and what they wanted
WorkTen to twelve hour days, six days a week; pay of $400 to $500 a year; about 1.75 million working children; tens of thousands killed on the job each year.Factories grew faster than safety rules; no workers' compensation; courts held that workers accepted the risks of a job.Photographers like Lewis Hine documented the conditions, and reformers and settlement house workers publicized them and pushed for safety and child labor laws.
The cityTwo-thirds of New Yorkers in tenements; whole floors sharing one toilet; rooms without windows; disease killing as many as one infant in five in the worst districts.Cities grew faster than water, sewer, and housing systems; buildings packed families into spaces meant for half their number.Jacob Riis exposed tenement life in How the Other Half Lives (1890) and argued conditions, not character, caused poverty. He wanted housing codes and enforcement.
CorruptionPolitical machines traded jobs, contracts, and favors for votes; graft on city contracts; corporations bought state legislatures.Machines met real needs for immigrants and the poor, so voters stayed loyal; government was small and much of it for sale.Reformers and journalists exposed graft and pushed for honest elections and civil service; voters had to be offered services the machine provided.
Corporate powerAbout 318 trusts controlled two-fifths of manufacturing by 1904; Standard Oil refined about 90 percent of the nation's oil; the richest 1 percent owned about half the property.Laissez-faire ideas kept government out; big firms could sell at a loss until rivals closed; courts struck down regulations.Farmers from the Populist movement and middle-class reformers argued concentrated power was the core problem and government had to act as a counterweight.

The pattern. Strong answers name the shared cause: industrial growth outran the rules, institutions, and ideas meant to manage it, and the government that might have responded was small, slow, or for sale.
