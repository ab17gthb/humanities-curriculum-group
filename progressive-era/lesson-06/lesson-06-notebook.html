Teacher Answer Key

ReformWhat it didWhich wall it attackedStill standing today?
Underwood Tariff and income tax (1913)Cut average duties from about 40 to about 25 percent and replaced the lost revenue with a graduated income tax under the Sixteenth Amendment.The tariff wall; the tax made the cut affordable.Yes, transformed. Tariffs remain a policy tool, and the income tax became the federal government's main revenue.
Federal Reserve (1913)Created twelve regional Reserve banks under a federal board, able to expand or contract currency and lend to banks in a panic.The banking wall (the money trust and uncontrolled currency).Yes. The Federal Reserve remains the nation's central bank and its lender of last resort.
Clayton Act (1914)Banned named monopoly practices (price discrimination, tying contracts, interlocking directorates) and protected unions from conspiracy treatment.The trust wall.Yes. It remains a core antitrust statute alongside the Sherman Act.
Federal Trade Commission (1914)Created a permanent commission to investigate corporations and stop unfair methods of competition.The trust wall, by continuous supervision rather than lawsuits alone.Yes. The FTC still polices competition and consumer protection.

The through line. Strong answers name permanence: each reform built an institution or a revenue stream that kept working after the campaign ended (a tax system, a central bank, a statute courts still use, a standing commission), instead of relying on one president's energy or one court case.
