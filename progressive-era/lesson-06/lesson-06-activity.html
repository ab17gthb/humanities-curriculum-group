Sort the Record: New Freedom or New Nationalism?

Directions: In 1912 the two programs were defined against each other. New Freedom tools remove privilege and restore competition. New Nationalism tools regulate and manage the economy through federal power and expert commissions. Wilson signed every action below. For each one, mark NF or NN in the verdict column and write your reason in a few words.

Action, with yearVerdictYour reason
1. Underwood Tariff (1913) cuts import duties from about 40 to 25 percent.
2. First income tax (1913) under the Sixteenth Amendment funds the government.
3. Federal Reserve Act (1913) creates twelve regional banks under a Washington board.
4. Clayton Act (1914) bans price discrimination and tying contracts by name.
5. Clayton Act (1914) declares unions are not conspiracies and limits injunctions.
6. Federal Trade Commission (1914) investigates corporations and can halt unfair methods.
7. Tariff Commission (1916) puts a board of experts in charge of advising on rates.
8. Federal Farm Loan Act (1916) builds a federal credit system for farmers.
9. Adamson Act (1916) sets an eight-hour day for railroad workers by federal law.
10. Keating-Owen Act (1916) bars interstate sale of goods made with child labor.

The Verdict on Wilson

1. Count your marks. Which program did Wilson govern with more often, by your own sorting?

2. Which two actions were hardest to classify? For each, state the argument for the side you rejected.

3. Write the paragraph: "Wilson governed as Wilson" or "Wilson governed as Roosevelt." Use at least three sorted actions as evidence, including one that cuts against your claim.

1. By the key's sort, New Nationalism, five actions to four with one mixed, and the NN share grows in the later years (1916 items are all NN).

2. The Federal Reserve (3) and the income tax (2) are designed to be the hard cases; the Clayton labor sections (5) are an acceptable third. Credit any pairing where the rejected side's argument is stated accurately.

3. Strong paragraphs note the time pattern: Wilson's 1913 work is mostly New Freedom, his 1914 to 1916 work mostly New Nationalism, and they account for the shift rather than merely counting.
