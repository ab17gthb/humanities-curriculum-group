Part 2: Short Answer

7. In one town, angry voters collected signatures twice in one year: first to force a vote on removing their mayor, then to put their own tax law on the ballot after the council refused it. Name the two tools the voters used, in the order used.

8. Before the Seventeenth Amendment, a corporation that wanted a friendly senator did not need to persuade the voters of a state. Explain what it had to do instead, and why reformers called that arrangement corruptible.

Teacher Answer Key

Part 1: 1-B, 2-D, 3-A, 4-F, 5-E, 6-C.

7. Recall (removing the mayor), then initiative (citizens placing their own law on the ballot). The second is not a referendum: the law came from the citizens, not from the council, and partial credit belongs to students who catch that distinction even if they name it imperfectly.

8. Senators were chosen by state legislatures, so a corporation needed to influence or purchase a relatively small number of legislators, often in closed proceedings, rather than win a statewide popular vote. Reformers called it corruptible because the decision-makers were few, identifiable, and repeatedly dependent on the interests seeking favors.

Teacher note. This check is formative. The diagnostic item is number 7: students who confuse initiative and referendum have the vocabulary but not the mechanism. Reteach with the question "who wrote the law?" before Lesson 4.
