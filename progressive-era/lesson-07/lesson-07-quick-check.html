Part 2: Short Answer

7. State the antis' strongest public argument in their own terms, then write the suffragist reply that history judged more persuasive. Two sentences maximum for each.

8. Explain why a purely state-by-state strategy might never have finished the job, naming the region that blocked it and the reason.

Teacher Answer Key

Part 1: 1-B, 2-A, 3-D, 4-C, 5-E, 6-F.

7. Anti argument, fairly stated: most women do not want the vote; women's influence belongs to the home, and dragging them into partisan politics would coarsen women without purifying politics. Suffragist reply: rights are not decided by polling the contented; every expansion of the electorate was opposed by those already inside it, and "protection" without representation left women's wages, property, and children governed by laws they had no voice in.

8. The South. Southern states defended state control of elections as a states' rights principle, many southern legislators opposed any federal voting amendment as a precedent for federal enforcement of the Fifteenth Amendment, and fifty years of state campaigning there had produced no full-suffrage state. Only a federal amendment could override that wall, which is why the federal road ultimately mattered.

Teacher note. Formative. Question 7 grades fairness as much as knowledge: students who can state the anti argument without caricature are the ones ready for the writing task's counterargument requirement.
