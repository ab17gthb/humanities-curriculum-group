Guided Notes: The Long Road to the Nineteenth Amendment

Lesson question: Why did the suffrage movement win in 1920 after seventy years of losing?

Unit question: How did Americans respond to the problems created by industrialization, urbanization, and political corruption?

Key Terms

suffrage: the right to vote

Seneca Falls (1848): the convention that opened the women's rights movement, issuing a Declaration of Sentiments modeled on the Declaration of Independence

NAWSA: the National American Woman Suffrage Association, formed in 1890 by the reunited wings of the movement

Silent Sentinels: Alice Paul's pickets who stood at the White House gates from January 1917

Night of Terror (1917): the November night when guards at the Occoquan Workhouse beat the imprisoned suffrage pickets

Winning Plan: Carrie Chapman Catt's strategy of coordinating state campaigns with a federal amendment drive

Nineteenth Amendment (1920): amendment barring denial of the vote on account of sex

Teacher Answer Key

A blanks: Seneca Falls; 1848; Fifteenth; voted; Minor; not a right; 1890; NAWSA (the National American Woman Suffrage Association).

B blanks: Wyoming; Colorado; Utah; Idaho; political machines; settlers (women settlers and families); Populist. 1878; 1887; two-thirds; three-fourths.

C blanks: women; did not want; home; brewers and distillers (the liquor industry); Prohibition; urban political machines.

D blanks: Winning Plan; Silent Sentinels; 1917; Night of Terror; force-feeding (were force-fed); January 1918; war.

E blanks: June 4; thirty-six; Tennessee; Harry Burn; his mother; August 26, 1920.

Exit ticket 2: women voters were expected to support Prohibition, so the industry was defending its market against the electorate suffrage would create, not against the amendment's text. Exit ticket 3: the banners turned the president's own war rationale (fighting for democracy abroad) into the indictment of its absence at home, and punishing peaceful pickets during that war produced the Occoquan scandal.
