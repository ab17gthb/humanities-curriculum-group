1912 Comparison Chart: Four Candidates

Directions: Read the four platform cards, which summarize each campaign's stated positions. Use them to complete the chart. Then answer the analysis questions.

Theodore Roosevelt, Progressive Party ("Bull Moose"): New Nationalism

Big corporations are here to stay, so the federal government must be strong enough to control them. Create powerful commissions to supervise corporations, set rules for competition, and protect workers with minimum wages, limits on hours, and insurance against sickness, injury, and old age. Lower the tariff. Give women the vote and expand direct democracy.

Woodrow Wilson, Democratic Party: New Freedom

Monopoly is the problem, and regulating it would only protect it. Break up the trusts and restore competition so small businesses and farmers can compete on fair terms. Cut the tariff, reform the banking system so credit is not controlled by a few New York banks, and keep government strong as a referee but out of the business of managing corporations.

William Howard Taft, Republican Party

Defend the record of the last four years: more antitrust prosecutions than any president before, postal savings, and railroad regulation through law and the courts. Government must act through courts and Congress, within the Constitution, not through presidential crusades or new parties built around one man.

Eugene V. Debs, Socialist Party

Competition and regulation both leave ownership in the hands that caused the problem. Put the railroads, mines, and major industries under public ownership, run for use instead of profit. Until workers own the economy, every reform will be reversed the moment it threatens the owners.

Analysis

1. Two of these men agree on the diagnosis and disagree completely on the cure. Name them, and state the disagreement in one sentence.

2. Which candidate would Jacob Riis have found least satisfying on the tenement problem, based on the cards alone? Use one line from that candidate's card.

3. Every card except one assumes private ownership of industry continues. Which one does not, and what does its candidate claim will happen to reforms passed under the others?

1. Roosevelt and Wilson. Both name corporate power as the problem; Roosevelt would regulate big corporations, Wilson would dissolve their monopoly and restore competition.

2. Taft, on the cards as written: his program is courts and process, with no housing or conditions plank. Accept Debs as wrong here because his card does address workers' conditions through ownership; the expected answer is Taft, defended from the card's emphasis.

3. Debs. He claims reforms under private ownership will be reversed whenever they threaten the owners, so only public ownership makes reform permanent.
