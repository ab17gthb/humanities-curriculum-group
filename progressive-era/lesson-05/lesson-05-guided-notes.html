Guided Notes: Taft, the Split, and the Election of 1912

Lesson question: Why did the Republican Party split in 1912, and what did the split decide?

Unit question: How did Americans respond to the problems created by industrialization, urbanization, and political corruption?

Key Terms

Mann-Elkins Act (1910): law extending federal regulation of the railroads, passed under President Taft

Payne-Aldrich Tariff (1909): tariff law, promised as reform, that lowered some rates and raised others

Ballinger-Pinchot affair: the 1910 fight in which Taft fired forester Gifford Pinchot after Pinchot accused the Interior secretary of favoring corporate land claims

Progressive Party: the party Roosevelt's supporters formed in 1912, nicknamed Bull Moose

Eugene V. Debs: the Socialist candidate for president in 1912, who argued for public ownership of railroads and major industries

New Nationalism: Roosevelt's 1912 program of regulating large corporations through a strong federal government

New Freedom: Wilson's 1912 program of breaking up monopoly and restoring competition

Teacher Answer Key

A blanks: 1908; ninety; twice; Mann-Elkins; temperament (style and appearance); judge.

B blanks: Payne-Aldrich; raised; praised (defended publicly). Ballinger; fired; 1910. U.S. Steel; Roosevelt; his own integrity (him personally).

C blanks: direct primaries; Roosevelt; state conventions (party organizations); Taft; walked out; Progressive; as strong as a bull moose.

D: Roosevelt, Progressive (Bull Moose), New Nationalism: regulate big business. Wilson, Democratic, New Freedom: break up monopoly, restore competition. Taft, Republican, defend the record and the courts. Debs, Socialist, public ownership of major industries.

E blanks: 42; 435; 88; third-party; two; outpolled Wilson by more than a million votes.

Exit ticket 1: accept any ranking defended with facts; the strongest cases usually pick Pinchot (it turned a policy dispute into a loyalty test) or U.S. Steel (it made the fight personal between the two men).
