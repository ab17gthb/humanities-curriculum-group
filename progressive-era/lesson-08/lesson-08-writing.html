Seminar Prep: How Much Did Progressivism Change America?

The seminar prompt: Evaluate the extent to which Progressivism changed American society between 1900 and 1920. Come to the seminar with this sheet complete. You will be asked to defend a thesis, cite facts from across the unit, and answer the strongest case against you.

Step 1: Two Theses

Write the strongest change thesis you can: one sentence claiming Progressivism transformed American life, naming the two deepest changes.

Write the strongest limits thesis you can: one sentence claiming the transformation was shallower than it looks, naming the two strongest limits.

Which one will you defend in the seminar? Note why, in a phrase.

Step 2: Harvest the Evidence

For each row, write one fact that supports the change thesis and one that supports the limits thesis.

Step 3: Prepare Answers

1. A classmate says, "The federal government of 1920 would be unrecognizable to a citizen of 1900." Give your best supporting fact, then your best complicating fact.

2. A classmate says, "Progressivism was reform for some Americans at the price of others." Who are the some, who are the others, and does the record support the word price?

3. Which single artifact of the era, a law, an amendment, an institution, or an organization, best proves your thesis? Defend the choice against the obvious rival pick.

Teacher Seminar Guide

Sample theses. Change: "Progressivism permanently rebuilt the federal government into an active regulator of the economy and expanded the electorate through two amendments, changes no later generation has reversed." Limits: "Progressivism modernized government for the industrial economy while leaving the color line intact, labor unempowered, and its boldest moral reform, Prohibition, headed for repeal."

GroundEvidence of deep changeEvidence of limits
Democracy and electionsInitiative, referendum, recall, direct primaries; direct election of senators (Seventeenth Amendment).Disfranchisement of Black southerners completed in the same years; the new tools coexisted with white primaries.
Business and consumersMeat Inspection and Pure Food and Drug Acts, the Federal Reserve, the FTC, the Clayton Act.Regulation supervised big business rather than shrinking it; Wilson himself ended up using Roosevelt's commission model.
WorkersHours laws upheld, factory codes after Triangle, workers' compensation, the eight-hour railroad day.Child labor law struck down (1918); no minimum wage, no protected right to organize.
Race and immigrationThe NAACP founded (1909) and winning in court by 1915-1917; settlement houses defended immigrants.Federal segregation under Wilson; literacy test enacted 1917; eugenic sterilization laws from 1907.
WomenThe Nineteenth Amendment doubled the electorate in 1920.It arrived seventy-two years after Seneca Falls, carried racial compromises, and left Black women in the South disfranchised in practice.

Running the seminar. Open with Step 3, question 2; it forces both records onto the table early. Keep a visible fact tally on the board, one column per thesis. Close by returning to the unit's essential question and asking which lesson's evidence students reached for most; that answer tells you what the review day needs to rebuild before the test.
