Guided Notes: The Human Cost of the Depression

Lesson question: How deep did the Depression cut, and how did ordinary families live through it?

Unit question: How did the New Deal change the relationship between Americans and their federal government, and what did it leave undone?

Key Terms

Great Depression: the severe economic collapse that began in the United States in 1929 and lasted through the 1930s, marked by bank failures, mass unemployment, and falling prices

deflation: a general fall in prices across the economy, which raises the real burden of debts and rewards waiting to spend

bank run: a rush of depositors to withdraw their money from a bank at the same time, driven by fear that the bank will fail

unemployment: the condition of people who are able to work and seeking work but cannot find a job

breadline: a line of people waiting for free food given out by a charity, a church, or a city agency

Hooverville: a shantytown built by homeless people from scrap wood, tin, and cardboard during the Depression, named in bitter reference to President Hoover

Dust Bowl: the region of the southern Great Plains where drought and poor farming practices turned topsoil to dust in the 1930s, driving many farm families from their land

Summary table. The collapse: production fell by nearly half between 1929 and 1932 and about one worker in four was out of work in 1933, so wages stopped across whole cities. Banks and prices: about nine thousand banks failed and deposits were lost, while deflation raised the real burden of every debt. Food and shelter: breadlines and soup kitchens fed thousands daily until donations and city funds ran out, and eviction and foreclosure pushed families into Hoovervilles built from scrap. Families and migration: families doubled up, postponed marriage and children, sent men and teenagers onto freight trains, and Dust Bowl families drove Route 66 west to California.

Exit ticket 1. No bank kept all its deposits in cash, because most deposits were out on loan, so a rush of depositors withdrawing at once could force even a sound bank to close before it could collect its loans.

Exit ticket 2. Any two, with causes. Doubling up followed eviction and foreclosure. Postponed marriage and a falling birth rate followed stopped wages. Freight train riding by men and teenagers followed the absence of work at home. Women becoming family earners followed the loss of a husband's wage.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that measures depth with at least two facts. The strongest answers combine the production and unemployment figures with a human record item such as breadlines, Hoovervilles, doubling up, or Dust Bowl migration, and note that the collapse reached careful savers and steady workers.
