Teacher Answer Key

AgencyKind of reliefWhat it providedWho it missed or shorted
FERADirect reliefFederal money sent through the states as grocery orders, rent help, and cash for families in need, led by Harry Hopkins from May 1933.Families in states with low standards, and Black families where payments differed by race, received far less for the same need.
CCCWork reliefCamp work for young men planting trees, fighting fires, and building park roads and trails, at thirty dollars a month with twenty five dollars sent home.Women, older men, and married men could not enroll, and the camps were segregated with Black enrollment held low.
CWAWork reliefAbout four million jobs on small public projects for the winter of 1933 to 1934, hired within weeks.Everyone it hired, when Roosevelt shut it down in the spring of 1934 over cost and its workers fell back on direct relief.
PWAWork reliefLarge construction, dams, bridges, schools, and courthouses, funded by the government and built by private contractors under Harold Ickes.The unemployed who needed work at once, because careful review made PWA jobs arrive slowly and in smaller numbers.
NRARecovery program, not reliefCodes of fair competition for each industry setting prices, wages, and hours, with the Blue Eagle for firms that complied and the right to organize protected.Small firms burdened by the codes, and buyers who faced code prices they could not pay while idle.

Relief in one sentence. Strong answers name both halves the chart records. The agencies fed families, hired millions, and built lasting structures within a year, and their help stopped short because delivery was segregated and uneven, whole groups fell outside the designs, and unemployment still stood at about one worker in five in 1934.
