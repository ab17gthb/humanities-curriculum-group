Writing Task: How Far Did Help Reach

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: What did the New Deal agencies actually do in people's lives, and how far did their help reach? A strong answer works agency by agency through the distinction between direct relief and work relief, and it measures the reach honestly, including segregated and uneven delivery and unemployment still high in 1934. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Direct relief meant money, food, or goods given to people in need without work required in return. Work relief meant government paid jobs on public projects instead of direct relief payments.

2. The Federal Emergency Relief Administration, led by Harry Hopkins, sent federal relief money to the states. State standards differed widely, so the same need drew generous help in one state and very little in another.

3. The Civilian Conservation Corps employed about a quarter million young men within months at thirty dollars a month, most of it sent home to their families.

4. The Civil Works Administration hired about four million people for the winter of 1933 to 1934 and was shut down in the spring of 1934 over cost. The Public Works Administration, led by Harold Ickes, funded dams, bridges, schools, and courthouses built by private contractors.

5. The National Recovery Administration wrote codes of fair competition setting prices, wages, and hours, and firms that complied displayed the Blue Eagle. Large firms often wrote codes that favored themselves.

6. The CCC camps were segregated by race, relief often differed by race, and unemployment in 1934 still stood at about one worker in five, far above any level known before 1929.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question, what the agencies did and how far the help reached. (Blueprint 7 and Blueprint 8)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 through 4 support relief at work. (Blueprint 7) Bank items 5 and 6 support the reach and the gaps, with item 2 also serving uneven delivery. (Blueprint 8) A full credit essay draws on both groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. An essay that lists agencies without the direct and work relief distinction has assembled names without the lesson argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that measures the whole record, millions helped and hired against one worker in five still unemployed in 1934.

Model opening for teacher reference. In 1933 the federal government entered daily life at the level of the grocery order and the paycheck. Hopkins sent federal relief money through the states to families with nothing, the CCC paid a quarter million young men thirty dollars a month and sent most of it home, and one winter the CWA hired four million people outright. No American government had ever done relief on this scale. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the PWA and NRA evidence, and closing on the honest measure: camps segregated, relief uneven, and one worker in five still jobless in 1934.

Common weak answers. An alphabet list (the CCC, CWA, PWA, and NRA did many things) names agencies without saying what any family received. Reach only (millions were helped) and gaps only (the New Deal failed) each repeat half the reading. Credit requires both halves in one judgment.
