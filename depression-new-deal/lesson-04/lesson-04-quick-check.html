Quick Check: Relief at Work

Directions: Read the background and the table, then answer the questions.

Background. The Federal Emergency Relief Administration sent federal money through the states as direct relief. The Civilian Conservation Corps paid young men thirty dollars a month in camps, most of it sent home. The Civil Works Administration hired about four million people for the winter of 1933 to 1934. The Public Works Administration funded large construction built by private contractors. Contemporary estimates placed unemployment at about one worker in four in 1933 and about one worker in five in 1934.

1933About one in four
1934About one in five

1. A mother with three small children and no income needs food this week and cannot take a camp or construction job. Name the form of relief that fits her, define it, and name the agency that delivered it.

2. A CCC enrollee earns thirty dollars a month. How much goes home, what work does he do in camp, and why does the lesson call the corps relief twice over?

3. Use the table. By how much did the unemployed share fall between 1933 and 1934, and why does the reading still call the 1934 figure a measure of failure as well as progress?

4. Name two gaps in the agencies' reach stated in the reading, one about who was served and one about how help was delivered.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Name the form of relief, define it, and name the agency. Direct relief, money, food, or goods given directly to people in need without work required in return. The Federal Emergency Relief Administration, led by Harry Hopkins, delivered it by sending federal money to the states to pass on to people in need. (Blueprint 7)

2. Trace the CCC wage and the work. Twenty five of the thirty dollars went straight home to his family. In camp he planted trees, fought fires, and built park roads and trails. The corps was relief twice over because the enrollee ate, slept, and worked in camp while his family at home received a monthly check for rent and food. (Blueprint 7)

3. Use the table on the fall in unemployment. The share fell from about one in four in 1933 to about one in five in 1934, a fall of about one worker in twenty. The reading still treats 1934 as failure as well as progress because one worker in five without a job stood far above any level the country had known before 1929. (Blueprint 8)

4. Name two gaps in the reach. Any two, accurately stated. The CCC camps were segregated by race and Black enrollment was held far below the Black share of the unemployed. Relief payments differed by state and often by race within a state. Most agencies served men first and reached women through a narrow set of jobs. (Blueprint 8)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the distinction diagnostic for the whole lesson. A student who answers work relief for the mother has the labels without the logic, and should redo section A of the guided notes before the writing task.
