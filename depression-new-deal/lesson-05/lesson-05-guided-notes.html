Guided Notes: Too Little, Too Much

Lesson question: Why did the New Deal draw attack from every direction by 1935, and what did the attacks reveal about the country?

Unit question: How did the New Deal change the relationship between Americans and their federal government, and what did it leave undone?

Key Terms

Share Our Wealth: Huey Long's program and movement, begun in 1934, which promised to cap large fortunes and guarantee every family a homestead and a minimum income

Townsend Plan: Dr. Francis Townsend's proposal that the federal government pay two hundred dollars a month to every citizen over sixty, on condition the money be spent within the month

old-age pension: a regular payment made by a government to people who have reached a set age and left regular work

demagogue: a leader who gains power by arousing popular fears, prejudices, and hopes with simple promises, rather than by answering arguments

American Liberty League: the organization founded in 1934 by wealthy businessmen and conservative Democrats to oppose the New Deal as a threat to property and the Constitution

Schechter v. United States: the 1935 Supreme Court decision that struck down the National Industrial Recovery Act, holding that Congress had given away its lawmaking power and reached business beyond its authority

United States v. Butler: the 1936 Supreme Court decision that struck down the Agricultural Adjustment Act's processing tax as an unconstitutional use of federal power over farm production

Exit ticket 1. The mass movements charged that the New Deal did too little, leaving great fortunes untouched and the old without income. The Liberty League charged that it did too much, threatening property and passing the limits of the Constitution.

Exit ticket 2. The League could only publish and persuade, and it never built a following among voters. The Court could void laws. In Schechter (1935) it struck down the National Industrial Recovery Act on delegation and commerce grounds, and in Butler (1936) it struck down the Agricultural Adjustment Act's processing tax, removing two central New Deal programs outright.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that holds both directions of attack and uses at least two facts. The strongest answers add what the attacks revealed: a public that wanted security the first New Deal had not provided, organized through radio and clubs, and a constitutional order that still limited how far the federal government could go to provide it.
