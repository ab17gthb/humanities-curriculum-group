Writing Task: A New Expectation

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did Roosevelt's first hundred days change what Americans expected their government to do in a crisis? A strong answer covers the banking rescue and the wider Hundred Days, and it holds the reading's two judgments at once: speed and confidence were real achievements, and most programs were experiments whose results nobody could yet know. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Roosevelt proclaimed the bank holiday on March 6, 1933, closing every bank in the country so examiners could sort sound banks from failed ones.

2. Congress passed the Emergency Banking Act on March 9, in a single day, allowing sound banks to reopen under federal license. When the first banks opened, deposits exceeded withdrawals in city after city.

3. In the first inaugural address Roosevelt said that the only thing we have to fear is fear itself. In the first fireside chat on March 12 he explained banking in plain language and asked listeners to keep money in the reopened banks.

4. The Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to reduce production toward parity. The Tennessee Valley Authority built dams, controlled flooding, and brought electricity and development to a whole river valley.

5. The National Industrial Recovery Act set up codes of fair competition for industry. The Glass-Steagall Act separated commercial and investment banking and created federal insurance of bank deposits.

6. The special session ran from March 9 to June 16, 1933. Congress passed bill after bill, often with little debate, across banking, farming, industry, and relief.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the lesson question directly by naming the new expectation, that the federal government would act in a crisis, and act first. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 through 3 support the banking rescue. (Blueprint 5) Bank items 4 through 6 support the wider Hundred Days. (Blueprint 6) A full credit essay draws on both groups.
  • The second judgment stated and supported: the programs were experiments, passed in days with little debate, whose results nobody in 1933 could know. An essay that celebrates speed without the experiment earns partial credit.
  • An ending that identifies the change at the level of the citizen and the state, not only a list of laws.

Model opening for teacher reference. Before March 1933 Americans expected the federal government to stand back in an economic crisis. In one hundred days Roosevelt closed every bank in the country, won a banking law in a single day, explained the rescue on the radio in plain language, and signed laws that paid farmers, dammed a river valley, coded industry, and insured deposits. A government that would do all of that, at that speed, had redefined its own job. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the experiment judgment, and closing on the expectation rather than on any single law.

Common weak answers. A list of laws with no claim about expectation names the season without answering the question. The fear sentence quoted with no banking sequence treats the address as a slogan. Praise of speed with no mention that the programs were untested repeats only half of the reading.
