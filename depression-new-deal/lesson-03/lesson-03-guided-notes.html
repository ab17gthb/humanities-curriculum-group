Guided Notes: Roosevelt and the First Hundred Days

Lesson question: How did Roosevelt's first hundred days change what Americans expected their government to do in a crisis?

Unit question: How did the New Deal change the relationship between Americans and their federal government, and what did it leave undone?

Key Terms

bank holiday: the closing of all the nation's banks by presidential proclamation on March 6, 1933, so that examiners could sort sound banks from failed ones before reopening

Emergency Banking Act: the law passed by Congress on March 9, 1933, that gave the president broad power over banking and allowed sound banks to reopen under federal license

fireside chat: one of President Roosevelt's radio addresses to the nation, delivered in plain conversational language from the White House

Hundred Days: the first phase of Roosevelt's presidency, from March 9 to June 16, 1933, in which Congress passed an exceptional volume of New Deal legislation

Glass-Steagall Act: the 1933 law that separated commercial banking from investment banking and created federal insurance of bank deposits

Agricultural Adjustment Act: the 1933 law that paid farmers to reduce production of basic crops and livestock in order to raise farm prices toward parity

Tennessee Valley Authority: the federal corporation created in 1933 to build dams, control flooding, and bring electricity and development to the Tennessee River valley

The Hundred Days at a Glance. Banking rescue: the bank holiday, the Emergency Banking Act, and the first fireside chat sorted the banks, reopened the sound ones under federal license, and persuaded depositors to bring money back, which changed the expectation that Washington would stand back during a panic. Farm and valley programs: the Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to cut production toward parity, and the Tennessee Valley Authority built dams and brought electricity to a whole region, which changed the expectation that farm prices and regional poverty were private problems. Industry and banking rules: industrial codes set wages, hours, and prices, and the Glass-Steagall Act separated banking and insured deposits, which changed the expectation that the rules of business were beyond federal reach.

Exit ticket 1. The reopening could only succeed if examiners had first sorted sound banks from failed ones while withdrawals were stopped, so that a federal license meant a bank had actually been inspected.

Exit ticket 2. Any two, accurately stated. The Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to reduce production toward parity. The Tennessee Valley Authority built dams, controlled flooding, and brought electricity and development to the Tennessee River valley. The National Industrial Recovery Act set up industry codes of fair competition. The Glass-Steagall Act separated commercial and investment banking and created federal deposit insurance.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names the new expectation and uses two facts. The strongest answers pair the speed of March 1933, banks closed March 6, a banking law March 9, banks reopening after the March 12 chat, with the caution that the programs were experiments whose results were still unknown.
