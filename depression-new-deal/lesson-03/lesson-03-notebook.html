Teacher Answer Key

ActionWhat Congress didWhat changed for ordinary people
The bank holiday of March 6 stopped withdrawals, the Emergency Banking Act of March 9 let sound banks reopen under federal license, and the first fireside chat of March 12 explained the plan in plain language.Depositors brought money back to reopened banks instead of draining them, and a depositor could once again tell a licensed bank from a failed one.
The Agricultural Adjustment Act of May 1933 paid farmers to reduce production of basic crops and livestock toward parity, funded by a processing tax.Farm families received federal payments, and farm prices began to rise from levels below the cost of production.
The Tennessee Valley Authority Act of May 1933 created a federal corporation to build dams, control flooding, and bring electricity and development to the Tennessee River valley.Valley families gained flood control, navigation, and the first electric power in farm homes private companies had not served.
The National Industrial Recovery Act of June 1933 set up codes of fair competition for each industry, setting wages, hours, and prices, and protected the right to organize.Workers in coded industries gained minimum wages, maximum hours, and a federal statement that organizing was lawful.
The Glass-Steagall Act of June 1933 separated commercial banking from investment banking and created federal insurance of bank deposits.A depositor in a failed bank would be repaid by the government up to a set limit, which removed the reason to join a bank run.

The Hundred Days in one sentence. Strong answers name the new expectation and ground it in the chart. In one hundred days the federal government rescued the banks, paid farmers, took up a whole river valley, coded industry, and insured deposits, so acting in a crisis stopped being an exception and became what Americans expected Washington to do.
