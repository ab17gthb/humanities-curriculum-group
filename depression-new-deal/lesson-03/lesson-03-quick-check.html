Quick Check: The Banking Rescue

Directions: Read the passages and the background, then answer the questions.

Passage 1. From the First Inaugural Address, March 4, 1933, Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library transcript: the only thing we have to fear is fear itself, nameless, unreasoning, unjustified terror which paralyzes needed efforts to convert retreat into advance.

Passage 2. From the First Fireside Chat, March 12, 1933, FDR Library transcript. Roosevelt explained that when a person deposits money in a bank, the bank puts the money to work in loans, and he asked listeners to keep their money in the reopened banks. He closed by saying that the machinery to restore the financial system had been provided and that together the country could not fail. Cuts in this summary are shown in the lesson reading; the passages quoted word for word above are verbatim.

Background. Roosevelt proclaimed the bank holiday on March 6. Congress passed the Emergency Banking Act on March 9. Sound banks reopened under federal license in stages after March 12. The Hundred Days ran from March 9 to June 16, 1933.

1. In Passage 1, what does Roosevelt name as the thing to fear, and what does he say that fear does to needed efforts?

2. Put the four events in order and give each date: banks reopen under federal license, the bank holiday is proclaimed, the Emergency Banking Act passes, the first fireside chat is broadcast.

3. Name two laws of the Hundred Days from sections E and F of the reading that were not banking laws, and state what each one did.

4. A depositor in June 1933 keeps money in a bank that later fails. Name the law that protects her deposit, the agency idea it created, and the limit on that protection stated in the reading.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What does Roosevelt name as the thing to fear, and what does fear do? Fear itself. Roosevelt calls it a nameless, unreasoning, unjustified terror which paralyzes needed efforts to convert retreat into advance. Credit requires both the naming and the paralysis, because together they make the address a policy instrument and not only a famous sentence. (Blueprint 5)

2. Put the four events in order with dates. The bank holiday is proclaimed, March 6. The Emergency Banking Act passes, March 9. The first fireside chat is broadcast, March 12. Sound banks reopen under federal license in stages, beginning after March 12. (Blueprint 5)

3. Name two non banking laws of the Hundred Days and what each did. Any two, accurately stated. The Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to reduce production toward parity. The Tennessee Valley Authority built dams, controlled flooding, and brought electricity and development to the Tennessee River valley. The National Industrial Recovery Act set up industry codes of fair competition. (Blueprint 6)

4. Name the law that protects a deposit in a failed bank, and its limit. The Glass-Steagall Act, which created federal insurance of bank deposits. The depositor is repaid by the government up to a set limit, not without limit. (Blueprint 6)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 2 is the sequence diagnostic. Students who place the chat before the banking law have the story as performance rather than as a rescue, and should be sent back to sections B through D before the writing task.
