Guided Notes: The Second New Deal

Lesson question: How did the Second New Deal of 1935 move beyond the first, and who was it built to protect?

Unit question: How did the New Deal change the relationship between Americans and their federal government, and what did it leave undone?

Key Terms

Works Progress Administration: the agency created in 1935, led by Harry Hopkins, that employed about eight and a half million people over its life building roads, schools, and airports and funding arts and writing projects

Social Security Act: the law of August 1935 that created old-age insurance, unemployment insurance with the states, and aid to dependent children and other vulnerable groups

old-age insurance: the part of Social Security that pays a monthly benefit to retired workers, funded by payroll taxes on workers and their employers

unemployment insurance: the joint federal and state system, created in 1935, that pays temporary benefits to workers who lose their jobs

payroll tax: a tax taken from workers' wages and matched by employers, used to fund Social Security

National Labor Relations Act: the 1935 law, called the Wagner Act, that guaranteed workers the right to organize unions and bargain collectively and created a board to enforce those rights

collective bargaining: negotiation between an employer and a union chosen by the workers over wages, hours, and working conditions

Teacher Answer Key

A blanks: emergency; settlement. A laws, the three named: a work program (the WPA); an insurance system (the Social Security Act); a labor law (the Wagner Act). The tax measure, the Revenue Act of 1935, accompanies them.

(1) 1935; B leader blanks: Harry Hopkins; (2) eight and a half; (3) three; B proposer blank: states.

B structures, any two: roads; schools; courthouses; airports.

C blanks: Federal One; Theatre; (4) two thousand; 1939.

(5) 1935; D tax blank: payroll; D match blank: matched; D right blank: right; D age blank: sixty-five.

D other protections: unemployment insurance, run jointly with the states; federal aid to dependent children (with aid to the blind and to the old without insurance, paid through state grants).

E blanks: agricultural; domestic; Black; women; reporting; 1950s.

F blanks: Wagner; unions; collectively; unfair; elections; charges.

(6) seventy-five.

G troubles: a worker's old age; a lost job; a fight to organize (labor rights). Accept old age, unemployment, and union organizing in any order.

Table, model entries. WPA: duty, direct federal employment of the jobless on public projects; covered the unemployed who could be put on project rolls, about eight and a half million over its life, and left out those the local project system did not reach. Social Security Act: duty, standing insurance against old age and job loss plus aid to dependent children; covered workers in the employments the act defined, and left out agricultural and domestic workers at the founding. Wagner Act: duty, federal protection of organizing and collective bargaining through a standing board; covered most private sector workers who chose to organize, and left the right dependent on the board's enforcement. Revenue Act: duty, financing the new state from the top of the income scale; fell on the largest incomes, estates, and corporations.

Exit ticket 1. An emergency measure answers a collapse and is expected to end with it, as the 1933 laws were. A settlement accepts a permanent duty the government carries in ordinary times too, as the 1935 laws did for old age, unemployment, work relief, and labor rights.

Exit ticket 2. The act's definition of employment excluded agricultural labor and domestic service in a private home. The exclusion fell hardest on Black workers, above all in the South, and on working women.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names the move from emergency relief to permanent federal duties and uses at least two facts. The strongest answers also name who the settlement was built to protect and who stood outside it at the founding.
