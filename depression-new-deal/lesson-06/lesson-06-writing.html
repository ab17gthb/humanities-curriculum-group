Writing Task: The Settlement of 1935

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did the Second New Deal of 1935 move beyond the first, and who was it built to protect? A strong answer does more than list new laws. It explains the change in kind, from emergency measures meant to stop a collapse to permanent duties the federal government accepted for ordinary times, and it answers the second half of the question honestly, naming both the people the settlement protected and the people it left outside at the founding. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The Works Progress Administration, created in May 1935 under Harry Hopkins, employed about eight and a half million people across its life building roads, schools, courthouses, and airports. Its Federal One projects employed actors, musicians, artists, and writers, and its writers collected life histories including interviews with more than two thousand formerly enslaved people.

2. The Social Security Act of August 14, 1935 created old-age insurance funded by a payroll tax paid by workers and matched by employers. Its text states that a qualified individual "shall be entitled to receive" a monthly old-age benefit beginning at sixty-five. It also created unemployment insurance with the states and aid to dependent children.

3. The act's definition of employment excluded agricultural labor and domestic service in a private home. Those fields held a large share of all Black workers, above all in the South, and a large share of working women, who therefore stood outside the new insurance at its founding.

4. The National Labor Relations Act, the Wagner Act, signed July 5, 1935, guaranteed workers the right to organize unions of their own choosing and to bargain collectively. It created the National Labor Relations Board to run elections and hear charges, and defined firing a worker for union activity as an unfair labor practice.

5. The Revenue Act of 1935 raised the top income tax rate to seventy-five percent on incomes above five million dollars. It raised only part of the cost of the new programs and answered, in part, the charge that the New Deal had left great fortunes untouched.

6. The laws of 1933 had been emergency measures written in weeks to stop a collapse. The laws of 1935 were written as a settlement: standing duties administered by permanent agencies and funded by taxes written into law.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question: how 1935 moved beyond 1933, and who the settlement was built to protect. (Blueprint 11 and Blueprint 12)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1, 2, 5, and 6 support the program side: the WPA and Federal One, the Social Security entitlement and its payroll tax, the wealth tax, and the emergency to settlement contrast. (Blueprint 11) Bank item 4 supports the labor side: the Wagner Act guarantee, the board, and the unfair labor practice. (Blueprint 12) A full credit essay uses the labor evidence or explicitly accounts for its absence.
  • The founding exclusion named and placed correctly, as a boundary written into the definition of employment that left agricultural and domestic workers, falling hardest on Black workers and women, outside the insurance at the start. (Blueprint 11) An essay that describes the settlement as protection for all Americans without the boundary has answered half the question.
  • Explanation connecting evidence to the claim: permanence, entitlement, and enforcement, not a catalogue of agencies.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Second New Deal moved beyond the first by changing what the federal government was for. The laws of 1933 had been emergency measures, written in weeks to stop a collapse, and most of them were built to end when the emergency did. The laws of 1935 were a settlement. The Social Security Act made old-age support an entitlement, stating in its text that a qualified individual shall be entitled to receive a monthly benefit, and funding it with a payroll tax that workers and employers would go on paying in ordinary times. The Wagner Act gave the right to organize an enforcement agency, the National Labor Relations Board, in place of declarations employers could ignore. A full model continues in this pattern, adds the WPA, and closes by naming the boundary: the settlement protected industrial workers first, and left the farm and domestic workers outside its founding definition of employment.

Common weak answers. Program lists (the WPA built things, Social Security paid pensions) name what happened without the change in kind the question asks for. Protection without the boundary overstates the settlement and misses the lesson's second half. Boundary only, without the entitlement and enforcement evidence, turns a settlement with an exclusion into a simple failure, which the record does not support either.
