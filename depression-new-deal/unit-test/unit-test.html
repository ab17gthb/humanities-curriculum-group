Part 3: Sources (15 points)

Answer each question in complete sentences, citing the sources by letter.

Source A

President Franklin D. Roosevelt, first fireside chat on the banking crisis, March 12, 1933. From the transcript held by the Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum.

“First of all, let me state the simple fact that when you deposit money in a bank the bank does not put the money into a safe deposit vault. It invests your money in many different forms of credit, in bonds, in commercial paper, in mortgages and in many other kinds of loans. . . . What, then, happened during the last few days of February and the first few days of March? Because of undermined confidence on the part of the public, there was a general rush by a large portion of our population to turn bank deposits into currency or gold, a rush so great that the soundest banks could not get enough currency to meet the demand.”

A1. (2 points) According to Roosevelt, what caused the rush that even the soundest banks could not meet?

A2. (3 points) Why did explaining the banking system on the radio serve the rescue as no statute could on its own? Answer from the source and the lesson.

Source B

The Social Security Act, August 14, 1935, Title I, Section 1. From the United States Statutes at Large, Volume 49.

“For the purpose of enabling each State to furnish financial assistance, as far as practicable under the conditions in such State, to aged needy individuals, there is hereby authorized to be appropriated for each fiscal year a sum sufficient to carry out the purposes of this title.”

B1. (2 points) State in your own words what this section set the federal government to fund, and through whom the help would reach people.

B2. (3 points) The Social Security Act built three systems. Name the other two besides old-age assistance, and name one group of workers the Act left out at the start.

Source C

Dorothea Lange, photograph of Florence Owens Thompson and her children, Nipomo, California, March 1936. Farm Security Administration, Library of Congress.

Lange, a photographer working for the federal relief agencies, took this picture at a camp of migrant pea pickers and sent it to Washington. Newspapers printed it within days, and the government rushed food to the camp.

C1. (2 points) What conditions in the country produced the scene in this photograph? Name two.

C2. (3 points) This photograph was made by a photographer paid by the federal government. What does its existence, and the response to it, show about how the federal role had changed since the breadline years?

Part 3: Sources

A1. The rush was caused by undermined public confidence. Depositors across the country tried at the same time to turn deposits into currency or gold, a demand no bank, however sound, could meet from its cash on hand. (Blueprint 5)

A2. The statutes could close, examine, and license banks, but reopening would fail if depositors withdrew again at once. The chat rebuilt the confidence the system ran on by explaining in plain language where deposits go and why the reopened banks could be trusted. Deposits flowed back after the broadcast, which is the evidence the explanation did the work. (Blueprint 5)

B1. The section authorizes federal money so that each state can give financial assistance to aged people in need. The help would reach people through the states, under state plans, with the federal government paying. (Blueprint 11)

B2. Besides old-age assistance, the Act created old-age insurance paying retired workers from payroll taxes, and unemployment insurance run with the states. Either also earns credit: aid to dependent children. Left out at the start: agricultural workers and domestic workers, an exclusion that fell hardest on Black workers and on women. (Blueprint 11)

C1. Any two: mass unemployment that left migrant families without wages, farm collapse and low crop prices, drought and the Dust Bowl driving families off the land, and the seasonal poverty of migrant field labor. The family in the photograph were migrant pea pickers living in a camp. (Blueprint 2)

C2. Under Hoover the federal government had refused direct relief and left feeding people to charities and cities. By 1936 a federal agency employed the photographer, documented the need as official business, and answered publication with a federal food shipment. The government's role had changed from referee to provider, and documenting need had become part of governing. (Blueprint 2)
