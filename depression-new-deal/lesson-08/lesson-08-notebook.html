Teacher Answer Key

Part of the accountThe factsOne name, place, or date that proves it
Changed: the federal roleThe federal government became a permanent presence in economic life: deposits insured, securities markets regulated, old-age and unemployment insurance running, labor rights enforced by a federal board, and a national floor under wages and limit over hours from 1938.FDIC, 1933; SEC, 1934; Social Security Act, 1935; Fair Labor Standards Act, 1938.
Built: the physical recordDams on the Tennessee, Columbia, and Colorado rivers; bridges, schools, courthouses, and airports; paved roads; park trails and lodges; power lines into farm country. The Tennessee Valley Authority remade a whole river region. Much of it remains in daily use.TVA and Norris Dam, begun 1933; a traveler in 1940 used New Deal concrete all the way across the country.
Held: the New Deal coalitionWhite southerners, northern city voters, union members, farmers, and increasingly Black voters in the North held the Democratic Party in national power for a generation, through five straight presidential elections, because the party delivered something concrete to each group.Five straight presidential elections from 1932; the white South's room to run its own racial order counted as one of the deliveries, and as a price.
Undone: full recoveryUnemployment never fell below fourteen percent in any year of the 1930s, rose again in the recession of 1937 to 1938, and stood near seventeen percent in 1939. Full employment arrived only with the war spending of the 1940s.About 25 percent in 1933; about 14 percent in 1937; about 19 percent in 1938.
Undone: equal treatmentSocial Security excluded agricultural and domestic workers at the start, falling hardest on Black workers and women. The CCC ran segregated camps, relief was uneven under local control, federal housing maps redlined minority neighborhoods out of credit, and repatriation removed people of Mexican descent, including citizens.Redlining on HOLC security maps, 1937; segregated CCC camps; the Social Security exclusions of 1935.
The judgment, and its measureMeasured against the collapse it answered, the New Deal was relief and reform on an unmatched scale. Measured against full recovery and equal treatment, it stopped short, and war spending finished the Depression. A complete judgment names its measure.The two measures of the reading's closing section; war spending of the 1940s as the boundary fact.

The lesson in one sentence. Strong answers name the measure before the verdict. Example: Judged against the collapse of 1933, the New Deal permanently rebuilt the federal government's role and left a physical country still in use; judged against full recovery and equal treatment, it left unemployment above fourteen percent all decade and whole groups of Americans outside its protections.
