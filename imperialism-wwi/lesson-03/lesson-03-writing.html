Writing Task: Extend or Contradict?

The prompt. Did taking the Philippines extend American ideals or contradict them? Write an essay arguing one answer. Use evidence from at least two of the four evidence cards below, and answer the strongest argument for the side you did not choose.

The cards quote or summarize the lesson documents. Write the history in your own sentences and quote phrases, not whole cards.

Card 1: The Expansionist Case, 1898

Senator Albert J. Beveridge, in his speech The March of the Flag (1898): the rule that government rests on consent of the governed, he argued, applies only to those capable of self-government. "We govern the Indians without their consent, we govern our territories without their consent, we govern our children without their consent." The islands would give American farmers and factories new markets. Source: Albert J. Beveridge, March of the Flag, 1898, as banked in the Primary Source Project (Indianapolis pamphlet of 1898; Teaching American History text).

Card 2: The President's Promise, 1898

President William McKinley, Benevolent Assimilation proclamation, December 21, 1898: the United States came "not as invaders or conquerors, but as friends, to protect the natives in their homes, in their employments, and in their personal and religious rights." Its mission was "benevolent assimilation substituting the mild sway of justice and right for arbitrary rule." The same proclamation extended American military government over the whole archipelago. Source: William McKinley, proclamation of December 21, 1898, as printed in James D. Richardson, A Compilation of the Messages and Papers of the Presidents, vol. 10. The omissions are the proclamation's administrative orders on taxes, ports, and property.

Card 3: The League's Answer, 1899

American Anti-Imperialist League Platform, adopted at Chicago, October 1899: "We maintain that governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed. We insist that the subjugation of any people is "criminal aggression" and open disloyalty to the distinctive principles of our Government." The platform demanded that Congress announce to the Filipinos the independence for which they had fought. Source: American Anti-Imperialist League Platform, 1899, as banked in the Primary Source Project (Encyclopedia.com text).

Card 4: The War's Cost, 1899 to 1902

A summary, not a quotation. The Philippine Republic, inaugurated January 23, 1899, fought the United States rather than accept annexation, and after November 1899 the war became a guerrilla war fought with village burning, reconcentration camps in Batangas, and the water cure. The Office of the Historian gives more than 4,200 American dead and more than 20,000 Filipino combatants dead, and estimates as many as 200,000 Filipino civilian deaths from violence, famine, and disease, an estimate, not a count.

Useful sentence frames. The promise was ____________, the practice was ____________. A reader might argue ____________, but that cannot explain ____________.

Write the Essay

Write two to three paragraphs, about 300 to 400 words. Open with your claim, support it from two cards, and close by answering the counterargument.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the question with extend or contradict and ranks the evidence. An essay listing good and bad points without a verdict does not answer the prompt.
  • Accurate evidence from at least two cards. Extend: McKinley's promise of rights and just government (Card 2), Beveridge's duty and markets argument (Card 1), and, from the lesson, schools, courts, and the elected assembly of 1907 under civil government. Contradict: rule without consent (Card 3), a treaty signed with no Filipino heard, and the war's methods and estimated 200,000 Filipino civilian deaths (Card 4).
  • A real counterargument, answered rather than dismissed. The strongest extend essays concede the war's cost and argue the promise was the policy's intent; the strongest contradict essays concede the proclamation's language and show the gap between the words and the military government, the Insular Cases, and the casualty figures.
  • Accuracy discipline: no claim that Filipinos consented to the treaty (none signed it), no claim that the Senate vote was overwhelming (57 to 27, one vote above the required two thirds), no claim that the 200,000 figure is a counted total (it is an estimate, and some estimates run higher), and no treatment of the Philippines as empty or governmentless in 1899 (the republic was inaugurated on January 23, 1899).

Scoring guide. 4: verdict stated and ranked, two cards used accurately, counterargument answered. 3: verdict and accurate evidence, counterargument named but weakly answered. 2: evidence present but no verdict, or a verdict resting on one card. 1: summary of the annexation story without an argument.
