Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedThe strongest argument made at the timeExtend or contradict American ideals? Why?
The treaty and the Senate, 1898 to 1899The Treaty of Paris ceded the Philippines to the United States for 20 million dollars, with no Filipino heard. The Senate ratified on February 6, 1899, by 57 to 27, one vote more than required.Expansionists cited markets, a coaling station toward China, and a claimed duty to govern. Opponents answered that rule without consent broke the founding principle.Contradict, on the consent argument: a government founded on consent of the governed bought a people from their former ruler.
The republic and the proclamation, 1898 to 1899Aguinaldo proclaimed independence on June 12, 1898, and the Philippine Republic was inaugurated at Malolos on January 23, 1899. McKinley's proclamation of December 21, 1898, claimed the islands; Aguinaldo protested on January 5, 1899.McKinley promised friendship, protection of rights, and just government in place of arbitrary rule. Aguinaldo answered that he had never agreed to American sovereignty and had fought for independence.Both readings are defensible here. The proclamation's language extends the vocabulary of American ideals; its military government and threat of the strong arm contradict consent in practice.
The war, 1899 to 1902Fighting began February 4, 1899. The republic turned to guerrilla warfare. American methods included village burning, reconcentration camps in Batangas, and the water cure. Aguinaldo was captured in March 1901; the war was declared over on July 4, 1902.The administration held that order had to precede self-government. Critics, including soldiers' own letters home, described a war against a people fighting for the independence Americans claimed to defend.Contradict, at its strongest here: an estimated 200,000 Filipino civilian deaths, against 4,200 American and 20,000 Filipino combatant deaths, is the cost the annexation argument has to carry.
The courts and civil government, 1901 to 1907The Insular Cases made the Philippines an unincorporated territory where the Constitution applied only in part. Taft became civil governor in 1901, and an elected assembly met in 1907 under an American governor general.Officials presented schools, courts, and the assembly as preparation for self-government. Filipino leaders noted that they had already proclaimed a republic in 1899 and were given no date for independence.Mixed, and students should say so: institutions of self-government were built, inside a legal category that denied the islands full constitutional standing and kept final authority in American hands.
