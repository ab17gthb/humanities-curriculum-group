# Image Sources: Imperialism and World War I Lesson 8

All images are real public-domain images obtained from Wikimedia Commons or the Library of Congress. No image was generated or altered beyond resizing for print. No image in this lesson postdates its subject, so no date disclosure is required in the student captions; each caption still carries the image's own date.

## 1. big-four.jpg (in img/, used in lesson-08-activity.html)
- Title: Council of Four at the Paris Peace Conference (the "Big Four")
- Creator: Edward N. Jackson, United States Army Signal Corps
- Date: May 27, 1919
- Repository: Wikimedia Commons (file: Big four.jpg); original in the United States Army Signal Corps records, held through the Library of Congress
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Big_four.jpg
- Public-domain basis: Work of a United States federal government employee made in the course of duty in 1919; listed as Public domain on Wikimedia Commons.
- Caption identification, left to right: David Lloyd George (Britain), Vittorio Orlando (Italy), Georges Clemenceau (France), Woodrow Wilson (United States), as cataloged with the photograph.

## Quotation check
Every quotation below is reproduced from the named edition. Cuts are marked with ellipses where they occur.

- Woodrow Wilson, "Fourteen Points," January 8, 1918, Points I and XIV, quoted in the activity. Text from the TPSP audited records: progressive-era.json (source note: Miller Center edition of the address) and industrial-world-wars.json, record wilson-fourteen-points-1918 (source note: World War I Document Archive). Both records carry the same wording for Points I and XIV.
- Treaty of Versailles, Article 231, quoted in the activity and the quick check. Text from the TPSP audited record progressive-era.json, treaty-of-versailles-1919 (source note: Avalon Project, Yale Law School). Articles 231 to 232 in industrial-world-wars.json, record versailles-treaty-1919, carry the same Article 231 wording.
- Treaty of Versailles, Article 232, summarized and labeled as a summary in the activity; quoted nowhere. Same TPSP records as Article 231.
- Covenant of the League of Nations, Article 10, first sentence quoted in the activity and the quick check. Text from the TPSP audited record industrial-world-wars.json, league-covenant-1919 (source note: Avalon Project, Yale Law School).
- Lodge Reservation 2, quoted with cuts marked by ellipses in the activity. Text from the Congressional Record, 66th Congress, November 19, 1919, as printed for the Senate ratification resolution; wording cross-checked against the text printed in the United States Naval Institute Proceedings, December 1919 ("Diplomatic Notes"). The excerpt in the student document omits the em dash punctuation of the printed original where the ellipses fall, so that no em dash appears in the lesson.
- Senator William E. Borah, Senate speech against the League, November 19, 1919, one sentence quoted in the activity ("My objections to the League have not been met by the reservations"), from the Congressional Record speech as transcribed by American Rhetoric. The Lincoln sentence Borah quoted in the same speech ("Entertain no compromise; have none of it") is presented in the activity as Borah quoting Lincoln, which is how Borah presented it.
- Warren G. Harding, address before the Home Market Club, Boston, May 14, 1920, quoted in the reading (short form), the notebook key, the quick check key, and Writing Card 5 (full passage). Wording checked against the published text of the speech as reproduced by Encyclopaedia Britannica ("Return to Normalcy"). The short form in the reading takes the first two clauses of the passage and is punctuated as a complete quotation of those clauses only.
- All other source material in the lesson is labeled as a summary where it appears. Vote counts (November 19, 1919: 39 to 55 and 38 to 53; March 19, 1920: 49 to 35), the Chicago riot death count (38, of whom 23 Black), the strike count (more than 3,600 strikes, some four million workers), the January 2, 1920 arrest scale (several thousand), the Buford deportation count (249), and the 1920 election figures (about 60 percent of the popular vote, 404 electoral votes to 127) are stated at the precision given in the lesson reading and standard reference accounts.
