The pattern. The United States proposed the League and then refused to join it. In two or three sentences, give the cause you judge most important, and name what the country chose instead in 1920.

Teacher Answer Key

SubjectWhat happenedWho acted, and howResult
The peace termsWilson's Fourteen Points (January 8, 1918) proposed open agreements, freedom of the seas, and a general association of nations. At Paris the League Covenant entered the treaty, but freedom of the seas was dropped and colonial claims became mandates.Wilson negotiated in person with Lloyd George, Clemenceau, and Orlando. The Treaty of Versailles, signed June 28, 1919, charged Germany with responsibility for the war in Article 231 and required reparations under Article 232.Germany lost territory, its colonies, and most of its armed forces, and owed reparations in an amount a commission would set. The treaty went to the Senate for ratification.
The League fightThe Senate divided among Wilson's supporters, Lodge's reservationists, and Borah's irreconcilables. Reservation 2 denied any United States obligation under Article 10 unless Congress acted in each case.Lodge, as Foreign Relations chairman, wrote fourteen reservations. Wilson toured the country in September 1919, collapsed at Pueblo, and suffered a stroke on October 2. From his sickroom he ordered Democrats to vote against the reserved treaty.The treaty failed on November 19, 1919, in both forms, and again on March 19, 1920, at 49 to 35, short of two thirds. The United States never ratified it and never joined the League.
The year 1919More than 3,600 strikes involved some four million workers. The Red Summer brought race violence to Chicago, where 38 people died, and to Elaine, Arkansas. Bombings fed fear of revolution.Workers struck in steel, coal, and Seattle. Attorney General Palmer raided radical offices on November 7, 1919, arrested several thousand people on January 2, 1920, and deported 249 aliens on the Buford in December 1919.The Red Scare broke radical organizations and violated the rights of many who had no part in any plot. Disorder at home weakened Wilson's call for leadership abroad.
The election of 1920Republican Warren G. Harding promised a return to normalcy, in his May 1920 words, "not heroics, but healing; not nostrums, but normalcy."Harding campaigned quietly against Democrat James M. Cox, who defended the League. Voters chose Harding with about 60 percent of the popular vote.Harding won 404 electoral votes to 127. Normalcy, less reform at home and no League abroad, set the direction of the Twenties.

The pattern. Strong answers assign the refusal chiefly to the collision between Article 10 and the Senate: Lodge's Reservation 2 made membership conditional on Congress, Wilson treated that condition as the destruction of the League and ordered his party to kill the reserved treaty, and the irreconcilables voted no on every version, so no form of the treaty could reach two thirds. Wilson's stroke and the disorder of 1919 deepened the deadlock. In 1920 the country chose Harding and normalcy instead.
