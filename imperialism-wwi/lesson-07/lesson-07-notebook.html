Teacher Answer Key

Measure or changeWhat the government asked or promisedWhat it actually didWho gained and who lost
War Industries Board and Liberty BondsAsked industry to cooperate voluntarily and asked the public to lend money to equip the army.Directed production through federal priorities and price fixing. Bond drives raised roughly 17 billion dollars, about two thirds of the cost of the war, under strong social pressure to buy.The army gained equipment and finance. Buyers gained interest on a safe loan. Workers gained jobs, while households that did not buy faced public pressure.
Committee on Public InformationSaid it would educate the public with facts about the war.Ran a national persuasion campaign: pamphlets, news releases, films, posters, and about 75,000 Four Minute Men speaking in theaters.The government gained unified support for enlistment, bonds, and food saving. The public gained information and lost a neutral account of the war.
Espionage and Sedition ActsSaid the laws would protect the war effort against spies and obstruction.Produced more than two thousand prosecutions. Schenck was imprisoned for leaflets, Debs for a speech, and the Supreme Court allowed wartime speech to be punished under the clear and present danger test.Prosecutors gained a wide legal weapon. Dissenters, socialists, and labor organizers lost liberty, and German Americans faced suspicion, lost institutions, and in Robert Prager's case mob killing.
Women's war work and the Great MigrationAsked women and Black southerners to fill the jobs the war and the draft had emptied.About a million women entered war work, and more than 400,000 Black southerners moved north in three years, by Emmett J. Scott's federal count.Women gained wages and the Nineteenth Amendment in 1920. Black migrants gained wages, schools, and voting rights, and met crowded housing and the riot violence of 1919.

The pattern. Strong answers name the shared structure: in every row the federal government reached further into daily life than it ever had in peacetime, directing production, money, speech, and work, and in every row the results split. The same expansion of government power that equipped an army and opened jobs also prosecuted speech and fed suspicion, so gains and losses fell on different Americans at the same time.
