# Image Sources: Lesson 7 slide deck, The Home Front

Every file in this folder is a crop of a real public domain poster documented in lesson-07/img/SOURCES.md. No image was generated. Crops are centered portrait crops to the slide slot ratio (1046x1500); nothing is stretched.

## 1. flagg-port.jpg (title slide, Schenck slide)
- **Derived from:** lesson-07/img/flagg-i-want-you.jpg (see lesson SOURCES.md entry 3)
- **Title:** I Want You for U.S. Army
- **Creator:** James Montgomery Flagg (1877 to 1960), artist
- **Date:** 1917 poster; the drawing was first published as the cover of Leslie's Weekly, July 6, 1916, and the Army reused it as a recruiting poster in 1917
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, via Wikimedia Commons
- **Public domain basis:** United States Army recruiting poster published in 1917; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons tags the file Public domain.
- **Disclosure carried into the speaker notes:** Flagg drew Uncle Sam in 1916, before American entry into the war, and the Army reused the drawing in 1917.

## 2. christy-port.jpg (Section A divider)
- **Derived from:** lesson-07/img/christy-fight-or-buy-bonds.jpg (see lesson SOURCES.md entry 2)
- **Title:** Fight or Buy Bonds, Third Liberty Loan
- **Creator:** Howard Chandler Christy (1873 to 1952), artist
- **Date:** 1917
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (LCCN 2002708939), via Wikimedia Commons
- **Public domain basis:** Poster published in the United States in 1917 for a federal loan drive; copyright expired. Library of Congress lists no known restrictions; Wikimedia Commons tags the file Public domain.

## 3. hopps-port.jpg (Section B divider)
- **Derived from:** lesson-07/img/hopps-mad-brute.jpg (see lesson SOURCES.md entry 1)
- **Title:** Destroy This Mad Brute: Enlist, U.S. Army
- **Creator:** Harry R. Hopps, artist, for the United States Army
- **Date:** 1917
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, via Wikimedia Commons
- **Public domain basis:** United States government recruiting poster published in 1917; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons tags the file Public domain.
- **Disclosure carried into the speaker notes:** This is an Army recruiting poster, not a Committee on Public Information product. The lesson activity tests that distinction.
