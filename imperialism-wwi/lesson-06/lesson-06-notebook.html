The pattern. What single fact about this system best explains why the war cost the United States about 116,500 dead even though its army fought in France for only part of 1918? Answer in one or two sentences, using at least two rows as evidence.

Teacher Answer Key

StageHow it workedWhat it depended onWhat it cost
The draftThe Selective Service Act of May 1917 set up conscription run by local civilian boards. About 24 million men registered, about 2.8 million were drafted, and draftees trained in camps across the country.Registration by nearly every man of military age, local boards to classify men fairly, camps, equipment, and officers to train them.Men taken from farms, shops, and families; training at first with scarce equipment, including wooden rifles.
The crossingUnder the convoy system, troopships and supply ships sailed in groups under destroyer escort, zigzagging and running dark. About 2 million soldiers reached France by November 1918.Escort vessels, enough ships, set routes, and the draft and camps feeding trained men to the ports.Crowded bunks below deck on every crossing, and ships and men still lost, though losses fell sharply under convoy.
The frontPershing kept the American Expeditionary Forces an independent army. Doughboys met trench warfare, machine guns, artillery, and gas, then attacked at St. Mihiel (about 550,000 men) and in the Meuse-Argonne (about 1.2 million).The crossing for men and supplies, artillery and replacements, and roads behind the line to move both.An estimated 26,000 American dead in the Meuse-Argonne alone, and about 53,000 battle deaths in the war overall.
Disease and the endInfluenza moved through the camps in the spring of 1918 and struck hardest in the autumn, crossing in troopships. The armistice of November 11, 1918, stopped the fighting at 11 in the morning.Medical staff, hospital space, and quarantine in the camps, all strained at the height of the fighting.About 63,000 deaths from disease and other nonbattle causes, more than battle deaths, most from influenza and pneumonia. An estimated 675,000 Americans died of influenza in all.

The pattern. Strong answers name the concentration of men as the shared cause: the same system that gathered, housed, shipped, and supplied millions of men in crowded camps, trains, ships, and trenches also spread influenza through them, so disease killed more American soldiers than battle did (about 63,000 nonbattle deaths against about 53,000 battle deaths) even in a short American war.
