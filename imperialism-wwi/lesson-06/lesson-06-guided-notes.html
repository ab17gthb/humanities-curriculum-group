Guided Notes: Americans in the War

Lesson question: How did the United States mobilize, and what did its soldiers meet in France?

Unit question: Why did the United States seek power overseas, and how did the First World War change the country that fought it?

Key Terms

conscription: compulsory enrollment for military service, also called the draft

American Expeditionary Forces: the United States forces sent to fight in Europe under General John J. Pershing

convoy system: the grouping of troopships and supply ships to sail together under naval escort against submarines

trench warfare: fighting from lines of dug trenches facing each other across open ground called no man's land

armistice: an agreement to stop fighting, signed on November 11, 1918, that ended the fighting in the First World War

Doughboys: a nickname for American infantry soldiers in the First World War

influenza pandemic: the worldwide outbreak of influenza in 1918 and 1919 that killed millions of people, including an estimated 675,000 in the United States

Exit ticket, question 1: ships sailed in groups under naval escort, so a submarine faced many guns and one torpedo could sink only one ship. Question 2: Pershing refused to feed American soldiers into British and French units as replacements; he insisted the American Expeditionary Forces fight as an independent army under American officers in their own sector. Question 3: accept either the disease figures (about 63,000 nonbattle deaths, more than battle deaths, most from influenza and pneumonia) or the segregation record (most Black soldiers assigned to labor; the 369th Infantry fought under French command for about 191 days and was decorated by France), explained as part of the war itself rather than background to it.
