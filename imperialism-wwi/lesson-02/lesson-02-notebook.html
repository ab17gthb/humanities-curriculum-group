The pattern. The Teller Amendment and the Platt Amendment both claimed to protect Cuban independence. In one or two sentences, explain how the two documents pulled in opposite directions.

Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedEvidence for liberationEvidence for empire
The road to war: Cuba, the Maine, and the pressCuba rebelled against Spain in 1895. Spain's reconcentration policy forced civilians into towns, where disease and hunger killed large numbers. The Maine exploded in Havana on February 15, 1898, killing 266. The inquiry blamed a mine but fixed responsibility on no one.American opinion rallied to a real cause: ending the Spanish rule that had produced the reconcentration deaths, as in the Gillam cartoon's pairing of the Maine with the starving Cubans.The demand for war ran ahead of the evidence. Newspapers competing for readers converted an explosion of unknown responsibility into a case for war.
The promise: the Teller AmendmentIn April 1898 Congress declared Cuba free and independent, demanded Spain withdraw, and attached the Teller Amendment, promising not to annex Cuba and to leave its government to the Cuban people.The amendment is the strongest documentary evidence for liberation: a public promise, made before the fighting, that Cuba would not be taken.The promise covered Cuba only. It said nothing about Puerto Rico, Guam, or the Philippines, leaving the door open to taking Spain's other colonies.
The fighting: Manila Bay and CubaDewey destroyed the Spanish Pacific fleet at Manila Bay on May 1, 1898; no American sailor was killed by Spanish fire. In Cuba, troops took El Caney and the San Juan Heights on July 1 (the Rough Riders on Kettle Hill, with regulars including the 9th and 10th Cavalry). Santiago surrendered July 17.The fighting ended Spanish rule in Cuba in under four months.The first battle was fought in the Philippines, a colony unconnected to Cuban independence, and American forces ended the war holding Manila, Santiago, and Puerto Rico.
The settlement: the Treaty of Paris and the Platt AmendmentThe Treaty of Paris (December 10, 1898): Spain relinquished Cuba and ceded Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines (for twenty million dollars). Cuba became independent in 1902, after writing the Platt Amendment into its constitution; Guantanamo Bay was leased in 1903.Cuba was not annexed, and it did become a formally independent republic, as the Teller Amendment had promised.The treaty transferred an island empire to the United States, including the Philippines, whose leaders had declared a republic and did not consent. The Platt Amendment, giving Washington a right to intervene and to hold naval stations, made Cuba a protectorate in practice.

The pattern. The Teller Amendment protected Cuban independence by renouncing American sovereignty in advance. The Platt Amendment protected the same independence by giving the United States a right to intervene and to hold naval stations. The first limited American power; the second used it as the guarantee, limiting the freedom it guaranteed.
