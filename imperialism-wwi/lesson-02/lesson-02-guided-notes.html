Guided Notes: The Spanish-American War

Lesson question: Was the war with Spain fought to free Cuba, to take an empire, or both?

Unit question: Why did the United States seek power overseas, and how did the First World War change the country that fought it?

Key Terms

yellow journalism: sensational newspaper reporting that stretched or invented facts to sell papers and stir public feeling

USS Maine: the American battleship that exploded and sank in Havana harbor on February 15, 1898, killing 266 officers and men and becoming the rallying cause of the war

Teller Amendment: the 1898 congressional provision promising that the United States would not annex Cuba and would leave its government to the Cuban people

Rough Riders: the First United States Volunteer Cavalry, the regiment led by Theodore Roosevelt that won fame in the fighting around Santiago, Cuba

Treaty of Paris: the December 1898 treaty that ended the war and transferred Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines from Spain to the United States

cession: the formal transfer of territory from one country to another by treaty

Platt Amendment: the 1901 congressional provision, written into Cuba's constitution, that gave the United States the right to intervene in Cuba and to hold naval stations there

The Settlement at a Glance. Cuba: Spain relinquished all claim; Cuba was not ceded; independent in 1902 after a temporary American occupation, but a protectorate in practice under the Platt Amendment, with a United States naval station leased at Guantanamo Bay in 1903. Puerto Rico and Guam: ceded to the United States by Article II of the Treaty of Paris; held as American territory. The Philippines: ceded by Article III in return for twenty million dollars; held as an American possession although Filipino leaders had declared a republic and had not consented to the transfer.

Exit ticket 1. A cession is the formal transfer of territory from one country to another by treaty. A protectorate is a state that is formally independent but whose affairs a stronger power partly controls. Ceded: Puerto Rico, Guam, or the Philippines. Protectorate: Cuba under the Platt Amendment.

Exit ticket 2. The inquiry reported a mine as the cause and stated that no evidence fixed responsibility on any person. The yellow press treated Spanish guilt as settled because Hearst's Journal and Pulitzer's World were competing for readers, and dramatic, certain headlines sold papers. Accuracy about responsibility was not what the competition rewarded.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any position supported by one accurate fact, such as the Teller Amendment promise or Cuba's independence for liberation; the cessions of Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines for empire; one of each for both.
