# Image Sources: Imperialism and World War I Lesson 2, The Spanish-American War

All images are real public-domain works, downloaded into this folder (`lesson-02/img/`) and used in the lesson documents. No image was generated. One file is cropped from its archival scan, as noted in its entry; all other alteration is resizing for print by the PDF builder.

## 1. remember-the-maine-gillam.jpg

- **Title:** Remember the Maine! And Don't Forget the Starving Cubans!
- **Creator:** Victor Gillam
- **Date:** May 7, 1898 (published in *Judge* magazine)
- **Repository:** Wikimedia Commons; the record is also carried in the audited TPSP bank (record id `gillam-remember-the-maine-1898`, Progressive Era bank)
- **Source URL:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0c/Remember_the_Maine%21_And_Don%27t_Forget_the_Starving_Cubans%21_-_Victor_Gillam.jpg (file page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Remember_the_Maine!_And_Don%27t_Forget_the_Starving_Cubans!_-_Victor_Gillam.jpg)
- **Public-domain basis:** Published in the United States in 1898, before January 1, 1931; copyright expired. The color lithograph shows a mourning Uncle Sam, wearing a black armband, standing before an open album whose left page shows the Maine as it was and its wreck as it is, and whose right page shows starving Cubans drawn from photographs, under the printed slogan transcribed in the TPSP bank record: "REMEMBER THE MAINE! AND DON'T FORGET THE STARVING CUBANS!" (Note: the TPSP bank's one-line visual description of this cartoon is inaccurate; the description here follows the image itself.)
- **Used in:** lesson-02-activity.html (Station 1)

## 2. our-victorious-fleets.jpg

- **Title:** Our Victorious Fleets in Cuban Waters
- **Creator:** Currier and Ives (publisher)
- **Date:** 1898
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (item 2001700391); the record is also carried in the audited TPSP bank (record id `our-victorious-fleets-1898`, Progressive Era bank)
- **Source URL:** https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/pga/00800/00849v.jpg (item page: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001700391/)
- **Public-domain basis:** Published in the United States in 1898, before January 1, 1931; copyright expired. The color lithograph shows American warships, naming the battleship Iowa and the cruiser New Orleans, under a bright sky. The TPSP bank headnote records that the print is believed to be among the last the firm produced.
- **Used in:** lesson-02-quick-check.html

## 3. rough-riders-dinwiddie.jpg

- **Title:** Colonel Roosevelt and his Rough Riders at the top of the hill which they captured, Battle of San Juan
- **Creator:** William Dinwiddie (1867 to 1934), photographer
- **Date:** 1898. The photograph is a posed group portrait on the captured hilltop, not a picture taken during the July 1 charge. Secondary accounts of the full-frame photograph date it to about July 3, 1898, two days after the fighting on the San Juan Heights, and identify the men, left to right, as troopers of the 3rd Cavalry, the 1st Volunteer Cavalry (the Rough Riders, with Colonel Theodore Roosevelt at center), and the 10th Cavalry. This dating and identification are disclosed in the activity caption.
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, LCCN 96521936, via Wikimedia Commons (Commons imageinfo metadata: Artist "Dinwiddie, William, 1867-1934, photographer"; DateTimeOriginal 1898; LicenseShortName "Public domain"; LOC service file https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/cph/3g00000/3g07000/3g07900/3g07934v.jpg)
- **Source URL:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a3/Colonel_Roosevelt_and_his_Rough_Riders_at_the_top_of_the_hill_which_they_captured%2C_Battle_of_San_Juan%29_-_by_William_Dinwiddie_LCCN96521936.jpg
- **Public-domain basis:** Photograph taken in 1898 and published in the United States before January 1, 1931; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons and the Library of Congress both mark the image public domain.
- **File note:** The Library of Congress scan frames the photograph with an archival color-target border. The file used in the lesson is cropped from that scan to the photograph itself, including its original caption slip; the photograph's content is otherwise unaltered. The uncropped scan is the file at the Source URL above.
- **Used in:** lesson-02-activity.html (Station 5)
