Guided Notes: Open Doors and the Big Stick

Lesson question: How did the United States project power in Asia and Latin America without ruling formal colonies there?

Unit question: Why did the United States seek power overseas, and how did the First World War change the country that fought it?

Key Terms

Open Door: the 1899 and 1900 United States policy calling for equal trading rights for all nations in China and for China to remain one country

sphere of influence: a region in which one outside power holds dominant trading and investment privileges over the others

Boxer Rebellion: the 1900 uprising in China by the Boxers, a society opposed to foreign influence, put down by an eight-nation force that included United States troops

Roosevelt Corollary: Theodore Roosevelt's 1904 addition to the Monroe Doctrine claiming a United States right to intervene in Latin American countries guilty of chronic wrongdoing or unable to pay their debts

dollar diplomacy: President Taft's policy of using American loans and investment, backed by government pressure, to extend United States influence abroad

Panama Canal: the ship canal across the Isthmus of Panama, built by the United States between 1904 and 1914, linking the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans

Gentlemen's Agreement: the 1907 and 1908 understanding in which Japan limited the emigration of its laborers to the United States and the United States ended the segregation of Japanese students in San Francisco schools

F steps in Nicaragua: 1909, the United States backed the overthrow of President Jose Santos Zelaya. Then it arranged American bank loans and an American collector of customs. 1912, when Nicaraguans revolted, about 2,500 Marines and sailors landed to protect the government the loans supported, and a Marine guard stayed for years.

Exit ticket, question 1: China 1899, the diplomatic note. Hay asked the powers for promises of equal trade treatment, so American trade gained access without the United States governing any Chinese territory. Nicaragua after 1909, dollar diplomacy. American bank loans and an American customs collector gave the United States control over the revenue a government depended on, without annexing the country. Question 2: the United States may intervene and exercise an international police power, in practice taking over functions such as customs collection, in order to prevent a European power from intervening to collect the debts itself. Question 3: accept any instrument with an accurate cost, for example China and the indemnity of about 333 million dollars over 39 years, Colombia and the lost province of Panama, canal workers and about 5,600 deaths, Japanese families and blocked labor migration, or Dominican and Nicaraguan peoples and customs revenue collected by Americans.
