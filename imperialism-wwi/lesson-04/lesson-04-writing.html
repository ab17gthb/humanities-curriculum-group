Writing Task: Power Without Colonies?

The prompt. Write a short essay answering the lesson question: how did the United States project power in Asia and Latin America without ruling formal colonies there? Your essay must cover at least one instrument used in Asia and one used in Latin America, it must use at least three of the evidence cards below, and it must name what one of your instruments cost the people on the receiving end.

The cards summarize facts from the lesson reading and the instruments of power stations. Write the history in your own sentences. Do not copy card wording into the essay.

Card 1: Notes to the Powers, 1899

Secretary of State John Hay sent notes asking each power to promise equal treatment for the trade of all nations inside its sphere of influence in China, under the Chinese treaty tariff. The United States held no sphere of its own and took no Chinese territory to govern.

Card 2: The Bill for the Boxer Rebellion, 1901

An eight-nation force lifted the siege of the Beijing legations in August 1900. The Boxer Protocol then forced China to pay the powers an indemnity of about 333 million dollars over 39 years. Chinese Christians had died in the tens of thousands during the uprising.

Card 3: A Province and a Canal Zone, 1903 to 1914

United States warships kept Colombian troops from reaching Panama in November 1903, Panama declared independence, and its treaty gave the United States a canal zone ten miles wide. About 5,600 workers, most of them West Indian laborers, died during the American building years. Colombia received 25 million dollars in 1921.

Card 4: A Police Power, 1904 and 1905

Roosevelt's Annual Message of December 6, 1904, claimed that chronic wrongdoing could force the United States to exercise an international police power in the Western Hemisphere. In 1905 the United States took over the Dominican Republic's customhouses and paid foreign creditors a fixed share of the import taxes.

Card 5: Dollars, Then Marines, 1909 to 1912

Under dollar diplomacy the United States backed the overthrow of Nicaragua's president in 1909 and arranged American bank loans and an American collector of customs. When Nicaraguans revolted in 1912, about 2,500 Marines and sailors landed, and a Marine guard stayed for years.

Card 6: A Quiet Bargain with Japan, 1907 and 1908

San Francisco withdrew its order segregating Japanese students, and Japan agreed to stop issuing passports to laborers bound for the mainland United States. No law was passed and no treaty was signed. California barred Japanese immigrants from owning farmland in 1913.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the lesson question by naming a mechanism, for example controlling trade access, a route, or a government's revenue instead of governing territory. An answer that only lists events does not earn this point.
  • At least one Asian instrument and one Latin American instrument, accurately used. Cards 1, 2, and 6 carry Asia (notes and force in China, the bargain with Japan). Cards 3, 4, and 5 carry Latin America (the canal zone, the Corollary and customs control, loans in Nicaragua).
  • At least three cards used accurately, with the facts stated in the student's own sentences.
  • A cost named and assigned to the people who paid it: China and the 333 million dollar indemnity, Colombia and the lost province, canal workers and about 5,600 deaths, Japanese families and blocked migration, or Caribbean peoples and customs revenue and occupations.
  • Accurate use of the lesson vocabulary, with at least three of these used correctly: Open Door, sphere of influence, Boxer Rebellion, Roosevelt Corollary, dollar diplomacy, Panama Canal, Gentlemen's Agreement.
  • No invented evidence. Quotations, statistics, or events that appear in neither the reading, the stations, nor the cards cost the evidence point.

Model line of argument. The United States projected power by controlling access and income rather than territory. In Asia, Hay's notes claimed equal trade in China for American merchants while the United States governed no Chinese land, and the Gentlemen's Agreement settled a migration dispute by bargain. In Latin America, the United States held a canal zone across Panama, collected the Dominican Republic's customs under the Corollary, and placed Nicaragua's customs behind American loans. The costs fell outside the United States: an indemnity charged to China, a province lost by Colombia, and customs revenue and political choices taken from Caribbean peoples.
