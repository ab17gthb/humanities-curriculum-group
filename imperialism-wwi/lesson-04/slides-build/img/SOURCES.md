# Lesson 4 Image Sources: Open Doors and the Big Stick

All images are public domain. Files are stored in both `img/` (this record) and `../assets/` (the copies the HTML documents reference), following the Gilded Age lesson convention.

## putting-foot-down.jpg
- Title: Putting His Foot Down
- Creator: J. S. Pughe (John S. Pughe, 1870 to 1909), artist
- Date: 1899 (Puck, vol. 46; Library of Congress catalog date 1899)
- Repository: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (LCCN 2012647332)
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Putting_his_foot_down_-_J.S._Pughe._LCCN2012647332.jpg (file page; catalog: https://lccn.loc.gov/2012647332)
- Public-domain basis: Published in the United States in 1899; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons license tag: Public domain.
- What it actually is: A chromolithograph cartoon. Uncle Sam, holding a paper marked "Trade Treaty China," stands with one foot on a map of China while rulers of the European powers cut at the map with shears. Verified by viewing the file.
- Used in: lesson-04-activity (Station 1).

## pekin-castle-1900.jpg
- Title: The Allied Armies Launch a General Offensive on Pekin Castle
- Creator: Kasai Torajiro, artist
- Date: 1900
- Repository: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (LCCN 2002718902; digital ID ds 05466)
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Allied_Armies_launch_a_general_offensive_on_Pekin_Castle_LCCN2002718902.jpg
- Public-domain basis: Published in 1900; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons license tag: Public domain.
- What it actually is: A Japanese chromolithograph print of allied troops attacking a gate in Beijing during the Boxer Rebellion, not a photograph. American and Japanese flags are visible among the attacking troops. The printed captions in the lesson state that it is a print, not a photograph. Verified by viewing the file.
- Used in: lesson-04-activity (Station 2).

## gatun-locks-1913.jpg
- Title: Gatun Locks Looking Toward Atlantic Entrance of Canal
- Creator: Photographer for the Byron Company (New York, N.Y.)
- Date: About 1913
- Repository: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (digital ID cph.3c17347)
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gatun_locks_looking_toward_Atlantic_entrance_of_canal_3c17347v.jpg
- Public-domain basis: Published in the United States about 1913; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons license tag: Public domain.

- Used in: lesson-04-activity (Station 3).

## taft-policies-1909.jpg
- Title: Baby, Kiss Papa Good-By (Puck cover, vol. LXV, no. 1669)
- Creator: Samuel D. Ehrhart, artist; published by Keppler and Schwarzmann, Puck Building, New York
- Date: February 24, 1909
- Repository: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (digital ID cph.3g06425)
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Taft_Roosevelt_policies.jpg
- Public-domain basis: Published in the United States in 1909; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons license tag: Public domain.
- What it actually is: Outgoing President Theodore Roosevelt, dressed as a cowboy, hands a baby labeled "My Policies" to incoming President William Howard Taft, who wears a nursemaid's apron and bonnet. Roosevelt's secretary William Loeb, dressed as a bellboy, carries Roosevelt's "Big Stick" behind them. Verified by viewing the file.
- Disclosure: This cartoon documents the 1909 transfer of Roosevelt's policies to Taft. It is NOT a cartoon about dollar diplomacy, and it is not captioned as one. The activity's Station 5 source line states this plainly, the teacher key repeats it, and the station's questions use the cartoon only for the handoff and the big stick. A stand-alone period cartoon specifically about dollar diplomacy could not be verified against a named repository record during the build, so this verified alternative was used under the unit's fallback rule, with the policy itself carried by Taft's quoted words and the Nicaragua account.
- Used in: lesson-04-activity (Station 5).

## big-stick-caribbean.jpg
- Title: The Big Stick in the Caribbean Sea
- Creator: William Allen Rogers
- Date: 1904
- Repository: Library of Congress, via Wikimedia Commons
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tr-bigstick-cartoon.JPG
- Public-domain basis: Published in the United States in 1904; copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons categories include PD-old and CC-PD-Mark tags.
- What it actually is: Roosevelt, barefoot and carrying a large club, wades in a sea labeled "Caribbean Sea" towing a line of warships. Legible ship labels include "The Receiver," "The Sheriff," and "Debt Collector." The shores are labeled Santo Domingo, Cuba, Mexico, and Panama. Verified by viewing the file.
- Used in: lesson-04-quick-check.

## Text source notes (not images)
- Open Door note: John Hay to Andrew D. White, September 6, 1899, Papers Relating to the Foreign Relations of the United States, 1899, pp. 129 to 130. Quoted in the activity with one omission, the clause "and lend its cooperation in securing like assurances from the other interested powers," marked with an ellipsis and disclosed on the station's source line. Public domain (United States government document).
- Hay circular note, July 3, 1900: quoted passage in the activity Station 2 from Papers Relating to the Foreign Relations of the United States, 1900. The quoted sentences are continuous in the original. Public domain.
- Roosevelt Corollary: Theodore Roosevelt, Fourth Annual Message, December 6, 1904, text of the American Presidency Project. The activity quotes one continuous passage without cuts, and says so on its source line. Public domain.
- Taft on dollar diplomacy: William Howard Taft, Fourth Annual Message, December 3, 1912, text of the American Presidency Project: "The diplomacy of the present administration has sought to respond to modern ideas of commercial intercourse. This policy has been characterized as substituting dollars for bullets." Quoted as two continuous sentences in the activity Station 5. Public domain.
- Summaries labeled as summaries: the Boxer Rebellion account in Station 2, the Panama treaty terms in Station 3, and the Nicaragua account in Station 5 are paraphrase summaries of the standard record, labeled as summaries on their source lines. They are not presented as quotations.
