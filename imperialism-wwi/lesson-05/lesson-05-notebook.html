The pattern. Each link in the chain either raised the cost of staying out of the war or lowered the cost of going in. Choose two links and show, for each one, which of those two things it did.

Teacher Answer Key

LinkDateWhat happenedWhat it changed for the next decision
War in EuropeAugust 1914The alliance system turned the Sarajevo shooting into a general war: Austria-Hungary against Serbia, Russia mobilized, Germany against Russia and France through Belgium, Britain against Germany on August 4.Wilson proclaimed neutrality the same day Britain entered. The United States claimed full neutral rights to trade with all sides, which put it on a collision course with both the British blockade and the German submarine.
The LusitaniaMay 7, 1915The submarine U-20 sank the British liner off Ireland in eighteen minutes. Nearly 1,200 died, including 128 Americans. The ship carried munitions, and travelers had been warned.The submarine question became the central dispute between Washington and Berlin. Wilson chose notes over a break in relations, demanding disavowal, reparation, and prevention, so every later sinking was judged against promises already made.
The Sussex PledgeMay 4, 1916After the Sussex was torpedoed and Wilson threatened to break relations, Germany promised that merchant vessels would not be sunk without warning and without saving human lives, unless a ship tried to escape or resist. The note also expected the United States to press Britain, and reserved Germany's freedom if it did not.The pledge set the standard Germany would be judged by in 1917, and it bought a year of relative calm at sea. Its condition gave Germany a stated ground for taking the promise back.
Preparedness and the election1916The preparedness movement pressed for a larger army and navy. The National Defense Act of June 1916 provided for a larger army, a naval act funded three years of building, and Wilson won reelection under the slogan "He kept us out of war."The country entered 1917 with a president reelected as a peace candidate and with the legal start of a military buildup already made. Entry, if it came, would not begin from nothing, and Wilson had to explain a reversal to the voters who had just reelected him for keeping out.
Submarines unleashedFebruary 1, 1917German leaders resumed unrestricted submarine warfare, calculating that sinkings could starve Britain into peace within months, before an American army could be raised. They accepted American entry as a probable cost.The Sussex Pledge was dead, and with it the basis of Wilson's diplomacy since 1915. Wilson broke relations on February 3 and tried armed neutrality, his last alternative. American merchant ships sunk in March made the question immediate.
The Zimmermann TelegramPublished March 1, 1917British intelligence decoded Foreign Minister Zimmermann's January message: if the United States entered the war, Mexico was to be offered an alliance, financial support, and the recovery of Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona. Zimmermann admitted the message was genuine.The dispute stopped being only about ships and rights at sea. Germany had named American territory as a war aim, which united opinion in regions the submarine issue had left doubtful and weakened the case for staying out.
Revolution in RussiaMarch 1917Revolution forced Czar Nicholas II to abdicate, and a provisional government took power in Russia.The Allies lost their autocratic partner, so Wilson could present intervention as a war of democracies against empires. The change decided nothing at sea, but it supplied the language of the war message.
The war messageApril 2 to 6, 1917Wilson asked Congress to declare war, resting his case on the submarine campaign and declaring that "the world must be made safe for democracy." The Senate voted for war on April 4 and the House on April 6.Neutrality ended after thirty-two months. The United States entered as an associated power of the Allies, committed to raising and transporting the army Germany had gambled could not arrive in time.

The pattern. Strong answers sort the links by function. Raising the cost of staying out: the Lusitania (American dead and a public standard Germany had to meet), the resumption of unrestricted warfare (the pledge broken and American ships sunk), and the Zimmermann Telegram (American territory named as a prize). Lowering the cost of going in: preparedness (laws, money, and a start on a force already in place) and the Russian Revolution (a democratic argument the president could make without contradiction). The Sussex Pledge cuts the other way for a time: it lowered the cost of staying out by giving diplomacy a result, which is why its collapse in February 1917 closed the middle position.
