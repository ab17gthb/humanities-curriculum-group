Guided Notes: Neutrality Tested

Lesson question: Why did the United States enter the First World War in 1917 after three years of declared neutrality?

Unit question: Why did the United States seek power overseas, and how did the First World War change the country that fought it?

Key Terms

neutrality: a nation's policy of taking no side in a war between other countries

Lusitania: a British passenger liner sunk by a German submarine off Ireland on May 7, 1915, killing nearly 1,200 people, including 128 Americans

unrestricted submarine warfare: a naval policy of sinking ships in a declared war zone without warning, including neutral and passenger ships

Sussex Pledge: Germany's promise of May 4, 1916, after the Sussex attack, that its submarines would not sink merchant vessels without warning and without saving human lives

Zimmermann Telegram: a secret January 1917 message from Germany's foreign minister proposing that Mexico ally with Germany against the United States and recover Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona

contraband: goods, such as weapons and military supplies, that a belligerent claims the right to seize from neutral ships bound for its enemy

preparedness: the movement and program in the United States before 1917 to strengthen the army and navy in case the country was drawn into the war

Exit ticket, question 2: Germany's leaders recalculated. They judged that unrestricted sinkings could force Britain to make peace within months, before an American army could be raised and reach France, so the pledge's benefit, keeping the United States out, was worth less than the submarine campaign's promise of victory. Exit ticket, question 3: accept any link defended with two facts. Submarine warfare answers should cite the February 1 resumption, the March sinkings of American ships, and Wilson's stated grounds. Telegram answers should cite the territorial offer and the timing of publication on March 1. Preparedness answers should argue the country could only act in April 1917 because the 1916 acts had started the buildup, though this choice needs the student to explain why readiness made entry possible rather than caused it.
