Quick Check: The Last Links

Directions: Read the telegram extract and the background note, then answer the questions.

Background. The ship Lusitania, sunk by a German submarine on May 7, 1915, with 128 Americans among the nearly 1,200 dead, opened the argument over submarine warfare that ran for two years. The telegram below, published on March 1, 1917, closed it.

The telegram. Arthur Zimmermann, Zimmermann Telegram, 1917 (English translation, Ambassador Page's February 24, 1917 cable; National Archives), as carried in the TPSP source bank. Verbatim; cuts marked with ellipses.

"We intend to begin on the first of February unrestricted submarine warfare. ... In the event of this not succeeding, we make Mexico a proposal of alliance on the following basis: make war together, make peace together, generous financial support and an understanding on our part that Mexico is to reconquer the lost territory in Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona."

neutrality: a nation's policy of taking no side in a war between other countries

unrestricted submarine warfare: a naval policy of sinking ships in a declared war zone without warning, including neutral and passenger ships

1. From the telegram extract alone, name the two things Germany planned or proposed, and state the condition that had to be met before the second one took effect.

2. The Lusitania was sunk in May 1915, yet the United States did not declare war until April 1917. Name the German promise of May 1916 that helped hold off war, and state what that promise required of German submarines.

3. An American freighter in 1915 carried rifles and shells consigned to Britain. Under the rules stated in this lesson, what could a British or German warship each lawfully do about that cargo, and why did the British blockade, rather than German action, decide whose ships actually reached Britain?

4. Which brought the United States into the war: the resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare on February 1, 1917, or the publication of the Zimmermann Telegram on March 1? Choose one and defend it with a fact that the other choice cannot explain.

Teacher Answer Key

1. [Blueprint 7] Germany planned to begin unrestricted submarine warfare on February 1, 1917, and proposed an alliance with Mexico on the basis of war and peace made together, generous financial support, and Mexico's recovery of Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona. The condition: the Mexican proposal took effect only "in the event of this not succeeding," that is, only if the submarine campaign failed to keep the United States neutral and war with the United States became certain.

2. [Blueprint 6] The Sussex Pledge of May 4, 1916, given after the Sussex torpedoing and Wilson's threat to break diplomatic relations. It required that merchant vessels not be sunk without warning and without saving human lives, unless a ship attempted to escape or offered resistance. Full credit requires the pledge's name or date and the warning and saving lives requirement.

3. [Blueprint 6] The rifles and shells were contraband, goods a belligerent claimed the right to seize from neutral ships bound for its enemy, so a warship of either side could stop and search the freighter and seize the cargo. What neither could lawfully do was sink the ship without warning or leave its crew to drown, the line Germany's submarines crossed. The blockade decided actual trade because Britain's surface navy controlled the sea lanes and stopped shipping at scale, while the British blockade cut American trade with Germany to almost nothing; American goods and loans therefore flowed to the Allies.

4. [Blueprint 7] Accept either choice defended with a fact the other cannot explain. Submarine warfare: Wilson broke diplomatic relations on February 3, before the telegram was published, and his war message rested its case on sinkings, including three American merchant ships in March; the telegram announced no new German action at sea. Telegram: sinkings in 1915 and 1916 had been met with notes and pledges, while the telegram named American territory as a war prize and, once Zimmermann admitted it was genuine, removed doubt about German intentions toward the United States itself.

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 2 tags to blueprint entry 6 (the outbreak of war in Europe and American neutrality, 1914 to 1916) and questions 1 and 4 tag to entry 7 (the road to intervention). Question 3 tags to entry 6 through contraband, neutral rights, and the blockade.
