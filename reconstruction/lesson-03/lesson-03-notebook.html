Teacher Answer Key

StepWhat Congress didWhat it changed on the groundWhat it settled or left open
The committee hears the SouthCongress refused to seat the restored states' delegations and created the fifteen member Joint Committee on Reconstruction to take testimony on southern conditions.Nothing yet on the ground; the committee's hearings built the public record on which Congress would act.It settled that Congress, not the president, would set the terms of return. It left the terms themselves unwritten.
Section 1: citizenship definedCongress proposed an amendment whose first section made all persons born or naturalized in the United States citizens and bound the states to privileges or immunities, due process, and equal protection.Citizenship no longer depended on any state's law or on the Dred Scott ruling; the Black Codes' denials now stood against the Constitution's text.It settled who was a citizen. It left the courts to say how far the three limits would reach.
Sections 2 to 4: the machinerySection 2 priced disfranchisement in lost representation, Section 3 barred leading former Confederates from office, and Section 4 guaranteed the Union debt and voided Confederate debts.Tennessee ratified and returned; the other ten states refused, and the amendment hung unratified into 1867.It settled the amendment's terms. It left enforcement dependent on states that had just rejected it.
Memphis, New Orleans, and the electionsCongress investigated the 1866 attacks and carried the reports into the fall campaign; voters returned Republican majorities above two thirds in both houses.The attacks, stated plainly, killed 46 people in Memphis and more than 30 in New Orleans and showed freedpeople unprotected by local authority.It settled that Congress could act over Johnson's veto. It left the building of a protected electorate to new law.
The Reconstruction ActsCongress set aside the ten governments, divided them into five military districts, registered an electorate including Black men, and required new constitutions, ratification, and its own approval for return.Some 700,000 Black voters entered the rolls, conventions met across the South, and seven states were readmitted by the summer of 1868.It settled the governments' route back. It left the settlement resting on an electorate federal power had created and would have to keep protecting.
Stanton, impeachment, and acquittalCongress shielded its settlement with the Tenure of Office Act; when Johnson removed Stanton in defiance of it, the House impeached him and the Senate tried him.The Senate voted 35 to 19 on the broadest article, one vote short of conviction; Johnson finished his term weakened and the settlement stood.It settled that Congress's program would survive Johnson. It left the presidency's power against a hostile Congress tested but intact.

The lesson in one sentence. Strong answers join the constitutional text to the machinery under it: Congress defined citizenship in the Constitution, then, when the states refused the definition and violence showed freedpeople unprotected, it rebuilt the southern governments on a registered electorate that included Black men and defended that settlement through a presidential impeachment. Credit answers that name only the amendment or only the Acts when the sentence explains how the second step answered the failure of the first.
