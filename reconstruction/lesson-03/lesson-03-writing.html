Writing Task: Conqueror, Constitution Maker, or Both?

The prompt

Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did Congress rewrite the Constitution and the South's governments between 1866 and 1868, and at what cost? Take a position on how Congress acted. Did it act as a conqueror, as a constitution maker, or as both at once? A strong answer states Section 1 of the Fourteenth Amendment first from its own words, then follows the settlement through at least two of its tests, the states' refusal and the 1866 attacks, the military districts and registration, or the removal of Stanton and the trial, and judges what kind of power Congress was exercising in each. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim. The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Section 1 of the Fourteenth Amendment, proposed June 1866: all persons born or naturalized in the United States are citizens of the United States and of their state, and no state may abridge citizens' privileges or immunities, deprive any person of due process, or deny any person equal protection of the laws.

2. The states' answer, 1866 and 1867: Tennessee ratified the amendment and returned; the other ten former Confederate states refused, most by overwhelming votes, leaving the amendment unratified.

3. Memphis and New Orleans, 1866: attacks by white mobs and police killed 46 people in Memphis and more than 30 in New Orleans, with no protection from local courts or police; the elections of 1866 then gave Republicans more than two thirds of both houses.

4. The Reconstruction Acts, March 1867: the ten governments set aside as provisional, five military districts under Union generals, registration of an electorate including Black men, and new constitutions with Black male suffrage plus ratification of the Fourteenth Amendment as the price of return.

5. Registration and the vote, 1867 and 1868: some 700,000 Black voters entered the rolls, conventions wrote new constitutions across the South, Waud's The First Vote pictured the new electorate voting in 1867, and seven states were readmitted by the summer of 1868.

6. The removal and the trial, 1868: Johnson removed Secretary of War Stanton on February 21 in defiance of the Tenure of Office Act; the House impeached him, Article I charging the removal itself, and the Senate acquitted by one vote, 35 to 19, leaving the settlement in force.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question: how Congress rewrote the Constitution and the southern governments, and a position, conqueror, constitution maker, or both, on the power it exercised. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 supports the amendment's text and Section 1. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 2 supports the refusal that forced Congress beyond the amendment alone. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 3 supports the violence and the veto proof Congress of 1866. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 4 supports the Acts' military machinery and terms of return. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 5 supports the electorate the Acts created and the readmissions of 1868. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 6 supports the impeachment crisis and its one vote margin. (Blueprint 6)
  • A full credit essay must use item 1 or item 4; a verdict on Congress's power without the amendment's own words or the Acts' own terms is an opinion without its documents.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the position. An essay that lists the amendment, the districts, and the trial without judging what kind of power each step exercised has assembled the settlement's record without its argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that states the cost and the foundation together: the settlement stood in the summer of 1868 on an electorate the Acts had registered and protected, built at the cost of military government in ten states and a presidency weakened by a trial it survived by one vote.

Model opening for teacher reference. Congress acted as both, in sequence, and the order is the argument. In 1866 it behaved as a constitution maker, writing citizenship into the Constitution and offering the amendment to the states as the price of return. When ten states refused the offer and the attacks in Memphis and New Orleans showed what the 1865 governments would protect, Congress governed as a conqueror in form: governments set aside, generals in command of districts, an electorate registered under army supervision. Yet the conquest was run to a constitutional end, conventions, constitutions, ratification, readmission, and by the summer of 1868 seven states were back and the amendment was law. The power was military. The product was constitutional. A verdict that keeps only one of those sentences has read half the record.
