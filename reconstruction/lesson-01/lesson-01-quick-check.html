Quick Check: Fifty Dollars

Directions: Read the passage and the background, then answer the questions.

Sec. 2. Be it further enacted, That all freedmen, free negroes and mulattoes in this State, over the age of eighteen years, found on the second Monday in January, 1866, or thereafter, with no lawful employment or business, or found unlawfully assembling themselves together either in the day or night time, and all white persons so assembling with freedmen, free negroes or mulattoes, or usually associating with freedmen, free negroes or mulattoes on terms of equality, or living in adultery or fornication with a freedwoman, free negro, or mulatto, shall be deemed vagrants, and on conviction thereof, shall be fined in the sum of not exceeding, in the case of a freedman, free negro or mulatto, fifty dollars, and a white man two hundred dollars, and imprisoned at the discretion of the court, the free negro not exceeding ten days, and the white man not exceeding six months.

Source: Mississippi Legislature, 'Mississippi Vagrant Law,' 1865 (Laws of the State of Mississippi, 1866; University of Nebraska-Lincoln transcription). Quoted verbatim from the audited bank record, Section 2 of the law.

Background. Mississippi's legislature passed the Black Codes in late 1865, under a government restored by President Johnson's plan: pardons issued, a convention of the prewar white electorate performing the three acts, the state treated as restored without congressional terms or any requirement of Black suffrage or equal rights. The Freedmen's Bureau, created in March 1865, worked in the same state at the same time, supervising labor contracts and hearing freedpeople's complaints in its own courts.

1. In the passage, which freedpeople does the law deem vagrants? State the age line and the employment condition in the law's own words.

2. State the law's two fines, for a freedman and for a white man, and the two maximum jail terms, exactly as the passage gives them.

3. Using the background, give two facts: one about the government that passed this law and how it came to power, and one about the federal agency working in the same state. State what each fact shows about the settlement of 1865.

4. A defender argues that a vagrancy law is ordinary labor discipline that any state may enact. State the strongest answer to that argument from the passage itself, naming two features of the text an ordinary, race neutral vagrancy law would not contain.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Which freedpeople are deemed vagrants? All freedmen, free negroes and mulattoes in the state, over the age of eighteen years, found on the second Monday in January 1866 or thereafter with no lawful employment or business, together with those found unlawfully assembling. Credit requires the age line and the employment condition in the law's words. (Blueprint 2)

2. State the fines and jail terms. Fines not exceeding fifty dollars in the case of a freedman, free negro or mulatto, and two hundred dollars in the case of a white man. Imprisonment at the discretion of the court, the free negro not exceeding ten days and the white man not exceeding six months. (Blueprint 2)

3. Give two facts, one about the government and one about the Bureau. The government fact: Mississippi's legislature sat under Johnson's restoration, pardons issued, a convention of the prewar white electorate performing the three acts, the state treated as restored with no congressional terms and no requirement of Black suffrage or equal rights; it shows the Codes were not a rogue act but the restored government's own first work under the president's terms. (Blueprint 1) The Bureau fact: the Freedmen's Bureau, created in March 1865, was supervising labor contracts and hearing freedpeople's complaints in Bureau courts in the same state at the same time; it shows the settlement ran two systems at once, a state rebuilding labor control and a federal agency contesting it county by county. (Blueprint 2)

4. State the strongest answer from the passage, naming two features. The passage writes race into the offense and into the penalty. Section 2 deems vagrants by racial category, freedmen, free negroes and mulattoes, sets a different fine and a different maximum jail term by race, and reaches beyond unemployment to assembly and to white persons associating with freedpeople on terms of equality, conduct no labor discipline rationale explains. An ordinary vagrancy law defines an offense for everyone on the same terms; this text defines the offender by race and polices equality itself. A defense that addresses only unemployment and not the racial categories, the split penalties, or the equality clause earns partial credit. (Blueprint 2)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 test close reading of the statute, the skill Station 2 of the activity grades. Question 3 carries both of the lesson's blueprint entries, Johnson's restoration terms for Blueprint 1 and the Codes and the Bureau for Blueprint 2. Question 4 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires weighing a law's stated purpose against its printed categories.
