## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- Jourdon Anderson, the Edisto petitioners, and Mississippi's legislators: no portrait was downloaded for this lesson. Their sections in the deck use text and stat slides rather than a likeness, and this file records that choice honestly rather than filling the space.
- No image of violence, of any victim, or of the era's terror is used anywhere in the lesson, in a document, the deck, or the teacher notes.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- Mississippi Vagrant Law, 1865. Audited TPSP bank record mississippi-black-code-1865 in reconstruction.json. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 2, and the quick check (Section 2), with cuts marked with ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines. The bank record's sourceNote, named in those Source lines, is: Mississippi Legislature, 'Mississippi Vagrant Law,' 1865 (Laws of the State of Mississippi, 1866; University of Nebraska-Lincoln transcription).
- Petition of the Freedmen of Edisto Island, October 28, 1865. Audited TPSP bank record edisto-island-petition-1865. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 3, as the record carries it, including the record's own [...] cut marks, disclosed in the Source line. The bank record's sourceNote, named in the Source line, is: Henry Bram, Ishmael Moultrie, Yates Sampson (committee of freedmen), 'Petition of the Freedmen of Edisto Island,' 1865 (Critical Explorers; original spellings preserved; original spelling preserved).
- Jourdon Anderson, letter to Colonel P. H. Anderson, August 7, 1865. Audited TPSP bank record jourdon-anderson-letter-1865. Quoted verbatim in the activity, Station 4 (the wages and closing questions passages), with cuts as the record carries them and disclosed in the Source line. The bank record's sourceNote, named in the Source line, is: Jourdon Anderson, 'Letter to Colonel P. H. Anderson,' 1865 (Bill of Rights Institute; originally in the Cincinnati Commercial, 1865).
- General Orders No. 3, Galveston, June 19, 1865. Audited TPSP bank record general-orders-no-3-1865. Quoted verbatim and in full in the activity, Station 5. The bank record's sourceNote, named in the Source line, is: Gordon Granger, 'General Orders No. 3,' June 19, 1865 (Gilder Lehrman Institute; original held by the National Archives).
- Johnson's restoration terms, activity Station 1, are a labeled teaching summary, stated as a summary on the station's face and in its Source line, built from the public record of the Amnesty Proclamation of May 29, 1865, and the 1865 provisional governor proclamations. The builder's choice, as the skeleton permits: the summary route was taken rather than fetching and verifying the American Presidency Project transcription during this build, and the summary is labeled so no reader can mistake it for a quotation.
- The guided notes summary table, the notebook chart, and the writing evidence bank are teacher and student apparatus written for this lesson from the documents named, not quotations, and none is presented as a period text.
