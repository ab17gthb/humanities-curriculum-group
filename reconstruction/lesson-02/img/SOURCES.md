## Text editions used in this lesson, recorded here as the spec directs
- Thirteenth Amendment, in full: audited TPSP bank record thirteenth-amendment-1865 in ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/reconstruction.json. The record's sourceNote names United States Congress, 'Thirteenth Amendment,' 1865 (U.S. Constitution, Amendment XIII). Quoted verbatim from the bank text field in the activity, extracted programmatically during this build. Printed in full.
- Iowa's ratification resolution: audited TPSP bank record iowa-ratification-thirteenth-amendment-1866 in the same bank file. The record's sourceNote names Iowa General Assembly, 'Joint Resolution No. 1: Ratification of the Thirteenth Amendment,' 1866 (Acts and Joint Resolutions of the Eleventh General Assembly of Iowa, 1866). Quoted verbatim from the bank text field in the activity, extracted programmatically during this build. Printed in full.
- Civil Rights Act of 1866, Section 1: audited TPSP bank record civil-rights-act-1866 in the same bank file. The record's sourceNote names U.S. Congress, 'Civil Rights Act of 1866,' 1866 (Facing History & Ourselves; 14 Stat. 27-30). Quoted verbatim from the bank text field in the activity and the quick check, extracted programmatically during this build. The bank text ends the section with an ellipsis in brackets; that ellipsis is preserved in both printings and disclosed in their Source lines.
- The freedmen's conventions record (activity Station 4) is a labeled teaching summary built from the public record of the 1865 and 1866 state conventions of freedpeople. It is labeled a summary on its face, in its heading and its Source line.
- Johnson's Civil Rights veto message (activity Station 5) is a labeled teaching summary, and the choice is stated here as the unit plan directs: the builder attempted to fetch and verify the message from the American Presidency Project transcription named in the skeleton during this build. The Project pages reached were other veto messages, and the Johnson Civil Rights veto text itself was not verified from that transcription, so no wording from the message is quoted anywhere in this lesson. The station summarizes the message's arguments from the public record and is labeled a summary on its face, in its heading and its Source line. The summary's account of the arguments was cross-checked against two independent transcriptions of the March 27, 1866 message found in web search results (the House Divided project at Dickinson College and the Digital History project at the University of Houston), whose openings agree with each other; neither transcription is named as an edition in the student documents, because the station prints no quotation.

## Subjects for which no image is used, recorded honestly
- Andrew Johnson: no portrait is used in the student documents or the deck. The veto is taught through the labeled summary, and no verified likeness was needed to teach it; the deck's Section F carries Stevens and Trumbull, the statute's faction leader and its author.
- The ratification itself: no image of a statehouse vote or of the engrossed amendment is used, because no file with a public domain basis read during this build was verified for either subject. Ratification is taught in text, in the count, and in Iowa's resolution.
- No image of violence is used anywhere in this lesson, following the unit's image discipline.
