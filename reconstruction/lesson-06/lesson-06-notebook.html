Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedWhat it claimed, or what it was meant to do
Terror organized, 1866 to 1869Six former Confederate soldiers founded the Klan at Pulaski, Tennessee, in 1866. At Nashville in 1867 it was organized as the Invisible Empire, with dens and borrowed titles, under Forrest as first Grand Wizard. Forrest ordered it dissolved in January 1869.The organization claimed the forms of a government, an empire with officers, while hiding its members. The disguise let neighbors terrorize neighbors and deny it by day.
Night riding and its targetsDisguised mounted men rode after dark, called targets out by name, and whipped or shot them. Raids were timed to elections, school terms, and contract settlements, against Black voters, Republican organizers, teachers, and officeholders.The terror claimed that the new electorate existed on sufferance. Each visit was addressed to a whole community, pricing the vote, the school, and the contract in fear.
Colby's testimony, 1869 to 1872Klansmen whipped Georgia legislator Abram Colby for three hours on October 29, 1869, after he refused $5,000 to change sides and $2,500 to surrender his seat. In 1872 he told a congressional committee, naming a lawyer, a doctor, and farmers among his attackers.Colby's refusal, on the record, claimed that a freedman's vote and seat were not for sale at any price the county could name. His testimony made terror a federal question in a freedman's own words.
Forrest's answer, 1869 to 1871Forrest ordered the Klan dissolved in 1869, saying its violence had escaped his control, and in 1871 he denied to Congress that he had directed violence or known outrages as policy.The denials conceded the violence and disputed only its command. They claimed the organization its founder described, a protective association, against the organization the testimony described.
The Enforcement Acts, 1870 to 1871Congress passed three acts making conspiracy against constitutional rights and interference with voting federal crimes, placing federal supervisors at the polls and empowering federal marshals to arrest offenders. The Klan Act of April 1871 reached conduct under color of state law.The acts claimed the settlement would be enforced by the government that had written it, in federal court, past state officers who would not act.
The Klan Act at work, 1871 to 1872In October 1871 Grant proclaimed insurrection in nine South Carolina counties and suspended habeas corpus there. Marshals and troops made hundreds of arrests and prosecutors brought hundreds of indictments. By 1872 the first Klan in South Carolina was broken.The campaign claimed federal power, used whole, could break organized terror within a year. It also showed the answer's premise, a national will to sustain it, which the statute could not supply.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers carry the aim and the answer together. Example: The Klan's terror aimed at the Reconstruction electorate, using night riding against voters, organizers, teachers, and officeholders to destroy the Republican governments' base. Abram Colby, the Georgia legislator whipped in 1869 for refusing to sell his seat, carried the account to Congress in 1872. Breaking the first Klan took the Enforcement Acts used at full strength, and in South Carolina it took the proclamation of October 1871, the suspension of habeas corpus in nine counties, and hundreds of federal arrests and indictments, after which the Klan there was broken by 1872. The answer rested on a national will to sustain it.
