## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Joint Resolution proposing the Fifteenth Amendment, activity Station 1 and quick check: the audited TPSP bank record fifteenth-amendment-joint-resolution-1869 in reconstruction.json, whose sourceNote names Hamilton Fish's certification of ratification, via the National Park Service. Quoted verbatim from the bank record's text field, extracted programmatically during this build so no transcription drift is possible. The resolution is quoted in full at Station 1; the quick check quotes Article XV only.
- Revels's speech on the District of Columbia schools, activity Station 2 and quick check: the audited TPSP bank record revels-school-desegregation-speech-1871 in reconstruction.json, whose sourceNote names the Congressional Globe; via BlackFacts and BlackPast. Quoted verbatim from the bank record's text field, extracted programmatically. Station 2 joins three passages from the record and marks the cut with ellipses; the record's own terminal cut marker is preserved in the bank text and is not reprinted after the final passage quoted.
- Waud's "The First Vote," activity Station 3 and the reading figure: the audited TPSP bank record first-vote-waud-1867 in reconstruction.json, whose sourceNote names Harper's Weekly, November 1867, and presents the image by description. The bank's description is quoted verbatim at Station 3, and the print itself is reproduced from the verified Library of Congress file recorded as image 1 above.
- Nast's "Emancipation," activity Station 4: the audited TPSP bank record nast-emancipation-1865 in reconstruction.json, whose sourceNote names the Library of Congress and presents the image described. The bank's description is quoted verbatim at Station 4. The print is NOT reproduced in any document or in the deck: its scenes of enslavement include a whipping, and under this unit's image discipline that violence is taught in text, not as a picture. The choice is named on the station's face.
- The 1868 South Carolina convention and legislature, activity Station 5: a labeled teaching summary built from the public record, labeled a summary on its face in the activity, in this file, and in the teacher key. No period text is quoted at that station.
- The Fifteenth Amendment text in the reading and guided notes is paraphrased and stated, not quoted beyond its operative phrases, which follow the bank record's text of the joint resolution.

## Figures stated as counts

- Some seven hundred thousand Black voters enrolled in 1867, as stated in the unit skeleton for this lesson.
- South Carolina convention of 1868: 124 delegates, 76 of them Black, as stated in the public record summarized at activity Station 5.
- Revels's seating vote: 48 to 8, February 25, 1870, as carried in the standard congressional record summaries consulted during this build.
- Pinchback's tenure as acting governor: December 9, 1872, to January 13, 1873, five weeks, as stated in the Pinchback definition in the unit spec and carried verbatim in the guided notes.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- Thomas Nast's "Emancipation" is deliberately not reproduced (see above). No image of violence, of enslavement's punishments, or of any victim is used anywhere in this lesson, following the unit's image discipline.
- The South Carolina legislature composite (image 5) is cataloged on its file page with the date 1876, although the body it portrays was first elected in 1868. The lesson describes the print by its cataloged title and does not claim a sitting date for it.
- No separate verified public domain image of a Union League chapter meeting was located during this build; the League is carried in prose and in the deck's text slides rather than pictured with an unverified image.
- No portrait of Harriet Revels, Josephine Bruce, or other family members is used; the lesson's portraits are the three officeholders' Brady-Handy photographs and Waud's engraving.
