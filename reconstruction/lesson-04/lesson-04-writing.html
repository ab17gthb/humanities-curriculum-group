Writing Task: Settled in Text, Resting on Protection

The prompt. Write a developed essay of about 300 words answering the lesson question from this lesson's documents: who governed the reconstructed South, and what did the Fifteenth Amendment settle and leave open? Judge whether the settlement of 1870, the ratified amendment above a governing coalition, completed the work of securing the vote, or left that work resting on a federal protection the new governments could not supply themselves. Defend the judgment against the strongest rival reading in the record. Your essay must account for the amendment's exact wording and for Revels's argument on the Senate floor. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and The New Electorate Speaks activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording. Keep the paired records whole wherever you use them: the amendment's prohibition beside the grounds it left standing, and the coalition's roster beside the protection under it.

Evidence Bank

1. Registration under the Reconstruction Acts enrolled some seven hundred thousand Black voters by the fall of 1867, supervised by army officers, with Black registrants outnumbering white in several states. The Union League organized the new electorate in churches and schoolhouses, and the conventions of 1867 and 1868 wrote constitutions founding public schools and writing civil rights into state law.

2. The Reconstruction governments rested on a coalition of Black voters, northern newcomers its enemies called carpetbaggers, and white southern Republicans its enemies called scalawags. Black officeholders rose by the hundreds in the state legislatures, and South Carolina's convention of 1868 sat 76 Black delegates among its 124, its lower house the only Black majority in the South.

3. Hiram Rhodes Revels, a Mississippi minister who had recruited Black regiments and served as a Union chaplain, was elected by the state legislature to the United States Senate in 1870, the first Black member of Congress, in the seat Jefferson Davis had left. P. B. S. Pinchback governed Louisiana for five weeks in 1872 and 1873, and Blanche K. Bruce, elected in 1874, served the first full Senate term by a Black senator.

4. The Fifteenth Amendment, proposed in February 1869 and ratified in February 1870, forbids denying or abridging a citizen's right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, and gives Congress power to enforce the article by appropriate legislation. It grants the vote to no one outright.

5. In February 1871 Revels argued on the Senate floor that Black Washingtonians wanted the District of Columbia's separate colored schools abolished and all children placed on an equality in the common schools, and that prejudice, falling equally on the refined and the degraded whatever their conduct, had no cause to justify it and must be admitted wicked and wrong. His side lost the vote.

6. The new order had been created by federal law, the Reconstruction Acts' registration, the amendments' guarantees, and the army's presence behind both, and it depended on federal protection its governments could not supply themselves. Within a generation, states used literacy tests and poll taxes, written without a racial word, to close the polls the amendment had opened.

Scaffold

Build the essay in four moves. Open with your judgment in one sentence, naming what the settlement settled and what it rested on. Then give the settlement at full height, the electorate, the governments, and the amendment's text, before you qualify it. Then turn to the opening: the grounds the text left standing, or the protection the governments could not supply, and show one document working inside that opening. Close by answering the strongest rival reading and stating what the survival of the vote depended on.

Sentence starters you may use: "The settlement of 1870 settled ___ in the Constitution's text, and left ___ to be decided by ___." "The strongest evidence for completion is ___, because ___." "The opening the text left was ___, and ___ shows it at work." "Against the reading that ___, the record shows ___."

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A judgment choosing one reading, completion or protection dependent, stated directly and held through the essay. Either reading can earn full credit if the amendment's wording and Revels's argument are carried and the rival reading is answered. An essay that lists the era's firsts in order without judging what secured them has summarized the lesson rather than answered it. (Blueprint 7, Blueprint 8)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, drawn from at least two different parts of the record, the electorate and its conventions, the coalition and its roster, the amendment's text, or Revels's speech. An essay built on the roster of firsts alone cannot judge what the roster rested on. (Blueprint 8)
  • The Fifteenth Amendment's wording stated exactly wherever the verdict leans toward completion: a prohibition on denial on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, with an enforcement clause, which grants the vote to no one outright. A completion essay that inflates the text into a grant of suffrage has misread its central document. (Blueprint 7)
  • Revels's argument accounted for, not decorated with: prejudice assigned no justifying cause, the admissions of wicked and wrong drawn from it, and the lost vote on the schools bill carried as part of the record, since the speech is both the settlement's highest statement and an early measure of its enforcement. (Blueprint 8)
  • The strongest rival reading stated at full strength and answered. Against a protection verdict: by February 1870 a Black electorate was enrolled, governing, and sending senators to Washington under a ratified amendment, a settlement in text and in fact, not a promise. Against a completion verdict: a text that leaves every nonracial ground of denial standing, above governments that cannot protect their own voters, has settled the wording of the question and left its answer to whoever keeps the power to enforce it. (Blueprint 7, Blueprint 8)
  • The settlement's base assigned at the close: the registration the Reconstruction Acts ordered, the army's presence behind it, and Congress's will to use the enforcement power, the supports whose withdrawal the rest of this unit records. (Blueprint 8)

Model opening for teacher reference. The settlement of 1870 was complete in the Constitution's text and incomplete in everything underneath the text. Above it stood a ratified amendment forbidding racial denial of the vote and a senator, elected by a legislature the new electorate had made, arguing on the Senate floor that prejudice had no cause to justify it. Beneath it stood governments created by an army supervised registration, unable to protect their own voters with their own power, and a constitutional text that had forbidden three grounds of denial and left the rest for any legislature patient enough to write a test without a racial word. Revels lost the schools vote in the same chamber that had seated him. That pairing, a seat won and an argument lost, is the settlement's measure.

Common weak answers. A completion essay built on bank 3 alone cannot explain why the amendment's own wording mattered, or why Revels's side lost in 1871. A protection essay built on bank 6 alone cannot explain why the settlement outlasted its enforcement in the Constitution's text, where the amendment still stands.
