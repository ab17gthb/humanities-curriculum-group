Guided Notes: The New Political Order

Lesson question: Who governed the reconstructed South, and what did the Fifteenth Amendment settle and leave open?

Unit question: What did Reconstruction settle about freedom, citizenship, and the federal Union, and what did it leave open?

Key Terms

Fifteenth Amendment: the amendment Congress proposed in February 1869 and the states ratified in February 1870, which forbade the United States and the states to deny or abridge a citizen's right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, the third and last of the Reconstruction amendments

Hiram Rhodes Revels: the Mississippi minister, freeborn in North Carolina, who had recruited Black regiments and served as a Union army chaplain, elected to the United States Senate in 1870 as the first Black member of Congress, seated in the chamber Jefferson Davis had left, where he argued for desegregating the District of Columbia schools

Blanche K. Bruce: the Mississippi officeholder born enslaved in Virginia, who rose through tax and education offices to election to the United States Senate in 1874, the second Black senator and the first to serve a full term, seated while the settlement that elected him was already being dismantled

P. B. S. Pinchback: the Louisiana leader, freeborn in Georgia and a Union officer in the war, who rose through the state senate to serve as acting governor of Louisiana for five weeks in the winter of 1872 and 1873, the first Black man to govern an American state

carpetbaggers: the hostile name white southern Democrats gave northern men who came South during Reconstruction, teachers, ministers, merchants, investors, Union veterans, and office seekers among them, pictured in Democratic print as adventurers carrying everything they owned in a carpetbag

scalawags: the hostile name white southern Democrats gave white southerners who joined the Republican Party, upcountry Unionists, former Whigs, and poor farmers among them, whose votes, alongside Black voters and northern newcomers, built the Reconstruction governments

Union League: the organization of patriotic clubs, spread through the South from 1867 under the Loyal League name, which organized Black voters and white Unionists into the Republican Party, meeting in churches and schoolhouses to register voters, teach the new politics, and turn out the electorate the Reconstruction Acts created

E. Pinchback: P. B. S. Pinchback rose through Louisiana's state senate to its presidency, became lieutenant governor when that office fell vacant, and in December 1872 served as acting governor for five weeks, the first Black governor of an American state. Bruce: Blanche K. Bruce, born enslaved in Virginia, rose through tax and education offices to the Senate in 1874, the second Black senator and the first to serve a full term.

F. The two sections: Section 1 forbids the United States and the states to deny or abridge a citizen's right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, and Section 2 gives Congress power to enforce the article. Left open, and the split: the amendment grants the vote to no one and forbids only one ground of denial; southern states later used literacy tests and poll taxes, written without a racial word, to close the polls. It also split the old alliance of abolition and woman suffrage.

G. The achievement and its base: by 1870 the reconstructed South was running a democracy broader than the country had ever attempted, with Black men voting, holding office, writing constitutions, and sitting in the Senate, all of it created by federal law and the army's presence behind it. The open question: whether Congress and the North would keep enforcing it.

The New Order at a Glance. An electorate and its charters, 1867 to 1868: army supervised registration enrolled some seven hundred thousand Black voters, the League organized them, and the conventions founded schools and wrote civil rights into law. The coalition and its names: Black voters, newcomers called carpetbaggers, and white southern Republicans called scalawags governed together. The roster at the top, 1870 to 1874: Revels took Davis's Senate seat in 1870, Pinchback governed Louisiana for five weeks in 1872 and 1873, and Bruce won a full Senate term in 1874. The amendment and its limits, 1869 to 1870: the amendment forbade denying the vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude and armed Congress to enforce it, while leaving every other ground open.

Exit ticket 1. The Union League is the organization of patriotic clubs, spread through the South from 1867 under the Loyal League name, which organized Black voters and white Unionists into the Republican Party, meeting in churches and schoolhouses to register voters, teach the new politics, and turn out the electorate the Reconstruction Acts created. The Fifteenth Amendment is the amendment Congress proposed in February 1869 and the states ratified in February 1870, which forbade the United States and the states to deny or abridge a citizen's right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude. The ratification year was 1870.

Exit ticket 2. The reconstructed South was governed by Black voters, the largest bloc in most states, with northern newcomers its enemies called carpetbaggers and white southern Republicans its enemies called scalawags, a coalition that sent Revels and Bruce to the Senate.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer carrying all three. Example: Black voters and officeholders governed with northern newcomers and white southern Republicans. The amendment settled that the vote could not be denied on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, and armed Congress to enforce it. It left every other ground open, and rested on federal protection.
