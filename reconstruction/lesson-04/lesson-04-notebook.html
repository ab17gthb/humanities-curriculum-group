Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedWhat it claimed about the vote and citizenship
An electorate made, 1867Registration boards opened across the ten states set aside by the Reconstruction Acts, supervised by army officers, and by the fall some seven hundred thousand Black voters stood enrolled where none had voted before, outnumbering white registrants in several states.The enrollment claimed that Black men were voters by right of the new settlement, not by favor. A name on the roll was citizenship performed, and the electorate it made would write constitutions and staff governments.
Leagues and conventions, 1867 to 1868The Union League spread through churches and schoolhouses under the Loyal League name, registering voters and teaching the new politics. The conventions of 1867 and 1868, with Black delegates in every one and a majority in South Carolina's, founded public school systems and wrote civil rights into state law.The conventions claimed the new electorate could also be a constitution making people. The charters they wrote were the most democratic the South had known, and Black delegates sat in them as authors, not as petitioners.
The coalition governsThe governments rested on Black voters, the largest bloc in most states, northern newcomers its enemies called carpetbaggers, and white southern Republicans its enemies called scalawags. Black officeholders rose by the hundreds in the state legislatures and filled county and school offices.The coalition's existence claimed that governing was not a white office by nature. The hostile names are the counterclaim preserved in the record, an attempt to make the coalition's members illegitimate by labeling their origins.
The roster at the top, 1870 to 1874Mississippi's legislature sent Hiram Rhodes Revels to the Senate in 1870, the first Black member of Congress, in the seat Jefferson Davis had left. Pinchback governed Louisiana for five weeks in 1872 and 1873. Blanche K. Bruce, elected in 1874, served the first full Senate term by a Black senator.The roster claimed citizenship at full height: a senator arguing on the floor, a governor in a statehouse, a full term served. Revels's 1871 speech for equal schools in the District of Columbia is the claim stated as an argument, that prejudice has no justifying cause.
The amendment, 1869 to 1870Congress proposed the Fifteenth Amendment in February 1869 and the states ratified it in February 1870. Section 1 forbids denying or abridging the vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, and Section 2 arms Congress to enforce it.The amendment settled the racial ground of denial in constitutional text and left every other ground standing. Literacy tests and poll taxes, written without a racial word, were later built on the ground it left open, and the whole order rested on federal protection.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers carry the government and the amendment together. Example: The reconstructed South was governed by a coalition of Black voters and officeholders, northern newcomers called carpetbaggers, and white southern Republicans called scalawags, under constitutions that founded public schools and wrote civil rights into state law. Senator Hiram Rhodes Revels argued on the Senate floor in 1871 that the District of Columbia's schools should place all children on an equality. The Fifteenth Amendment settled that the vote could not be denied on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, and gave Congress power to enforce the rule. It left standing every denial written on other grounds, the opening through which literacy tests and poll taxes later closed the polls.
