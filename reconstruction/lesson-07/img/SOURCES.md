## Images deliberately not reproduced
- Thomas Nast, "Worse Than Slavery," Harper's Weekly, October 24, 1874. The bank record presents this cartoon by description, and the activity's Station 5 teaches it from that description, with the bank's sourceNote named in the station's Source line. The print is not reproduced in any document or in the deck: it pictures a man hanging from a tree and a schoolhouse burning, and this unit teaches the counterrevolution's violence in testimony and text, not as a picture on a classroom page. That choice is stated on the station's face and in the deck's speaker notes for slide 20.
- Thomas Nast, "Colored Rule in a Reconstructed (?) State," Harper's Weekly, March 14, 1874. Taught in the reading (section F) and in the deck (slide 21) from the bank record's description and transcribed captions only, as evidence of the image war and of its maker's limits. The drawing carries the era's racist caricature and is never reproduced and never used as decoration anywhere in this lesson, following the unit spec.
- The White League, the Klan, Coushatta, and Liberty Place: no photograph or print of any of them, of their members, or of their victims is used anywhere in this lesson, in a document, the deck, or the teacher notes. Their sections use text and stat slides.
