# Reconstruction: Unit Manifest

**Essential question:** What did Reconstruction settle about freedom, citizenship, and the federal Union, and what did it leave open?

**Course position:** The sixth unit of the US History course (courseId u6), built fifteenth and last in the build order, and the closing unit of the course's early survey, which ends at the 1877 split the planner's two part course shapes are built on. It opens in April 1865's aftermath with presidential Reconstruction, the Freedmen's Bureau, and the Black Codes, and closes with the Compromise of 1877 and the withdrawal of the last federal troops. The Civil War is prior knowledge here, taught in the Sectional Crisis and Civil War unit and named in prose only. The Thirteenth Amendment's passage by Congress on January 31, 1865, is likewise prior knowledge, a wartime event the Sectional unit named; its ratification and the settlement built on it are this unit's ground, and this unit's Thirteenth Amendment definition is carried character for character from the Sectional unit's Lesson 8 definition, exactly as that build directed, verified by script against the Sectional build spec and confirmed unaltered in the guided notes, the deck notes, and the study guide. The Gilded Age boundary holds: no industrialization, big business, or labor movement content is taught, and the story stops at 1877, with the settlement's long shadow named in framing prose only and no twentieth century content anywhere. One image discipline governs the whole unit and is stated in the skeleton's frame: the counterrevolution's violence is taught in testimony and text, stated plainly and never lingered on, and no image of violence, of the Klan's work, or of any victim is reproduced in any student document, deck, or teacher notes document; the era's cartoons, which the bank presents described, are taught from the bank's descriptions, and the two prints whose imagery is itself violence, Nast's "Worse Than Slavery" and Frost's 1876 cartoon, are described and never reproduced.

**Built:** 2026-10-22. 51 documents, 247 printed pages. 8 slide decks, 222 slides, 31,435 words of speaker notes, 8 printable teacher notes documents (75 pages).

**Copyright accounting:** No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this unit. Every quotation is a public domain period text quoted from a named edition: the audited bank records, whose sourceNotes name the transcriptions the bank audited (the Constitution itself, the Laws of Mississippi, the Congressional Record and Globe, the enrolled acts, the Joint Select Committee report, and the named modern transcriptions of period texts the bank carries), and the Avalon Project transcription of the Articles of Impeachment in Lesson 3.
