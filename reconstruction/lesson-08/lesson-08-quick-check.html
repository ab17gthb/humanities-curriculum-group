Quick Check: Settled in Text

Directions: Read the two documents and the background, then answer the questions.

Document A. "Section 1. Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States, or any place subject to their jurisdiction. Section 2. Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation."

Source: United States Congress, Thirteenth Amendment, 1865 (United States Congress, 'Thirteenth Amendment,' 1865 (U.S. Constitution, Amendment XIII).), as carried in the audited TPSP record.

Document B. "A full-page illustration by A. B. Frost in Harper's Weekly, October 21, 1876, titled 'Of Course He Wants to Vote the Democratic Ticket!' Two white men hold guns to a Black man's head at a Southern polling place. The print attributes the spoken dialogue to a 'Democratic "Reformer"': 'You're as free as air, ain't you? Say you are, or I'll blow yer black head off!'"

Source: A. B. Frost, 'Of Course He Wants to Vote the Democratic Ticket,' 1876 (A. B. Frost, 'Of Course He Wants to Vote the Democratic Ticket,' 1876 (Harper's Weekly, October 21, 1876; image described).), as carried in the audited TPSP record. The bank presents the print by description, with its caption transcribed.

Background. Tilden won the popular vote of 1876 by some quarter of a million votes and stood one electoral vote short. Twenty electoral votes were disputed. The Electoral Commission awarded every disputed vote to Hayes by eight to seven, for a count of 185 to 184. In the bargain that followed, the last federal troops were promised out of South Carolina and Louisiana, and in April 1877 Hayes ordered them withdrawn.

1. In Document A, state what Section 1 abolishes, and state the one exception the section allows.

2. In Document A, state what Section 2 adds to the amendment, and state why an enforcement section belongs in the settled column of the audit.

3. In Document B, state what the voter is ordered to say, and state the threat that stands behind the order.

4. Set the two documents against each other. State what Document A had settled by 1865, and state what Document B shows still open in the fall of 1876.

Teacher Answer Key

1. State the abolition, and the exception. Section 1 abolishes slavery and involuntary servitude within the United States and every place subject to its jurisdiction. The one exception is punishment for a crime of which the party has been duly convicted. (Blueprint 15)

2. State what Section 2 adds, and its place in the audit. Section 2 gives Congress power to enforce the article by appropriate legislation. It belongs in the settled column because the enforcement power stands in the constitutional text itself, beyond repeal by ordinary law, whatever later Congresses chose to do with it. (Blueprint 15)

3. State the order, and the threat. The voter is ordered to say that he wants to vote the Democratic ticket, and to say he is free: the line the print gives its Democratic "Reformer" is "You're as free as air, ain't you? Say you are, or I'll blow yer black head off!" The threat is death at gunpoint at the polling place. (Blueprint 15)

4. State the settled item, and the open item. Settled by 1865: slavery and involuntary servitude stood abolished in the Constitution itself, with Congress armed to enforce the abolition. Still open in the fall of 1876: the freedom the amendment promised at the point of use, a Black citizen's vote cast without a pistol at his head, eleven years after ratification and six years after the Fifteenth Amendment. (Blueprint 15)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 to 3 test close reading of the lesson's closing documents. Question 4 tests the lesson's audit itself, the settled text of 1865 read against the open polling place of 1876.
