Guided Notes: 1876 and the End

Lesson question: What did Reconstruction settle, and what did its ending leave open?

Unit question: What did Reconstruction settle about freedom, citizenship, and the federal Union, and what did it leave open?

Key Terms

Compromise of 1877: the informal bargain that settled the disputed election of 1876, by which Democratic leaders accepted the Republican Rutherford B. Hayes as president in return for a cabinet seat for a southerner, support for southern internal improvements, and the withdrawal of the last federal troops protecting the Republican governments of South Carolina and Louisiana, ending federal Reconstruction

Rutherford B. Hayes: the Ohio Republican, Civil War general and three time governor, who lost the popular vote in 1876, won the presidency through the Electoral Commission's rulings and the Compromise of 1877, and in April 1877 ordered the last federal troops from the statehouses at Columbia and New Orleans

Samuel J. Tilden: the New York Democratic governor and corruption fighter who won the election of 1876's popular vote by some quarter of a million votes and claimed the presidency on the disputed returns, whose party in the end accepted the bargain that seated Hayes rather than risk a contested inauguration

Electoral Commission: the fifteen member body Congress created in January 1877, five senators, five representatives, and five Supreme Court justices, which awarded every disputed electoral vote of 1876 to Hayes by votes of eight to seven, giving him the presidency by a single electoral vote, 185 to 184

Redeemers: the name the South's white Democratic leaders gave themselves as they retook the southern state governments across the 1870s, presenting their return as the rescue of the region from misrule and governing afterward for low taxes, cheap government, retrenched schools, and white supremacy

home rule: the Redeemers' slogan for ending federal supervision of southern governments and elections, the restoration of the South to the rule of its white Democratic majorities, the condition the Compromise of 1877 delivered and the phrase under which Redemption governed

Redemption: the name white southern Democrats gave their overthrow of the Reconstruction governments and their own return to power, accomplished state by state across the 1870s by violence, fraud, economic pressure, and northern weariness, and completed when the last federal troops withdrew in the spring of 1877

Teacher Answer Key

For the teacher

Teach this lesson last in the Reconstruction unit, after Lesson 7 on the retreat of 1872 to 1876, and before the review day and the unit test. It is also the closing lesson of the course's early survey, and it stops at 1877 on purpose. The amendments' later enforcement history and the twentieth century are named in framing only, in the reading's final section, and are not taught here. It pairs with the lesson activity, The Settlement Audited, whose five stations return to the Thirteenth Amendment, the Fourteenth Amendment's first section, the joint resolution proposing the Fifteenth Amendment, and Frost's 1876 cartoon, alongside a labeled teaching summary of Hayes's First Inaugural Address, and with the writing task, Ended, or Only Stood Down. Students need the whole unit behind them, in particular Lesson 2 on the Thirteenth Amendment, Lesson 3 on the Fourteenth, Lesson 4 on the Fifteenth, Lesson 6 on enforcement, and Lesson 7 on the retreat, because this lesson audits the settlement those lessons built. The election of 1876, the Electoral Commission, the Compromise of 1877, and the April 1877 withdrawal are new here. Students will learn how the disputed election was settled, what the bargain cost, and how to state the unit's closing account, what Reconstruction settled in constitutional text and what its ending left open. The lesson matches Common Core literacy standards for reading and writing about primary sources (RH.1, RH.2, RH.6, and RH.8 in the grades 9 to 10 and 11 to 12 bands) and the C3 Framework indicators for evaluating sources and communicating conclusions (D2.His.13 and D4.2). Timing is one class period of about fifty minutes for the reading and guided notes, with the activity in a second period. Materials are the printed reading, guided notes, and activity, and the slide deck with its printed teacher notes.

Summary table. The election and the commission: Tilden won the popular vote by some quarter of a million votes and stood one electoral vote short, while twenty electoral votes from Florida, Louisiana, South Carolina, and Oregon were disputed; the Electoral Commission of fifteen members awarded every disputed vote to Hayes by eight to seven, for a count of 185 to 184. The bargain and the withdrawal: the Democrats let the count finish in return for a southern cabinet seat, support for southern internal improvements, and withdrawal of the last federal troops; Hayes named David M. Key of Tennessee postmaster general, and in April 1877 the troops left the statehouses at Columbia and New Orleans and the last two Reconstruction governments fell. The settled account: the Thirteenth Amendment's abolition, the Fourteenth Amendment's citizenship, due process, and equal protection, and the Fifteenth Amendment's protection of the vote against racial denial stood in the Constitution, where no ordinary law could repeal them. The open account: land was never redistributed, freedpeople's schools and governments stood under rulers pledged against them, and the amendments' enforcement was stood down in a single spring.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer naming one settled item and one open item. Settled: slavery abolished, citizenship defined by birth with due process and equal protection guaranteed, or the vote protected against denial on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, all in constitutional text. Left open: land never redistributed, freedpeople's institutions left under hostile rulers, or the amendments' enforcement stood down in 1877 (any one named earns the point).
