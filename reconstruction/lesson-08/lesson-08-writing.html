Writing Task: Ended, or Only Stood Down

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words, judging one claim: the spring of 1877 ended Reconstruction. Judge whether the lesson's record supports that claim, or whether 1877 ended only Reconstruction's federal enforcement while the settlement itself stood. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your judgment.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading, activity, and documents. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording, except where a bank item quotes a document.

Evidence Bank

1. The election of 1876: Tilden won the popular vote by some quarter of a million votes and stood one electoral vote short. Twenty electoral votes, from Florida, Louisiana, South Carolina, and Oregon, were disputed. (Lesson reading, section A.)

2. Frost, Harper's Weekly, October 21, 1876: a Black voter at a southern polling place, with guns held to his head, is ordered to say he is free and to vote the Democratic ticket. The amendment protecting his vote was six years old. (Activity, Station 4, audited TPSP record.)

3. The Electoral Commission, January and February 1877: fifteen members awarded every disputed electoral vote to Hayes by eight votes to seven, for a final count of 185 to 184. (Lesson reading, section C.)

4. The bargain and the withdrawal: the Democrats let the count finish in return for a southern cabinet seat, support for southern internal improvements, and the withdrawal of the last federal troops. In April 1877 the troops left the statehouses at Columbia and New Orleans, and the last two Reconstruction governments fell. (Lesson reading, sections D and E.)

5. The settled text: the Thirteenth Amendment abolished slavery and armed Congress to enforce the abolition; the Fourteenth defined citizenship by birth and guaranteed due process and equal protection against the states; the Fifteenth protected the vote against denial on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude. No settlement of 1877 repealed any of it. (Activity, Stations 1 to 3, audited TPSP records.)

6. Hayes's First Inaugural Address, March 5, 1877, in teaching summary: complete protection of all citizens named the supreme subject and a moral obligation, answered by honest and efficient local self government for the southern states, in an address that does not name the troops whose withdrawal followed in April. (Activity, Station 5, labeled teaching summary.)

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A judgment argued from the record, not from the date alone. The year 1877 must be tested against both columns of the lesson's audit before it is called an ending or a stand down. (Blueprint 15)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 carries the disputed election itself. (Blueprint 15) Bank item 2 carries the settlement's condition at the polling place before the count began. (Blueprint 15) Bank item 3 carries the mechanism that decided the count, a party line commission. (Blueprint 15) Bank item 4 carries the bargain's price and the April withdrawal. (Blueprint 15) Bank item 5 carries the settled text no settlement repealed. (Blueprint 15) Bank item 6 carries the bargain's public face, protection pledged and enforcement unnamed. (Blueprint 15)
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the judgment. An ended essay should show the working settlement dismantled in sequence, electorate terrorized, governments fallen, troops withdrawn, and hostile rulers installed in every southern state. A stood down essay should show the distinction between a text and its machinery, the amendments standing in the Constitution with their enforcement clauses intact while the marshals, supervisors, and soldiers who had carried them were withdrawn.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest claim of the other side. An ended answer must answer bank item 5, the unrepealed text. A stood down answer must answer bank items 2 and 4, the voter at gunpoint and the fallen governments, which show what enforcement's end cost the people the settlement was written for.

Model opening for teacher reference. The spring of 1877 ended Reconstruction's enforcement and did not end Reconstruction. The distinction is the lesson's own audit. What ended is itemized in the record: a commission decided a disputed election on party lines, a bargain traded the last federal protection in the South for an inauguration, and in April the troops walked out of two statehouses and the last Reconstruction governments fell behind them. What did not end is itemized in the same record: slavery abolished, citizenship defined, and the vote protected against racial denial, in constitutional text that no bargain at Wormley House could repeal and no order of April 1877 did. The settlement stood as law. Its enforcement stood down, and the essay's remaining work is to weigh which of those two facts governed the lives the settlement was written for.

Common weak answers. Reconstruction ended because the troops left (a single cause) skips the settled text the prompt requires the essay to weigh. Reconstruction never ended because the amendments survive (the mirror error) skips Frost's voter and the fallen governments. An essay with no answer to the other side's strongest claim earns partial credit, because the prompt asks for a weighing, not a verdict alone.
