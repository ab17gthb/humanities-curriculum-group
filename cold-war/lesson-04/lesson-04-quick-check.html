Quick Check: The Cloud over Enewetak

Directions: Study the photograph and the background, then answer the questions.

Background. The photograph records the Ivy Mike test of November 1952, which released an estimated 10.4 megatons. The Soviet Union tested a thermonuclear weapon in August 1953, and Castle Bravo released an estimated 15 megatons in March 1954. In January 1954 Dulles announced massive retaliation, and the administration also used covert action in Iran in 1953 and Guatemala in 1954. The Warsaw Pact followed in 1955, Sputnik in 1957, and Eisenhower's farewell warning in 1961.

1. What is happening in the photograph? Name the test, its date, the kind of weapon, and its estimated yield.

2. State the doctrine Dulles announced in January 1954 in the lesson definition's terms, and name the method Dulles described for making its threat work.

3. The weapon in the photograph was hundreds of times more powerful than the bombs of 1945. State the routine the 1951 Duck and Cover film taught children, and explain the gap between the routine and this weapon.

4. Put the wider contest in sequence. Name the alliance formed in 1955, the two countries where covert action overthrew governments in 1953 and 1954, and the satellite launched in October 1957.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What is happening, with the test, date, weapon kind, and estimated yield? The photograph shows the mushroom cloud of Ivy Mike, the first test of a hydrogen bomb, a thermonuclear weapon, at Enewetak Atoll in November 1952. Its yield is estimated at 10.4 megatons, and the figure must be given as an estimate. (Blueprint 7)

2. State the January 1954 doctrine and its method. Massive retaliation is the Eisenhower administration's declared policy of answering Communist aggression with the threat of a full nuclear strike, rather than matching force at the point of attack. The method was brinkmanship, pushing a dangerous confrontation to the edge of war in order to force the other side to back down. (Blueprint 7)

3. State the Duck and Cover routine and the gap. The film taught children to duck and cover when they saw the flash, under a desk or against a wall, covering the back of the neck and the face, fast and wherever they were. A desk protects against flying glass and debris at a distance. Against a thermonuclear weapon of the size in the photograph near a city, the routine could not deliver the safety it promised. (Blueprint 7)

4. Put the wider contest in sequence. The Warsaw Pact was formed in May 1955, joining the Soviet Union and the Communist states of Eastern Europe in answer to NATO. Covert action helped overthrow the government of Iran in August 1953 and of Guatemala in June 1954. Sputnik, the first artificial satellite, was launched by the Soviet Union in October 1957 and opened the space race. (Blueprint 8)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the sourcing and scale diagnostic. Students who write only that a bomb exploded have not named the test, the weapon kind, or the estimated yield. Question 3 separates students who can weigh a home front source against the test record from students who repeat the film's promise at face value.
