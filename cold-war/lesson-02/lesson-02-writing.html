Writing Task: Won, Lost, or Settled

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did the Cold War turn into a shooting war in Asia, and why did Korea end without victory? A strong answer does two things at once. It explains the turn from paper to shooting, through the shocks of 1949, NSC-68, and the invasion of June 1950, and it explains the ending, through the changed aim of October 1950, the Chinese intervention, the held limit, and the armistice. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. On August 29, 1949, the Soviet Union tested its first atomic bomb. On October 1, 1949, Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China after winning the Chinese civil war, and the Nationalist government fled to Taiwan.

2. NSC-68, finished in April 1950, described the Soviet Union as seeking absolute authority over the rest of the world and called for American defense spending to rise to roughly three times its level, to check and to roll back the Kremlin's drive for world domination.

3. On June 25, 1950, North Korea invaded South Korea across the 38th parallel. The United Nations Security Council condemned the invasion during a Soviet boycott, and Truman sent American forces in under the United Nations flag without a declaration of war.

4. On September 15, 1950, Marines landed at Inchon. Seoul was retaken within two weeks, United Nations forces crossed the 38th parallel, and the aim of the war changed from preserving South Korea to unifying Korea by force.

5. China attacked in force on November 25, 1950, with hundreds of thousands of troops. Around the Chosin Reservoir, about 120,000 Chinese troops surrounded some 30,000 United Nations troops, and Seoul fell again in January 1951.

6. Truman relieved MacArthur on April 11, 1951 rather than widen the war, and the armistice of July 27, 1953 stopped the shooting on a line close to the 38th parallel, after more than 36,000 American deaths and with no peace treaty.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, explain how the Cold War became a shooting war in Asia, explain why the war ended without victory, and end by judging which decision carried the most weight in that ending.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question, how the Cold War turned into a shooting war in Asia and why Korea ended without victory. (Blueprint 3 and Blueprint 4)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 through 3 support the turn to shooting: the shocks of 1949, the NSC-68 logic of a global struggle, and the invasion of June 25, 1950 answered under the United Nations flag without a declaration of war. (Blueprint 3) Bank items 4 through 6 support the ending: the changed aim after Inchon, the Chinese intervention and the stalemate, and the dismissal and armistice that closed the war on its starting line. (Blueprint 4) A full credit essay draws on both groups.
  • Dates and figures used as evidence, not decoration. The dates of the two shocks, the invasion, the landing, the intervention, the dismissal, and the armistice each carry a step in the argument, and strong essays say what the step is.
  • A closing judgment about the decisive decision that follows from the evidence. Any of the three leading candidates earns full credit when argued: the crossing of the parallel in October 1950, which traded a won war for a war with China; the holding of the limit in April 1951, which accepted an ending without victory as the price of avoiding a wider war; or the NSC-68 framing itself, which made a local invasion a test the United States believed it had to meet by force.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Cold War became a shooting war in Asia because Washington had already decided, on paper, that it was a single worldwide struggle. The Soviet bomb of August 1949 and the Communist victory in China that October were read as two fronts of one design, and NSC-68 in April 1950 priced the answer as a military buildup meant to check and roll back Soviet expansion anywhere it moved. When North Korea crossed the 38th parallel that June, the United States answered within days, under the United Nations flag and without a declaration of war. The war then outran its original aim. Inchon rescued South Korea by September, and the decision to cross the parallel and unify Korea by force brought China in with hundreds of thousands of troops in November. From that point the ending was a matter of limits. Truman dismissed MacArthur rather than widen the war, the front froze near the parallel, and the armistice of July 1953 settled the war roughly where it had begun. A full model continues in this pattern and closes by judging which decision, the crossing, the limit, or the framing, carried the most weight.

Common weak answers. The invasion only answer (North Korea attacked, so America fought, and the war was a tie) has no account of why the United States believed a Korean invasion was its war to fight, which is what the 1949 shocks and NSC-68 supply. The MacArthur only answer treats the dismissal as the cause of the ending, which reverses the order: the stalemate and the Chinese intervention came first, and the dismissal held a policy the intervention had already imposed. A chronology with no judgment lists the war's turns without ever weighing which decision shaped the ending, which is the weighing the question asks for.
