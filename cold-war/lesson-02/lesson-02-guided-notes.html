Guided Notes: The Cold War in Asia: China and Korea

Lesson question: How did the Cold War turn into a shooting war in Asia, and why did Korea end without victory?

Unit question: How did the struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union reshape the world and American life, and why did it end the way it did?

Key Terms

NSC-68: the secret American planning paper of 1950 that described the Cold War as a global struggle and called for a large buildup of American military strength

People's Republic of China: the Communist state Mao Zedong proclaimed in 1949 after his forces won the Chinese civil war, recognized by the Soviet Union and not by the United States

Korean War: the war of 1950 to 1953 in which the United States and United Nations forces fought North Korea and China to preserve a non-Communist South Korea

38th parallel: the line of latitude chosen in 1945 to divide Soviet and American occupation zones in Korea, which became the border between North and South Korea

limited war: a war fought for limited aims with some weapons and targets deliberately left unused, rather than for the enemy's total defeat

stalemate: a condition in fighting in which neither side can gain ground or force a decision, however long the fighting continues

armistice: an agreement to stop fighting; the Korean armistice of 1953 ended the shooting without a peace treaty or a political settlement

A. The Shocks of 1949

On (1) ____________ 29, 1949, the Soviet Union tested its first atomic bomb, ending the American monopoly. On October 1, Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China, and Chiang Kai-shek and his defeated Nationalists fled to (2) ____________. Together the two events put a Communist government over a quarter of the world's people.

B. NSC-68: The Answer on Paper

NSC-68 was finished in (3) ____________ 1950. It described the Soviet Union as seeking absolute authority over the rest of the world and called for defense spending to rise to roughly (4) ____________ times its current level. Truman set the paper aside as too costly, and it waited for an event that would make its price look necessary.

C. Invasion and Intervention

On (5) ____________ 25, 1950, North Korean troops crossed the 38th parallel and took Seoul in three days. The United Nations (6) ____________ Council, meeting during a Soviet boycott of its sessions, condemned the invasion and called on member states to repel it. Truman ordered American forces into the fight under the United Nations flag, without asking Congress for a declaration of war.

D. Inchon and the Drive North

North Korean troops drove the defenders into the (7) ____________ Perimeter around the port of Pusan. On (8) ____________ 15, 1950, Marines landed at Inchon, far behind the front. Seoul was retaken within two weeks, and United Nations forces crossed the 38th parallel and drove toward the (9) ____________ River to unify Korea by force.

E. China Enters and the War Settles

Chinese troops struck in late October. On (10) ____________ 25, they attacked in force, hundreds of thousands of soldiers who had crossed the Yalu in secret. At the (11) ____________ Reservoir, Marines fought their way out of encirclement in bitter cold. Seoul was retaken in (12) ____________ 1951, and by that summer the front had settled near the 38th parallel in stalemate.

F. MacArthur and Truman: Who Commands a Limited War

MacArthur wanted to blockade the Chinese coast and bomb Chinese bases in (13) ____________. Truman refused, holding the war to limited aims, and relieved MacArthur of his commands on (14) ____________ 11, 1951, defending the act as the price of civilian control of the military.

G. Armistice Without Victory

Armistice talks opened in July 1951 and ran for two years, stalling over the prisoners. The shooting stopped on (15) ____________ 27, 1953, when the commanders signed an armistice on a line close to the 38th parallel. More than 36,000 Americans had died, and no peace treaty followed.

Summary table. The shocks: on August 29, 1949 the Soviet Union tested its first atomic bomb, and on October 1, 1949 Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China while the Nationalists fled to Taiwan; together they made Communist expansion read as one global design and raised the question of who had lost China. The paper answer: NSC-68, finished in April 1950, described that design and called for defense spending to rise to roughly three times its current level; Truman shelved it as too costly until war made it policy. The war in motion: North Korea invaded on June 25, 1950, the United States entered under the United Nations flag without a declaration of war, Inchon reversed the war in September, the drive to the Yalu brought China in during November, and the front settled in stalemate near the 38th parallel. The settlement: Truman dismissed MacArthur on April 11, 1951 rather than widen the war, and the armistice of July 27, 1953 stopped the shooting on roughly the line where the war began, with more than 36,000 American dead and no peace treaty.

Exit ticket 1. The Soviet atomic bomb test of August 29, 1949, which ended the American atomic monopoly, and the proclamation of the People's Republic of China on October 1, 1949, after Mao Zedong's forces won the Chinese civil war. Evidence for each is its date and its consequence: the bomb gave Moscow the weapon years early, and the proclamation put a Communist government over a quarter of the world's people.

Exit ticket 2. A limited war is a war fought for limited aims with some weapons and targets deliberately left unused, rather than for the enemy's total defeat. The commander was General Douglas MacArthur, relieved on April 11, 1951 after pressing in public for a wider war against China.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names at least one cause of the shooting war and one reason for the ending without victory, with two supporting facts. The strongest answers pair the invasion of June 25, 1950 and the NSC-68 logic behind the response with the Chinese intervention, the stalemate at the 38th parallel, and the armistice of July 27, 1953.
