Quick Check: The First Capsule

Directions: Study the photograph and the background, then answer the questions.

Background. On December 1, 1969, the Selective Service System held the first draft lottery of the Vietnam era. Capsules holding the 366 birth dates were drawn in order on television, and the order fixed which young men would be called first in 1970. The lottery replaced the choices of local draft boards, under which the burden of the draft had fallen hardest on the poor and the young. By the night of the drawing the war the lottery supplied had been running on the authority of the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution for five years, American troops were being withdrawn under Vietnamization, and the Tet Offensive of 1968 had already broken the public's belief in official claims of progress.

1. Describe what the photograph shows, and explain what the drawing decided. Name the man drawing the capsule, the date of the drawing, and the first birth date drawn.

2. The war the lottery supplied ran on a congressional resolution, not a declaration of war. Name the resolution, give its month and year, and state the authority it gave the president in its own terms.

3. State the reason the United States first gave for holding South Vietnam, and give the escalation in figures: advisers at the end of 1963, the force by the end of 1965, and the force by 1968.

4. Explain how the Tet Offensive could be a military defeat for its attackers and still turn the war, and define the policy under which American troops were coming home by the night of the drawing.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Describe the photograph and what the drawing decided. Representative Alexander Pirnie draws the first capsule from the container at Selective Service headquarters on December 1, 1969. The drawing fixed the order in which men born from 1944 through 1950 would be called for induction in 1970, by birth date. The first date drawn was September 14, which received sequence number 1. Credit requires the name, the date of the drawing, and the first birth date. (Blueprint 12)

2. Name the resolution, its date, and the authority it gave. The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, passed in August 1964 and approved on August 10. It authorized the president to take all necessary measures to repel any armed attack against the forces of the United States and to prevent further aggression, and all necessary steps, including the use of armed force, to assist treaty states requesting help. (Blueprint 11)

3. State the reason first given and the escalation figures. The reason was the domino theory, the belief that if one country fell to communism its neighbors would fall one after another. The figures: about sixteen thousand advisers at the end of 1963, more than 184,000 troops by the end of 1965, and more than half a million by 1968. (Blueprint 11)

4. Explain the Tet turn and define the withdrawal policy. The attackers were driven out everywhere with very heavy losses, and the Viet Cong never fully recovered, but the scale of the attacks, in more than one hundred places and on the embassy grounds in Saigon, refuted official claims that the enemy was weakening, and Johnson limited the bombing and left the 1968 race within two months. The withdrawal policy was Vietnamization, President Nixon's policy of withdrawing American troops while building up South Vietnam's forces to carry the fighting themselves. The definition must match the guided notes. (Blueprint 12)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the observation diagnostic: students who cannot say what the drawing decided have not yet connected the home front record to the war's manpower system. Questions 2 and 3 predict success on the blueprint 11 half of the unit test, and question 4 on the blueprint 12 half, because each requires the figures and the definitions in the student's own words.
