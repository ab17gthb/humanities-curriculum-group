# The Cold War Unit: Build Manifest

Every finished item, its file, its page count, and the test-blueprint entries it teaches or assesses. Blueprint numbers are from cold-war-skeleton.md. Every document ships as an HTML source with a PDF of the same name, built with templates/build_pdf.py and render-checked before listing. The full unit is 51 documents and 234 printed pages, plus 8 slide decks (215 slides) with a printable teacher notes document for each deck (79 pages).

**Unit essential question:** How did the struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union reshape the world and American life, and why did it end the way it did?

## Unit status: CONTENT COMPLETE (2026-10-22)
All eight lessons (six documents each), the review day, and the unit test are built in the approved design and render-checked: 51 documents, 234 printed pages. All eight slide decks are built to the slide standard: 215 slides, 33,689 words of speaker notes, every slide between 121 and 190 words, every key term and its full guided-notes definition verbatim in the notes in the strict joined form (verified programmatically for all 56 terms in both the guided notes and the deck notes at the end of the build), and a printable teacher notes document per deck (79 pages total). Reading prose runs 855 to 899 words across the eight readings by the standard counting method (856, 897, 899, 895, 889, 896, 891, 855), every reading inside the 850 to 900 band with no low-end exception this time. Zero em dashes and zero en dashes in any HTML source or extracted deck text, verified across the whole unit at the end of the build, and no instance of the banned construction anywhere in the unit. Honest gaps carried in the lessons themselves and recorded here: (1) Lesson 1 uses no photograph of Churchill delivering the Iron Curtain speech at Fulton, because the Truman Library's photograph is marked copyrighted on its record; the Karsh portrait stands in its place, captioned as a portrait. (2) Lesson 5 uses no photograph of Castro: the one downloaded file requested under a Library of Congress name did not match the image it was requested by and its true provenance could not be verified, so it was not used, and sections A, B, and E of that deck are carried by text and stat slides. (3) Lesson 8 uses no photograph of the night of November 9, 1989 itself, of Yeltsin on the tank in August 1991, or of the flag change in December 1991, because the famous versions are agency photographs under copyright and no public-domain record was verified; the December 1, 1989 Department of Defense photograph of crowds at the Brandenburg Gate is used with its true date in every caption. The Reagan and Gorbachev INF signing photograph was downloaded and verified as an image but held out of the deck because its Commons file page licensing line could not be confirmed line by line; the treaty is taught on a stat slide and the gap is recorded in lesson-08/img/SOURCES.md. Gorbachev portraits on Commons from the RIA Novosti archive are CC BY-SA, not public domain, and none is used. (4) Lesson 7's SALT II signing photograph is a 630 by 425 source and its deck crop prints visibly softer than the unit's other deck photographs, and no clean Commons photograph of the March 8, 1983 evangelical address was found, so the evil empire slide is a text slide. (5) Lesson 2's two Naval History and Heritage Command Inchon photographs are the archive's small online copies and print softer than the high-resolution images around them, a fact stated in that deck's speaker notes; one Commons category photograph of a Marine command post near Chosin is credited in SOURCES.md as an uncredited official United States photograph rather than with a service credit its file page did not show. (6) One passage of the Bundy memorandum as carried in the bank record contains an em dash, and under the unit's zero-em-dash rule that passage was deliberately not quoted in Lesson 6; other verbatim Bundy fragments carry its station instead. (7) Lesson 8's deck notes give the completed INF destruction count of 2,692 missiles as the standard Arms Control Association figure, stated as such, not as a bank record. (8) Four records in the bank's Cold War era file are carried as quotations only at the lengths their records carry: the Wheeling speech text is the Congressional Record reading of February 20, 1950, there being no separate surviving Wheeling transcript, and its station Source line says so and notes that reporters heard 205 where the Record text says 57 cases; MacArthur's farewell is quoted from the bank's audio excerpt record at its carried length; and Robert Kennedy's 1967 letter is quoted at the length its bank record carries. (9) Pagination tails that follow the model units' own patterns: Lesson 2's activity key ends on a short tail page, Lesson 4's guided-notes key spills a short tail onto its fifth page, the unit test's final key page is about a third full, and the study guide's last page is about a third full, each mirroring the World War II set's corresponding document. Plate gaps by standing decision, unchanged from the pilot: TPSP source analyses and photo sets are linked from the audited bank, not rebuilt, and TPSP premium DBQ number 21, "Did Containment Serve American Interests?", is the planned lead of this unit's TPSP picker in the integration pass.
