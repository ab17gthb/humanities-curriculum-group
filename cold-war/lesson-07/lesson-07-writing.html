Writing Task: Why Détente Ended

The prompt. Answer this question in a developed essay of about 300 words: Did détente fail because it was tried, or because it was abandoned? Take one side and defend it from the documents. A strong answer states what détente actually built, using the agreements' own texts, and it dates the ending rather than treating the whole decade as one mood. It also answers the strongest fact on the other side. If you argue that détente failed on its own terms, you must deal with the agreements it was still producing in 1979. If you argue that it was abandoned, you must deal with what the agreements left unrestrained while they were in force. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and state the danger of 1983 no more strongly than the documents support.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. By 1969 the Soviet Union had drawn near the United States in nuclear strength and faced border clashes with China, while the United States held more than half a million troops in Vietnam. Nixon and Kissinger built détente to give Moscow rewards it would lose by pushing too hard.

2. The SALT I interim agreement of May 1972 froze construction of additional fixed land based ICBM launchers after July 1, 1972. Its Article IV expressly allowed modernization and replacement of the missiles and launchers each side already held.

3. In the Helsinki Final Act of August 1975, the West recognized Europe's postwar borders and the Soviet bloc pledged respect for human rights, confirming the right of the individual to know and act upon his rights. Dissidents in Moscow and Prague used the pledge against their own governments.

4. Critics at home attacked détente from two directions. The right charged that the Soviet Union kept building while the United States held back, and Senator Jackson tied trade to Soviet Jewish emigration. President Carter made human rights a test of policy and protested the treatment of dissidents by name.

5. In December 1979 Soviet forces invaded Afghanistan. Carter embargoed grain sales, boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics, and withdrew SALT II, signed at Vienna in June 1979, from Senate consideration. The treaty was never ratified.

6. In March 1983 Reagan called the Soviet Union the focus of evil in the modern world and announced the Strategic Defense Initiative, a proposed defense in space meant to render nuclear weapons impotent and obsolete. That autumn brought the Korean airliner shootdown, killing 269 people, and the Able Archer exercise, which some in Moscow may have read, on a debated reading of the evidence, as cover for a real attack.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that takes one side and can be carried by documents. The tried and failed side rests chiefly on bank item 2, the freeze that allowed modernization, supported by item 4, the critics who named the weakness while the policy was running. (Blueprint 13) The abandoned side rests chiefly on bank item 5, a structure still producing a signed treaty in June 1979 and dismantled after the invasion, supported by item 1, the reasons the structure was built to answer. (Blueprint 13)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately and in date order. Bank item 3 serves either side, as proof that détente produced a document with real effects inside the Soviet bloc, or as proof that its strongest effect was one its signers had not intended. (Blueprint 13) Bank item 6 establishes where the story ends, in the language and the proposed capability of 1983. (Blueprint 14)
  • A direct answer to the strongest fact on the other side. An essay for the tried and failed side that never mentions SALT II, signed six months before the invasion, has not met the record. An essay for the abandoned side that never mentions Article IV has not met it either.
  • Precision about 1983. The Able Archer danger is stated as a debated reading of the evidence, as bank item 6 states it. An essay that presents a Soviet belief in a coming attack as settled fact loses credit under this line even when its verdict is sound.

Model opening for teacher reference. Détente was abandoned more than it failed, and the dates show it. The structure was built for stated reasons in 1969, near parity, a Soviet split with China, and an American war in Vietnam, and it produced agreements on schedule, a launcher freeze in 1972, the Helsinki settlement in 1975, and a second SALT treaty signed at Vienna in June 1979. Six months later the invasion of Afghanistan ended the political conditions the structure needed, and the American answer, an embargo, a boycott, and a withdrawn treaty, dismantled what remained. A full model in this line concedes Article IV directly, that the freeze never restrained modernization, and argues that a limited instrument working as written is a different thing from a policy that collapsed of itself. A model for the other side opens with the same concession reversed, resting on the freeze that limited launchers while the weapons inside them multiplied, and argues that a structure so limited had no strength to survive its first real shock.

Common weak answers. A verdict without documents (détente was naive, or Reagan simply ended it) earns partial credit at most, because the prompt requires the agreements' own texts. A one sided decade, in which nothing was built or nothing was weak, fails against bank items 2 and 5 together. An essay that inflates the Able Archer episode into a proven near attack loses credit under the precision line.
