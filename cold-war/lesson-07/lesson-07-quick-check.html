Quick Check: Naming the Adversary

Directions: Read the document and the background, then answer the questions.

Document. Ronald Reagan, address to the Annual Meeting of the National Association of Evangelicals, Orlando, Florida, March 8, 1983, printed word for word from the audited TPSP bank record. Edition named in the bank record: Ronald Reagan, "Evil Empire Speech," March 8, 1983 (Wikisource). Cuts are marked in the text as the bank record marks them.

[...] Yes, let us pray for the salvation of all of those who live in that totalitarian darkness, pray they will discover the joy of knowing God. But until they do, let us be aware that while they preach the supremacy of the state, declare its omnipotence over individual man, and predict its eventual domination of all peoples on the Earth, they are the focus of evil in the modern world. [...]

So, I urge you to speak out against those who would place the United States in a position of military and moral inferiority. [...] in your discussions of the nuclear freeze proposals, I urge you to beware the temptation of pride, the temptation of blithely declaring yourselves above it all and label both sides equally at fault, to ignore the facts of history and the aggressive impulses of an evil empire, to simply call the arms race a giant misunderstanding and thereby remove yourself from the struggle between right and wrong and good and evil. [...]

Background. Reagan spoke fifteen days before he announced the Strategic Defense Initiative, a proposed defense in space against nuclear missiles. The speech broke with the summit language of the decade before it. Under détente, presidents Nixon, Ford, and Carter had signed agreements with the Soviet government, including the SALT agreements of 1972 and 1979 limiting nuclear delivery vehicles and the Helsinki Accords of 1975, in which the Soviet bloc pledged respect for human rights. That structure had already collapsed in December 1979, when Soviet forces invaded Afghanistan and President Carter answered with an embargo, a boycott, and the withdrawal of SALT II from the Senate.

1. In the first passage, what does Reagan call the Soviet Union, and what three things does he say its leaders preach, declare, and predict?

2. Reagan warns against labeling both sides equally at fault. State what he says that habit ignores, naming two things from the text.

3. Using the background, name two agreements of the détente decade that this speech's language broke with, and state what each one did.

4. The background dates the collapse of the détente structure to December 1979. State what happened then, and name two of the three answers President Carter gave.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What does Reagan call the Soviet Union, and what three things does he say its leaders preach, declare, and predict? He calls the Soviet Union the focus of evil in the modern world. In his words, its leaders preach the supremacy of the state, declare its omnipotence over individual man, and predict its eventual domination of all peoples on the Earth. Credit requires the name and all three verbs with their objects. (Blueprint 14)

2. State what labeling both sides equally at fault ignores. Reagan says it ignores the facts of history and the aggressive impulses of an evil empire. He adds that it calls the arms race a giant misunderstanding and removes the speaker from the struggle between right and wrong and good and evil. Any two of those, accurately taken from the text, earn credit. (Blueprint 14)

3. Name two détente agreements this language broke with, and state what each did. Any two of the following, accurately stated. The SALT agreements of 1972 and 1979 limited nuclear delivery vehicles by negotiation between the two governments. The Helsinki Accords of 1975 settled Europe's postwar borders by Western recognition and carried a Soviet bloc pledge to respect human rights. The contrast the question tests is one of register. The agreements were written and signed in negotiated language between the two sides. The speech judged one side in moral terms and refused the equal fault framing. (Blueprint 13)

4. State what happened in December 1979, and name two of Carter's answers. Soviet forces invaded Afghanistan, killed its leader, and installed a government that took Moscow's direction. Carter answered with an embargo on grain sales, a boycott of the 1980 Moscow Olympics, and the withdrawal of the SALT II treaty from Senate consideration. Any two of the three answers earn credit. (Blueprint 13)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 test close reading of the speech itself, and students who paraphrase the name as a general insult, without the focus of evil phrase and the three verbs, have not yet read the passage as an argument. Questions 3 and 4 test the sequence the writing task depends on, agreements in negotiated language first, collapse in 1979, and moral language in 1983.
