Teacher Answer Key

StageWhat happenedDirection of the contestEvidence or result
Détente built, 1972 to 1975Nixon opened relations with China in February 1972 and signed the first SALT agreements in Moscow that May, freezing missile launchers. Trade in grain and technology followed, with regular summit meetings.Easing. Both sides accepted negotiated limits and gave each other rewards that restraint protected.The SALT freeze held launchers at their 1972 numbers, though modernization of existing launchers remained allowed under Article IV of the interim agreement.
Helsinki and human rights, 1975Thirty five nations signed the Helsinki Accords. The West recognized Europe's postwar borders, and the Soviet bloc pledged respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms.Easing on its face, pressure inside the Soviet bloc over time.Dissidents in Moscow and Prague formed watch groups, quoted the pledge back to their governments, and sent the record of violations abroad.
Critics and strain, 1973 to 1979Critics on the right charged that the agreements let the Soviet Union keep building while the United States held back. Senator Jackson tied trade to the emigration of Soviet Jews. Carter made human rights a test of policy and protested the treatment of dissidents by name.Straining. The bargain lost support at home from two directions at once.The summit relationship grew thinner each year, and SALT II, signed in June 1979, entered a Senate already doubtful about the policy behind it.
Collapse, December 1979Soviet forces invaded Afghanistan, killed its leader, and installed a government that took Moscow's direction. Carter answered with a grain embargo and an Olympic boycott.Raising pressure, sharply and at once.Carter withdrew SALT II from Senate consideration and declared the Persian Gulf a zone to be defended by any means necessary.
Confrontation returns, 1981 to 1983Reagan raised defense spending, aided anti-Communist insurgencies under the Reagan Doctrine, called the Soviet Union an evil empire in March 1983, and announced the Strategic Defense Initiative the same month.Raising pressure, in spending, in aid, in language, and in proposed capability.In September 1983 a Soviet fighter downed a Korean airliner, killing 269. In November some in Moscow read Able Archer, on a debated reading of the evidence, as possible cover for a real attack.

The turn in one sentence. Strong answers join an easing fact to a hardening fact from two rows. The rivals stepped back when parity, the China split, and Vietnam gave both sides reasons to accept limits. The contest hardened again after Afghanistan ended the bargain and a new president chose buildup, insurgent aid, and moral language.
