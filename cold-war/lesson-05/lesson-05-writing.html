Writing Task: Which Letter Mattered More?

The prompt. In the last three days of the missile crisis, Nikita Khrushchev sent President Kennedy two very different letters, one on October 26 and a harder one, made public, on October 27. Answer this question in a developed essay of about 300 words: Which letter mattered more in ending the crisis? Argue from the documents in the Thirteen Days File, including the cable that records what happened in secret on the night of October 27. A strong answer states what each letter offered, follows what Kennedy accepted, what he set aside, and what he settled in secret, and then judges which letter the settlement actually followed. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each one supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson documents. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Khrushchev's letter of October 26, as Kennedy's reply of October 27 carries it, offered to remove the weapons systems from Cuba under United Nations observation and to halt further shipments, in exchange for lifting the quarantine and American assurances against an invasion of Cuba. It did not mention Turkey.

2. Khrushchev's letter of October 27, made public, added a condition: the United States would remove its analogous means, the Jupiter missiles, from Turkey. Its argument rested on geography: Cuba is 90 miles from the American coast, and Turkey adjoins the Soviet Union.

3. Kennedy's advisers answered the October 26 letter and set the October 27 letter aside in public. Kennedy's reply of October 27 restated the first letter's terms as the basis for settlement and did not accept a Turkey trade.

4. On the night of October 27, Robert Kennedy told Ambassador Dobrynin in secret that the president saw no unsurmountable difficulty in withdrawing the bases from Turkey, needed four to five months, and could say nothing public about it. Only a handful of people in Washington knew.

5. Earlier that day, October 27, a Soviet missile had shot down an American U-2 over Cuba, killing Major Rudolf Anderson, and another American U-2 had strayed over Siberia. Robert Kennedy warned Dobrynin that the situation might get out of control, with irreversible consequences, and asked for an answer within a day.

6. On October 28, Khrushchev announced the withdrawal of the missiles. The announced settlement followed the October 26 terms: missiles out, quarantine lifted, an American pledge not to invade Cuba. The Jupiter missiles were quietly removed from Turkey months later, a trade both governments denied at the time.

Write the Essay

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers the question directly, one letter or the other, and rests on the settlement's full shape rather than on which letter the student finds more dramatic. (Blueprint 10)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 and 3 establish the October 26 case: the first letter supplied acceptable terms, and Kennedy's reply adopted them as the settlement's basis. (Blueprint 10) Bank items 2 and 4 establish the October 27 case: the second letter named the Turkey price, and the secret assurance that night paid it on the second letter's terms, four to five months and no publicity. Item 5 supplies the pressure that explains the timing of the secret meeting, and item 6 supplies the fact both sides of the argument must absorb, that the announced settlement followed the first letter while the completed settlement included the second letter's demand. A full credit essay uses items from both groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. The decisive distinction is between the settlement as announced and the settlement as completed. An essay that never separates the two cannot judge the question, whatever letter it picks.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest argument on the other side. For October 26: that the secret Turkey assurance shows the second letter's price was paid, answered by noting who set the announced terms and when the withdrawal was actually announced. For October 27: that Kennedy never accepted it in public and Khrushchev settled without a public Turkey pledge, answered by asking whether the October 28 announcement would have come at all without the assurance given the night before.

Model opening for teacher reference. The October 27 letter mattered more, because it named the price that actually closed the bargain. The October 26 letter offered a clean trade, missiles out of Cuba for an American pledge not to invade, and Kennedy could and did accept those terms in his reply. But the settlement did not rest there. On the night of October 27, after a U-2 had been shot down over Cuba, Robert Kennedy assured Ambassador Dobrynin in secret that the Jupiter missiles would leave Turkey within four to five months, on the one condition that nothing be said in public. The next morning Khrushchev announced the withdrawal. A full model continues in this pattern, adds the October 26 evidence and the announced settlement of October 28, answers the argument that the public terms followed the first letter, and closes on the distinction between the settlement the world was shown and the settlement the documents record.

Common weak answers. Retelling the crisis day by day describes the letters without judging between them. Treating the public announcement of October 28 as the whole settlement repeats the 1962 public story the Dobrynin cable corrects, and misses the secret part entirely. A claim that the October 27 letter failed because Kennedy rejected it confuses public rejection with effect: the question is what the settlement followed, and the file shows the Turkey demand answered in secret within hours of the letter's arrival.
