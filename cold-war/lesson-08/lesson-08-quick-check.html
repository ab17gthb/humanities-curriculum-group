Quick Check: Crowds at the Brandenburg Gate

Directions: Study the photograph and the background note, then answer the questions.

Background. The Brandenburg Gate stood in the closed border zone between East and West Berlin from 1961, when the Berlin Wall was built, until 1989. Ordinary Berliners could not approach it from either side. This photograph was taken on December 1, 1989, three weeks after crowds breached the wall on November 9. The gate is already freely accessible from the East Berlin side in the photograph; the crossing to the West at the gate opened officially on December 22, 1989. The photographer was an American Air Force staff sergeant making a military record of the scene.

1. The photograph shows a crowd standing in a place where standing had been forbidden for twenty eight years. What does the crowd's presence at the gate itself, in daylight and in large numbers, show that a written announcement of new travel rules could not show by itself?

2. The photograph is dated December 1, 1989, not November 9. State what had happened on November 9, in sequence, and explain why the change was still visible a month later.

3. Put these in order and give each date: the INF Treaty is signed, Gorbachev resigns, Reagan speaks at the Brandenburg Gate, the Berlin Wall is breached, German reunification.

4. A student writes that Reagan's 1987 speech tore down the wall. Using the record from this lesson, name two events between the speech and this photograph that the claim leaves out, and say what each one did.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What does the crowd's presence at the gate show? It shows the division ending as a physical fact and not only as a policy statement. An announcement can be withdrawn by the government that issued it; a crowd standing freely at the most guarded point of the border, photographed in daylight a month after the breach, shows that the guards no longer enforced the closure and that ordinary people had taken possession of the space. The photograph also fixes the source's limits: it is an American military record, made to show Americans the ending, and it shows celebration from one side of the gate in December, not the frightened crossings of November 9 itself. (Blueprint 15)

2. What happened on November 9, and why was it still visible on December 1? The East German government announced loosened travel rules; its spokesman, not fully briefed, said on television that they applied immediately; thousands of East Berliners went to the crossings and demanded to pass; the guards, holding no orders for crowds of that size, stood aside, and crowds breached the wall at the Brandenburg Gate. It was still visible on December 1 because the breach had not been reversed: no Soviet or East German force resealed the border, the gate stood open from the East, and its formal opening to the West followed on December 22. (Blueprint 15)

3. Put the five events in order, with dates. Reagan speaks at the Brandenburg Gate, June 12, 1987. The INF Treaty is signed, December 8, 1987. The Berlin Wall is breached, November 9, 1989. German reunification, October 3, 1990. Gorbachev resigns, December 25, 1991. (Blueprint 15)

4. Name two events between the speech and the photograph that the claim leaves out. Any two, with their work stated. The INF Treaty of December 1987, in which the same two leaders agreed to destroy a class of nuclear weapons, showing the relationship had moved from challenge to negotiation. The Polish election of 1989, which brought Solidarity into government with Gorbachev's acceptance, showing the empire's guarantee was being withdrawn. Hungary opening its border in 1989, which let East Germans leave and raised the pressure the November concession tried to relieve. The Leipzig demonstrations, which grew through the autumn of 1989 and forced the government's hand. The garbled press conference and the crowd's own action on November 9, which opened the wall on a night no speechwriter had scheduled. The student's claim fails because the speech set a test in 1987 and the wall stood for two more years; the events above are the mechanism that brought it down. (Blueprint 15)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 2 is the one to read closely: a student who gives the announcement without the crowd and the guards has the headline without the mechanism, and the writing task will expose the same gap. Question 4 separates students who can weigh a famous sentence against a sequence from students who repeat the sentence as the cause.
