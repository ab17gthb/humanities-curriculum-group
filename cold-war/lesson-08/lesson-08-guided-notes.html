Guided Notes: The Wall Falls: The Cold War Ends

Lesson question: Why did the Cold War end between 1989 and 1991, and what did its ending settle and leave unsettled?

Unit question: How did the struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union reshape the world and American life, and why did it end the way it did?

Key Terms

glasnost: Mikhail Gorbachev's policy of openness in the Soviet Union from 1985, allowing public discussion, criticism of officials, and a freer press

perestroika: Gorbachev's program of restructuring the Soviet economy and political system, meant to make a failing system work and ending by loosening the party's control of both

Solidarity: the Polish labor movement founded in 1980 at the Gdansk shipyard, the first independent union in the Communist bloc, which survived martial law to lead Poland's turn from Communist rule in 1989

INF Treaty: the treaty of 1987 in which the United States and the Soviet Union agreed to destroy all their land-based missiles with ranges between about 300 and 3,400 miles, the first agreement to eliminate a class of nuclear weapons

Berlin Wall: the wall the East German government built in 1961 to stop its citizens fleeing to the West, which became the central symbol of the divided Europe, and which crowds breached on November 9, 1989

German reunification: the joining of East and West Germany into one state in October 1990, ending the division imposed after the Second World War

dissolution of the Soviet Union: the ending of the Soviet Union in December 1991, when its republics declared independence and the state that had stood since 1922 ceased to exist

G. The coup failed within three days: hard line officials seized Gorbachev at his vacation home and announced a state of emergency, Boris Yeltsin rallied resistance from atop a tank outside the Russian parliament, crowds defended the building, and army units refused orders to attack. The Soviet Union ended in December 1991: Ukraine voted for independence, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus declared the Soviet Union dissolved, Gorbachev resigned on December 25, and the Soviet flag came down over the Kremlin.

The Ending at a Glance. The reformer: Gorbachev takes power in March 1985 over a stagnated Soviet Union; glasnost opens public argument and perestroika loosens central control, and both weaken the party they were meant to save; Reykjavik in 1986, the Brandenburg Gate challenge in June 1987, and the INF Treaty that December. The year it broke: Poland votes Solidarity into government, Hungary opens its border, the Leipzig demonstrations grow through the autumn, Gorbachev refuses to use the Soviet army, and on November 9 a garbled announcement, the crowds, and the guards standing aside open the wall. The settlement: East German free elections in March 1990, German reunification inside NATO on October 3, 1990, the Warsaw Pact dissolved, and the Baltic states independent. The ending: the failed coup of August 1991, Ukraine voting to leave, the republics declaring the union dissolved, and Gorbachev resigning on December 25, 1991, with the flag coming down over the Kremlin.

Exit ticket 1. Glasnost was openness: loosened censorship, freed political prisoners, and public discussion of hidden failures. Perestroika was restructuring: loosening central control to make the economy and the political system work. Either weakening, stated accurately, earns the second half: open discussion exposed the economy's failure and undercut the party's claim to rule, or restructuring loosened the party's grip on the economy and the state, or the two together taught citizens that obedience was a choice.

Exit ticket 2. A spokesman who had not been fully briefed announced loosened travel rules and said they applied immediately. Thousands of East Berliners went to the crossings and demanded to pass. The guards, holding no orders for crowds of that size, stood aside and let the people through, and crowds climbed the wall at the Brandenburg Gate.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that gives a cause for the ending and separates the settled from the unsettled. Settled: Germany reunified inside NATO, the Warsaw Pact dissolved, the Baltic states independent, and the Soviet Union itself gone by December 25, 1991, all without the direct superpower war both sides had armed against. Left unsettled: the future of the new states on the Soviet map, the control of the Soviet nuclear arsenal now spread across republics, and the place of a reunified Germany and a single remaining superpower in Europe. Strongest answers name Gorbachev's refusal to shoot as the hinge between the two lists.
