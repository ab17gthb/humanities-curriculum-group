Writing Task: Why the Cold War Ended When It Did

The prompt. The Cold War lasted from the 1940s to 1991, and it ended in three years of crowded events rather than in a negotiated surrender or a final battle. Write a developed essay of about 300 words answering this question: why did the Cold War end between 1989 and 1991, and which single cause carried the most weight in producing that ending? You may defend Gorbachev's choices, the crowds of Eastern Europe and Moscow, American pressure under Reagan, or the Soviet system's own failure. State the strongest argument against your own verdict, at full strength, and answer it. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and The End in Sequence. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording. Keep the November 9 sequence whole as you use it: a concession announced, its timing garbled, a crowd that acted on the words, and guards who stood aside.

Evidence Bank

1. In 1985 Gorbachev inherited a Soviet Union stagnated for more than a decade, with half empty shops, failing harvests, a costly war in Afghanistan, and an arsenal matching America's. His reforms, glasnost and perestroika, loosened censorship and central control, exposed the system's failure in public, and taught citizens that obedience was a choice. He had set out to save the system, not to end it.

2. At Reykjavik in October 1986, Reagan and Gorbachev came within reach of abolishing nuclear weapons before breaking down over missile defense. In June 1987 Reagan challenged Gorbachev at the Brandenburg Gate to tear down the wall as the unmistakable sign of reform. In December 1987 the two leaders signed the INF Treaty, the first agreement to destroy an entire class of nuclear weapons.

3. In 1989 Poland voted Solidarity into government, Hungary opened its border, and the Leipzig demonstrations grew from hundreds to hundreds of thousands. Through that autumn Gorbachev refused to use the Soviet army stationed in Eastern Europe to hold Communist governments in power, breaking with the practice of 1956 and 1968.

4. On November 9, 1989, an East German spokesman who had not been fully briefed announced loosened travel rules as effective immediately. Thousands of East Berliners demanded passage at the crossings, the guards stood aside, and crowds breached the wall at the Brandenburg Gate. No shot was fired.

5. German reunification followed on October 3, 1990, with the united Germany inside NATO and Gorbachev's consent obtained by negotiation. The Warsaw Pact dissolved in 1991, and the Baltic states recovered their independence.

6. In August 1991 hard line officials seized Gorbachev and announced an emergency. Yeltsin rallied resistance from a tank, crowds defended the Russian parliament, and army units refused orders to attack, and the coup failed in three days. In December, Ukraine voted for independence, the republics declared the Soviet Union dissolved, and Gorbachev resigned on December 25, 1991.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A verdict that names one cause as carrying the most weight in the ending of 1989 to 1991, stated early and held to the end. Any of the four causes in the bank can earn full credit; an essay that lists causes without weighing them has summarized the lesson rather than judged it. (Blueprint 15)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, drawn from at least two different parts of the sequence, the reform years, the diplomacy, the 1989 crowds, the 1990 settlement, or the 1991 collapse. An essay built on November 9 alone cannot explain December 1991. (Blueprint 15)
  • The strongest argument against the verdict stated at full strength and answered. Against a Gorbachev verdict: the stagnation predated him, and the crowds acted on their own initiative at moments he did not choose. Against a crowds verdict: crowds had marched in 1953, 1956, and 1968 and been dispersed by force, and what changed was the refusal in Moscow to order the shooting. Against a Reagan verdict: the 1987 speech moved no wall, the INF Treaty took two signatures, and the decisions of 1989 and 1991 were taken in Europe and Moscow. Against a system verdict: failing systems can endure for decades, and this one ended through dated choices by named people. (Blueprint 15)
  • The November 9 sequence kept whole wherever it is used: concession, garbled timing, crowd, guards standing aside, and Soviet nonintervention. An essay in which the wall simply fell, or was torn down by a speech, has failed the lesson's central record. (Blueprint 15)
  • The settled and the unsettled separated at the close: Germany reunified inside NATO, the Warsaw Pact gone, and the Soviet Union dissolved by December 25, 1991, against the open questions of the new states and the divided arsenal the ending left behind. (Blueprint 15)

Model opening for teacher reference. The Cold War ended when it did because one man's refusal met a crowd's willingness, and of the two, the refusal carried the greater weight, since crowds had tested empires before and been shot for it. Gorbachev inherited a stagnated system in 1985 and reformed it in ways that taught its subjects to argue back; then, through the autumn of 1989, he declined to use the army that had held Eastern Europe since 1945, and Poland voted, Leipzig marched, and Berlin walked through its wall. A full model continues by answering the crowds' claim at full strength, that no refusal would have mattered had no one marched, and closes on what the sequence settled and what it left open.

Common weak answers. A verdict built on inevitability alone, in which the system was bound to fall on the schedule it fell, cannot explain November 9 or August 1991, both of which turned on decisions made that week. A celebration of the 1987 speech that omits bank 3 and bank 4 cannot explain why the wall stood for two more years. A crowds account that omits Gorbachev's refusal cannot explain why 1989 differed from 1968. Treating the dissolution as a surrender signed with the United States repeats an error the record does not support: no such document exists.
