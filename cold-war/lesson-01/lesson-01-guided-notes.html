Guided Notes: Allies to Adversaries: Containment Takes Shape

Lesson question: How did wartime allies become rivals, and what strategy did the United States adopt to meet the Soviet Union?

Unit question: How did the struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union reshape the world and American life, and why did it end the way it did?

Key Terms

superpower: a nation with power and influence far beyond that of other states, able to project military and political strength across the world

Iron Curtain: the boundary Winston Churchill named in 1946 between the Soviet-dominated states of Eastern Europe and the free states of the West, and the division of Europe it described

containment: the American strategy of blocking the further expansion of Soviet power and communism, first argued by George Kennan and adopted as the guiding policy of the early Cold War

Truman Doctrine: the policy President Truman declared in 1947, that the United States would support free peoples resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or outside pressures

Marshall Plan: the American program of 1948 to 1952 that gave about thirteen billion dollars in aid to rebuild the economies of Western Europe after the Second World War

Berlin Airlift: the American and British air supply of West Berlin in 1948 and 1949, flown around the Soviet blockade of the city's land routes

NATO: the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, the alliance formed in 1949 in which the United States, Canada, and Western European nations pledged to treat an attack on one member as an attack on all

A. Partners of Necessity

The United States and the Soviet Union became allies because both faced (1) ____________, not because they trusted each other. Victory in 1945 left two powers standing far above the rest, each a superpower. Soviet armies held (2) ____________ Europe, and American and British armies held the West. Where the armies stopped, the new map hardened.

B. The Alliance Unravels

At Yalta in February 1945 the allies had pledged (3) ____________ elections in the liberated states of Eastern Europe. The pledge broke in Poland first, where Moscow installed a government of its choosing. Soviet troops also stayed in northern (4) ____________ past the date agreed for withdrawal, and Moscow demanded joint control of the Turkish straits.

C. Two Documents Name the New Reality

On February 22, 1946, George Kennan sent Washington a cable of about (5) ____________ thousand words, the Long Telegram. He argued that Soviet power withdrew when it met strong resistance. Eleven days later Winston Churchill spoke at (6) ____________, Missouri, with President Truman on the platform, and named the iron curtain that had descended across the continent.

D. A Strategy Takes Shape

Kennan's answer became containment, the strategy of blocking the further (7) ____________ of Soviet power and communism. Containment did not promise to roll Soviet power back where it already stood. It promised to hold the line where Soviet power had not yet reached. Kennan read the contest as (8) ____________ and economic before it was military.

E. The Doctrine and the Plan

In March 1947 Britain said it could no longer aid Greece or Turkey. President Truman asked Congress for (9) ____________ million dollars for both countries and declared that the United States would support free peoples resisting subjugation. That June, Secretary of State George Marshall proposed aid to rebuild Europe. The Marshall Plan gave about (10) ____________ billion dollars from 1948 to 1952.

F. The Test at Berlin

Berlin sat inside the Soviet occupation zone, divided among the four victorious powers. In June 1948 the Soviet Union closed the roads, railways, and canals linking the western sectors to the West, a (11) ____________ of the city's land routes. The Western powers answered with an airlift that supplied two million people by plane for (12) ____________ months, until the blockade was lifted in May 1949.

G. An Alliance, Two Germanys, and a Second Bomb

In April 1949 the United States signed the treaty creating (13) ____________, the first peacetime alliance in American history. The Western zones of Germany became the Federal Republic, and the Soviet zone became the (14) ____________ Democratic Republic. In August 1949 the Soviet Union tested its first (15) ____________ bomb, ending the American monopoly.

Summary table. The alliance breaks: the wartime partners were divided by ideology before 1945, the Yalta pledge of free elections broke in Poland and across Eastern Europe, and Soviet pressure on Iran and Turkey showed the rivalry reaching past the occupied zone. The strategy named: Kennan's Long Telegram of February 22, 1946 argued that Soviet expansion grew from ideology and insecurity and that Soviet power withdrew before strong resistance, and Churchill's Iron Curtain speech at Fulton named the division in public eleven days later. The doctrine and the plan: the Truman Doctrine of March 1947 promised support to free peoples resisting subjugation, first as four hundred million dollars for Greece and Turkey, and the Marshall Plan gave about thirteen billion dollars from 1948 to 1952 to rebuild Western Europe so hunger and chaos could not deliver it to Communist parties. The test and the alliance: the Berlin Airlift supplied West Berlin by plane for eleven months until the blockade was lifted in May 1949, NATO in 1949 committed the United States to Europe's defense in advance, Germany divided into two states, and the Soviet bomb of August 1949 ended the American atomic monopoly.

Exit ticket 1. Containment was the American strategy of blocking the further expansion of Soviet power and communism. George Kennan first argued it, in the Long Telegram of February 1946.

Exit ticket 2. The Truman Doctrine carried out containment by a public promise and by aid to governments under pressure, starting with four hundred million dollars for Greece and Turkey, framed as support for free peoples resisting subjugation by armed minorities or outside pressures. The Marshall Plan carried it out by economic rebuilding, giving about thirteen billion dollars to restore Western Europe so that the disorder Soviet power fed on would disappear.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that traces the turn with at least two facts. The strongest answers hold the sequence together: allies of necessity against Hitler, divided by ideology, facing each other across a Europe their armies had divided; the broken Yalta pledges and the pressure on Iran and Turkey; and the American answer built between 1946 and 1949, Kennan's analysis, the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, the Berlin Airlift, and NATO.
