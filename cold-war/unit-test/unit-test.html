Part 3: Sources

A1. Truman states that it must be the policy of the United States to support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation. The two kinds of pressure he names are armed minorities and outside pressures. One point for the policy stated in the source's words, one point for both pressures named. (Blueprint 2)

A2. The help should be primarily through economic and financial aid, which Truman says is essential to economic stability and orderly political processes. The Marshall Plan carried the same idea to Western Europe from 1948 to 1952, giving about thirteen billion dollars in aid to rebuild Western European economies so that hunger and chaos could not deliver them to Communist parties. Full credit requires the kind of aid, Truman's stated reason, and the Marshall Plan connection. (Blueprint 2)

B1. NSC-68 describes the Soviet Union as animated by a new fanatic faith, antithetical to the American faith, unlike previous aspirants to hegemony. It says the Soviet Union seeks to impose its absolute authority over the rest of the world. Both parts must come from the source for full credit. (Blueprint 3)

B2. The paper calls for a more rapid build-up of political, economic, and military strength in the free world, and names that build-up the only course consistent with progress toward achieving the American purpose. The shocks of 1949, the Communist victory in China and the first Soviet atomic bomb, had set up the paper's argument, and the Korean War, which began weeks after it was written in April 1950, turned a paper shelved as too costly into policy, as American defense spending rose sharply to fight a limited war in Asia. (Blueprint 3)

C1. The photograph is a grainy black and white aerial photograph taken from high altitude over Cuba on October 14, 1962. It shows cleared sites in the Cuban countryside with long shapes identified as missile transporters and launch equipment, with the installations marked by military analysts. The caption supplies the place and date and the description supplies what the frame shows. (Blueprint 10)

C2. The sites were Soviet nuclear missile installations under construction ninety miles from Florida, able to strike much of the eastern United States with little warning, which is why Kennedy treated the discovery as an emergency. On October 22 he announced a naval quarantine of offensive weapons bound for Cuba. The confrontation lasted thirteen days and was settled when the Soviet Union withdrew the missiles for an American promise not to invade Cuba, with American Jupiter missiles secretly removed from Turkey months later. Full credit requires the reason, the quarantine, and both parts of the settlement, or the public part plus one accurate consequence such as the hotline or the Limited Test Ban Treaty. (Blueprint 10)
