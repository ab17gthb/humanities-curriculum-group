Guided Notes: The Red Scare at Home

Lesson question: How did the fear of communism change American life at home, and what ended the scare?

Unit question: How did the struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union reshape the world and American life, and why did it end the way it did?

Key Terms

Second Red Scare: the fear from the late 1940s through the 1950s that Communists had infiltrated American government and institutions, and the investigations, loyalty tests, and blacklists the fear produced

House Un-American Activities Committee: the House of Representatives committee that investigated alleged Communist influence in American life, most famously in its hearings on the film industry in 1947

loyalty oath: a sworn statement of loyalty to the government and denial of Communist membership, required of federal employees and many other workers during the Second Red Scare

Hollywood Ten: the ten screenwriters and directors who refused to answer the House Un-American Activities Committee's questions in 1947, were cited for contempt of Congress, and went to prison

blacklist: a list of people barred from employment in an industry because of suspected Communist ties or refusal to cooperate with investigators, circulated in film and broadcasting during the Second Red Scare

McCarthyism: the practice of making public accusations of Communist disloyalty without firm evidence, named for Senator Joseph McCarthy, who made his charges the center of a national campaign from 1950 to 1954

Army-McCarthy hearings: the televised Senate hearings of 1954 in which the Army and Senator McCarthy accused each other of improper conduct, watched by millions, after which the Senate censured McCarthy

The Scare at a Glance. The machinery: the government ran loyalty investigations of millions of federal workers under Executive Order 9835, required loyalty oaths, and held HUAC hearings, and federal workers, teachers, and union members paid with jobs lost on suspicion and association, a few hundred dismissed on loyalty grounds and many more resigning under investigation. The cases and the campaign: courts convicted Alger Hiss of perjury and a jury convicted the Rosenbergs, who were executed in 1953, while Senator McCarthy's changing list claims from 1950 made public accusation a national campaign and ended Hollywood careers through the blacklist without any trial. The turn: the televised Army-McCarthy hearings of 1954 showed McCarthy's methods to millions, Joseph Welch asked whether he had no sense of decency, and the Senate voted 67 to 22 to condemn his conduct that December, after which his power collapsed and the scare faded.

Exit ticket 1. The Second Red Scare was the fear from the late 1940s through the 1950s that Communists had infiltrated American government and institutions, and Truman created the federal loyalty program by Executive Order 9835 in 1947.

Exit ticket 2. The House Un-American Activities Committee held the 1947 Hollywood hearings. The ten witnesses who refused to answer, the Hollywood Ten, were cited for contempt of Congress and went to prison.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer pairing one machinery fact with one ending fact. The strongest answers run from the loyalty program, the oaths, and the blacklist at work to the televised hearings of 1954, the Welch exchange, and the Senate vote of 67 to 22 to condemn McCarthy.
