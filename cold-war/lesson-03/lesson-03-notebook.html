Teacher Answer Key

Part of the scareWhat happenedThe claim or chargeThe evidence or the result
The loyalty programTruman created the program by Executive Order 9835 in March 1947, with an investigation of every person entering federal civilian employment and checks of workers already employed.Federal workers had to be of complete and unswerving loyalty, and association with a listed subversive organization could count against them.Millions were checked. A few hundred lost federal jobs on loyalty grounds, and many more resigned while under investigation.
HUAC and HollywoodThe House Un-American Activities Committee held film industry hearings in October 1947. Friendly witnesses named names, and unfriendly witnesses were asked about party membership.The committee claimed Communist influence in the film industry and the right to answers about beliefs and associations.The Hollywood Ten refused on First Amendment grounds, were cited for contempt of Congress, and went to prison in 1950.
The blacklistStudio heads barred the ten from work in November 1947, and lists of barred workers spread through film and broadcasting.Named workers were said to hold Communist ties or to have refused cooperation with investigators.Hundreds lost work without a trial or a public charge, on membership, petitions, associations, or acquaintance alone.
The spy casesAlger Hiss was convicted of perjury in 1950. Julius and Ethel Rosenberg were convicted of conspiracy to pass atomic secrets and executed in 1953.Hiss was charged with lying about passing documents. The Rosenbergs were charged with conspiracy to commit espionage.Hiss served prison time. The Rosenberg case rested heavily on David Greenglass's testimony, and both the evidence and the death sentence are argued over still.
McCarthy and his fallMcCarthy's Wheeling claim of February 1950 opened a four year campaign. The televised Army-McCarthy hearings ran 36 days in 1954, and the Senate condemned his conduct that December.McCarthy claimed to hold a list of Communists in the State Department, 57 cases in the Congressional Record text, a different number from the one reporters heard at Wheeling.The Senate committee that checked his claims found no Communists in the department. Joseph Welch's decency exchange discredited his method on television, and the Senate voted 67 to 22 to condemn him.

The scare in one sentence. Strong answers name both the machinery and the turn. The fear changed American life because loyalty investigations, oaths, hearings, and a blacklist put jobs and careers at risk on suspicion and association, and it ended because the televised hearings of 1954 exposed McCarthy's methods to millions and the Senate voted 67 to 22 to condemn his conduct, after which the campaign collapsed and the scare faded.
