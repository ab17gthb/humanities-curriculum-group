Writing Task: Judging the Scare

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did the fear of communism change American life at home, and what ended the scare? A strong answer separates the two halves of the record. It shows the machinery the fear built, loyalty investigations, oaths, hearings, and the blacklist, and it names the real espionage that gave the fear its fuel, before it explains how television and the Senate ended the campaign in 1954. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. In August 1949 the Soviet Union tested its first atomic bomb, and Communist forces proclaimed the People's Republic of China weeks later. Soviet agents had also passed atomic secrets to Moscow, so the fear had real espionage inside it.

2. Executive Order 9835 of March 1947 required a loyalty investigation of every person entering federal civilian employment. Millions of workers were checked, association with a listed organization could count against a worker, and employees signed loyalty oaths.

3. In October 1947 the House Un-American Activities Committee asked Hollywood witnesses whether they were now, or had ever been, members of the Communist Party. The Hollywood Ten refused to answer, were cited for contempt of Congress, and went to prison in 1950.

4. Studio heads barred the ten from work in November 1947, and a blacklist spread through film and broadcasting. Hundreds lost work without a trial, on suspected ties, petitions, associations, or refusal to cooperate.

5. Alger Hiss was convicted of perjury in January 1950. Julius and Ethel Rosenberg were convicted of conspiracy to pass atomic secrets, on testimony that rested heavily on Ethel's brother, and were executed in June 1953.

6. McCarthy's Wheeling claim of February 1950 spoke of 205 names in reporters' hearing and 57 cases in the Congressional Record text. The televised Army-McCarthy hearings ran 36 days in 1954, Joseph Welch asked whether McCarthy had no sense of decency, and the Senate voted 67 to 22 to condemn his conduct.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the prompt, that the fear changed American life through a machinery of investigations, oaths, hearings, and blacklisting, and that the scare ended when television exposed McCarthy's methods and the Senate condemned his conduct in 1954. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 supports the fuel, real shocks and real espionage. (Blueprint 5) Bank items 2, 3, and 4 support the machinery itself. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 5 supports the spy cases that kept the fear credible. (Blueprint 5) Bank item 6 supports both McCarthy's method, the changing numbers stated as claims, and the ending. (Blueprint 6) A full credit essay draws on at least two groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. An essay that lists hearings and names without the claim and evidence distinction has assembled the lesson's cast without its argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that keeps the two judgments separate, as the lesson does: the Senate condemned conduct by 67 to 22, and the case by case questions about espionage, the loyalty program, and the blacklist remained open after him.

Model opening for teacher reference. The fear of communism changed American life because it was built into machinery, not left as a mood. A federal worker in 1948 kept a job only after investigators checked FBI and committee files and a loyalty board weighed associations as well as acts. A screenwriter in 1947 could go to prison for refusing a question and lose a career to a list no court had approved. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the shocks of 1949 and the spy cases as the fuel, then closing on the 36 days of televised hearings, the Welch exchange, and the Senate vote of 67 to 22.

Common weak answers. A McCarthy alone answer (one senator frightened the country) misses the loyalty program, HUAC, and the blacklist that were running before Wheeling and outlasted him. An invented fear answer (the scare was made up from nothing) ignores the Soviet bomb, the fall of China, Korea, and real atomic espionage. Credit requires both records held at once: real espionage as the fuel, and machinery that punished association as the change in American life.
