Quick Check: The Map of 1763

Directions: Study the map and the background note, then answer the questions.

Background. This map was drawn to show the peace, not the war. Its title promises the British and Spanish dominions as the treaty concluded at Paris on February 10, 1763 settled them. On the map, France's mainland empire is gone: Canada and the country east of the Mississippi are Britain's, Louisiana west of the river is Spain's, and Florida has passed from Spain to Britain. The peoples who lived across the continent's interior appear on it as names in the landscape, not as parties to the settlement.

1. The map shows the continent divided cleanly between Britain and Spain. Name two facts from this lesson that the clean division leaves out, and state what each one shows about the difference between the map and the ground.

2. State what the Treaty of Paris gave Britain, what it gave Spain, and the one mainland place France kept. Then state why the Ohio and Great Lakes nations did not regard the treaty as binding on them.

3. Put these in order and give each date: the Albany Plan of Union is proposed, Braddock's army is destroyed, Pitt takes direction of the war, the Proclamation of 1763 is issued, the Treaty of Paris is signed.

4. A student writes that 1763 settled North America. Using the record from this lesson, name two dated events of 1763 itself that the claim leaves out, and say what each one did to the settlement the map records.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Two facts the clean division leaves out. Any two, accurately stated. The Ohio and Great Lakes nations still held the interior in fact and had never ceded it; no Indigenous nation sat at the table in Paris. In the spring of 1763 those nations rose under leaders including Pontiac and took most of the British interior forts within weeks, which a map of settled dominions cannot show. Settlers were already crossing the Appalachian line the Proclamation drew that October, so even the victor's own subjects did not observe the map's order. The Mitchell Map used at Station 4 makes the same point from the other side: imperial maps drew claims across country the mapmakers did not hold. (Blueprint 15)

2. What the treaty gave, and why the nations rejected its authority. Britain received Canada and all French territory east of the Mississippi River except New Orleans, and Florida from Spain. Spain received Louisiana west of the Mississippi. France kept New Orleans on the mainland. The Ohio and Great Lakes nations did not regard the treaty as binding because they had never ceded their country to France, had not been consulted, and held their land still; they read the treaty as France surrendering something France had never owned. (Blueprint 15)

3. Put the five events in order, with dates. The Albany Plan of Union is proposed, 1754. Braddock's army is destroyed on the Monongahela, July 1755. Pitt takes direction of the war, 1757. The Treaty of Paris is signed, February 10, 1763. The Proclamation of 1763 is issued, October 7, 1763. (Blueprint 15)

4. Name two dated events of 1763 that the claim leaves out. Any two, with their work stated. The rising of the Ohio and Great Lakes nations in the spring of 1763, which took most of the interior forts and besieged Detroit and Fort Pitt, showing that the transferred country had to be fought for again in the year of the transfer. The Proclamation of October 7, 1763, which forbade colonial settlement west of the Appalachians and ordered settlers already there to remove, showing that the victory's first year ended with the victor restraining its own colonists. A complete answer may add the ministerial resolve of the same year, that the colonies should pay toward their own defense out of a debt nearly doubled. The student's claim fails because 1763 was a hinge, not a settlement: the treaty closed the war between empires and opened the renegotiation of every other term at once. (Blueprint 15)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the one to read closely: a student who describes the map's colors without naming the peoples and events outside its frame has missed the lesson's discipline, that a period map is a claim, not a photograph of the ground. Question 4 separates students who can test a settlement claim against the same year's record from students who treat a signing date as an ending.
