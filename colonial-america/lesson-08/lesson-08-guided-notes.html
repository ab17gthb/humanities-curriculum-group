Guided Notes: Empire and War

Lesson question: How did Britain win North America, and what did the victory cost everyone who fought it?

Unit question: How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

Key Terms

French and Indian War: the North American war of 1754 to 1763 between Britain and France and their Indigenous allies, part of the worldwide Seven Years' War, which ended with Britain taking Canada and the French lands east of the Mississippi

Seven Years' War: the worldwide war of 1756 to 1763 in which Britain and Prussia fought France, Austria, and their allies across Europe, North America, the Caribbean, and Asia

Albany Plan of Union: Benjamin Franklin's proposal of 1754 that the colonies unite under one government for defense and relations with Indigenous nations, rejected by the colonial assemblies and the Crown

William Pitt: the British statesman who took direction of the war in 1757, paid for victory with borrowed millions, and sent Britain's army and navy to North America in force

Treaty of Paris (1763): the treaty of February 1763 that ended the French and Indian War, giving Britain Canada and all French territory east of the Mississippi River except New Orleans

Pontiac's Rebellion: the uprising of 1763 in which Indigenous nations of the Great Lakes and Ohio Valley, joined under leaders including the Ottawa chief Pontiac, attacked British forts and settlements after the French defeat

Proclamation of 1763: the royal order of October 1763 forbidding colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains, meant to reserve the land for Indigenous peoples and prevent new frontier wars

G. The debt and the resolution: Britain's national debt had nearly doubled during the war, and its ministers resolved that the colonies, defended at such expense, should pay toward their own defense. The rising and the answer: in the spring of 1763 the Ohio and Great Lakes nations rose under leaders including the Ottawa chief Pontiac and took most of the British forts in the interior within weeks, fighting to keep their country now that the French counterweight was gone. The Proclamation of October 1763 forbade colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains and reserved that country for Indigenous peoples.

The Victory at a Glance. How Britain won: Pitt's direction from 1757, borrowed millions, regular regiments and the navy sent in force, colonial soldiers serving beside the regulars and assemblies voting men and money, Louisbourg in 1758, Quebec in 1759, Montreal in 1760, and the Treaty of Paris of February 10, 1763. Cost to Britain: a national debt nearly doubled and the expense of holding a continent. Cost to the colonies: the Proclamation line of October 1763 closing the west their soldiers had fought expecting to enter. Cost to the Ohio and Lakes nations: the French counterweight lost, their country transferred on paper at a table where they had no seat, and the war of 1763 they fought to keep it.

Exit ticket 1. The Albany Plan of Union is Benjamin Franklin's proposal of 1754 that the colonies unite under one government for defense and relations with Indigenous nations, rejected by the colonial assemblies and the Crown. The Proclamation of 1763 is the royal order of October 1763 forbidding colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains, meant to reserve the land for Indigenous peoples and prevent new frontier wars. The plan died in the assemblies; the proclamation drew its line along the Appalachians.

Exit ticket 2. After William Pitt took direction of the war in 1757, Britain borrowed millions and sent its regulars and navy in force, with colonial soldiers serving beside them and assemblies voting men and money. Louisbourg fell in 1758, Quebec was won on the Plains of Abraham in September 1759, and Montreal surrendered in 1760.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that states the winning and names a cost for each people. Example: Britain won by Pitt's money, regulars, and navy joined to colonial men and funding, sealed at Quebec in 1759 and written into the treaty of 1763. Britain paid with a debt nearly doubled. The colonies paid with the Proclamation line closing the west. The Ohio and Great Lakes nations paid with the loss of the French counterweight and a treaty that disposed of their country without them.
