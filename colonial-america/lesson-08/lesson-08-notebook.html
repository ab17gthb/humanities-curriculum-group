Teacher Answer Key

Question of the warWhat the documents showWhat was decided
Who would hold the Ohio Valley, and on what terms?Three peoples claimed the valley by 1750. The Ohio nations lived and hunted there and kept their country by playing France against Britain. Virginia claimed it by charter and speculators' grants. France claimed it as the link between Canada and Louisiana and built a chain of forts. Washington's journal of 1753 reads the Forks as a fort site with the absolute Command of both Rivers.The war decided the imperial claim and not the ground claim. Britain took the valley on paper in 1763, and the Ohio nations still held it in fact, rising that spring to keep it. The Proclamation reserved the country west of the Appalachians to Indigenous peoples for the present.
Could the colonies unite to defend themselves?Franklin's Join, or Die of May 9, 1754 pictured the colonies as a snake in eight pieces. His Albany Plan proposed one general government, a President-General appointed by the Crown and a Grand Council chosen by the assemblies, holding Indian treaties, defense, and a power to lay general taxes. The Albany Congress adopted the plan.Every colonial assembly rejected the plan, unwilling to surrender its own powers, and the Crown let it drop. The colonies fought the war as thirteen separate governments, voting men and money assembly by assembly, and the union question was left for another generation.
How was the war won, and who did the winning?The war opened in disaster: Fort Necessity surrendered in July 1754 and Braddock's army was destroyed on the Monongahela in July 1755, with two years of frontier raids after. Pitt took direction in 1757, borrowed millions, sent regulars and the navy in force, and promised repayment for colonial men and money. Colonial soldiers served beside the regulars in large numbers.Louisbourg fell in 1758, Quebec was won in September 1759 with Wolfe and Montcalm both killed, and Montreal surrendered in 1760. The victory belonged jointly to British regular power and sea power and to the colonial soldiers and assemblies who served and paid beside them.
What did the peace give, and whom did it leave out?The Treaty of Paris of February 10, 1763 gave Britain Canada and all French territory east of the Mississippi except New Orleans, and Florida from Spain. France's Louisiana west of the river went to Spain. The treaty's articles divide the continent between empires and name no Indigenous nation as a party.France was off the North American mainland. The peoples who lived in the ceded country, who had never ceded it themselves, were left out of the making of the peace, and read the treaty as a French surrender of something France had never owned.
Who would pay for the victory, in money and in land?Britain's national debt nearly doubled during the war, and its ministers resolved that the colonies should pay toward their own defense. The Ohio and Great Lakes nations rose in the spring of 1763 under leaders including Pontiac and took most of the interior forts within weeks, after Amherst ended gift giving and restricted powder and settlers kept crossing the mountains.London answered with the Proclamation of October 1763, forbidding settlement west of the Appalachians and ordering settlers already there to remove. The bills were presented to everyone at once: money demanded of the colonies, land closed to their settlers, and the nations' country reserved on paper while settlers crossed the line anyway.

The lesson in one paragraph. Strong answers state the winning and the three costs together. Example: Britain won North America by Pitt's direction from 1757, borrowed millions, regular regiments and the navy sent in force, and colonial soldiers and assemblies serving and paying beside them, a combination that took Louisbourg in 1758, Quebec in 1759, and Montreal in 1760. The Treaty of Paris in February 1763 gave Britain Canada and the lands east of the Mississippi. The costs arrived the same year: a British debt nearly doubled and a ministerial resolve that the colonies should pay for their own defense, a Proclamation line that closed the western country colonial veterans expected to enter, and for the Ohio and Great Lakes nations the loss of the French counterweight, the transfer of their country without their consent, and the war under Pontiac they fought in 1763 to keep it.
