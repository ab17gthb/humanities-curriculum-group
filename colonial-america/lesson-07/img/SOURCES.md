## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, stated honestly
- The Zenger trial, 1735: no period image of the trial exists, and later illustrations are artists' reconstructions. No image is used for the trial itself. The lesson uses the Daye Press photograph for the printing trade and text slides for the trial.
- Colonial household interiors and family scenes: the located paintings and prints are later reconstructions or held under restrictions this build did not verify as public domain on a file page. No image is used. The household is taught through the will record in the activity and text slides.
- A period map of the colonies in the 1740s: not required by any document in this lesson and not used, rather than filling the space with a map the lesson does not teach from.

## Quoted sources, editions, and notes

### Zenger trial narrative, Hamilton's summation, 1735
- Edition: Andrew Hamilton, defense summation in the trial of John Peter Zenger, 1735, from the 19th-century State Trials reprint, via Constitution.org, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record zenger-trial-narrative-1735 and named in that record's sourceNote.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 1, and lesson-07-quick-check.html, the document passage. Passages are quoted from the bank record's text, with cuts marked by ellipses in the documents and disclosed in the Source lines. The reading, guided notes, notebook, and deck paraphrase Hamilton and quote no words of his beyond short phrases carried inside the bank-derived activity and quick check passages.

### Edwards, Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God, 1741
- Edition: Jonathan Edwards, "Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God," 1741, VIVA Pressbooks, American Literature I, from the 1741 Boston printing, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record edwards-sinners-angry-god-1741 and named in that record's sourceNote.
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 2 only, in four passages taken from the bank record's text, with cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source line. Other files paraphrase the sermon or name its images in the reading's own summary of them.

### Whitefield's journal, November 1740 (edition used for this lesson)
- Edition used: George Whitefield, A Continuation of the Reverend Mr. Whitefield's Journal, from his leaving New-England, October 1740, to his arrival at Falmouth in England (London, 1741), as transcribed by the Text Creation Partnership and held by the Oxford Text Archive, Bodleian Libraries, University of Oxford, record N03935, consulted at https://ota.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/repository/xmlui/bitstream/handle/20.500.12024/N03935/N03935.html?sequence=5&isAllowed=y
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 3, two passages from the entries for November 1 and November 2, 1740, in New York. Spelling is as the transcription carries it (Auditories, shew, favouring), and the Station 3 Source line says so on its face. The passages are quoted exactly as the transcription prints them, including its capitalization.
- Note on other Whitefield material: the Christian Classics Ethereal Library author page for Whitefield (https://www.ccel.org/ccel/Whitefield) was consulted for the biographical count used on the deck's stat slide, more than 18,000 sermons in his lifetime, which the slide's speaker notes attribute to the CCEL summary. No journal wording in this lesson is taken from the CCEL page. The journal quotations come from the Oxford Text Archive transcription named above.

### The will of Judith Johns, 1735
- Edition: Last Will and Testament of Judith Johns, written April 3, 1735 and proved in the Goochland County, Virginia, court on May 20, 1735, as transcribed from the county record and consulted in the transcription carried at FamilySearch, "Last Will and Testament of Judith Johns," https://www.familysearch.org/memories/memory/174009284
- Where quoted: lesson-07-activity.html, Station 5, two passages, with spelling as the transcription carries it (ly, wareing, fole, dyvided, publick saile). The inventory that follows the will in the county record is summarized in the station, not quoted, and the summary says the inventory listed the items named.

### Station 4, the assembly's purse
- Station 4 of the activity is a teaching summary written for this lesson from the standard record of colonial government. It is labeled on its face as a teaching summary and not a quotation, in the station's Source line and in its heading.
