# Image Sources: Lesson 2, The Spanish and French Americas

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/.

## Map of Tenochtitlan, 1524 (tenochtitlan-map.jpg; deck crops title-169.jpg and tenochtitlan-port.jpg; used in the activity, the notebook page, and the quick check)
- Title: Map of Tenochtitlan, 1524
- Creator: Friedrich Peypus (1485 to 1534), probably after a drawing made by one of Cortes's men, as stated on the Wikimedia Commons file page
- Date: printed 1524, Nuremberg, with the letters of Hernan Cortes
- Holder: Original at the Newberry Library, Chicago (inventory number Ayer 655.51.C8 1524b, as stated on the file page), via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Map_of_Tenochtitlan%2C_1524.jpg
- Public domain basis: Public domain, as stated in the license field of the Commons file page (a colorized woodcut published in 1524; the creator died in 1534).

## Potosi, from the Chronicle of Peru, 1553 (potosi-cieza.jpg; deck crop potosi-port.jpg)
- Title: Potosi. Drawing from the Chronicle of Peru, 1553 (woodcut heading the chapter on Potosi in Pedro Cieza de Leon's Cronica del Peru)
- Creator: Unknown woodcut artist of the 1553 Seville printing, as carried in the Chronicle; the Commons file page credits the scan to A. Skromnitsky
- Date: 1553
- Holder: Wikimedia Commons file page for the 1553 Chronicle illustration
- Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8e/Capitulo-CIX.jpg
- Public domain basis: Public domain, as stated in the license field of the Commons file page (illustration published in 1553).

## The habitation at Quebec, 1608 (champlain-quebec.jpg; deck crop quebec-port.jpg; used in the activity)
- Title: Champlain Habitation de Quebec (the Quebec settlement, with its buildings keyed by letter)
- Creator: Samuel de Champlain, as stated on the Wikimedia Commons file page
- Date: drawn from the settlement founded in 1608; carried in Champlain's works (file page source: The Works of Samuel de Champlain, Champlain Society, Toronto, 1925, reprinted 1971, volume II, page 39)
- Holder: Wikimedia Commons, from the Champlain Society edition
- Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a9/Champlain_Habitation_de_Quebec.jpg
- Public domain basis: Public domain, as stated in the license field of the Commons file page (work of Samuel de Champlain, died 1635).

## Codex Mendoza, folio 37r (codex-mendoza.jpg; deck crop mendoza-port.jpg)
- Title: Codex Mendoza, folio 37r (a tribute and town page of the Aztec tribute record)
- Creator: Indigenous Nahua painters and scribes, working for the Spanish Crown, about 1541
- Date: about 1541
- Holder: Bodleian Library, Oxford (the Codex Mendoza manuscript), via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Codex_Mendoza_folio_37r.jpg
- Public domain basis: Public domain, as stated in the license field of the Commons file page (manuscript of about 1541).

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- The de Bry engraving of mining at Potosi (Historia Americae, 1596), carried on Wikimedia Commons as File:Theodoor de bry.jpg, was downloaded, inspected, and set aside: its Commons file page carries a CC BY-SA 4.0 license for the scan rather than a public domain basis, so it does not meet this unit's public domain rule. The 1553 Chronicle woodcut is used for Potosi instead.
- Theodor de Bry's engravings for the 1598 Latin edition of Las Casas depict conquest violence in graphic detail. No image of violence is used as decoration in this lesson, so none is used; Las Casas is taught from his text.
- Saint Domingue and the sugar plantations: no period image with a verified public domain basis on a Commons file page or holding archive record was verified for this build. The Code Noir is taught from its text, and the sugar islands are taught in text and in the comparison tables.
- Bartolome de las Casas and Hernan Cortes portraits: the familiar portraits are later imaginative likenesses with no verified public domain basis relied on for this build. Both men are taught in text.
