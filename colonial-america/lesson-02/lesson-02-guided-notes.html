Guided Notes: The Spanish and French Americas

Lesson question: How did Spain build an empire on conquest, and how did France build one on trade?

Unit question: How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

Key Terms

conquistador: a Spanish military leader who conquered Indigenous peoples in the Americas in the 1500s, seeking land, gold, and glory for Spain and himself

encomienda: the Spanish system that granted a colonist the right to demand tribute and labor from Indigenous people in a given area, in return for supposed protection and Christian teaching

mestizo: a person of mixed Spanish and Indigenous ancestry, ranked in the caste order of Spanish America between Spaniards and Indigenous peoples

New Spain: Spain's viceroyalty in North America and the Caribbean, governed from Mexico City, which at its height stretched from Panama to the present-day American Southwest

mission: a religious settlement founded by Catholic orders among Indigenous peoples in the Spanish and French colonies to convert them and remake their way of life

fur trade: the exchange central to French and Dutch colonization, in which Europeans traded manufactured goods to Indigenous hunters for beaver pelts and other furs

hacienda: a large estate in Spanish America worked by Indigenous or enslaved labor, producing food and goods for nearby towns and mines

Summary table. How it came: Spain came by conquest, Cortes taking Tenochtitlan in 1521 with Indigenous allies and Pizarro seizing Peru; France came by settlement for trade, Champlain founding Quebec in 1608. What it lived by: Spain lived by silver from Potosi, tribute, and the produce of haciendas; France lived by the fur trade in Canada and by sugar in the islands. Whose labor it used: Spain used Indigenous labor under the encomienda and later drafts, and enslaved African labor; France used Indigenous hunters as trading partners in the north and enslaved Africans on the sugar plantations. How it dealt with Indigenous nations: Spain ruled Indigenous peoples as subjects inside a ranked order and gathered them into missions; France allied with Indigenous nations, married into them, and fought beside them, because its partners were too strong to rule.

Exit ticket 1. The encomienda was the Spanish system that granted a colonist the right to demand tribute and labor from Indigenous people in a given area, in return for supposed protection and Christian teaching. The man who attacked it in print was Bartolome de las Casas, whose Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies appeared in 1552.

Exit ticket 2. The colony held only a few thousand settlers in a thin line along the St. Lawrence, and the furs it lived by lay in Indigenous country and were gathered by Indigenous hunters. France had to ally, trade, and renew its councils because the peoples it depended on were strong enough to set terms of their own.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names conquest and rule for Spain and trade and alliance for France, with two supporting facts, and that closes the contrast honestly: France's sugar islands ran on enslaved labor under the Code Noir of 1685, so both empires rested on land taken and labor coerced.
