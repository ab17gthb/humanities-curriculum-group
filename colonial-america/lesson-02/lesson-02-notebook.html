Teacher Answer Key

QuestionSpainFrance
How it took holdBy conquest. Cortes took Tenochtitlan in 1521 with Indigenous allies and smallpox working inside the city, and Pizarro seized Peru a decade later on the same pattern.By settlement for trade. Champlain founded Quebec in 1608, and the colony stayed a thin line of farms and posts along the St. Lawrence.
What it lived bySilver above all, from Potosi (found 1545), with the Crown taking its fifth, and food and goods from haciendas serving the mining towns.The fur trade in the north, beaver pelts exchanged for manufactured goods, and sugar from the Caribbean islands, which outsold the furs.
Whose labor carried itIndigenous labor, demanded under the encomienda and later under official drafts, in mines, fields, and building yards, and enslaved African labor across the empire.Indigenous hunters as partners in the north, whose pelts, routes, and canoes the trade ran on, and enslaved Africans on the sugar plantations.
How it treated Indigenous nationsAs subjects to be ruled, taxed, ranked in a caste order below Spaniards and mestizos, and gathered into missions that remade their way of life.As allies to be courted, through councils, gifts, intermarriage, and shared war, above all against the Haudenosaunee, because the partners were too strong to rule.
Where enslaved labor stoodPresent across the empire from the start, and growing after Las Casas's early suggestion of African labor as a substitute for Indigenous labor, a position he later repented.Written into law by the Code Noir of 1685, which made slavery hereditary, required Catholic baptism, and punished flight with mutilation, on the sugar islands.

The difference that counted most. Strong answers name the row, state the difference in one sentence, and follow it to its consequence by 1700. On labor, Spain consumed the Indigenous population it ruled through the encomienda and the drafts, while France in the north depended on living partners and therefore had to keep its alliances worth keeping. On Indigenous nations, the difference is rule against alliance, and its consequence runs both ways: Spain's subjects were governed and taxed inside the empire, while France's partners kept their own governments, armies, and terms. On enslaved labor, the strongest answers refuse the simple contrast, because by 1700 both empires rested on enslaved Africans, Spain across its mainland and France under a written code in the islands.
