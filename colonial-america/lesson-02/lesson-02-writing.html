Writing Task: Two Ways of Holding a Hemisphere

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did Spain build an empire on conquest, and how did France build one on trade? A strong answer does two things at once. It explains the Spanish system, through the conquest, the encomienda, and the ranked society of New Spain, and it explains the French system, through the fur trade and its alliances, ending with the judgment the lesson closes on: whether the contrast survives the sugar islands and the Code Noir. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Cortes entered Tenochtitlan in 1519 with about 450 Spaniards, in the count of Bernal Diaz, who described a great lake city of towns, bridges, and crowds of canoes. The city fell in August 1521 after a siege carried by tens of thousands of Indigenous allies, above all the Tlaxcalans, and by smallpox inside the city.

2. At Potosi in the Andes, a mountain of silver found in 1545 drew a city of more than 100,000 people. Drafted Indigenous laborers worked the mines, the Crown took its fifth of the output, and haciendas supplied the mining towns with food and goods.

3. The encomienda granted a colonist the right to demand tribute and labor from Indigenous people in a given area, in return for supposed protection and Christian teaching. Bartolome de las Casas attacked the system at court for decades, and his Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies (1552) claimed above twelve million dead in forty years, figures historians read as his claims rather than settled counts. The New Laws of 1542 limited the encomienda, and colonists resisted them.

4. Spanish America ranked its people by birth: peninsulares born in Spain at the top, creoles below them, mestizos of mixed Spanish and Indigenous ancestry below them, and Indigenous communities and enslaved Africans at the bottom. Missions on the borders gathered Indigenous families into supervised towns.

5. Champlain founded Quebec in 1608, and New France stayed a colony of a few thousand settlers living by the fur trade. French traders married into Indigenous nations, set prices in council, and fought beside their partners against the Haudenosaunee for two generations, because the furs, routes, and canoes belonged to peoples strong enough to set terms.

6. France's richest colonies were the sugar islands. The Code Noir of 1685 required Catholic baptism for the enslaved, made children of enslaved parents enslaved, and punished a month of flight with the loss of ears and a brand. By 1700 both empires rested on land taken and labor coerced.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question, how Spain built an empire on conquest and how France built one on trade. (Blueprint 3 and Blueprint 4)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 through 4 support the Spanish system: the conquest of 1519 to 1521 carried by Indigenous allies and smallpox, Potosi and the haciendas, the encomienda and Las Casas with the New Laws, and the ranked order of New Spain with its missions. (Blueprint 3) Bank items 5 and 6 support the French system and its limit: the fur trade and the alliances it required, and the sugar islands under the Code Noir. (Blueprint 4) A full credit essay draws on both groups.
  • Dates and figures used as evidence, not decoration. The years 1519, 1521, 1545, 1552, 1608, and 1685, the 450 men, the fifth taken by the Crown, and the few thousand settlers each carry a step in the argument, and strong essays say what the step is. Las Casas's figures, if used, must be carried as his claims, as the bank carries them.
  • A closing judgment on the contrast that accounts for the islands. Either verdict earns full credit when argued: the contrast holds, because rule and alliance were genuinely different instruments with different costs, and the islands were the exception that French law had to write separately; or the contrast fails at its base, because both empires ended on enslaved labor and taken land, and the Canadian alliance was a product of weakness rather than a different intention.

Model opening for teacher reference. Spain and France held the same hemisphere with opposite hands. Spain conquered first and ruled afterward. Cortes marched on Tenochtitlan with a few hundred men and took it with an Indigenous army and an epidemic, and the empire Spain built on the victory ran on commanded labor: the encomienda's tribute and drafts, the silver of Potosi, and a society ranked by birth from peninsulares down to the enslaved. France, with a few thousand settlers on the St. Lawrence, could command no one. Its fur trade belonged to Indigenous hunters, so it bought its empire council by council, with manufactured goods, marriages, and shared war. A full model continues in this pattern and closes by judging whether that contrast survives Saint Domingue, where French law, in the Code Noir of 1685, ran an empire of enslaved labor as complete as Spain's.

Common weak answers. The peaceful France answer (Spain conquered, France only traded) cannot account for bank item 6, the Code Noir and the sugar islands, both of which are in the bank and in the prompt's closing demand. The Spanish strength alone answer treats 450 men as the conquest's explanation, which bank item 1 contradicts on its face. A chronology with no judgment lists conquest and trade side by side without ever weighing the difference or its limit, which is the weighing the question asks for.
