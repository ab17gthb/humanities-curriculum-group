Guided Notes: Two Worlds Before Contact

Lesson question: What did the Americas and Europe each look like in 1491, and what changed when they met?

Unit question: How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

Key Terms

Columbian Exchange: the transfer of plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres that began with the voyages of Columbus in 1492

Tenochtitlan: the capital of the Aztec empire, built on an island in Lake Texcoco in present-day Mexico, one of the largest cities in the world in 1500

Cahokia: the largest city of the Mississippian culture, near present-day St. Louis, a center of trade and ceremony known for its great earthen mounds

Iroquois Confederacy: the league of five, later six, Native nations in present-day New York, joined under the Great Law to keep peace among themselves and deal jointly with outsiders

caravel: a small, fast sailing ship developed by the Portuguese in the 1400s, able to sail against the wind and suited to long ocean voyages

Reconquista: the centuries of campaigns by Christian kingdoms to take the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rulers, completed in 1492, which shaped Spain's ambitions in the Americas

epidemic: the rapid spread of a disease through a population that has little resistance to it

A. A Hemisphere Full of People

The Americas in 1491 held perhaps (1) ____________ million people or more, a figure the reading labels a contemporary scholarly estimate. Farming anchored most of that world. Maize, beans, and (2) ____________ fed dense populations in Mesoamerica and the Andes and villages across the Eastern Woodlands. Hundreds of nations governed themselves and made war and peace with their neighbors.

B. Tenochtitlan on the Lake

Tenochtitlan, the capital of the Aztec empire, stood on an island in Lake (3) ____________ in present day Mexico. It held perhaps two hundred thousand people in 1500, more than (4) ____________ held at the same date. The city fed itself in part from (5) ____________, raised garden plots built in the shallow lake, and at its center stood the Templo Mayor.

C. Cahokia and the League

Cahokia, the largest city of the (6) ____________ culture, stood near present day St. Louis. At its height, between about 1050 and 1200, its people raised some one hundred twenty earthen mounds, the largest rising about (7) ____________ feet. In the northeast, five nations, the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, and Seneca, stood joined in the Iroquois Confederacy under the (8) ____________ Law, keeping peace among themselves and dealing jointly with outsiders.

D. Europe on the Eve

In January 1492 Ferdinand and Isabella completed the Reconquista by accepting the surrender of (9) ____________, the last Muslim kingdom in Iberia. Portugal had spent the century pushing down the coast of (10) ____________ in search of gold and a sea route to Asia. Its sailors worked in the caravel, and compass, astrolabe, gunpowder weapons, and the (11) ____________ press completed the equipment of expansion.

E. Columbus Sails, Claims, and Reports

Columbus, a Genoese sailor sailing for Spain, crossed the Atlantic in the fall of 1492 seeking a sea route to (12) ____________. He believed he had reached its edge, and he called the peoples he met Indians. He made (13) ____________ voyages in all between 1492 and 1504. On the voyage home in 1493 he wrote a letter announcing his discoveries, which was printed in Spain and in Latin at (14) ____________ and spread across Europe within months.

F. The Columbian Exchange

The Columbian Exchange moved crops in both directions at once. Maize, potatoes, and tomatoes traveled (15) ____________ from the Americas. Wheat, sugar, horses, cattle, and pigs traveled west. (16) ____________ in time remade life on the plains of North and South America, and sugar built an appetite for labor that would reshape the Atlantic world.

G. Epidemic and Catastrophe

Europeans and Africans carried diseases, (17) ____________ first among them, into populations that had little resistance to them. An epidemic could empty a town in a season, leaving too few healthy people to plant, harvest, or care for the sick. Over the century after 1492, in many regions, by contemporary scholarly estimates, (18) ____________ people in ten died. Indigenous nations rebuilt their towns, adapted their farming and their diplomacy, and resisted the newcomers from inside that loss.

Summary table. The Americas in 1491: perhaps fifty million people or more, a contemporary scholarly estimate, in farming peoples and hunting peoples across two continents, governed by hundreds of nations of their own. Tenochtitlan and Cahokia: Tenochtitlan, the capital of the Aztec empire on an island in Lake Texcoco, perhaps two hundred thousand people in 1500, fed in part by chinampas, and Cahokia, the largest city of the Mississippian culture near present day St. Louis, at its height between about 1050 and 1200, with some one hundred twenty earthen mounds. Europe in 1491: rival kingdoms, a Spain completing the Reconquista at Granada in January 1492, and a Portugal probing down Africa in caravels, with compass, astrolabe, gunpowder weapons, and printing in hand. The meeting and the exchange: Columbus's four voyages for Spain between 1492 and 1504, his printed letter of 1493, the Columbian Exchange moving crops and animals in both directions, and epidemic disease, smallpox first, killing by contemporary scholarly estimates nine people in ten in many regions over the following century.

Exit ticket 2. The Iroquois Confederacy was the league of five Native nations in present day New York, joined under the Great Law. Under that law the five nations kept peace among themselves and dealt jointly with outsiders, sending speakers to a council that met at Onondaga.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names one fact about each world in 1491 and the deadliest change with its scale. The strongest answers pair them: an Americas of perhaps fifty million people with cities such as Tenochtitlan and leagues such as the Iroquois Confederacy, and a Europe of rival kingdoms with a newly unified Spain and the equipment of expansion, met through Columbus's voyages, and epidemic disease then killed, by contemporary scholarly estimates, nine people in ten in many regions over the following century.
