## Gaps recorded honestly
- No public domain photograph of Monks Mound at Cahokia with a verified public domain basis was downloaded for this lesson. The best known site photographs on Wikimedia Commons are licensed Creative Commons Attribution Share Alike, not public domain, and one National Archives aerial file page could not be fetched for verification during the build. The lesson therefore teaches Cahokia from the labeled summary in Station 3 of the activity and from text and stat slides in the deck, and uses no Cahokia image.
- No period image of the Haudenosaunee council or of a caravel with a verified public domain basis was selected for this lesson. Those subjects are taught with text and stat slides, and no substitute illustration is presented as a period record.
- No image of epidemic disease is used anywhere in this lesson. The catastrophe of Section G is taught from the demographic record in words and numbers only.

## Text sources quoted in this lesson, with editions
- Christopher Columbus, Columbus Reports on His First Voyage, 1493 (Gilder Lehrman Institute), as carried in the audited TPSP record, id columbus-first-voyage-1493, in colonial.json. Quoted in the activity, the quick check, the writing evidence bank, and the deck notes. Cuts are marked with ellipses in the documents.
- Stations 3, 4, and 5 of the activity are labeled teaching summaries on their faces, built from the public record: the Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site record for Station 3, the public record of the Haudenosaunee for Station 4, and the lesson reading's sorting of the Columbian Exchange for Station 5.
- Population figures are contemporary scholarly estimates and are labeled as estimates wherever they appear: perhaps fifty million people or more in the Americas in 1491, perhaps two hundred thousand people in Tenochtitlan in 1500, and nine people in ten dead in many regions over the century after 1492.

## Addendum, 2026-10-23: images added for the improved slide standard pilot (deck v2)

Five images were added to the deck for the v2 pilot, each verified public domain or CC0 on its file page before download. Deck crops (portrait, for the right-hand photo slot) live in slides-build/img/.

- Village of Secotan (secotan-de-bry-1590.jpg; deck crop secotan-port.jpg). Theodor de Bry engraving, 1590, after John White's 1585 drawing of the Algonquian town of Secotan in present day North Carolina. Library of Congress, LCCN 2001695723, via Wikimedia Commons file "Village of Secotan) - T.B LCCN2001695723.jpg". Public domain (work of 1590; LoC scan). Used on Slide 5.
- Monks Mound aerial (monks-mound-nara-aerial.jpg; deck crop monks-mound-port.jpg). "Great River Road - An Aerial View of Monks Mound", National Archives identifier 7718947, via Wikimedia Commons. Public domain as a work of the United States federal government. This closes the Cahokia gap recorded above: the earlier build could not fetch a NARA file page for verification; the Commons file page was verified on 2026-10-23. Used on Slide 10.
- Three Caravels in a Rising Squall (caravels-huys-bruegel-1565.jpg; deck crop caravels-port.jpg). Frans Huys after Pieter Bruegel the Elder, 1565, National Gallery of Art (NGA 47599), via Wikimedia Commons. CC0. Used on Slide 14, captioned in the notes as larger than Columbus's ships but of the period type.
- Columbus Letter ship woodcut (columbus-letter-ship-1493.jpg; deck crop letter-ship-port.jpg). "Oceanica Classis" woodcut from the Basel edition of Columbus's letter, 1493, via Wikimedia Commons file "Columbus Letter (Basel 1493) Illustration 3.jpg". Public domain. Used on Slide 17.
- Buffalo Hunt, A Numerous Group (catlin-buffalo-hunt.jpg; deck crop catlin-hunt-port.jpg). George Catlin, chromolithograph from Catlin's North American Indian Portfolio (1844), painted from his 1830s travels, via Wikimedia Commons. Public domain. Used on Slide 19, dated honestly in the notes as a picture of the horse culture three centuries after horses spread.
- Evaluated and not used: the 1886 Popular Science Monthly engraving of the Hiawatha wampum belt (public domain) was downloaded for Slide 12, but the belt's wide format crops past legibility in the deck's portrait photo slot, so Slide 12 stays text. The no-image decision for epidemic disease stands.
