# Colonial America — Unit Manifest

**Essential question:** How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

**Course position:** The first unit of the US History course (courseId u1), built ninth in the build order. It opens in 1491, before colonization, with the Americas and Europe on the eve of contact, and closes at the hinge of 1763, with the Treaty of Paris, Pontiac's Rebellion, and the Proclamation line drawn. The Founding unit owns the imperial crisis from 1763, and this unit does not open it: the Stamp Act is named nowhere in any file. Indigenous peoples are historical actors throughout, with their own politics, diplomacy, and strategies stated from the record. Enslavement is taught with the documentary sobriety the course uses for hard subjects, from documents and the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database's counts, labeled as its estimates wherever they appear.

**Built:** 2026-10-22. 51 documents, 232 printed pages. 8 slide decks, 216 slides, 33,047 words of speaker notes, 8 printable teacher notes documents (77 pages).

**Topics (6):** 1 Two worlds meet: the Americas and Europe before and at contact · 2 Empires in America: Spain, France, and England compete · 3 Regional colonial societies: Chesapeake, New England, and the Middle Colonies · 4 The Atlantic system: trade, mercantilism, and enslaved labor · 5 Colonial life: government, religion, and family · 6 Empire tested: the imperial wars and the hinge of 1763.

**Test blueprint:** 15 entries, two per lesson except Lesson 8 (one). Entry-to-topic map and per-lesson item assignments are in the skeleton: ~/workspace/curriculum-planner/colonial-america-skeleton.md.

**TPSP source plan:** The bank HAS a colonial era file: ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/colonial.json, era "Colonization and Colonial America (1492-1763)", 20 audited records; the neighboring early-modern-empire.json carries the Díaz entry into Tenochtitlan and the Code Noir of 1685, both used in Lesson 2. There is NO colonial premium DBQ in the audited bank: DBQ 1, "Breaking with Britain," belongs to the Founding unit. No picker lead is invented; when the planner integration is built, the picker leads with the colonial era records themselves. This unit's quotations are drawn from the bank's verified records wherever the bank carries the text, and otherwise from the named editions in each lesson's img/SOURCES.md.

**Copyright accounting:** No copyrighted work is quoted anywhere in this unit. Every quotation is a public domain period text quoted from a named edition: the audited bank records, Hening's Statutes at Large, McCord's South Carolina statutes, the Statutes of the Realm in transcription, the Avalon Project, Founders Online, the Text Creation Partnership, or Virtual Jamestown. Modern transcriptions were checked for their basis before use; where an edition modernizes spelling, the Source line says so.

**Definition overlap check:** The nine earlier-built units were searched for shared vocabulary terms (mercantilism, triangular trade, Great Awakening, salutary neglect, Enlightenment, joint-stock company, chattel slavery, Navigation Acts, and the rest of this unit's list). No earlier-built unit carries a definition for any of this unit's 56 terms, so no character-for-character carry was owed; the check and its result are recorded here so the Founding and Early Republic builds, when they come, can run the same check against this unit.

## Lesson 1 — Two Worlds Before Contact

Unit opener. The Americas and Europe in 1491 through the first exchanges. Lesson question: "What did the Americas and Europe each look like in 1491, and what changed when they met?"

- Reading: 892 words, 4 pages. Seven sections matching the skeleton's scope: the Americas on their own terms (Tenochtitlan, Cahokia, the Haudenosaunee league), Europe on the eve (a unified Spain, the Reconquista completed in January 1492, Portuguese caravels), Columbus's voyages and his printed 1493 letter, and the Columbian Exchange closing on the demographic catastrophe, with population figures labeled contemporary estimates. Sources: the Columbus letter, audited bank record (Gilder Lehrman Institute edition), quoted in the activity, quick check, writing bank, and deck notes, cuts marked.
- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 7 pages, five stations. Notebook page: 2 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, tagged to blueprint entries 1 and 2. Writing task: 4 pages.
- Deck: 26 slides, speaker notes 128 to 176 words a slide, teacher notes document 9 pages. Images: the 1524 map of Tenochtitlan (Newberry Library, public domain) and the del Piombo portrait said to be Columbus (Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access), captioned "said to be" with its 1519 date.
- Honest flags: No Cahokia image is used. The best known Monks Mound photographs on Commons are CC BY-SA, not public domain, and one National Archives aerial file page could not be fetched for verification. Cahokia is taught from a labeled summary and text and stat slides. No period image of the Haudenosaunee council or of a caravel with a verified public domain basis was selected, and no image of epidemic disease is used; those subjects run in text and numbers, and img/SOURCES.md says so.

## Lesson 5 — The Middle Colonies and the Atlantic Economy

- Guided notes: 5 pages (28 blanks). Activity: 7 pages, five stations. Notebook page: 2 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, tagged to entries 9 and 10. Writing task: 3 pages.
- Deck: 28 slides, speaker notes 140 to 185 words a slide, three giant stat slides, teacher notes document 10 pages. ## Lesson 6 — Slavery and the Slave Trade

## Lesson 8 — Empire and War: The French and Indian War

The Ohio Valley contest through the settlement of 1763. Lesson question: "How did Britain win North America, and what did the victory cost everyone who fought it?"

- Reading: 892 words, 5 pages. Sources: Franklin's Join, or Die cartoon (1754) from the audited bank record (Library of Congress edition). Washington's Journal of Major George Washington (Williamsburg, 1754) quoted verbatim from the Founders Online, National Archives transcription, spelling as the edition carries it; the 1754 capitulation is paraphrased only, and no English wording of it is put in quotation marks. The Albany Plan, the Treaty of Paris (Articles IV and VII read and paraphrased), and the Proclamation of 1763 (quoted verbatim) come from the Avalon Project transcriptions at Yale. The Pontiac station is a teaching summary labeled on its face; no speech is quoted for Pontiac because no verbatim text was verified from a named edition. Casualty figures are labeled estimates; the British debt is stated only as nearly doubled, with no invented pound figure. The Stamp Act is named nowhere in the lesson or the unit.
- Guided notes: 5 pages. Activity: 7 pages, five stations. Notebook page: 3 pages. Quick check: 2 pages, tagged to entry 15. Writing task: 4 pages.
- Deck: 28 slides, speaker notes 130 to 179 words a slide, teacher notes document 10 pages. Images: the Join, or Die woodcut itself (Library of Congress item 2002695523, no known restrictions), the Mitchell Map (1755 to 1757), the Bowen and Gibson map of North America under the Treaty of Paris, and West's Death of General Wolfe (1770), deck only, captioned in the notes as a commemoration painted eleven years after the battle.
