Guided Notes: New England: Covenant and Community

Lesson question: What kind of community did New England's settlers mean to build, and who could belong to it?

Unit question: How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

Key Terms

Mayflower Compact: the agreement signed aboard the Mayflower in 1620 by the Pilgrims, creating a government by the consent of the governed for the Plymouth colony

Puritans: English Protestants who sought to purify the Church of England of Catholic practices and built Massachusetts Bay as a model godly society

covenant: in Puritan belief, a solemn agreement with God that bound a church or a whole community to live by his laws and brought his judgment if they failed

Anne Hutchinson: the Massachusetts Bay religious dissenter whose teaching that faith alone brought salvation challenged the colony's ministers and led to her trial and banishment in 1637 and 1638

Roger Williams: the Massachusetts minister banished in 1636 for arguing that the king had no right to grant Indigenous land and that government could not compel worship, who founded Rhode Island on religious toleration

Fundamental Orders of Connecticut: the document of 1639, often called the first written constitution in English America, that set up Connecticut's government by the votes of its freemen

Salem witch trials: the crisis of 1692 in Massachusetts in which accusations of witchcraft led to trials that hanged nineteen people and pressed one man to death before the colony repented

Summary table. The founding: at Plymouth in 1620 forty-one men made a government by consent in the Mayflower Compact, and Massachusetts Bay from 1630 built Winthrop's covenant commonwealth; women, children, servants, and Native peoples stood outside the signing and the franchise, and Plymouth survived by a Wampanoag alliance Massasoit chose after the epidemic of 1616 to 1619. The town: families in meetinghouse towns, land broadly granted, town meetings, tax supported ministers, and schools ordered in 1647; everyone paid the minister's tax whether or not they belonged to the church. The dissent: Williams banished in 1636 and Hutchinson tried in 1637 and banished, because dissent read as a tear in the covenant. The wars: the Pequot fort at Mystic burned in 1637 with hundreds killed, and Metacom's coalition in 1675 and 1676 struck back at fifty years of land loss in the deadliest per capita war in the colonies' history; Native captives were sold into slavery and Native power in southern New England was broken. Salem: in 1692 the court admitted spectral evidence, hanged nineteen people, and pressed Giles Corey to death before ministers attacked the evidence and the colony repented.
