# Image and Source Notes: Lesson 4, New England: Covenant and Community

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages or by the holding institution named below. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/.

## The Mayflower Compact, 1620 (compact.jpg; deck crop compact-port.jpg)
- Title: The Mayflower Compact, 1620
- Creator: Jean Leon Gerome Ferris (1863 to 1930), painter; printed and distributed by the Detroit Publishing Company
- Date: Painting from Ferris's series The Pageant of a Nation; the artist died in 1930, and the Detroit Publishing Company print was published in the United States before 1931
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, digital identifier cph.3g07155, via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Mayflower_Compact_1620_cph.3g07155.jpg
- Public domain basis: Commons file page marks the image Public domain (PD-US, published before January 1, 1931; the Library of Congress places no restrictions on this Detroit Publishing Company print).
- Caption discipline: this is a later artist's imagining of the signing. No picture of the signing was made in 1620, and the documents in this lesson say so on their faces wherever the image appears.

- Title: Landing of the Pilgrims on Plymouth Rock, 1620
- Creator: Joseph Andrews (1806 to 1873), engraver, after Peter Frederick Rothermel (1817 to 1895)
- Date: 1869
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1869_pilgrims_Plymouth_Massachusetts_engr_byAndrews_LC_00035u.jpg

- The same engraving is held by the Metropolitan Museum of Art as object 49.116.69, which the Met's collection page describes as an etching and engraving of 1869, after Rothermel, by Joseph Andrews.
- Caption discipline: an imagining made 249 years after the landing, captioned as such where it appears in the deck notes.

## A Map of New England (hubbard-map.jpg; deck crop hubbard-map-port.jpg)
- Title: A map of New England, being the first that ever was here cut
- Creator: John Foster (1648 to 1681), woodcut, printed at Boston for William Hubbard's A Narrative of the Troubles with the Indians in New-England
- Date: 1677
- Holder: via Wikimedia Commons (the "White Hills" Boston edition of the map)
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hubbard_map_1677.JPG
- Public domain basis: Commons file page marks the image Public domain, a work published in 1677.
- Notes: the first map printed in English America. It is oriented with west at the top. Numbers beside town names mark places attacked in the wars of 1675 and 1676, keyed to Hubbard's text. The Massachusetts Historical Society and the Library of Congress hold copies of the 1677 printings.

## Witchcraft at Salem Village (salem.jpg; deck crop salem-port.jpg)
- Title: Witchcraft at Salem Village
- Creator: unattributed engraver, published in William A. Crafts, Pioneers in the Settlement of America: From Florida in 1510 to California in 1849 (Boston: Samuel Walker and Company, 1876). The central figure in the courtroom is usually identified as Mary Walcott.
- Date: 1876
- Holder: via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Witchcraft_at_Salem_Village.jpg
- Public domain basis: the Commons file page states the file is in the public domain in the United States, its first publication having occurred prior to January 1, 1931 (1876).
- Caption discipline: an engraving of 1876, made 184 years after the trials. No picture of the 1692 trials was made at the time. Wherever the image appears in this lesson, the caption says so, and the trial record at Activity Station 5 is presented as the evidence.

## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- John Winthrop portrait: a Commons file page for the 1834 Charles Osgood copy portrait (Massachusetts Historical Society) was located in search results, but the file page itself could not be fetched for verification during this build, so no Winthrop portrait is used. Winthrop is carried in text, in his own words.
- Anne Hutchinson and Roger Williams: no contemporary portrait of either exists. Later statues and imagined likenesses were not verified to a public domain file page during this build and are not used; both figures are taught from the trial record and the banishment record.
- The Pequot fort at Mystic and King Philip's War: no period image of the Mystic massacre or of Metacom verified to a public domain file page was used as decoration. The wars are carried in plain text and in Foster's 1677 map, a period document drawn for Hubbard's account of the same wars.
- A New England meetinghouse: no photograph or print of a seventeenth century meetinghouse was verified to a public domain file page during this build, so none is used; the town is taught from the reading and from Foster's map.

## Quoted texts and their editions
All period quotations in this lesson are drawn from the audited Primary Source Project bank file colonial.json, and each document's Source line names the edition the bank record's sourceNote names:
- Pilgrim settlers, "Mayflower Compact," 1620 (Yale Avalon Project; from Francis Newton Thorpe, The Federal and State Constitutions, 1909). Quoted in the reading (Station 1 of the activity quotes it in full form with one cut, marked by ellipses).
- John Winthrop, "A Modell of Christian Charitie," 1630 (VIVA Pressbooks, American Literature I; seventeenth-century spelling retained). Quoted in the reading and at Station 2, with cuts marked by ellipses.
- John Winthrop, letter describing Boston, 1634 (Gilder Lehrman Institute). The closing sentence on smallpox and title is quoted in the reading and in the writing task's evidence bank; the population figure of about 4,000 souls is quoted from the same record.
- Massachusetts Bay General Court, "Anne Hutchinson Trial Examination," 1637 (East Tennessee State University; from John Winthrop's Short Story, 1644). Quoted in the reading, the quick check key, and at Station 3, with cuts marked by ellipses. The record is the court's own, and the documents say so.
- Freemen of the Connecticut River towns, "Fundamental Orders of Connecticut," 1639 (Yale Avalon Project; from Francis Newton Thorpe, The Federal and State Constitutions, 1909). The preamble, Order 1, Order 4, and Order 10 are quoted in the reading's summary form and at Station 4, with cuts marked by ellipses.
- Essex County Court of Oyer and Terminer, "Records of the Trial and Execution of Sarah Good," 1692 (Salem Witch Trials Documentary Archive, via The American Revolution Primary Source Readings). The indictment and the Abbey deposition are quoted in the reading and at Station 5, with cuts marked by ellipses; the hanging date of July 19, 1692 and the count of seventeen head of cattle come from the same record.
- No period text is quoted from memory anywhere in this lesson. No summary in this lesson is presented as a transcription.
