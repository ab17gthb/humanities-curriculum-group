Guided Notes: Jamestown and the Chesapeake

Lesson question: How did a starving outpost become a tobacco colony, and what did tobacco demand?

Unit question: How did the meeting of Europe, Africa, and the Americas remake all three worlds, and what kind of society grew up in England's colonies?

Key Terms

joint-stock company: a business owned by investors who pool their money and share its costs, risks, and profits, the form used to fund the first English colonies

Jamestown: the first permanent English settlement in North America, founded in 1607 on the James River in Virginia by the Virginia Company

cash crop: a crop grown for sale and profit rather than for the grower's own use, as tobacco was grown in the Chesapeake

headright system: the Virginia policy that granted fifty acres of land to any person who paid for an immigrant's passage to the colony, used to recruit laborers

indentured servant: a laborer who agreed to work for a set number of years, usually four to seven, in exchange for passage to America and freedom dues at the end of the term

House of Burgesses: the first elected assembly in English America, which met at Jamestown in 1619, where landowning colonists chose representatives to make the colony's laws

Bacon's Rebellion: the Virginia uprising of 1676 led by Nathaniel Bacon, in which frontier settlers and former servants attacked Indigenous peoples and burned Jamestown, pushing great planters toward enslaved labor

The Colony at a Glance. The company: the Virginia Company of London, a joint-stock company chartered in 1606, pooled investors' money and shared the venture's costs, risks, and profits. It demanded a return, and for years the colony produced none, which is why a marketable crop mattered more than survival alone. The crop: John Rolfe's West Indian tobacco, planted from about 1612, became the cash crop England bought. It demanded fresh land as it exhausted the soil, and hands in every season. The labor: indentured servants recruited under the headright system, fifty acres for every passage paid, working four to seven year terms and dying in large numbers in their first summers. The turn: after Bacon's Rebellion of 1676, in which former servants burned Jamestown, great planters shifted decisively toward enslaved African labor, bound for life and unable to claim land or freedom at a term's end.

Exit ticket 1. A joint-stock company is a business owned by investors who pool their money and share its costs, risks, and profits. The Virginia Company of London founded Jamestown, and King James I chartered it in 1606.

Exit ticket 2. In the summer of 1619 the House of Burgesses, the first elected assembly in English America, met at Jamestown. Weeks later a ship arrived carrying some twenty Africans taken from a Portuguese slave ship, the first Africans recorded in England's mainland colonies. Their legal status is not clear in the surviving records and is still argued by historians.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer pairing one land fact with one labor fact. The strongest answers run from tobacco exhausting the soil and pushing settlement onto Powhatan land, to the labor supply: indentured servants under the headright system first, and after 1676 the decisive turn to enslaved African labor.
