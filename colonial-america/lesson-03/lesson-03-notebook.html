Teacher Answer Key

Part of the lessonWhat happenedThe problem or demandThe result
The ventureThe Virginia Company of London, a joint-stock company chartered by King James I in 1606, sent 144 men and boys in three ships to found a colony for profit.Investors had pooled their money and shared the venture's costs, risks, and profits, and they expected a return the colony did not produce for years.Jamestown was founded in May 1607 on a James River peninsula, as a business venture first and a settlement second.
The starving timeIn the winter of 1609 and 1610 about sixty of some five hundred colonists survived. The colony's accounts record the starving eating horses, dogs, and rats, and in documented cases the dead.A defensive site with brackish water and disease, a company of gentlemen and few farmers.The survivors were abandoning Jamestown when a relief fleet turned them back in June 1610. The colony lived, on a margin its records do not hide.
The chiefdomWahunsenacawh ruled some thirty peoples from Werowocomoco. He traded corn to the English, tested their strength, withheld food when it served him, and weighed using them against his enemies or driving them out.A few hundred hungry armed strangers had settled inside his country, useful for trade goods and dangerous in their hunger for land.Jamestown survived its first years by his decision as much as by English effort. John Smith's accounts of the dealing grew across 1608, 1616, and 1624, and the rescue appears only in the later tellings.
The cropJohn Rolfe planted a milder West Indian tobacco from about 1612, and England's market took all Virginia could ship.Tobacco exhausted the soil within a few years and demanded hands at every stage in every season.Tobacco became the cash crop. Settlement spread along the rivers onto Powhatan land, and the colony organized itself around recruiting bound labor.
The year 1619The House of Burgesses, the first elected assembly in English America, met at Jamestown. Weeks later a ship sold the colony some twenty Africans taken from a Portuguese slave ship.Colonists wanted a voice in their laws. The Africans' legal status, servant for a term or enslaved for life, is not clear in the surviving records and is still argued by historians.Virginia gained representative government and the beginning of African slavery in England's mainland colonies in a single summer. Slavery in law was built afterward, statute by statute.
The rebellionIn 1676 Nathaniel Bacon led frontier settlers and former servants against Indigenous peoples and against Governor Berkeley. They burned Jamestown, and the rising collapsed when Bacon died.Former servants who survived their terms found the best land taken and the governor unwilling to loose them against the Powhatan peoples.Virginia's great planters turned decisively toward enslaved African labor, bound for life, unable to claim land or freedom at a term's end.

The colony in one sentence. Strong answers name both the rescue and the demands. The outpost survived on Wahunsenacawh's corn, Smith's discipline, and the 1610 relief fleet, and became a tobacco colony when Rolfe's crop found England's market. Tobacco then demanded fresh land, taken from the Powhatan peoples as the soil failed, and labor, supplied first by indentured servants under the headright system and, after former servants burned Jamestown in 1676, decisively by enslaved Africans.
