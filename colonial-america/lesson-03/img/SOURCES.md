## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- No verified public domain period image of the starving time, of Bacon's Rebellion, or of the burning of Jamestown in 1676 was used in this lesson. Those subjects are taught in text and in the stat slides rather than illustrated with later romantic paintings.
- A Wellcome Collection etching of a tobacco plant (Wellcome V0042731) was considered and rejected: its Commons file page licenses it CC BY 4.0, not public domain, and this unit uses public domain images only.
- No image of the first Africans of 1619 exists. The subject is taught in text, with the honest uncertainty about their legal status stated, and is not illustrated.

## Quoted source log for this lesson
- John Smith, A True Relation, 1608 (Pocahontas Archive, Lehigh University; from Philip L. Barbour, The Complete Works of Captain John Smith). Audited TPSP bank record smith-true-relation-1608, colonial.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 1, with cuts marked and Smith's spelling as the edition carries it.
- John Smith, Letter to Queen Anne, 1616 (Hanover College; spelling modernized, as the bank record's sourceNote states). Audited TPSP bank record smith-letter-queen-anne-1616, colonial.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 2, with cuts marked.
- John Smith, The General History of Virginia, New England and the Summer Isles, 1624 (Hanover College; spelling modernized). Audited TPSP bank record smith-general-history-1624, colonial.json. The rescue scene is described and compared in the reading, section D, and in the deck, and is not quoted at length in the student documents.
- Nathaniel Bacon, Declaration in the Name of the People, 1676 (American History from Revolution to Reconstruction, as the bank record's sourceNote names its edition). Audited TPSP bank record bacon-declaration-1676, colonial.json. Quoted in the activity, Station 5, with cuts marked and Bacon's spelling as the edition carries it.
- The headright grant and the first meeting of the House of Burgesses are taught in the activity, Station 3, as a teaching summary of the Virginia Company's instructions of 1618 and the colony's report of the assembly of July 30 to August 4, 1619. The station states on its face that it is a summary written for this lesson, not a quotation. The starving time figures in Station 2 are likewise labeled on their face as a teaching summary compiled from the colony's own accounts.
