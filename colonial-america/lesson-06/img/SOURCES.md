# Image Sources: Lesson 6, Slavery and the Slave Trade

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages or by the holding institution. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/ and img/.

## Stowage of the British Slave Ship Brookes (brookes.jpg; deck crop brookes-port.jpg)
- Title: Stowage of the British slave ship "Brookes" under the regulated slave trade act of 1788
- Creator: Printed Ephemera Collection (publisher); plate produced for the Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade, Plymouth chapter, 1788
- Date: 1788 (Liverpool printing, per the Library of Congress citation for item 98504459)
- Holder: Library of Congress, Rare Book and Special Collections Division, Broadside Port. 282, no. 43; LC-USZ62-44000; digital ID cph.3a44236
- Source: https://www.loc.gov/item/98504459/ and the Wikimedia Commons file "Stowage of the British slave ship Brookes under the regulated slave trade act of 1788 LCCN98504459.jpg"
- Public domain basis: A work first published in 1788; the Library of Congress records the item as not in copyright, public domain. The Kennesaw State Digital Histories record for this item, sourced to the Library of Congress, states the same rights.
- Use rule for this lesson: the plate is used only in Station 1 of the activity, in the quick check, and on one deck slide, each time captioned with its own printed title, "Stowage of the British Slave Ship 'Brookes' under the Regulated Slave Trade Act of 1788," with its abolitionist makers and purpose stated in the caption and the speaker notes. It is not used as decoration anywhere in the lesson. Its printed note, quoted in the documents, states that after the act the Brookes was allowed 454 people and had carried as many as 609 before it.

## Olaudah Equiano, frontispiece portrait (equiano.png; deck crop equiano-port.jpg)
- Title: Olaudah Equiano, frontispiece to The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano, or Gustavus Vassa, the African
- Creator: Engraved by Daniel Orme (signed "D. Orme sculpt." on the plate), after W. Dinton
- Date: 1789
- Holder: Frontispiece of the London 1789 first edition; file via Wikimedia Commons, "Olaudah Equiano - Project Gutenberg eText 15399 (cropped).png," from Project Gutenberg eText 15399
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Olaudah_Equiano_-_Project_Gutenberg_eText_15399_(cropped).png
- Public domain basis: An engraving published in 1789; copyright expired in the United States and in countries with a term of the author's life plus 100 years or less, as the file's licensing states. Royal Museums Greenwich's collection record for this frontispiece confirms it is the only securely attributed image of Equiano and dates it to the 1789 first edition.

## Texts quoted in this lesson, with their editions
- Olaudah Equiano, The Interesting Narrative (1789), Middle Passage excerpt: quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record equiano-middle-passage in colonial.json; the edition named in that record is Documenting the American South, University of North Carolina, from the London 1789 first edition, original spelling and punctuation preserved.
- Phillis Wheatley, "On Being Brought from Africa to America" (1773), printed entire, all eight lines: quoted verbatim from the audited TPSP record wheatley-on-being-brought in colonial.json; the edition named in that record is Phillis Wheatley, "On Being Brought from Africa to America," 1773 (LitCharts). The date 1773 is stated wherever the poem appears in this lesson.
- Virginia, December 1662, Act XII, and September 1667, Act III: William Waller Hening, ed., The Statutes at Large, vol. 2, pp. 170 and 260, as transcribed by Virtual Jamestown, Virginia Center for Digital History, University of Virginia (https://jamestown.vcdh.virginia.edu/laws1.html). Original spelling preserved.
- South Carolina, An Act for the Better Ordering and Governing Negroes and Other Slaves in This Province, No. 670 (1740), Sections I, III, XXXVI, and XLV: David J. McCord, ed., The Statutes at Large of South Carolina, vol. 7 (Columbia, SC: A.S. Johnston, 1840), as transcribed in the South Carolina ETV Teaching American History transcription of the 1740 slave codes (digital.scetv.org), with the text checked against the US History Scene excerpts of the same act.
- Population and survival figures: Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (slavevoyages.org), stated as its estimates wherever they appear: some 12.5 million Africans forced aboard, about 10.7 million surviving the crossing.
- The Stono narrative in Station 4 of the activity and in the documents is a teaching summary written for this lesson and is labeled as a teaching summary on its face in the activity. It is not presented as a period text.

## Images searched for and not used
- A period map of the Atlantic slave trade and period views of rice and tobacco plantation work were searched for in the Library of Congress, the Metropolitan Museum Open Access, Smithsonian Open Access, and Wikimedia Commons. No file for those subjects was verified as public domain and downloaded for this lesson in this build, so Sections A, D, and E of the deck are taught with text and stat slides rather than with an unverified image, and no catalog number is claimed for any image not listed above.
- No image of violence, of the trade's victims in suffering, or of any named enslaved person other than Equiano's own published frontispiece portrait is used anywhere in this lesson. The Brookes plate is used only under the rule stated in its entry above.
