## Quotation log

### Flushing Remonstrance (audited bank record flushing-remonstrance-1657; edition: Edward Hart and 30 residents of Flushing, Flushing Remonstrance, 1657, Americans United, as the record's sourceNote names it)
- Quoted in the activity, Station 1, in three passages, with the record's spelling kept: the refusal to judge or condemn; the law of love, peace and liberty extending to Jews, Turks and Egyptians; and free egresse and regresse for those who come in love. Cuts inside passages are marked by ellipses where they occur in the lesson's reading paraphrase; the activity passages are continuous excerpts of the record's text.
- The reading and guided notes paraphrase the remonstrance and name its grounds without quotation, except the short phrases the record itself makes famous, presented as the petitioners' own words in paraphrase context.

### Maryland Toleration Act (audited bank record maryland-toleration-act-1649; edition: Maryland General Assembly, Maryland Toleration Act, 1649, Yale Avalon Project, as the record's sourceNote names it)
- Quoted in the activity, Station 2: the penalty clause on denying the holy Trinity, punished with death and confiscation; the protection clause, that no person professing to believe in Jesus Christ shall be troubled, Molested or discountenanced for religion; and the act's own list of reproachful religious names, fined at ten shillings an offence. Cuts are marked by ellipses. The act is taught whole in this lesson, protection and penalties together, in the reading (Section D), the activity, the quick check, and the deck (Section D, two slides).

### Navigation Acts, 1651 and 1660 (not in the bank; edition used stated here as the spec requires)
- Edition used: the Statutes of the Realm text of the Navigation Act of 1651 and the Navigation Act of 1660 (12 Charles II, c. 18), as transcribed in the excerpts collection hosted by East Tennessee State University at faculty.etsu.edu/history/documents/navacts.htm, checked against the same transcription of the 1660 act at Founding.com (Excerpts from the Navigation Acts, 1660 to 1696). The builder read both transcriptions in full for the passages quoted and confirmed they agree word for word in those passages.
- Quoted in the activity, Station 3: the 1651 act's clause barring goods of Asia, Africa, or America from England in foreign ships; the 1660 act's shipping clause (English or colonial ships, master and three fourths of mariners English, under forfeiture of goods and ship); and the 1660 act's enumeration clause (sugars, tobacco, cotton-wool, indigoes, ginger, fustic, or other dyeing wood, shipped only to other English plantations or to England, Ireland, Wales, or Berwick upon Tweed). Cuts are marked by ellipses and disclosed in the station's Source line. Spelling follows the transcription, with the 1651 act's Commonwealth spellings (onely, moyety in the full text) kept where quoted.
- No Avalon Project or British History Online transcription of the acts was used in this build; the Avalon Project carries the Maryland Toleration Act used above, and the acts' transcription actually read and quoted is the Statutes of the Realm text as hosted above. Recorded here so the edition claim in every document matches the edition actually used.

### Summary and teaching records, labeled on their faces
- Activity Station 4 (Penn's invitation and the Lenape purchase) is a summary record written for this lesson, resting on the Pennsylvania charter of 1681 and Penn's published invitation of 1681 and its Dutch and German editions. No sentence in it is presented as Penn's exact words.
- Activity Station 5 (the Atlantic's cargoes) is a teaching table built for this lesson from the reading's flows, labeled as a summary device and not a period document.

## Figures stated as estimates
- Colonial population: about 250,000 in 1700 and about 2.5 million by 1775. Both figures are labeled estimates wherever they appear in this lesson, in the reading, guided notes, deck, notebook key, and writing evidence bank.
- Philadelphia as the largest city in British America by 1750 rests on contemporary counts and is stated in the reading without a precise population figure, because surviving counts differ.

## Gaps recorded honestly
- No period image of the Lenape, of a Lenape council, or of Shackamaxon is used in this lesson. The famous images of Penn's treaty are later paintings and prints (Benjamin West's 1771 painting and its engravings, and the Ferris paintings used above, which are identified in the notes as later imaginings). Section C of the deck therefore uses the 1893 Ridpath illustration of colonists on the Delaware for its second slide and text treatment for its divider, and no image in this lesson is captioned as a depiction of an actual Lenape person or event.
- No public domain period image of Flushing or of the remonstrance's signing was verified in this build, so Station 1 and slide 17 use the Visscher map of the colony the petitioners lived in, captioned as a map.
- The two Ferris paintings are romanticized early twentieth century works. They are used as opening and divider images only, are named as Ferris paintings in the speaker notes and here, and are never captioned as period evidence.
