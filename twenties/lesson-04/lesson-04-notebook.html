Teacher Answer Key

TopicWhat the government didWhat it left undone or refusedResult or risk
Harding and Teapot DomeHarding appointed able men such as Hughes, Mellon, and Hoover, and also Ohio friends. Secretary Fall leased the Teapot Dome and Elk Hills naval oil reserves in secret, without competitive bidding.Harding did not supervise the friends he trusted. No open bidding, no public notice, and no check on Fall's dealings with Sinclair and Doheny.Fall took $100,000 in cash from Doheny and loans and gifts from Sinclair, was exposed by the Walsh investigation, and went to prison in 1929.
Coolidge, Mellon, and the tax cutsMellon's program cut income tax rates in 1921, 1924, and 1926. The top rate fell from 73 percent to 25 percent. Budgets ran surpluses.Coolidge kept regulation light and twice vetoed farm price support. The government did not use its surpluses for farm relief or new programs.Most of the benefit of the cuts went to the wealthy, by the critics' account, while farmers and wage earners saw little change.
The Fordney-McCumber TariffThe act of 1922 raised duties on farm products and manufactured goods to some of the highest levels in American history.The administration never reconciled the tariff with the war debts. It refused to lower duties so Europeans could earn dollars by trade.Protected industries gained. European debtors lost the trade income they needed to pay the United States, which pushed repayment onto borrowed money.
The Washington ConferenceHughes proposed naval limits in public in November 1921. The Five-Power Treaty fixed fleets at 5 to 5 to 3, paused battleship building for ten years, and scrapped warships.The treaties carried no standing obligation to act and no connection to the League of Nations, which the United States still refused to join.The naval race stopped for a decade at a saving to all three treasuries. The limits bound ships, not the rivalries that had built them.
War debts and the Dawes PlanWashington insisted the Allies repay about ten billion dollars. Under the Dawes Plan of 1924, American banks lent to Germany, Germany paid reparations, and the Allies paid the United States.The United States refused to cancel or reduce the debts, and refused to treat the debts and German reparations as one question.Payments flowed while American credit flowed. The whole circle depended on continued American lending, which no one guaranteed.
The Kellogg-Briand Pact and the closed doorsFifteen nations signed the pact at Paris in August 1928, renouncing war as an instrument of national policy. The Senate approved it 85 to 1.The pact had no enforcement: no army, court, or penalty. The United States also stayed out of the World Court and the League.War was renounced on paper by most of the world, with no machinery to act when a signer broke its word.

The pattern in one sentence. Strong answers state the pairing the chart records. The governments acted boldly where action cost no standing commitment: cutting taxes, raising the tariff, limiting fleets by a treaty they wrote themselves, and collecting debts. They refused where action would bind the future: farm relief, the League, the World Court, enforcement of the pact, and cancellation of the debts. Accept any completion that carries that contrast with two rows of evidence.
