Writing Task: Done and Deliberately Undone

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: What did the Republican governments of the 1920s do at home and abroad, and what did they deliberately leave undone? A strong answer covers both halves of the question. It names the policies the administrations carried out at home and abroad, and it names the commitments they refused, on principle, to make. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, with at least one piece from each half of the question, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Secretary Mellon directed tax policy under all three Republican presidents. Congress cut income tax rates in 1921, 1924, and 1926, bringing the top rate down from 73 percent to 25 percent. Budgets ran surpluses.

2. The Fordney-McCumber Tariff of 1922 raised duties on farm products and manufactured goods to some of the highest levels in American history. Coolidge twice vetoed bills that would have committed the government to supporting farm prices.

3. Secretary of the Interior Albert B. Fall secretly leased the Teapot Dome and Elk Hills naval oil reserves without competitive bidding and took $100,000 in cash from Doheny and large loans and gifts from Sinclair. He went to prison in 1929.

4. At the Washington Conference of 1921 to 1922, the Five-Power Treaty fixed the battle fleets of the United States, Britain, and Japan at a ratio of 5 to 5 to 3, paused battleship building for ten years, and ordered warships scrapped. The United States negotiated the treaty itself and stayed out of the League of Nations.

5. The Allies owed the United States about ten billion dollars in war debts. Under the Dawes Plan of 1924, American banks lent money to Germany, Germany paid reparations to the Allies, and the Allies paid the United States. The United States refused to cancel the debts.

6. Fifteen nations signed the Kellogg-Briand Pact in Paris in August 1928, renouncing war as an instrument of national policy. The pact carried no penalty for breaking it, and the Senate approved it 85 to 1 after stating that it required no American enforcement. The United States also stayed out of the World Court.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, develop it with your evidence, and end by stating the pattern that connects what the governments did with what they left undone.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question, treating the refusals as deliberate policy and not as oversight or laziness.
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, with at least one from home policy (bank 1, 2, or 3) and at least one from abroad (bank 4, 5, or 6). Tax rates and the tariff support the home program claim. The farm vetoes and the Teapot Dome leases support the claims about limits and supervision at home. The Washington ratio, the Dawes circle, and the pact without enforcement support the foreign policy claim.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. An essay that lists policies without naming the refusals, or lists refusals without the policies, has answered half the question and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that states the pattern: the governments acted where action carried no standing commitment, and refused where a commitment would bind the future.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Republican governments of the 1920s ran an active state with a short agenda. At home they cut the top income tax rate from 73 percent to 25 percent, raised the Fordney-McCumber Tariff, and kept regulation light, while Coolidge twice vetoed farm price support and Harding's inattention let Secretary Fall lease the naval oil reserves in secret. Abroad the same governments led the Washington Conference, collected the war debts through the Dawes Plan circle, and signed a pact renouncing war. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the refusals (the League, the World Court, enforcement, debt cancellation) and closing on the pattern rather than on any single policy. Blueprint coverage: home evidence assesses Blueprint 5, foreign evidence assesses Blueprint 6.

Common weak answers. A list of accomplishments (taxes cut, fleets limited, war renounced) misses the question's second half and reads the decade as the administrations described it themselves. The opposite error, treating the governments as simply corrupt or idle, cannot account for the Washington treaties, the surpluses, or the third straight election victory in 1928. The lesson's argument is that the action and the refusal were one policy, chosen on purpose.
