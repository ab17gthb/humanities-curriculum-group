Teacher Answer Key

Ledger columnEntry, in your own wordsFact that supports the entryWhere the fact comes from
What the law achievedDrinking fell at first, and the legal saloon closed for good.Consumption dropped sharply in the first years, above all among working class drinkers priced out of the illegal market, and deaths from cirrhosis of the liver fell.Lesson reading, Drinking Moved section, and the closing section on the repeal coalition.
What the law cost, in violenceGangs settled business disputes with guns because they could not use courts.On February 14, 1929, seven men connected to the North Side gang were murdered in a Chicago garage, and no one was convicted.Lesson reading, Violence and Corruption section.
What the law cost, in corruptionOfficials at every level took a share of the illegal profit.The Prohibition Bureau dismissed hundreds of its own poorly paid agents for corruption, and officers and politicians were paid to ignore or protect the trade.Lesson reading, enforcement and corruption sections.
What the law built, in supplyAn organized illegal industry replaced the licensed trade.Smuggling across the Detroit River and from ships offshore, hidden stills, speakeasies estimated in the tens of thousands in New York City alone, and Capone's Outfit with breweries, gambling, and racketeering income.Lesson reading, supply sections. The speakeasy figure is an estimate and should be recorded as one.
What the law built, in politicsA repeal coalition crossed the old dry and wet lines.City voters, business leaders seeking tax revenue, and Pauline Sabin's Women's Organization for National Prohibition Reform, founded in 1929. The Wickersham Commission report of 1931 documented the failure and then split over the remedy.Lesson reading, closing section; activity Stations 4 and 5.

The experiment in one sentence. Strong answers name the mismatch the ledger records. The law set out to end the liquor traffic, and it reduced drinking at first, but demand remained, enforcement was too small and too open to bribery to close the supply, and the trade reorganized itself as an illegal industry with its own institutions and political protection.
