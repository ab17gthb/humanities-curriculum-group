Guided Notes: The Weak Spots and the Crash

Lesson question: Why was the prosperity of the 1920s uneven from the start, and what exactly broke in October 1929?

Unit question: How did the prosperity of the 1920s change American life, and why was that prosperity weaker than it looked?

Key Terms

overproduction: production of more of a good than buyers will purchase at prices that cover the cost of producing it

open shop: a workplace in which workers are not required to join a union in order to hold their jobs

welfare capitalism: an employer's policy of providing benefits such as pensions, paid vacations, and recreation in order to keep workers satisfied and to keep unions out

company union: an organization for discussing workers' grievances that is set up and financed by the employer rather than by an independent union

speculation: buying an asset in the hope of selling it soon at a higher price, rather than for the income the asset produces

buying on margin: buying stock by paying only part of the price and borrowing the rest from a broker, with the stock pledged as security for the loan

Black Tuesday: October 29, 1929, the day of the heaviest selling in the stock market crash of 1929, when about 16.4 million shares were traded

D. Paid at the Top, Borrowed at the Bottom

E. Florida Gave the Warning

F. A Market Built on Borrowed Money

The Dow Jones Industrial Average reached (24) ____________ on September 3, 1929. The Weak Places at a Glance (Key)

Farms. Condition: in depression all decade. Evidence: overproduction held wheat near $1.04 a bushel in 1929 against about $2.16 in 1919, debts stayed at wartime levels, and McNary-Haugen was vetoed twice.

Coal and textiles. Condition: sick industries. Evidence: coal lost customers to oil and natural gas and mine employment fell by roughly a quarter from 1923 to 1929; textiles had more capacity than demand and New England mills faced lower wage southern competition.

Labor. Condition: in decline. Evidence: membership fell from about five million in 1920 to about three and a half million in 1929 under the open shop drive, while welfare capitalism and company unions substituted employer controlled benefits and representation for independent unions.

Income and credit. Condition: concentrated and borrowed. Evidence: a Brookings estimate put about thirty percent of 1929 personal income with the richest five percent, while most durable goods were bought on installment plans.

The stock market. Condition: lifted by speculation and margin credit. Evidence: the Dow Jones average reached 381 on September 3, 1929, and brokers' loans were an estimated $8.5 billion that fall, so a price fall forced immediate selling.

Exit Ticket (Key)

Exit ticket 1. Roughly a quarter of the population lived on farms, so when farm income stayed low all decade, a large share of the country could not buy what its factories produced, and factory demand weakened with farm income.

Exit ticket 2. A margin buyer has borrowed most of the price and pledged the stock as security. When the price falls, the broker demands more cash at once to protect the loan. Buyers who cannot pay are sold out immediately, and that forced selling pushes prices down further, which triggers more demands for cash.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that covers both halves of the lesson question with two facts from the notes. The strongest answers hold that prosperity was uneven because farms, coal, textiles, and organized labor were weak all decade and consumption ran on installment debt while income concentrated at the top, and that what broke in October 1929 was a market built on speculation and margin loans, falling from a Dow Jones peak of 381 to about half that level by mid November. Answers should close by noting that only a small minority owned stock, so the crash by itself did not create the Depression.
