# Image Sources for The Twenties Lesson 1, The Business of America

All images are real public domain images held by the Library of Congress or the New York Public Library, or published in the United States before 1929, and were downloaded from Wikimedia Commons into `lesson-01/assets/` on October 21, 2026. Each download was verified as a valid image file and checked against its catalog record. No image was altered beyond resizing for print.

## 1. highland-park-assembly-line-1913.jpg (used in the quick check)

- Title: Assembly line at the Ford Motor Company's Highland Park plant
- Creator: Unknown photographer; Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division (LCCN2011661021)
- Date: 1913
- What it shows: Model T chassis at successive stages of completion on the moving line inside the Highland Park plant. Verified visually on download.
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Assembly_line_at_the_Ford_Motor_Company%27s_Highland_Park_plant_LCCN2011661021.jpg
- Public domain basis: Photograph taken in 1913 and held by the Library of Congress with no known restrictions. Wikimedia Commons classifies it as public domain.

## 2. ford-assembly-line-workers-1913.jpg (used in Activity Station 1)

- Title: Ford assembly line, 1913
- Creator: Unknown author
- Date: 1913
- What it shows: Workers assembling a Model T automobile on the line. The Michigan State University Making of Modern Michigan listing for the same Detroit Public Library image describes it as an 8x10 photograph of workers assembling a Model T, dated 1913.
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ford_assembly_line_-_1913.jpg
- Public domain basis: Photograph taken in 1913 and published in the United States before 1929. Copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons classifies it as public domain.

## 3. frigidaire-ad-1922.png (used in Activity Station 3)

- Title: Frigidaire iceless fridges, 1922 newspaper advertisement
- Creator: Frigidaire, a product of the General Motors Corporation; placed by Cox and Dance, distributors
- Date: 1922
- What it shows: A full newspaper advertisement for the Frigidaire electric refrigerator under the headline No More Ice, with the line A Home Is No Longer Modern Without Frigidaire. The advertisement text quoted in Station 3 was transcribed from this image and checked line by line against it.
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frigidaire_iceless_fridges_1922.png
- Public domain basis: Advertisement published in the United States in 1922, before 1929. Copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons classifies it as public domain.

## 4. woolworth-store-chicago-1928.jpg (used in Activity Station 5)

- Title: F. W. Woolworth Company 5 and 10 Cent Store, Michigan Avenue, Chicago
- Creator: Unknown author; postcard view held by the New York Public Library (NBY 417646)
- Date: 1928, as dated in the holding record
- What it shows: The street level storefront of the Woolworth five and ten cent store on Michigan Avenue in Chicago, with display windows and the company name across the front. Disclosure: the image is a color postcard view of the store, not a news photograph. It shows the named store at the named location.
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:F.W._Woolworth_Company_5_And_10_Cent_Store,_Michigan_Avenue,_Chicago_(NBY_417646).jpg
- Public domain basis: Published in the United States before 1929. Copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons classifies it as public domain.

## 5. piggly-wiggly-interior-1918.png (used in the notebook page)

- Title: Piggly Wiggly store interior, 1918
- Creator: Clarence Saunders, founder of Piggly Wiggly
- Date: 1918
- What it shows: The interior of the original Piggly Wiggly store in Memphis, Tennessee, the first self service grocery store, opened in 1916.
- Disclosure: This photograph is from 1918, before the 1920s. It is used because it shows the self service chain store form that the 1920s adopted at scale, and the caption in the notebook page states the 1918 date and the reason for its use. It is not presented as a 1920s photograph.
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piggly_Wiggly_store,_1918.png
- Public domain basis: Photograph taken in 1918 and published in the United States before 1929. Copyright expired. Wikimedia Commons classifies it as public domain.

## Teaching materials that are not images

- Activity Station 4 is a model installment contract prepared for this lesson from the standard terms of mid 1920s installment contracts. It is labeled as a model in its Source line and is not a transcription of a surviving contract.
- Activity Station 5 includes a small price comparison table labeled in the document as round teaching figures prepared for this lesson. The figures show the direction and rough size of the chain and independent price gap described by contemporaries. They are not copied from a single surviving price list.
- Model T prices in the reading, Station 1, and the writing evidence bank are Ford list prices for the touring car (1909 $850, 1912 $600, 1914 $490, 1916 $360), with the runabout low of $260 in 1925, as published in period price lists and summarized in modern Ford price histories.
