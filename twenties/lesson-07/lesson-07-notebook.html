Teacher Answer Key

Part of the accountThe factsOne name, place, or date that proves it
Push: leaving the SouthJim Crow law and disfranchisement fixed Black southerners in a lower place. Landlords controlled sharecroppers' accounts. Lynching enforced the order. The boll weevil ruined cotton, and the 1927 Mississippi flood drove families into guarded levee camps.Poll taxes and literacy tests; the boll weevil; the flood of 1927.
Pull: the call of the NorthNorthern mills, stockyards, auto plants, and railroads, short of workers, paid wages several times farm pay. A Black man could vote in most northern states. The Chicago Defender carried the news south by Pullman porter and set a date for leaving together.The Chicago Defender, founded 1905 by Robert S. Abbott; the Great Northern Drive, May 15, 1917; circulation about 125,000 in 1918.
Built: neighborhoods and institutionsMigrants filled neighborhoods with churches, businesses, newspapers, and Urban League offices, and defended their ground in the housing fight against high rents, restrictive covenants, and bombings.Chicago's Black Belt; Detroit's Black Bottom; Cleveland's Central Avenue; Harlem; Dr. Ossian Sweet, acquitted in Detroit in 1925.
Built: the Renaissance and the New NegroWriters and musicians claimed Black life as a subject for serious art and built a national audience. The New Negro refused the assigned place and asserted dignity rather than waiting for it as a favor.Hughes, The Weary Blues, 1926; Hurston's stories; Locke, The New Negro, 1925; Ellington at the Cotton Club from 1927; Bessie Smith's records.
Built: organizationsThe UNIA organized mass membership, race pride, and Black owned business. The NAACP documented lynching and pressed Congress for a federal law. The Urban League met arrivals and opened occupations.Garvey and the Black Star Line, 1919 to 1923; the Dyer bill, passed the House January 1922, killed by filibuster that December.
The answer, and its limitThe building answered exclusion by stating, in institutions, art, and votes, that Black Americans were citizens and creators. The limit is part of the record: the Dyer bill failed, and in 1921 a white mob destroyed Greenwood in Tulsa and no one was punished.Tulsa, May 31 and June 1, 1921: about thirty five blocks of Greenwood burned.

The lesson in one sentence. Strong answers complete the sentence with the building and its purpose. Example: Black Americans who moved north built neighborhoods, a national culture, and mass organizations that answered the country's exclusion by claiming the standing of citizens and creators, against a housing fight, a failed anti-lynching law, and the destruction of Greenwood.
