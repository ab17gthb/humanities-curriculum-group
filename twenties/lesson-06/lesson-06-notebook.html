Teacher Answer Key

The fightWhat happenedThe claim about who belonged, or what was trueThe answer or opposition it met
Restriction by lawThe Emergency Quota Act of 1921 set quotas at 3 percent of each nationality in the census of 1910. The National Origins Act of 1924 cut the rate to 2 percent of the census of 1890 and barred nearly all immigration from Asia. Total admissions fell to 164,667 a year.A nation could be ranked by ancestry before any individual was judged. Germany received 51,227 places a year; Italy received 3,845 and Greece 100.The ranking was written into law and administered for decades. Opposition in the 1920s came from immigrant communities and some employers and scientists, but the system itself was not overturned in the decade.
EugenicsHarry Laughlin of the Eugenics Record Office served the House immigration committee as an expert agent. Madison Grant's The Passing of the Great Race, published in 1916, argued the superiority of northern Europeans.Ability and character were inherited and could be ranked by ancestry, so exclusion could be presented as a scientific finding rather than a preference.Some scientists disputed the findings at the time. Later research discarded them, but in the 1920s the testimony carried expert authority inside Congress.
The second KlanFounded in 1915 and sold by commissioned recruiters, the order grew to membership estimates in the millions. Some 30,000 members paraded in Washington on August 8, 1925. It collapsed after D. C. Stephenson was convicted of murder in 1925 and exposed officials from prison.The real American was white, Protestant, and of the old stock. Catholics, Jews, immigrants, bootleggers, and Black Americans stood outside that definition.Newspapers, rival politicians, and the targeted communities opposed the order, and anti-Klan candidates won office in several states. The decisive blow was internal scandal rather than outside defeat.
Scopes and fundamentalismTennessee passed the Butler Act in March 1925. John Scopes stood trial at Dayton that July. On July 20, Darrow questioned Bryan outdoors, and Bryan conceded the days of creation could be periods. Scopes was convicted and fined 100 dollars.One literal reading of Genesis would decide what a public school could teach about human origins, and teachers who taught evolution broke the law.The ACLU backed the test case and national newspapers covered the trial as a contest over knowledge. The conviction was reversed on a technicality while the law itself was upheld, and Bryan died five days after the trial ended.
Sacco and VanzettiTwo Italian immigrant anarchists were convicted of murder at Dedham in 1921 on disputed identification and firearms evidence. Six years of appeals were denied, the Lowell committee upheld the verdict in 1927, and both men were executed on August 23, 1927.Vanzetti told the court he suffered because he was a radical and because he was an Italian. The defense argued the state had judged the men's identities and beliefs.Protests were held across the United States, Europe, and Latin America. The reviewing institutions, the trial judge, the state supreme court, the Lowell committee, and the governor, each confirmed the verdict.

The decade in one sentence. Strong answers join the two halves of the chart. Each fight was an attempt to fix a definition by authority: Congress, expert testimony, a mass organization, a state law, and a court each claimed the power to decide who counted as a real American or whose account of the world counted as true, and each attempt produced an organized answer from the people it placed outside.
