Guided Notes: The Culture Wars

Lesson question: What were the fights over immigration, religion, and science really about: who counted as a real American, and whose knowledge counted as true?

Unit question: How did the prosperity of the 1920s change American life, and why was that prosperity weaker than it looked?

Key Terms

quota system: a method of limiting immigration by setting a fixed maximum number of immigrants that may be admitted from each country in a year

National Origins Act: the federal immigration law of 1924 that set each country quota at 2 percent of its people counted in the census of 1890 and barred nearly all immigration from Asia

eugenics: a movement that claimed ability and character were inherited and could be ranked by ancestry, and that argued government should favor the groups it ranked high and restrict the groups it ranked low

Ku Klux Klan: the organization revived in 1915 that claimed to defend white Protestant America and used intimidation and violence against Black Americans, Catholics, Jews, immigrants, and others it declared un-American

fundamentalism: a Protestant movement that defended a literal reading of the Bible and opposed public school teaching that conflicted with it

evolution: the scientific theory that species change over generations and that present species, including human beings, developed from earlier forms of life

anarchism: a political belief that government and all ruling authority should be abolished and society organized without a state

The Two Fights at a Glance. Belonging row: Congress decided through the quota formula, and the Klan pressed the same definition in public life; southern and eastern Europeans, Asians, Catholics, Jews, immigrants, and Black Americans were placed outside, on the claim that ancestry determined worth and loyalty. Belief row: state legislatures and courts decided what could be taught and who would be punished; teachers of evolution and two anarchist defendants were placed outside, on the claim that one literal reading of the Bible and one standard of loyalty defined truth and innocence. Accept accurate wording drawn from the notes.

Exit ticket 1. The census of 1890 counted the foreign born before the large immigration from southern and eastern Europe, so applying even a small percentage to that year gave northern and western Europe large quotas and the newer source countries very small ones.

Exit ticket 2. Bryan conceded that the days of creation in Genesis could be periods rather than twenty four hour days. The Butler Act case rested on a literal reading of the Bible account, so the prosecution Bible expert admitting a nonliteral reading on the stand weakened the claim that the law simply defended what the Bible plainly said.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that joins the two halves. Strong answers argue that the fights were contests over definition: the quota tables, the eugenics testimony, and the Klan each tried to fix who counted as a real American, while the Butler Act, the Scopes trial, and the Sacco and Vanzetti case each turned on whose knowledge and whose testimony a court or a school would treat as true.
