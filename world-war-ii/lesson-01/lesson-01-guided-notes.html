Guided Notes: The Road to War: Neutrality and Its Critics

Lesson question: Between 1935 and 1941, how did the United States move from legislated neutrality toward war without declaring it?

Unit question: How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

Key Terms

isolationism: a policy of avoiding political commitments and alliances with other countries while keeping a country free to act on its own

Neutrality Acts: the laws passed by Congress in the 1930s that barred arms sales and loans to nations at war, written to keep the United States out of another world war

fireside chat: one of President Roosevelt's radio addresses to the nation, delivered in plain conversational language from the White House

cash-and-carry: the policy adopted in 1939 that let nations at war buy American arms and goods if they paid cash and carried them away in their own ships

Lend-Lease: the law of March 1941 that let the president lend or lease arms and supplies to any country whose defense he judged vital to the defense of the United States

Four Freedoms: the four freedoms Roosevelt named in his annual message of January 6, 1941 as the foundations of a just world: freedom of speech, freedom of worship, freedom from want, and freedom from fear

Atlantic Charter: the joint statement of aims issued by Roosevelt and Churchill in August 1941, pledging self-determination for peoples, freer trade, and freedom of the seas

A. The Memory of the First War

The United States sent two million men to France in the First World War. About (1) ____________ did not come home, and 200,000 more came home wounded. From 1934 to 1936 a Senate committee chaired by Gerald Nye investigated arms makers and bankers. Its hearings charged that such firms, fearing for their profits and their (2) ____________ to Britain and France, had pulled the country into the war in 1917.

B. The Neutrality Acts

Congress wrote the lesson of the Nye hearings into the Neutrality Acts. The 1935 act barred (3) ____________ exports to any nation at war once the president proclaimed a state of war. Later acts banned (4) ____________ to nations at war and barred American travel on their ships. The acts treated attacker and attacked alike. Their purpose was to keep American ships, goods, and credit out of foreign quarrels.

C. Wars Americans Watched

Japan invaded (5) ____________ in 1931 and China in 1937. Italy conquered Ethiopia in 1935 and 1936, and civil war in Spain drew German and Italian bombers to the side of Franco. Germany absorbed Austria in 1938, took the Sudetenland that fall, seized the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939, and invaded (6) ____________ on September 1, 1939. Britain and France declared war two days later.

D. A President Pushes Against the Current

In Chicago on October 5, 1937, Roosevelt compared aggression to disease and proposed that peace loving nations (7) ____________ aggressor nations, as a community quarantines the sick. Newspapers and Congress attacked the idea, and he let it drop. In December 1940, in a fireside chat, he told the nation it must become the great (8) ____________ of democracy, arming Britain while staying out of the fighting.

E. The Fall of France and the Turn to Aid

Germany overran France in six weeks in 1940, and Britain stood alone. Roosevelt sent Britain (9) ____________ aging destroyers for leases on British bases in the Atlantic, and Congress passed the first peacetime (10) ____________ in American history. Cash-and-carry had already replaced the arms embargo in 1939. In March 1941 Congress passed (11) ____________, the law that let the president lend or lease arms and supplies to any country whose defense he judged vital to the defense of the United States.

F. The Case for Staying Out

The (12) ____________ Committee, founded in September 1940, argued that the United States was safe behind two oceans, that its duty was defense at home, and that aid was pulling the country into a war its people had not chosen. Its best known speaker was the aviator Charles (13) ____________, who charged that the administration moved toward war step by step, each step presented as short of war.

G. The Charter and the Shots at Sea

In August 1941 Roosevelt met Churchill aboard warships off Newfoundland and issued the (14) ____________, pledging self-determination for peoples, freer trade, and freedom of the seas. American destroyers escorting convoys toward Iceland exchanged fire with German submarines. On October 31, 1941, a submarine sank the destroyer (15) ____________, killing 115 of her crew.

Summary table. The memory and the laws: the First World War cost about 116,000 American dead, the Nye Committee charged that arms makers and bankers had pulled the country into that war, and Congress answered with the Neutrality Acts barring arms sales and loans to nations at war, a policy of holding back written into law. The wars watched: Japan in Manchuria and China, Italy in Ethiopia, Spain, and Germany through Austria and Czechoslovakia to Poland, which pulled American sympathy toward the victims while the laws held the country back. The turn to aid: the fall of France, destroyers for bases, the first peacetime draft, cash-and-carry, and then Lend-Lease moved the country toward war in fact while it stayed neutral in law. The argument and the sea war: America First made the case for staying out, the Atlantic Charter joined American aims to a belligerent, and the Greer, Kearny, and Reuben James incidents brought shooting and American deaths before any declaration.

Exit ticket 1. The Neutrality Acts were written to keep the United States out of another world war. Any two bars earn credit: arms sales or exports to nations at war, loans to nations at war, American travel on the ships of nations at war.

Exit ticket 2. Under cash-and-carry, adopted in 1939, a nation at war could buy American arms and goods only if it paid cash and carried them away in its own ships. Under Lend-Lease, the law of March 1941, the president could lend or lease arms and supplies to any country whose defense he judged vital to the defense of the United States, with no cash payment required at the time.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that traces the movement with at least two facts. The strongest answers hold both ends of the road together: the country began with laws barring arms and loans to any nation at war, and by late 1941 it was lending arms to Britain, signing the Atlantic Charter, escorting convoys, and losing the Reuben James, all without a declaration of war.
