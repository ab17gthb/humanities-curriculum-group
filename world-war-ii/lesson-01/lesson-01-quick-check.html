Quick Check: The Arsenal and the Law

Directions: Read the passage and the background, then answer the questions.

The passage. We must be the great arsenal of democracy. For us this is an emergency as serious as war itself. We must apply ourselves to our task with the same resolution, the same sense of urgency, the same spirit of patriotism and sacrifice as we would show were we at war.

Source: Franklin D. Roosevelt, Arsenal of Democracy fireside chat, December 29, 1940 (Miller Center transcript), as carried in the audited TPSP record. A fireside chat is one of President Roosevelt's radio addresses to the nation, delivered in plain conversational language from the White House.

Background. The Neutrality Acts of the 1930s barred arms sales and loans to nations at war, written to keep the United States out of another world war. In November 1939 Congress replaced the arms embargo with cash-and-carry. In March 1941 it passed Lend-Lease. Britain had fallen behind on cash long before then, and France had fallen in June 1940.

1. In the passage, what role does Roosevelt assign the United States, and what does he ask Americans to bring to it? Answer in your own words, using one phrase from the passage as evidence.

2. The Neutrality Acts grew out of a Senate investigation of the First World War. What did that investigation charge, and what were the acts written to prevent?

3. Explain how Lend-Lease changed the rule that cash-and-carry had set. Name what a buyer no longer had to do, and who gained the power to decide which countries received arms.

4. A member of the America First Committee hears the passage in December 1940. State the objection the committee would raise, as a chain of steps.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What role does Roosevelt assign the United States, and what does he ask Americans to bring to it? He assigns the country the role of supplier rather than belligerent: the great arsenal of democracy, producing arms for the nations fighting aggression while staying out of the fighting. He asks Americans to treat the work as an emergency as serious as war itself, with the resolution, urgency, patriotism, and sacrifice they would show in war. Credit requires the role and at least one of the qualities, tied to the passage's wording. (Blueprint 2)

2. What did the investigation charge, and what were the acts written to prevent? The Nye Committee charged that American arms makers and bankers, fearing for their profits and their loans to the Allies, had pulled the United States into the First World War. The Neutrality Acts were written to prevent a repetition, barring arms sales and loans to nations at war so that trade and credit could not draw the country into another world war. (Blueprint 1)

3. How did Lend-Lease change the cash-and-carry rule? Under cash-and-carry a nation at war had to pay cash and carry its purchases away in its own ships. Under Lend-Lease it no longer had to pay cash at the time or use its own ships as a condition of sale. The president gained the power to lend or lease arms and supplies to any country whose defense he judged vital to the defense of the United States. (Blueprint 2)

4. State the America First objection as a chain of steps. Aid presented as short of war leads to convoys to protect the shipments, convoys lead to shooting at sea, and shooting leads to a full war the American people never voted for. The committee's positive claim, that two oceans made defense at home the safe course, earns full credit when included. Answers that mock the objection rather than stating it earn partial credit. (Blueprint 1)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 3 is the hinge diagnostic: students who can state what Lend-Lease removed, the cash condition, and what it added, presidential judgment, have the mechanism the unit test will ask for. Question 4 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires the opposition case stated in its own strongest form.
