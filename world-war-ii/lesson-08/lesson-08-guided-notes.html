Guided Notes: Victory and the Atomic Reckoning

Lesson question: How did the war end, what did the atomic bomb decide and leave undecided, and what country came home from the war?

Unit question: How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

Key Terms

Yalta Conference: the meeting of Roosevelt, Churchill, and Stalin in February 1945 that planned the postwar settlement, including the United Nations and Soviet entry into the war against Japan

Potsdam Conference: the meeting of Truman, Churchill, and Stalin in July 1945 in occupied Germany, where the leaders set occupation policy and argued over Europe's future

Potsdam Declaration: the Allied ultimatum of July 26, 1945 demanding Japan's unconditional surrender and warning of prompt and utter destruction if Japan refused

Manhattan Project: the secret American program that built the first atomic bombs, employing more than 100,000 people at sites across the country

atomic bomb: a bomb whose explosive power comes from splitting atoms, releasing energy on a scale no ordinary explosive can match

demobilization: the process of bringing armed forces back to civilian life and reducing a wartime military to peacetime size

GI Bill: the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944, which gave veterans unemployment pay, tuition for college and training, and government-backed home and business loans

F estimates: worldwide deaths estimated between 60 and 85 million; Soviet deaths about 27 million. Both are estimates; a student who states either as an exact count has missed the section's instruction.

G. Demobilization brought more than 12 million Americans in uniform home to civilian life, fast. The GI Bill gave veterans unemployment pay, tuition for college and training, and government-backed home and business loans. A limit, any one with its fact: women who had held war jobs were pushed toward home as veterans took the jobs, though many stayed in paid work; Black veterans came home to the segregated South many of them had left, and the benefits of the GI Bill reached them through institutions that were segregated in much of the country.

The War's End at a Glance. The peace planned: Yalta in February 1945, Germany divided into occupation zones, the United Nations founded, Soviet entry against Japan pledged, and Poland left vague; Potsdam in July 1945, occupation policy set and Europe's future argued over. The decision: the Manhattan Project tested at Trinity on July 16; the Franck Report's demonstration proposal set aside; the Interim Committee's recommendation for use accepted by Truman. The surrender: Hiroshima August 6, Soviet entry August 8 and 9, Nagasaki August 9, the emperor breaking the deadlock, acceptance August 14, the signing aboard the Missouri September 2. The country that came home: about 405,000 American dead against worldwide deaths estimated at 60 to 85 million; more than 12 million demobilized into the GI Bill; an economy doubled in output; women pushed toward home and Black veterans returning to segregation; the United States one of two great powers, its cities untouched.

Exit ticket 1. The declaration demanded the unconditional surrender of all Japanese armed forces, and it warned that the alternative for Japan was prompt and utter destruction. It also promised that Japan would not be destroyed as a nation; credit either part stated accurately, and require the demand and the warning for full credit.

Exit ticket 2. The Franck Report proposed that the bomb first be demonstrated on a desert or a barren island before representatives of all nations, so the world could see the weapon America had and had not used on a city. What was decided instead, on the Interim Committee's recommendation accepted by Truman: the bomb was used against Japan without a demonstration, at Hiroshima on August 6 and Nagasaki on August 9.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that runs the sequence, the cost as estimates, and one homecoming fact. Strongest answers note what the bomb left undecided: it ended the war against Japan, and it did not settle Europe's future, which the divided alliance carried out of Potsdam and into the next unit.
