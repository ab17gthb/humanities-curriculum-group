Guided Notes: The War in the Pacific

Lesson question: How did the Allies cross the Pacific and close on Japan, and what did each step toward the home islands cost?

Unit question: How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

Key Terms

Guadalcanal: the Solomon Islands campaign of August 1942 to February 1943, the first major Allied offensive of the Pacific war, fought on land, at sea, and in the air around one island and its airfield

Battle of Midway: the naval battle of June 1942 in which American carrier aircraft sank four Japanese carriers and ended Japan's ability to take the offensive in the Pacific

island hopping: the Allied Pacific strategy of seizing selected islands for airfields while bypassing strong Japanese garrisons and cutting them off from supply

naval blockade: the use of warships, submarines, and aircraft to cut off an enemy's sea trade and supplies

firebombing: the bombing of cities with incendiary weapons designed to start mass fires

kamikaze: a Japanese pilot who made a deliberate crash attack on an enemy ship; also the attack itself

unconditional surrender: the demand that an enemy give up without negotiated conditions, announced as Allied policy for Germany and Japan

Summary table. The turn at sea: the Coral Sea turned back the Port Moresby force in May 1942, and at Midway in June dive bombers sank four Japanese fleet carriers, which bought the United States the initiative and the chance to attack at Guadalcanal. The advance: island hopping on two roads, MacArthur toward the Philippines and Nimitz across the central Pacific, bought airfields one step at a time while bypassed garrisons withered without supply. The closing: the submarine blockade cut Japan off from its empire's oil, steel, and food, and the Marianas bought B-29 range over Japan, which the firebombing campaign then used city by city. The last steps: Iwo Jima bought a fighter base and emergency field at a cost of about 6,800 American dead, and Okinawa bought the invasion base at about 12,500 American dead, above 100,000 Japanese military dead, and heavy civilian deaths.

Exit ticket 1. A bypassed garrison was cut off from supply, so it could not be fed, reinforced, or moved, and it had no way to strike the advance that had passed it by.

Exit ticket 2. The Marianas bought range, putting Japan's home islands within round trip reach of the B-29. Iwo Jima bought a fighter base and an emergency landing field for damaged B-29s flying that route.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that pairs the method with the cost. The strongest answers name island hopping, the blockade, and the bombing as the method, and set them against Midway's four carriers, Iwo Jima's about 6,800 American dead, Okinawa's about 12,500 American dead and above 100,000 Japanese military dead, or the Tokyo raid's estimated 90,000 to 100,000 dead.
