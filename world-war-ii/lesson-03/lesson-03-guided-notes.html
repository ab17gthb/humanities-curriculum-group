Guided Notes: Mobilizing the Home Front

Lesson question: How did the United States turn a peacetime economy into the arsenal the war required, and who did the work?

Unit question: How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

Key Terms

mobilization: the process of preparing a nation's armed forces, industry, and economy for war

Selective Training and Service Act: the law of 1940 that created the first peacetime draft in American history, requiring men of set ages to register for military service as called

War Production Board: the federal agency created in 1942 to direct the conversion of industry to war production and to allocate steel, rubber, and other scarce materials

war bonds: government bonds sold to the public to raise money for the war, repaid with interest

rationing: a system limiting how much of a scarce good each person may buy, enforced with coupons or ration stamps

Office of Price Administration: the federal agency that set price ceilings and ran the rationing system during the war

Rosie the Riveter: the popular name and image for the American women who took factory and shipyard jobs during the war, used in songs and posters to recruit women to war work

Mobilization at a Glance. The draft and the services: the government required registration under the Selective Training and Service Act of 1940 and drafted most of the force, and more than 12 million Americans in uniform by 1945 did the serving, about 16 million across the war. The factories and the yards: the government converted plants, allocated materials, and placed the orders, and factory and shipyard workers, including about six million women entering paid work and the migrants to war industry cities, did the building, producing about 300,000 aircraft and Liberty ships in weeks. The agencies and the kitchen: the War Production Board directed production, the Office of Price Administration held price ceilings and ran rationing, and citizens paid through war bonds and a mass income tax while living under ration books in every kitchen.
