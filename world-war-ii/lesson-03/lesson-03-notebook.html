Teacher Answer Key

Part of the mobilizationWhat the government didWho did the workWhat it cost civilians
The draftCreated the first peacetime draft in the Selective Training and Service Act of 1940 and called men through local boards.More than 12 million in uniform by 1945, about 16 million serving across the war.Years of young lives given to service, and families carried on without the drafted men.
War productionConverted industry through the War Production Board, allocated steel, rubber, and aluminum, and placed the orders.Factory and shipyard workers built about 300,000 aircraft and Liberty ships in weeks.New cars and other consumer goods went unbuilt for the length of the war.
Paying for the warSold war bonds to the public and widened the income tax into a mass tax withheld from paychecks.Bond buyers in schools, theaters, and payroll plans, and every wage earner paying the new tax.A share of nearly every paycheck, lent to the government or taken in tax.
Rationing and pricesSet price ceilings and ran rationing of gasoline, rubber, sugar, and meat through the Office of Price Administration.Local ration boards, grocers, and the clerks who handled stamps and points.Meals, travel, and driving shaped by the ration book in every kitchen.
The new workforceRecruited women with posters and songs and drew workers to war industry cities.About six million women entering paid work, and Black, Mexican American, and Native American migrants in a second Great Migration.Crowded boomtown housing, strained schools and clinics, and women pushed out of the trades at the war end.

The conversion in one sentence. Strong answers name both halves the chart records. The government directed production, drafted the force, and paid through bonds and taxes until unemployment reached about 1.2 percent in 1944, and daily life was remade because civilians surrendered new cars, unlimited food and fuel, and, in the boomtowns, adequate housing for the length of the war.
