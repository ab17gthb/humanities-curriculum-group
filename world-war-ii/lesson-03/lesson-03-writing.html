Writing Task: The Government That Ended the Depression

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: How did the mobilization end the Depression, and what kind of government action ended it? A strong answer names the mechanism plainly, government direction of the economy for war, and supports it with production figures, the agencies and their controls, the way the war was paid for, and the workers who did the work. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and activity. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording.

Evidence Bank

1. Unemployment fell to about 1.2 percent in 1944, after standing near one worker in five on the eve of the war. The Selective Training and Service Act of 1940 created the first peacetime draft, and more than 12 million Americans were in uniform by 1945.

2. American factories produced about 300,000 aircraft across the war years. Kaiser yards cut the building time of a Liberty ship from months to weeks, and one Richmond ship was completed in under five days.

3. The War Production Board, created in 1942, decided which factories made what and allocated steel, rubber, copper, and aluminum. A plant could not simply choose its product or buy its metal on the open market.

4. The Office of Price Administration held price ceilings on most goods and ran rationing of gasoline, rubber, sugar, and meat, enforced with coupons and ration stamps in every kitchen.

5. The war was paid for by borrowing and taxation together. War bonds sold the debt to the public in schools, theaters, and payroll plans, and the income tax became a mass tax withheld from paychecks.

6. About six million women entered paid work during the war years, and Black, Mexican American, and Native American workers migrated to war industry cities in a second Great Migration, Richmond growing from about 24,000 people to about 100,000.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the prompt, that the mobilization ended the Depression and that it did so by government direction of the economy. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 and 2 support scale, the fall of unemployment and the output figures. (Blueprint 5) Bank items 3, 4, and 5 support the direction itself, allocation, price control and rationing, and payment by bonds and taxation. (Blueprint 6) Bank item 6 supports the workforce that carried the direction out. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6) A full credit essay draws on at least two groups.
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. An essay that lists output figures without the agencies and controls has assembled numbers without the lesson argument, and should earn partial credit.
  • An ending that names the comparison the reading closes on, war spending and direction on a scale the New Deal never attempted.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Depression did not end because private demand recovered on its own. It ended because the federal government took direction of the economy for war. Washington drafted the force under the 1940 law until more than 12 million wore a uniform, allocated the steel and aluminum through the War Production Board, held prices and rationed food and fuel through the Office of Price Administration, and paid the bill with bonds and a mass income tax. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the output figures and the six million women and the migrants who did the work, and closing on unemployment at about 1.2 percent in 1944.

Common weak answers. A production list (the country built many planes and ships) names output without saying who directed it. A patriotism answer (Americans worked hard and sacrificed) names effort without mechanism. Credit requires the direction named and evidenced: orders, allocation, controls, and payment, all run by the government.
