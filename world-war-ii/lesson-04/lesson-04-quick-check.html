Quick Check: The Ramp at Omaha

Directions: Study the photograph and the background, then answer the questions.

Background. The photograph records an amphibious landing on D-Day, June 6, 1944. About 156,000 Allied troops landed or dropped in France that day, on five beaches code named Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword. Before Normandy, the Allies had landed in North Africa in November 1942 and in Sicily and Italy in 1943, and their bombers had carried the air war to Germany from England. After Normandy came the drive across France, the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, the meeting at the Elbe in April 1945, and VE Day on May 8, 1945.

1. What is happening in the photograph? Name the operation's day, its date, and the kind of landing the lesson calls it, and say where the camera is.

2. Before this landing, the Allies fought two campaigns in the Mediterranean. Name both, in order, and give one result of each.

3. The troops in the photograph are at Omaha, the costliest American beach that day. Put Omaha in sequence: name the breakout that followed in July and the German offensive that followed in December.

4. Name the river where American and Soviet troops met in April 1945, the date Germany surrendered, and the name and date of the day victory in Europe was announced.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What is happening, with the day, date, landing type, and camera position? Troops are wading ashore at Omaha Beach during an amphibious landing, an attack from the sea onto a defended shore by forces carried in ships and landing craft, on D-Day, June 6, 1944. Sargent photographed from the landing craft itself, at the lowered ramp, behind the men going ashore. (Blueprint 8)

2. Name the two Mediterranean campaigns before Normandy, in order, with a result of each. North Africa first: Operation Torch landed in Morocco and Algeria in November 1942, and the campaign ended with the Axis surrender in Tunisia in May 1943 and about 250,000 prisoners. Sicily and Italy second: Sicily fell in about five weeks in 1943, Italy surrendered that September, and the mountain war then ran to the spring of 1945. The strategic bombing campaign from England may be credited as supporting evidence but does not replace either campaign. (Blueprint 7)

3. Put Omaha in sequence, with the July breakout and the December offensive. After the June landings and the buildup behind artificial harbors, American armor broke out at Saint-Lo in late July 1944 and the armies drove across France, liberating Paris on August 25. In December Germany launched the Battle of the Bulge through the Ardennes, its last major offensive in the west, beaten back by February 1945. (Blueprint 8)

4. Name the river meeting, the surrender date, and VE Day. American and Soviet soldiers met at the Elbe River near Torgau on April 25, 1945. Germany signed its unconditional surrender at Reims on May 7, 1945, and VE Day, the day victory in Europe was announced, was May 8, 1945. (Blueprint 8)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 1 is the sourcing diagnostic. Students who write only that soldiers are in the water have not connected the photograph to the landing type, the date, or the photographer's position on the craft. Question 2 separates students who hold the Mediterranean sequence from students who jump straight to Normandy.
