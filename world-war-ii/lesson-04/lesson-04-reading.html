The War in Europe and North Africa

Directions: Read the full text, then answer the questions at the end.

A. The War the Allies Inherited

In December 1941 Germany held most of Europe, from France to deep inside the Soviet Union. Germany had invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, opening the Eastern Front, the battlefront between Germany and the Soviet Union after June 1941, where the largest land fighting of the war took place.

At Stalingrad, the battle of August 1942 to February 1943 in which the Soviet army surrounded and destroyed a German army, the turning point of the war on the Eastern Front, Germany lost an entire army. The Soviet Union demanded a second front in France to pull German strength eastward away from its own front. The western Allies were not ready to invade France in 1942, so they chose an easier first campaign.

B. North Africa: The First Campaign

On November 8, 1942, American and British forces landed in Morocco and Algeria in Operation Torch, an amphibious landing, an attack from the sea onto a defended shore by forces carried in ships and landing craft. The landings put American ground troops into the war against Germany for the first time, and the Allies then raced east into Tunisia.

In February 1943 German armor broke through inexperienced American troops at the Kasserine Pass. The Allies recovered, pressed from west and east, and in May 1943 the Axis forces in Tunisia surrendered. About 250,000 Axis soldiers became prisoners. The Allies held the Mediterranean's southern shore, opened its sea lanes, and had learned to fight together.

C. Sicily, Italy, and the Argument

In July 1943 the Allies landed on Sicily, and the island fell in about five weeks. Italy's dictator, Benito Mussolini, was removed from power, and in September Italy surrendered. Germany occupied northern and central Italy at once. Allied troops at Salerno held their beachhead under heavy counterattack, and the advance north became a slow mountain war.

Rome fell on June 4, 1944, two days before the Normandy landings, and Italy saw fighting until the spring of 1945. The campaign tied down German divisions, knocked Italy out of the war, and fed an argument inside the alliance. The second front in France did not open until June 1944, while the Soviet Union carried the largest land fighting.

D. The Air War

From bases in England, Allied air forces carried out strategic bombing, the use of heavy bombers against an enemy's industry, transportation, and cities, aiming to break its capacity and will to make war. The Royal Air Force bombed at night, and the American Eighth Air Force bombed by day against factories, refineries, and rail yards. One mission against Schweinfurt in October 1943 lost sixty American bombers.

By 1944 long range P-51 Mustang escorts let the bombers reach deep into Germany, and the offensive turned against oil and transportation. By early 1945 German fuel and rail movement were collapsing. The civilian cost stands in the record too. A firestorm at Hamburg in July 1943 killed an estimated 37,000 people, and an estimated 25,000 died at Dresden in February 1945.

E. D-Day

D-Day, June 6, 1944, the day Allied forces landed in Normandy, France, in the largest seaborne invasion in history, opened the second front at last. About 156,000 Allied troops landed or dropped in France on the first day. Paratroopers came down behind the beaches overnight, and at dawn infantry landed on five beaches code named Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword.

The cost differed by beach. At Utah the advance moved inland quickly, while at Omaha troops crossed open sand under fire from bluffs above and suffered the heaviest American losses of the day. Artificial harbors, code named Mulberry, were towed across the Channel to land supplies without a captured port. In late July American armor broke out at Saint-Lo.

F. The Drive Across France and the Bulge

The breakout became a pursuit. Allied armies crossed France in weeks, Paris was liberated on August 25, 1944, and German forces fell back toward their own border. Speed created a problem, because fuel still came from the Normandy beaches. In September an airborne plan to cross the Rhine at Arnhem failed, and the advance slowed into autumn mud.

On December 16, 1944, Germany struck back through the Ardennes in the Battle of the Bulge, the last major German offensive in the west, launched through the Ardennes in December 1944 and beaten back by February 1945. Surprised American units bent, Bastogne held out surrounded, and by February the attack had failed. In March the Allies crossed the Rhine at Remagen.

G. The Elbe and VE Day

In April 1945 the western Allies drove across Germany while Soviet armies closed on Berlin, and on April 25 the two sides met at the Elbe River near Torgau. Hitler killed himself on April 30. Germany signed an unconditional surrender at Reims on May 7, and VE Day, May 8, 1945, the day victory in Europe was announced after Germany's unconditional surrender, closed the European war.

The accounting belongs at the end of any honest map. German soldiers and civilians died in the millions. The Soviet Union bore the largest share of the land war, and Soviet military and civilian deaths together are estimated at about 27 million across the whole war. These campaigns freed western Europe alongside that greater eastern war, not in place of it.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Explain why the Soviet Union demanded a second front in France, and name two things the Allies did instead in 1942 and 1943. The Soviet Union carried the largest land fighting of the war on the Eastern Front after June 1941 and wanted an invasion of France to draw German divisions and strength away from the east. Instead of invading France, the western Allies landed in North Africa in Operation Torch in November 1942, then invaded Sicily and mainland Italy in 1943, while the strategic bombing campaign from England grew through both years. Any two of those three earn credit.

2. Trace the Tunisian campaign from Torch to the surrender in May 1943. American and British forces landed in Morocco and Algeria on November 8, 1942, then raced east into Tunisia. In February 1943 German armor broke through inexperienced American troops at the Kasserine Pass. The Allies recovered, pressed from west and east at once, and forced the surrender of the Axis forces in May 1943, with about 250,000 Axis soldiers becoming prisoners. The victory gave the Allies the southern shore of the Mediterranean, open sea lanes, a base within reach of Italy, and armies that had learned to land, supply, and fight together.

3. State the aim of strategic bombing, then give one military result and one civilian cost. Strategic bombing is the use of heavy bombers against an enemy's industry, transportation, and cities, aiming to break its capacity and will to make war. Military results include the loss of sixty American bombers over Schweinfurt in October 1943 as a cost, the arrival of long range P-51 escorts in 1944, and the collapse of German fuel production and rail movement by early 1945. Civilian costs include an estimated 37,000 dead in the Hamburg firestorm of July 1943 and an estimated 25,000 dead at Dresden in February 1945. Both civilian figures must be stated as estimates.

4. Put D-Day in sequence, with the five beaches, the heaviest fighting, and the July breakout. D-Day was June 6, 1944, the largest seaborne invasion in history, landing about 156,000 Allied troops on the first day. The five beaches were Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword. The heaviest American losses came at Omaha Beach, where troops crossed open sand under fire from the bluffs. Artificial Mulberry harbors supplied the buildup, and in late July American armor broke out at Saint-Lo and began the drive across France.

5. Why does the size of the Eastern Front belong in any turning point judgment? Full credit answers start from scale. The Eastern Front carried the largest land fighting of the war from June 1941, Stalingrad destroyed a German army in the winter of 1942 to 1943, before any western landing in France, and Soviet military and civilian deaths together are estimated at about 27 million across the whole war. A judgment that names only a western campaign as the turning point has to explain what it does with that record. Answers may still defend Normandy, North Africa, or the air war, but they must weigh the eastern war rather than leave it out.
