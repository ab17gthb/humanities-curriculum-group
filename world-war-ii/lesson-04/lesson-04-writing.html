Writing Task: The Turning Point

The prompt. Which stage of the European war deserves to be called its turning point? Answer in a developed essay of about 300 words, argued from the station records in Five Maps, One War. Choose one stage: North Africa, Stalingrad and the eastern war, Italy, the air war, Normandy, or the Bulge. A strong answer does more than name a battle. It shows that the war ran one way before your stage and another way after it, it uses the stage's aim, result, and cost as evidence, and it answers the strongest claim of a rival stage. Your essay must address the scale of the Eastern Front directly, whatever stage you choose.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. North Africa: Operation Torch landed in Morocco and Algeria on November 8, 1942. After the setback at the Kasserine Pass in February 1943, the Axis forces in Tunisia surrendered in May 1943, and about 250,000 Axis soldiers became prisoners.

2. The eastern war: Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941. At Stalingrad, from August 1942 to February 1943, the Soviet army surrounded and destroyed a German army. Soviet military and civilian deaths together are estimated at about 27 million across the whole war.

3. Italy: Sicily fell in about five weeks in 1943 and Italy surrendered that September, but Germany occupied the peninsula and the mountain war ran until the spring of 1945. Rome fell on June 4, 1944.

4. The air war: strategic bombing from England turned against German oil and transportation in 1944, and by early 1945 German fuel and rail movement were collapsing. The civilian cost included an estimated 37,000 dead at Hamburg and an estimated 25,000 at Dresden.

5. Normandy: on D-Day, June 6, 1944, about 156,000 Allied troops landed or dropped in France across five beaches. The heaviest American losses came at Omaha. The breakout at Saint-Lo followed in late July, and Paris was liberated on August 25.

6. The end: Germany's last offensive in the west, the Battle of the Bulge, failed by February 1945 after spending reserves that could not be replaced. The Allies crossed the Rhine in March, met Soviet forces at the Elbe on April 25, and Germany surrendered on May 7, 1945. VE Day was May 8.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that names one stage and states the change it made, in before and after terms, not a claim that a stage was important or brave. (Blueprint 7 and Blueprint 8)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately, including the chosen stage's aim, result, and cost. Bank items 1, 3, and 4 support the Mediterranean and air stages. (Blueprint 7) Bank items 5 and 6 support Normandy and the road to VE Day. (Blueprint 8)
  • A direct treatment of the Eastern Front's scale. An essay for a western stage must reckon with Stalingrad's date, a German army destroyed in the winter of 1942 to 1943, and the estimate of about 27 million Soviet deaths across the whole war, and explain why its chosen stage still changed the war. An essay for Stalingrad must reckon with the fact that Germany still held western Europe in 1943 and that the war in the west required landings of its own.
  • One rival stage answered from the record rather than dismissed. Dates decide most rival claims in this set, so accurate dates in the answer are a strong sign of a full credit essay.

Model opening for teacher reference. The turning point of the European war was Stalingrad, because it is the first stage after which Germany was losing the war it had chosen to fight largest. From June 1941 the Eastern Front carried the biggest land fighting of the war, and in February 1943 the Soviet army completed the destruction of a German army there, sixteen months before any Allied soldier landed in France. A full model continues in this pattern, adding the Tunisian surrender as the western parallel of the same winter, granting Normandy its real weight as the opening of the second front, and closing on the difference between the front where Germany lost an army first and the fronts that followed.

Common weak answers. A biggest battle claim (Normandy was the largest invasion) measures size without showing a change in the war's direction. A last battle claim (the Bulge ended the war) confuses the end of the story with its turn. An essay that never mentions the Eastern Front cannot earn full credit under this prompt, whatever stage it chooses, because the prompt requires that scale to be addressed.
