Five Maps, One War: The War in Europe and North Africa

Directions: Work through the five stations in order. Each station holds one dated record of the European war: North Africa in 1942, Italy in 1943, Normandy in 1944, the Bulge in 1944, and Germany in 1945. For each station, state the Allied aim, the result, and the cost. At the end, judge which stage you would defend as the turning point of the European war.

Part 1: Five Stations

Station 1: North Africa 1942, Torch and the Road to Tunisia

Source: Summary prepared for this lesson from the standard campaign record. This station is a labeled summary, not a transcription of a wartime document. Figures are the standard record's figures.

DateRecord
November 8, 1942Operation Torch lands American and British forces in Morocco and Algeria
February 1943German armor breaks through American troops at the Kasserine Pass
May 13, 1943Axis forces in Tunisia surrender; about 250,000 soldiers become prisoners

The map at this stage shows a war at the edge of Europe. North Africa gave the western Allies a shore they could reach, an enemy they could engage, and a sea, the Mediterranean, whose southern lanes a victory would open. It also gave American troops their first ground campaign against Germany, fought alongside British forces that had battled in the desert since 1940.

State the Allied aim in North Africa, using the station record.

State the result and the cost. Name the setback on the road to Tunisia and what the final surrender figure shows.

Station 2: Italy 1943, Sicily, Salerno, and the Mountain War

Source: Summary prepared for this lesson from the standard campaign record. This station is a labeled summary, not a transcription of a wartime document.

DateRecord
July 10, 1943Allied forces land on Sicily
August 17, 1943Sicily is in Allied hands, after about five weeks
September 8, 1943Italy surrenders; Germany occupies northern and central Italy
September 9, 1943Allied troops land at Salerno and hold the beachhead
June 4, 1944Rome falls; the mountain war in Italy runs on to the spring of 1945

The map changes shape here. Italy left the Axis, yet the fighting in Italy grew harder rather than shorter, because German forces took over the peninsula's mountain spine and defended river lines and ridges one by one. Divisions the Allies used in Italy were divisions not used in France, and divisions Germany held in Italy were divisions not used against the Soviet Union. Both sides of that arithmetic were argued at the time.

State the Allied aim in the Sicily and Italy landings, using the station record.

State the result and the cost. Your answer must hold two facts together: Italy surrendered, and the war in Italy ran on for more than a year and a half.

Station 3: Normandy 1944, the Photograph and the Order

Source: Photograph by Chief Photographer's Mate Robert F. Sargent, United States Coast Guard, June 6, 1944, National Archives, audited record in the Primary Source Project bank, and General Dwight D. Eisenhower, Order of the Day, June 6, 1944, as printed in the National Archives Prologue edition named in the bank record. The order is quoted with cuts, marked by ellipses.

Eisenhower's order, read to the troops before the landings, in part:

"You are about to embark on the Great Crusade, toward which we have striven these many months. The eyes of the world are upon you. ... Your task will not be an easy one. Your enemy is well trained, well equipped and battle-hardened. He will fight savagely. ... We will accept nothing less than full Victory!"

The photograph shows the landing from the water, at ramp level. About 156,000 Allied troops landed or dropped in France on the first day, across five beaches code named Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword. The heaviest American losses of the day came at Omaha, the beach in this photograph.

State the Allied aim at Normandy in Eisenhower's own terms, then in yours.

State the result and the cost. Use the first day figure, the five beaches, and the Omaha record.

Station 4: The Bulge 1944

Source: Summary prepared for this lesson from the standard campaign record. This station is a labeled summary, not a transcription of a wartime document. Casualty figures are the standard estimates and are labeled as estimates.

DateRecord
December 16, 1944Germany attacks through the Ardennes forest in Belgium and Luxembourg

Cost, estimatedAbout 75,000 American casualties, an estimate; German losses ran higher, also estimated

The map at this stage shows a dent, the bulge that gave the battle its name, pushed into the Allied line by an army burning fuel and armor Germany could not replace. The offensive aimed to split the Allied armies and seize Antwerp, the port their supplies ran through. It surprised the Allies in the war's last winter, weeks after many had begun to speak of the war as nearly finished.

State the aim of the German offensive and the Allied aim in answering it.

State the result and the cost, and explain why a failed offensive still weakened Germany for the final campaign.

Station 5: Germany 1945, the Rhine, the Elbe, and the Surrender

DateRecord

The last map has no German front left to draw. Allied armies advanced from west and east at once, and the meeting at the Elbe joined the two wars this lesson has followed, the western campaigns from North Africa onward and the far larger eastern war that began in June 1941. Soviet military and civilian deaths together are estimated at about 27 million across the whole war, a figure for the entire Soviet war and not for this final campaign alone.

State the Allied aim in the spring of 1945 and the result, using the Remagen and Elbe dates.

State the cost as this station records it, and explain why the 27 million figure must be read as a whole war figure.

Part 2: The Verdict

Five stages stand in the record: North Africa, Italy, Normandy, the Bulge, and Germany in 1945. Behind all five stands the eastern war, where Stalingrad destroyed a German army in the winter of 1942 to 1943. Choose the one stage you would defend as the turning point of the European war. A turning point claim must show that the war ran one way before the stage and another way after it, and it must survive the strongest fact for a rival stage.

Write your verdict in a paragraph. Name your stage, give its aim, result, and cost from its station, state the strongest fact for a rival stage, and answer it.

Teacher Answer Key

Station 1: North Africa 1942

Allied aim. To land American ground troops in the war against Germany on a shore the Allies could reach, clear the Mediterranean's southern shore and its sea lanes, and engage Axis forces in Tunisia.

Result and cost. The Axis forces in Tunisia surrendered on May 13, 1943, with about 250,000 soldiers taken prisoner, a victory on the scale of a major army destroyed or captured. The cost included the February 1943 breakthrough at the Kasserine Pass, where inexperienced American troops lost men, tanks, and ground before the line was restored.

Station 2: Italy 1943

Allied aim. To take Sicily, knock Italy out of the Axis, and draw German divisions into a campaign in the peninsula.

Result and cost. Sicily fell in about five weeks and Italy surrendered on September 8, 1943. Germany then occupied the peninsula, the Salerno beachhead was held under counterattack, Rome fell only on June 4, 1944, and the mountain war ran on to the spring of 1945. Both halves earn credit only together.

Station 3: Normandy 1944

Allied aim. In Eisenhower's terms, a Great Crusade to destroy the German war machine and end Nazi rule over Europe, accepting nothing less than full victory. In plain terms, to land a mass army in France, open the second front, and hold a lodgment Germany could not throw back into the sea.

Result and cost. About 156,000 Allied troops landed or dropped on June 6, 1944, across Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword, and the lodgment held and grew. The cost was heaviest at Omaha, where troops waded ashore under fire from the bluffs, as the Sargent photograph shows from the ramp of the landing craft.

Station 4: The Bulge 1944

Aims. Germany aimed to split the Allied armies, seize Antwerp and its supply port, and force a negotiated end in the west. The Allied aim was to hold the line, relieve Bastogne, and restore the front.

Result and cost. Bastogne held, relief arrived on December 26, and the line was restored by February 1945. American casualties are estimated at about 75,000. The failure weakened Germany because the offensive spent armor, fuel, and aircraft that could not be replaced, weeks before the Rhine crossing and the final drive.

Station 5: Germany 1945

Allied aim and result. To cross the Rhine, overrun Germany from both sides, and force an unconditional surrender. The Rhine was crossed at Remagen on March 7, 1945, American and Soviet troops met at the Elbe on April 25, Hitler killed himself on April 30, and Germany surrendered at Reims on May 7, with VE Day on May 8.

Cost. The final campaign ran through a ruined country with heavy military and civilian losses on all sides. The figure of about 27 million Soviet deaths is an estimate for the whole Soviet war from 1941 to 1945, military and civilian together, and credit requires students to say so rather than attach it to the spring of 1945 alone.

Part 2: The Verdict (Grading Guide)

Full credit verdicts name one stage, support it with the station's aim, result, and cost, and answer a rival. A Normandy verdict argues that before June 1944 Germany could still hope to hold the west with a thin screen, and after it two mass fronts closed at once; it must answer Stalingrad, which destroyed a German army sixteen months earlier on the larger front. A Stalingrad verdict, argued from the framing paragraph and the reading, is full credit when it states the eastern scale plainly. A North Africa verdict argues the first army sized victory and the Mediterranean opened. A Bulge verdict is the hardest to defend and earns credit only when it argues that the battle spent Germany's last reserve, which shortened the final campaign, rather than claiming it changed the war's direction. Verdicts that list facts without a before and after claim earn partial credit.
