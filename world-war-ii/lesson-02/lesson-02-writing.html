Writing Task: Settled and Undecided

The prompt. Answer the lesson question in a developed essay of about 300 words: What did the attack on Pearl Harbor settle, and what did it leave undecided? A strong answer does two things at once. It names what December 1941 decided, with dates and votes, and it names what stayed open, including the strategy argument inside the Grand Alliance and the unbuilt arsenal. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. On December 7, 1941, Japanese carrier aircraft attacked Pearl Harbor, killing more than 2,400 Americans, damaging all eight battleships in the harbor and sinking four, and destroying 188 American aircraft. The Pacific Fleet carriers were at sea and survived.

2. On December 8, Roosevelt asked Congress to declare that a state of war had existed since the attack. The Senate voted 82 to 0 and the House 388 to 1. The neutrality debate that had divided the country since 1939 ended in a day.

3. On December 11, Germany and Italy declared war on the United States, a choice Germany was not bound by treaty to make. Congress answered the same day without a dissenting vote, and the country faced a two-ocean war.

4. At the Arcadia Conference the Allied staffs held to Germany first, concentrating the main effort on defeating Germany before Japan, because Germany was judged the stronger enemy. The Soviet Union demanded a second front in France from 1942 onward, and France was not invaded until June 1944.

5. In the first six months the record ran against the strategy: about 75,000 American and Filipino troops surrendered on Bataan on April 9, 1942, and Corregidor fell on May 6. The Doolittle Raid of April 18 did light damage and lifted morale at home.

6. In December 1941 the arsenal existed mostly as factory orders. Roosevelt's public targets for 1942 were 60,000 warplanes, 45,000 tanks, 20,000 antiaircraft guns, and eight million tons of merchant shipping.

Write the Essay

Write about 300 words. Begin with your claim, state what December settled with dates and votes, explain what stayed open, and end by judging which open question carried the most risk.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that answers both halves of the lesson question, what the attack settled and what it left undecided. (Blueprint 3 and Blueprint 4)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank items 1 through 3 support the settled side: the attack, the declaration and the end of the neutrality debate, and the German and Italian declarations that made the war two-ocean. (Blueprint 3) Bank items 4 through 6 support the open side: the Germany first agreement and the second front demand, the defeats of the first six months, and the unbuilt arsenal. (Blueprint 4) A full credit essay draws on both groups.
  • Dates and figures used as evidence, not decoration. The votes of December 8, the enemy declarations of December 11, and the production targets of January 1942 each carry a point in the argument, and strong essays say what the point is.
  • A closing judgment about risk that follows from the evidence. Either leading candidate earns full credit when argued: the second front delay, which strained the alliance whose land power was bearing the largest fighting, or the production question, on which every strategy in both oceans depended.

Model opening for teacher reference. The attack settled the country's argument and opened its hardest problems in the same week. It settled the argument completely: a country that had debated neutrality for six years declared war on Japan within a day of the attack, 82 to 0 in the Senate and 388 to 1 in the House, and when Germany and Italy declared war on December 11, the two-ocean shape of the war was settled as well, by the enemy's choice. Nothing after December was settled in the same way. The Grand Alliance agreed at Arcadia to beat Germany first, and the Soviet Union spent two years demanding the second front in France that the agreement implied and the calendar postponed. Underneath the strategy argument sat the production question: in December 1941 the arsenal was a set of factory orders and a list of public targets, 60,000 warplanes and 45,000 tanks for 1942, and Bataan showed in April what American forces looked like before the arsenal arrived. A full model continues in this pattern and closes by judging which open question carried the most risk, with the evidence already on the page.

Common weak answers. The settled only answer (the attack united the country and the country won) has no second half and cannot explain the second front argument or the spring of 1942. The undecided only answer treats the entry itself as uncertain, which the votes of December 8 and 11 contradict. A chronology with no sorting lists December's events without ever dividing them into decided and open, which is the division the question asks for.
