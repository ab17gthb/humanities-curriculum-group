Guided Notes: Pearl Harbor and the Arsenal of Democracy

Lesson question: What did the attack on Pearl Harbor settle, and what did it leave for Americans to decide?

Unit question: How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

Key Terms

Pearl Harbor: the United States naval base in Hawaii attacked by Japanese carrier aircraft on December 7, 1941, the attack that brought the United States into the Second World War

Axis powers: the alliance of Germany, Italy, and Japan that fought the Allied nations in the Second World War

Allies: the nations allied against the Axis in the Second World War, led by the United States, Great Britain, and the Soviet Union

Grand Alliance: the formal alliance of the United States, Great Britain, and the Soviet Union against the Axis, joined by many other nations in the Declaration by United Nations of January 1, 1942

Germany first: the Allied strategy of concentrating the main effort on defeating Germany before Japan, agreed by the American and British staffs and kept through the war

second front: the Allied invasion of German-held western Europe that the Soviet Union demanded from 1942 onward to draw German strength away from the Eastern Front

theater: a large geographic area of military operations, such as the European theater or the Pacific theater

Summary table. The attack: on December 7, 1941, Japanese carrier aircraft struck Pearl Harbor, killing more than 2,400 Americans, damaging all eight battleships in the harbor and sinking four, and destroying 188 aircraft, while the carriers at sea survived; it decided that the United States would fight. The declarations: Congress declared war on Japan on December 8, the Senate 82 to 0 and the House 388 to 1, and Germany and Italy declared war on the United States on December 11, which decided that the war would be a two-ocean war. The strategy: the Grand Alliance joined the United States, Great Britain, and the Soviet Union, with 26 nations signing the Declaration by United Nations on January 1, 1942, and the staffs held to Germany first while the Soviet Union demanded a second front. The open questions: how the alliance would share the fighting, when a second front could be opened, and whether American industry could reach the 1942 production targets of 60,000 warplanes, 45,000 tanks, and eight million tons of shipping.

Exit ticket 1. Any two, with evidence. The neutrality debate ended, shown by the votes of December 8 and the absence of a large minority. The war became a two-ocean war, settled when Germany and Italy declared war on December 11. The United States joined the Allies in full, shown by the Grand Alliance and the Declaration by United Nations.

Exit ticket 2. Germany first was the Allied strategy of concentrating the main effort on defeating Germany before Japan. The Soviet demand was a second front, an Allied invasion of German-held western Europe to draw German strength away from the Eastern Front.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names at least one settled fact and one open question with two supporting facts. The strongest answers pair the ended neutrality debate and the two-ocean war with the second front argument and the unbuilt arsenal of December 1941.
