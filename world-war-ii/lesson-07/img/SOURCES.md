# Image Sources: Lesson 7, The Holocaust and the War's Crimes

All images are public domain as stated on their Wikimedia Commons file pages. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/. Every photograph in this lesson is used where it is under discussion and captioned exactly from the holding record. No photograph is used as decoration. The deck title slide carries the Buchenwald barracks photograph, captioned on the slide, because that photograph is itself part of the lesson's evidence and returns under discussion in Section F.

## Prisoners in the barracks at Buchenwald (buchenwald-barracks.jpg; deck crops buchenwald-port.jpg and title-169.jpg)
- Title: Buchenwald Slave Laborers Liberation
- Creator: Private H. Miller, United States Army
- Date: April 16, 1945
- Holder: United States Army photograph, via Wikimedia Commons
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Buchenwald_Slave_Laborers_Liberation.jpg
- Public domain basis: Commons file page marks the image Public domain as a work of a United States Army photographer made on duty.
- Use: reading (full frame, small), deck title slide (16:9 crop, title-169.jpg, 1600 by 900, captioned on the slide), and deck Section F divider (portrait crop). The photograph shows prisoners in the multi-tier barracks bunks at Buchenwald near Weimar, Germany, photographed from a distance across the barracks room.

## Images considered and not used
- Warsaw ghetto photographs from the Stroop Report were not used. Their Commons file pages mark them public domain, and copies sit in the federal record at the National Archives. The best known of them show prisoners, including a child, being arrested at gunpoint during the ghetto's destruction in April and May 1943. Under this lesson's sobriety rule, which bars atrocity photographs used as illustration rather than as evidence under discussion, they were left out. The ghettos are taught in text, from the record of the Warsaw ghetto's population, walls, hunger, and disease. If a ghetto photograph is added in a later revision, it should be a street or wall photograph whose file page states public domain clearly, captioned from its holding record.
- No photograph of the killing centers' gas chambers or of bodies in close view is used anywhere in this lesson.
