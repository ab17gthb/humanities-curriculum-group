Quick Check: First-Hand Evidence

Directions: Read the document and the background, then answer the questions.

Document. Cable from General Dwight D. Eisenhower to General George C. Marshall, April 12, 1945, printed word for word from the audited record. Edition: United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, Holocaust Encyclopedia, punctuation modernized. The cut at the opening is marked in the text.

[...] the most interesting, although horrible, sight that I encountered during the trip was a visit to a German internment camp near Gotha. The things I saw beggar description. While I was touring the camp I encountered three men who had been inmates and by one ruse or another had made their escape. I interviewed them through an interpreter. The visual evidence and the verbal testimony of starvation, cruelty and bestiality were so overpowering as to leave me a bit sick. [...] I made the visit deliberately, in order to be in position to give first-hand evidence of these things if ever, in the future, there develops a tendency to charge these allegations merely to "propaganda."

Background. American forces reached the camp at Ohrdruf, near Gotha in Germany, on April 4, 1945. It was the first camp they entered. Eisenhower, the supreme Allied commander in Europe, toured it on April 12 and ordered it documented. He brought members of Congress and journalists to see it in the days that followed. The camp system he was inspecting belonged to the Final Solution's wider record: from 1942, Jews from across Europe had been deported to killing centers in occupied Poland, and the record supports a count of six million Jews murdered.

1. What two kinds of evidence does Eisenhower name in the cable, and from whom did he take testimony?

2. Eisenhower writes that he made the visit deliberately. State his reason, in his own words where you can.

3. The cable is dated April 12, 1945. Place it in the lesson's record. What had Allied governments already publicly done in December 1942, and what agency had the United States created in January 1944?

4. State the count the background gives for Jews murdered, and name the program that the killing centers in occupied Poland carried out.

Teacher Answer Key

1. What two kinds of evidence does Eisenhower name, and from whom did he take testimony? Visual evidence, what he saw himself while touring the camp, and verbal testimony. The testimony came from three men who had been inmates of the camp and had escaped, whom he interviewed through an interpreter. Credit requires both kinds of evidence and the identity of the witnesses. (Blueprint 14)

2. State Eisenhower's reason for making the visit deliberately. In his own words, he made the visit in order to be in position to give first-hand evidence of these things if ever, in the future, there developed a tendency to charge the allegations merely to propaganda. He was securing a witness at the highest level of command against a future denial. (Blueprint 14)

3. Place the cable in the record. In December 1942 the Allied governments had publicly condemned the extermination of the Jews of Europe, after receiving reports that included an August 1942 telegram reporting a German plan to murder them. In January 1944 the United States created the War Refugee Board, which worked in the war's last year to rescue Jews who could still be reached. The April 1945 cable therefore belongs to the documentation stage of the record, after knowledge and public condemnation and after the late rescue effort. (Blueprint 14)

4. State the count and the program. The record supports a count of six million Jews murdered, about two-thirds of the Jewish population of Europe. The killing centers in occupied Poland carried out the Final Solution, the Nazi program to murder every Jew in Europe. (Blueprint 13)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 test close reading of a single document. Students who answer question 2 with a general statement about witnessing history, without Eisenhower's stated reason, have not yet read the cable's last sentence as an argument aimed at the future. Question 3 predicts success on the writing task, because it requires the student to hold knowledge, response, and documentation in one sequence.
