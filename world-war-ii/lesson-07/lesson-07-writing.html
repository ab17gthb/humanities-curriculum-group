Writing Task: Knowledge and Response

The prompt. Answer this question in a developed essay of about 300 words: What does the record show about the distance between knowledge and response during the Holocaust? A strong answer does not claim that nothing was known, and it does not claim that nothing was done. Both claims fail against the documents. A strong answer measures the distance with dates: when the persecution became public law, when the reports reached the Allied capitals and what those governments said, when rescue was attempted and on what scale, and when the evidence was gathered for judgment. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and do not state any claim more strongly than its document supports.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading and stations. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank's wording.

Evidence Bank

1. The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 stripped Jews of German citizenship and forbade marriage between Jews and non-Jewish Germans. On Kristallnacht in November 1938, synagogues were burned and about thirty thousand Jewish men were arrested. The persecution was public law and public violence, reported around the world.

2. At Evian in 1938, nation after nation declined to admit more Jewish refugees, and the United States kept its immigration quotas fixed. In 1939 the St. Louis was turned away by Cuba, the United States, and Canada with 937 Jewish passengers aboard. Later research established that about a quarter of its passengers were murdered.

3. In August 1942 a telegram reached London and Washington reporting a German plan to murder the Jews of Europe. In December 1942 the Allied governments publicly condemned the extermination. By 1944, escaped prisoners and Polish couriers had confirmed the killing centers.

4. The United States created the War Refugee Board in January 1944. In the war's last year it funded rescue, pressed neutral countries to accept refugees, and supported Raoul Wallenberg in Budapest, whose papers and safe houses saved thousands. The Board's postwar estimate of tens of thousands saved is its own estimate.

5. At Ohrdruf in April 1945, Eisenhower ordered the camp documented and brought members of Congress and journalists to see it. He wrote that he had come deliberately, to give first-hand evidence if the allegations were ever charged to propaganda.

6. At Nuremberg in 1945 and 1946 the surviving leaders of Nazi Germany were tried for war crimes. Jackson opened the prosecution by promising proof from the Germans' own books, records, photographs, and films, and the tribunal sentenced twelve defendants to death.

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that measures the distance rather than asserting one side of it. The record will not support a claim that nothing was known, because the persecution was public from 1933 and the extermination was condemned by name in December 1942. It will not support a claim that nothing was done, because the War Refugee Board operated from January 1944 and Wallenberg's work in Budapest saved thousands. (Blueprint 14)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately and in date order. Bank items 1 and 2 establish that persecution and refusal were public and dated. (Blueprint 13 and Blueprint 14) Bank items 3 and 4 establish the sequence at the center of the question: reports and condemnation in 1942, a rescue agency in 1944. (Blueprint 14) Bank items 5 and 6 establish the closing movement of the record, documentation ordered in advance against denial, and judgment conducted from the perpetrators' own records. (Blueprint 14)
  • Precision about estimates and limits. The six million count is stated as the count the record supports. The War Refugee Board's figure of tens of thousands saved is credited as the Board's own estimate. The bombing debate is named as a debate the documents do not settle, if the essay raises it at all.
  • One sentence that refuses a claim the documents cannot carry, such as the claim that rescue on the scale of the crime was possible at any point after 1942, or the claim that the Allied governments learned of the murders only in 1945. Credit the limiting fact, not the refusal alone.

Model opening for teacher reference. The distance between knowledge and response can be measured in dates. The persecution was public law in 1935 and public violence in November 1938, and the governments that turned the St. Louis away in 1939 acted with that record in front of them. The extermination was reported to London and Washington by telegram in August 1942 and condemned by the Allied governments that December. A rescue agency with presidential authority, the War Refugee Board, was not created until January 1944, sixteen months after the condemnation, and it worked in the war's last year. A full model continues in this pattern, crediting Wallenberg's thousands and the Board's estimate as an estimate, closing with Ohrdruf's documentation and the Nuremberg trial, and refusing at least one claim the documents cannot carry.

Common weak answers. Indictment only (no one cared and nothing was done) fails against bank items 4 through 6 and earns partial credit. Absolution only (the Allies did what they could) fails against bank items 2 and 3, the fixed quotas and the sixteen months between condemnation and a rescue agency. An essay that inflates a figure past its evidence, for example stating the Board's estimate as a settled count, loses credit under the precision line even when its claim is sound.
