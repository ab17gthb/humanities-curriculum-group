# The World War II Unit: Build Manifest

Every finished item, its file, its page count, and the test-blueprint entries it teaches or assesses. Blueprint numbers are from world-war-ii-skeleton.md. Every document ships as an HTML source with a PDF of the same name, built with templates/build_pdf.py and render-checked before listing. The full unit is 51 documents and 231 printed pages, plus 8 slide decks (211 slides) with a printable teacher notes document for each deck.

**Unit essential question:** How did the Second World War transform the United States, and how far did the country live up to the democracy it claimed to defend?

## Unit status: CONTENT COMPLETE (2026-10-21)
All eight lessons (six documents each), the review day, and the unit test are built in the approved design and render-checked: 51 documents, 231 printed pages. All eight slide decks are built to the slide standard: 211 slides, 34,219 words of speaker notes, every slide between 127 and 190 words, every key term and its full guided-notes definition verbatim in the notes, and a printable teacher notes document per deck (80 pages total). Reading prose runs 831 to 852 words by the strict body-paragraph count (the method that reproduces the Depression and New Deal manifest's strict recount band of 815 to 898); counting section headings and figure captions as well gives 872 to 908. Lesson 3 sits at the low end (831 strict), in line with the Depression unit's Lessons 7 and 8 (831 and 815), which shipped. Zero em dashes and zero en dashes in any HTML source or extracted deck text, verified across the whole unit at the end of the build, and no instance of the banned construction anywhere in the unit. Honest gaps carried in the lessons themselves and recorded here: (1) Lesson 1 uses no America First rally photograph, because the Commons file for Lindbergh's 1941 rally speech is an Associated Press photograph marked copyrighted, and no fall of France or 1940 refugee photograph, because the candidates found were CC BY-SA or otherwise not public domain; Section E is carried by the Lend-Lease photograph and a giant stat slide, and both gaps are recorded in lesson-01/img/SOURCES.md. (2) Lesson 8 ships no GI Bill classroom or homecoming photograph, because no image with a stated public domain status on its file page was found; the country that came home is taught in text and in the reckoning figures, recorded under "Not obtained" in lesson-08/img/SOURCES.md. (3) The Franck Report is not quoted in Lesson 8's deck: no standalone audited TPSP record for it exists in the current bank (the June 1945 report survives there as a document inside the premium DBQ number 12 record), so the deck's two closing slides quote two verified passages of Justice Jackson's Nuremberg opening statement instead, one on the Pacific reckoning and one on evidence over vengeance. The Franck Report is still taught in the reading, the Decision File activity, and the writing task, and the activity's passages were verified against the bank record segment by segment; its Source line cites the atomicarchive.com transcription via National Archives Record Group 77, matching the bank record's source note. (4) In the same activity, three of the four Instrument of Surrender excerpts end with a period inserted before the ellipsis where the bank text continues the sentence; the wording is otherwise verbatim, and the difference is punctuation at the cut only, flagged here rather than silently rewritten. (5) One Lesson 8 deck note carries narrative wording inherited from the bank's Truman statement excerpt, whose opening words appear garbled in the bank excerpt itself; the note is teaching narrative, not a quotation, and no student-facing document quotes that passage. (6) Lesson 7's image record is deliberately small (three Signal Corps photographs) and its SOURCES.md records the images considered and not used; the lesson is taught from documents, with photographs used as evidence and captioned exactly. Two caption precisions from that record: the Ohrdruf photograph (USHMM Photograph 04649) shows Eisenhower viewing the bodies of prisoners killed during the camp's evacuation, and it appears only beside its cable in the deck, not on the title slide; and the Nuremberg defendants crop in the deck is an enlargement of a low-resolution source (478 by 367 pixels) and prints visibly softer than the unit's other deck photographs. Out of scope for this build by instruction, and still to come in a separate pass: the site unit page, planner integration, Firebase Storage upload, Knoweth Drive filing of the decks, and any TpT packaging. Plate gaps by standing decision, unchanged from the pilot: TPSP source analyses and photo sets are linked from the audited bank, not rebuilt, and TPSP premium DBQ number 20, "Did World War II Advance Freedom at Home as Well as Abroad?", is the planned lead of this unit's TPSP picker in the integration pass, with DBQ number 12 as the Lesson 8 extension.
