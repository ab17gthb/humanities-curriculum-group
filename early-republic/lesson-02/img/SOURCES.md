All images are public domain on the basis stated for each. Deck crops live in slides-build/img/ and were cropped from the downloads listed here. Document copies live in assets/.

## Congressional Pugilists, 1798 (lyon-pugilists.jpg; deck crops title-169.jpg and lyon-port.jpg; used in the activity, Station 4)
- Title: Congressional Pugilists
- Creator: Anonymous, American, 18th century
- Date: 1798, published at Philadelphia
- Holder: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (Open Access; object record 388906, which marks the work public domain, isPublicDomain true; Gift of Albert Ten Eyck Gardner, 1950; object number 50.587.13, as read from the Met collection API record for object 388906 during this build)
- Source: https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/dp/original/DP876932.jpg (image, as given in the Met API record for object 388906); object record consulted at https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/388906
- Public domain basis: Public domain, as stated in the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access record for the object (isPublicDomain true), an etching of 1798. The print shows Representative Roger Griswold attacking Representative Matthew Lyon with a cane and Lyon defending himself with fireplace tongs, on the floor of Congress Hall, February 15, 1798.

## Text editions used in this lesson, recorded here as the spec directs
- The Alien and Sedition Acts, Jefferson's draft of the Kentucky Resolutions, Madison's Virginia Resolutions, and Jefferson's First Inaugural Address: audited TPSP bank records alien-and-sedition-acts-1798, jefferson-kentucky-resolutions-1798, virginia-resolutions-1798, and jefferson-first-inaugural-1801 in ~/workspace/primary-source/vite-react-main/public/data/early-republic.json. Each record's sourceNote names the Yale Avalon Project as its edition. Quoted verbatim from the bank text fields in the activity and the quick check, with cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines.
- The Matthew Lyon prosecution record in Station 4 is a labeled teaching summary built from the public record, labeled a summary on its face in the activity: Lyon was a Democratic-Republican congressman from Vermont, convicted in federal court in Vermont in October 1798 under the Sedition Act for criticism of President Adams, fined one thousand dollars and jailed for four months in Vergennes, reelected by Vermont voters in December 1798 while imprisoned, and seated by the House. No period words are quoted in that station.
