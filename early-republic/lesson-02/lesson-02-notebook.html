Teacher Answer Key

StepWho acted, and what they didThe ground they claimed
The war scare, 1798French agents in the XYZ Affair demanded a bribe and a loan, American and French ships fought an undeclared naval war, and Federalist majorities prepared for a war that might come ashore.National safety in a war scare, with the added party fact that Irish and French immigrant voters backed Jefferson's Democratic-Republicans.
The four laws, summer 1798The Federalist Congress passed and President Adams signed the Naturalization Act, the Alien Friends Act, the Alien Enemies Act, and the Sedition Act, stretching the citizenship wait to fourteen years and making criticism of the government a crime.Congress claimed power to guard the government against dangerous immigrants and seditious writing, citing truth as a defense and juries as a safeguard in the Sedition Act.
The prosecutions, 1798 to 1800Federal prosecutors arrested about twenty five people and convicted some ten, nearly all Republicans, including Congressman Matthew Lyon, who was reelected from his jail cell in Vermont.The prosecutions rested on the act's own words, false, scandalous, and malicious writing, but in practice they fell on one party's speakers and editors.
The resolutions, 1798 and 1799Jefferson secretly drafted the Kentucky Resolutions and Madison secretly wrote the Virginia Resolutions, Virginia claiming a state duty to interpose and Kentucky in 1799 naming nullification.Compact theory: the Constitution was an agreement among the states delegating definite powers only, so the states kept the right to judge when the federal government exceeded them. Ten states rejected the claim and none joined it.
The deadlock and transfer, 1800 to 1801Jefferson and Burr tied at seventy three electoral votes, the House deadlocked through thirty five ballots and chose Jefferson on the thirty sixth, Adams left before dawn on March 4, 1801, and the Twelfth Amendment separated the ballots in 1804.Ballots and the Constitution's own machinery, repaired after use. A party that had jailed its opponents surrendered the government to them without a soldier in the street.

The answer that held. Strong answers name the row, the answerer, and the mechanism. On the resolutions, the answer was state legislatures arguing compact theory, and it failed on its own terms in 1799, since ten states rejected it and none joined. On the prosecutions, Lyon's answer was an election run from a cell, politics defeating a prosecution. On the transfer, the answer was the House finishing its constitutional work and the losing president going home, the mechanism that made every later transfer possible.
