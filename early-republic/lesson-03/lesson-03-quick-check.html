Quick Check: The Purchase and the Court

Directions: Read both passages, then answer the questions.

Passage A, Marbury v. Madison, 1803. Source: Chief Justice John Marshall, Marbury v. Madison, 1803 (Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law; punctuation modernized). Audited TPSP bank record, marbury-v-madison. Cuts are marked with ellipses.

It is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is. ... So if a law be in opposition to the constitution; if both the law and the constitution apply to a particular case, so that the court must either decide that case conformably to the law, disregarding the constitution; or conformably to the constitution, disregarding the law; the court must determine which of these conflicting rules governs the case. This is of the very essence of judicial duty. ... a law repugnant to the constitution is void; and that courts, as well as other departments, are bound by that instrument.

Passage B, Jefferson to John Breckinridge, August 12, 1803. Source: Founders Online, National Archives; from The Papers of Thomas Jefferson, vol. 41, ed. Barbara B. Oberg, Princeton University Press, 2014. Jefferson's spelling is as the transcription carries it. Cuts are marked with ellipses.

... the constitution has made no provision for our holding foreign territory, still less for incorporating foreign nations into our union. the Executive in siesing the fugitive occurrence which so much advanced the good of their country, have done an act beyond the constitution. ...

1. Work Passage A. State the duty Marshall assigns the judicial department in his own words, and state what a court must do when a law and the Constitution conflict in one case.

2. Passage A decided a case Jefferson's administration won. State what the Court refused to order in Marbury v. Madison, and state the ground it gave for the refusal.

3. Work Passage B. State Jefferson's objection to the Louisiana Purchase in his own words, and state what cure he proposed in the same letter and what became of it.

4. Set the two passages side by side. In 1803 one branch claimed a power the Constitution nowhere names in so many words, and the president confessed to an act beyond the Constitution. Name each, and state which of the two Jefferson could have stopped and chose not to fight.

Teacher Answer Key

1. Work Passage A. It is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is. When a law and the Constitution both apply to a case and conflict, the court must determine which rule governs, deciding conformably to the Constitution and disregarding the law, because a law repugnant to the Constitution is void. (Blueprint 6)

2. State the refusal and its ground. The Court refused to order Secretary of State Madison to deliver William Marbury's commission. Its ground was that the section of the Judiciary Act of 1789 granting the Court power to issue such an order gave it authority the Constitution did not grant, so the section was itself unconstitutional and the Court could not act under it. (Blueprint 6)

3. Work Passage B. The Constitution has made no provision for our holding foreign territory, still less for incorporating foreign nations into our union, and the executive, in seizing the fugitive occurrence, have done an act beyond the constitution. Jefferson's proposed cure was an appeal to the nation for an additional article to the constitution, approving and confirming an act the nation had not previously authorised. He drafted an amendment and abandoned it, and none was ever adopted. (Blueprint 5)

4. Set the passages side by side. The branch is the judiciary: Marbury claimed judicial review, the power to declare acts of Congress void, which the Constitution nowhere grants in those words. The president is Jefferson, confessing that the Louisiana Purchase was an act beyond the Constitution. The one Jefferson could have stopped, or at least fought, was neither in the event: he accepted Marbury because it left the commission undelivered, and the purchase was his own act, which he defended to Breckinridge as a guardian's risk taken for the nation's good. Credit either framing when the student names both powers accurately and states that Jefferson let both stand. (Blueprint 5 and Blueprint 6)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Question 4 is the lesson diagnostic. A student who can name judicial review but not Jefferson's confession has the Court's half of 1803 without the president's, and should redo sections C and F of the guided notes before the writing task.
