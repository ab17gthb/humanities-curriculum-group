Quick Check: A Hemisphere Declared, A Line Drawn

Directions: Read the passage, then answer the questions.

Section 8 of the act of March 6, 1820, in part. "That in all that territory ceded by France to the United States, under the name of Louisiana, which lies north of thirty-six degrees and thirty minutes north latitude, not included within the limits of the state, contemplated by this act, slavery and involuntary servitude, otherwise than in the punishment of crimes, whereof the parties shall have been duly convicted, shall be, and is hereby, forever prohibited ..."

Source: U.S. Congress, act of March 6, 1820 (National Archives, Our Documents transcript), as carried in the audited Primary Source Project bank record. Quoted with a cut, marked by ellipses.

1. State the rule this passage lays down. Name the territory it covers, the line it uses, how long the prohibition runs, and the one exception it allows.

2. State the limit built into the passage's own words. Which state does the phrase not included within the limits of the state contemplated by this act leave out of the ban, and on which side of the line does that state stand?

3. Put the outward settlement in order. Name the treaty of 1819 and what it took and drew, the British proposal of 1823, the image Adams used against it, and the date and two claims of Monroe's message.

4. Work the crisis the line answered. Give the Senate balance of 1819, the Tallmadge Amendment's two conditions and its fate in each house, and the settlement's three parts.

Teacher Answer Key

1. State the rule, its territory, its line, its duration, and its exception. In the territory ceded by France under the name of Louisiana that lies north of thirty six degrees and thirty minutes north latitude, slavery and involuntary servitude are forever prohibited. The one exception is involuntary servitude as punishment for crimes of which the parties have been duly convicted. (Blueprint 14)

2. State the built in limit and the state it leaves out. The ban excludes the state contemplated by the act, Missouri. Missouri stands north of the thirty six thirty line and entered the Union as a slave state, so the line's first and largest fact is its own exception. (Blueprint 14)

3. Put the outward settlement in order, with the treaty, the proposal, the image, and the message. The Adams-Onís Treaty of 1819 took Florida from Spain and drew the boundary between American and Spanish claims to the Pacific. In 1823 Britain proposed a joint declaration. Adams argued against coming in as a cockboat in the wake of the British man of war. Monroe's annual message of December 2, 1823 declared the continents closed to future European colonization and European interference in the hemisphere dangerous to the United States. (Blueprint 13)

4. Work the crisis, with the balance, the amendment and its fate, and the three parts. In 1819 the Senate stood at eleven free states and eleven slave states. The Tallmadge Amendment would have barred bringing more enslaved people into Missouri and freed children born to enslaved parents there at age twenty five. The House passed it on sectional lines; the Senate killed it. The settlement admitted Maine as a free state, admitted Missouri as a slave state, and drew the line at 36 degrees 30 minutes across the Louisiana Purchase lands, barring slavery north of it and permitting it south. (Blueprint 14)

Teacher note. This check is formative. Questions 1 and 2 carry blueprint entry 14 and test whether students can read Section 8's limits in its own words, including the Missouri exception. Question 3 carries blueprint entry 13. Question 4 carries blueprint entry 14. A student who describes the line as banning slavery in the North generally, without the Louisiana Purchase limit and the Missouri exception, has the slogan rather than the settlement the unit test will price.
