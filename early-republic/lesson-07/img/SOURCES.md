## Subjects for which no public domain image is used, recorded honestly
- Latin American independence era prints: no print was downloaded for this lesson. Candidate files could not be verified to a Commons file page with a readable public domain basis during this build, so the outward story is carried by the Monroe and Adams portraits and by the documents. No image in this lesson is captioned as showing an independence battle or congress.
- James Tallmadge and Jesse Thomas: no portraits were downloaded for this lesson. Both men are carried in their own words, Tallmadge in the amendment's text at Activity Station 5 and Thomas in the line his amendment drew.
- The Missouri Compromise as an 1820 artifact: no period map of the line was verified. The 1919 McConnell map is used and is captioned as a later map in every appearance.

## Quoted texts and their editions
All period quotations in this lesson are drawn from the audited Primary Source Project bank file early-republic.json or from the named transcriptions below, and each document's Source line names the edition used:
- President James Monroe, annual message, December 2, 1823 (Yale Avalon Project), bank record monroe-doctrine. Quoted at Activity Station 1 and in the reading and quick check framing, with cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines.
- U.S. Congress, act of March 6, 1820, Section 8 (National Archives, Our Documents transcript), bank record missouri-compromise-1820. Quoted at Activity Station 2 and in the quick check, with cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source lines.
- Thomas Jefferson to John Holmes, Monticello, April 22, 1820 (Founders Online, founders.archives.gov, document Jefferson/03-15-02-0518). Quoted at Activity Station 3 from the Founders Online transcription fetched during this build, which keeps Jefferson's spelling, including concieved, and his lowercase sentence openings. Cuts marked by ellipses and disclosed in the Source line.
- John Quincy Adams, cabinet argument on the joint declaration, November 1823. No verbatim diary text from a named scholarly edition could be verified during this build. Activity Station 4 is therefore a labeled teaching summary, labeled on its face, and no sentence in it is presented as a verbatim quotation except the cockboat image, which is Adams's recorded phrase.
- Representative James Tallmadge, amendment to the Missouri statehood bill, February 13, 1819, as printed in Lawrence Wilson, ed., The National Register, vol. VII (Washington City, 1819), p. 125. Quoted entire at Activity Station 5, as that transcription carries it.
- No period text is quoted from memory anywhere in this lesson.
