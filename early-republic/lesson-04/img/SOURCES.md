# Image Sources for Early Republic Lesson 4, Lewis and Clark and the Peoples of the West

All images are public domain works. Downloads were made on October 22, 2026, from the Wikimedia Commons renditions of the files cataloged below, after each file page's public domain tagging was verified through the Wikimedia Commons API during this build. Every downloaded file was opened and verified with PIL before use. Document copies are stored in the lesson's assets folder and img folder; deck versions are cropped to portrait (1046 by 1500) or to 16:9 for the title slide and stored in slides-build/img; no other alteration.

## Sources quoted in this lesson, with editions

- Treaty of Greenville, activity Station 1: Treaty of Greenville, August 3, 1795, preamble and Articles I, II, and III, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record treaty-greenville-1795 in early-republic.json, whose sourceNote names Charles J. Kappler, editor, Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties, volume 2, 1904. The quoted passages are verbatim from the bank record's text field, including its spelling (Greeneville in the record's preamble narrative is not in the passages quoted; Shawanoes, Putawatimes, Weea's as printed). Cuts inside Articles II and III are marked with ellipses and disclosed in the source line.
- Jefferson's instructions, activity Station 2, quick check, and reading: Thomas Jefferson to Meriwether Lewis, June 20, 1803, as transcribed and edited by Gerard W. Gawalt, Manuscript Division, Library of Congress (Rivers, Edens, Empires exhibition transcription, loc.gov/exhibits/lewisandclark/transcript57.html), fetched in full during this build. The object paragraph, the knowledge paragraph, and the friendly intercourse paragraph are quoted from that transcription with its spelling preserved (it's, knolege, line your will pursue as Jefferson drafted it). Cuts are marked with ellipses and disclosed in the source lines.
- Clark's journal, activity Station 3 and quick check: William Clark, journal entry of May 14, 1804, as carried in the audited TPSP bank record lewis-clark-journals-1804 in early-republic.json, whose sourceNote names the Journals of Lewis and Clark as carried by Project Gutenberg. The first part of the entry is quoted entire from the bank record's text field, spelling preserved (proceded, Missouris, opposit, ores, Perogue).
- Shoshone meeting, activity Station 4: Meriwether Lewis, journal entry of August 17, 1805, in The Journals of the Lewis and Clark Expedition, edited by Gary E. Moulton, University of Nebraska Press (1983 to 2001), as published in the edition's online text by the University of Nebraska, Lincoln (lewisandclarkjournals.unl.edu), fetched during this build. The passage on Clark's arrival and the Indian woman proving to be a sister of the chief Cameahwait, and the passage on communicating the objects of the journey through the medium of Labuish, Charbonneau, and Sacagawea, are quoted from that text, spelling preserved (Chif, Sah cah-gar-we-ah, Minnetares). The four language chain stated in the station is the entry's own interpreting sequence (Lewis, Labiche, Charbonneau, Sacagawea), as the Moulton text and its annotations give it.
- York record, activity Station 5: a labeled teaching summary, labeled on its face, built from the expedition journals (York's hunting, scouting, care of Sergeant Floyd, the November 1805 vote on the winter camp) and from William Clark's letters to his brother Jonathan Clark of 1808 and 1809 as carried in the Clark family papers scholarship (the letters complain of York's wish for freedom, threaten sale at New Orleans or hiring to a severe master, and record a beating), together with Clark's 1832 account to Washington Irving, which the summary itself flags as unverified because no independent record of a freeing, a business, or York's death has been found. No sentence in Station 5 is presented as a quotation.

## Figures stated as counts or estimates

- The party that set out in May 1804: some forty men, stated as an approximation everywhere in this lesson, as the reading states it.
- The Great Falls portage: about a month, as the reading states it.
- Sergeant Floyd's death, August 1804, and the two Blackfeet killed in July 1806: stated as the journals' record gives them, one man lost to illness and a single violent encounter, in the reading and the deck.
- Sacagawea's age at the hire: stated in the deck notes as about sixteen or seventeen, an estimate in most accounts, and taught as an estimate.

## Gaps recorded honestly

- Sacagawea and York: no portrait of either was made in their lifetimes, and no verified public domain likeness exists to use. Both are taught in this lesson through the journals, the records, and text slides, and the deck notes state the absence plainly rather than dressing an invention as a likeness.
- Fort Mandan and Fort Clatsop: no period image of either fort made during the expedition was verified public domain in this build, so neither fort is pictured. The villages and the track map carry the lesson's image work instead.
- The Shoshone council of August 1805: no period image exists. Russell's 1905 Lower Columbia painting is used elsewhere in the deck with its date attached, and it is not presented as a picture of the council.
- No catalog number is stated anywhere in this lesson for any image beyond the Smithsonian object number and the Amon Carter accession number recorded above, which are taken from the Wikimedia Commons file pages as cataloged there.
