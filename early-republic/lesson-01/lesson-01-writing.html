Writing Task: Which Measure Made the Government Real?

The prompt. Answer the closing question of the lesson in a developed essay of about 300 words: which of Hamilton's four measures, funding the debt at full value, assuming the state debts, chartering the Bank of the United States, or laying the excise tax on whiskey, did the most to make the federal government real? A strong answer does more than describe the measure it chooses. It states what real means for a government, works through the chosen measure and its strongest rival, and weighs the Farewell Address warning that the program itself helped create the parties. Use at least three pieces of evidence from the evidence bank, and explain how each piece supports your claim.

The bank is drawn from the lesson reading, stations, and documents. Write the history in your own sentences, and use the facts rather than copying the bank wording, except where a bank item quotes a document.

Evidence Bank

1. Hamilton, in his Opinion on the Constitutionality of the Bank, February 23, 1791, argued that every power vested in a government includes a right to employ all the means requisite and fairly applicable to its ends, that implied powers are delegated as fully as express ones, and that necessary often means no more than needful, requisite, incidental, useful, or conducive to. (Avalon Project, Yale Law School, as carried in the audited TPSP record.)

2. Funding paid the Revolutionary War debt at full value, and assumption moved the states' war debts onto the federal government, so the creditors of the states looked to the union for payment. Jefferson and Madison answered that funding rewarded speculators who had bought soldiers' certificates for a fraction of their worth, and that the Bank was a tool the Constitution never named. (Lesson reading, sections B and C.)

3. The Bank of the United States, chartered in 1791 for twenty years, held the government's money, issued a uniform currency, and lent, giving the union a working financial institution rather than a promise on paper. (Lesson reading, section B, and the unit vocabulary.)

4. The excise tax on distilled whiskey fell hardest on frontier farmers, for whom whiskey was the one form in which their corn could reach a market. In 1794 Washington called out a militia army of some thirteen thousand men from four states and rode west with it part of the way. The Whiskey Rebellion dissolved, two men were convicted of treason, and Washington pardoned both. (Lesson reading, section D.)

5. In the Farewell Address of September 1796, Washington warned against the baneful effects of the spirit of party generally and held that it is our true policy to steer clear of permanent alliances with any portion of the foreign world. The parties he warned against had formed in the argument over Hamilton's program. (Avalon Project, Yale Law School, as carried in the audited TPSP record, and lesson reading, sections C and G.)

6. The Neutrality Proclamation of 1793 declared a conduct friendly and impartial toward the powers at war, and the Jay Treaty of 1794 won the evacuation of the British frontier posts by June 1796 at the price of compensating British creditors, approved by the Senate by the bare two thirds required. (Founders Online, National Archives, and Avalon Project, Yale Law School, as carried in the audited TPSP records.)

Teacher Guide and Rubric

Full credit requires:

  • A claim that defines real before it names a measure. Real can mean credited, that lenders trust the government's word; staffed, that an institution does the work daily; obeyed, that its laws are collected and enforced; or accepted, that citizens and creditors look to the union first. The definition controls which measure wins, and strong essays say so. (Blueprint 1)
  • At least three pieces of evidence from the bank, used accurately. Bank item 1 supplies the doctrine under the Bank, implied powers and necessary read broadly. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 2 carries funding and assumption together with the strict reading answer to both. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 3 states what the Bank did every day. (Blueprint 1) Bank item 4 carries the excise from the tax book to the army that enforced it. (Blueprint 1 and Blueprint 2) Bank item 5 closes the arc with the cost, the parties the program created, named in the Farewell Address. (Blueprint 2) Bank item 6 shows the same government acting abroad in the same years and is creditable supporting evidence for any claim. (Blueprint 2)
  • Explanation that connects the evidence to the claim. The decisive link is measure against definition: students should argue that the measure they chose is the one without which their definition of real fails, not merely the largest or the most famous measure.
  • One or two sentences answering the strongest rival measure, such as the argument that the excise mattered most because enforcement is the test of a government, answered from the record: the excise could be enforced because funding, assumption, and the Bank had already given the government the credit and machinery the army marched under.

Model opening for teacher reference. The Bank did the most to make the federal government real, if real means an institution at work rather than a promise in a report. Funding told lenders the union would pay and assumption told state creditors to look to the union, but the Bank held the government's money, issued its currency, and lent in every state, every week, under a doctrine Hamilton stated in 1791: that a government may employ the means fairly applicable to its ends, and that necessary means needful and conducive, not indispensable. A full model continues in this pattern, weighing the excise and its army as the strongest rival, and closing on the Farewell Address, where the president who signed the program warned against the party spirit the argument over it had created.

Common weak answers. Hamilton was a genius (a judgment about a man, not a measure) skips the definition the prompt requires. The Bank alone, with no rival weighed, earns partial credit, because the prompt asks for a weighing. A claim that uses the Farewell Address as praise for Washington rather than as evidence about cost misses bank item 5 and the closing move the directions require.
