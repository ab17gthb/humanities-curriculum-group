Guided Notes: Washington's Republic: Hamilton, Parties, and the Farewell

Lesson question: What did Washington's government have to invent, and what did it warn against as it left?

Unit question: How did the United States turn its Constitution into a working government, and what did continental expansion do to the republic that government served?

Key Terms

cabinet: the heads of the executive departments who advise the president, an office the Constitution nowhere creates, which Washington formed from the departments Congress established in 1789

assumption: the federal government's taking over of debts owed by others, the policy by which Hamilton's funding plan had the United States assume the states' Revolutionary War debts

excise tax: a tax on the manufacture or sale of a product inside the country, such as the 1791 tax on distilled whiskey that paid part of Hamilton's program and fell hardest on frontier farmers

Bank of the United States: the national bank chartered by Congress in 1791 for twenty years, holding the government's money, issuing currency, and lending, which Hamilton defended under Congress's implied powers and Jefferson opposed

Whiskey Rebellion: the uprising of 1794 in western Pennsylvania against the federal excise tax on whiskey, which Washington answered by calling out the militia and leading it west, proving the government could enforce its laws

cotton gin: the machine patented by Eli Whitney in 1794 that separated cotton fiber from seed by machine, which made short-staple cotton profitable across the inland South and deepened the region's dependence on enslaved labor

Farewell Address: Washington's published letter of September 1796 announcing he would not seek a third term, which warned against permanent foreign alliances and against political parties, and set the two-term precedent presidents followed

Summary table. The invention of 1789: Congress created the Departments of State, Treasury, and War, their heads formed Washington's cabinet, and the Judiciary Act of 1789 organized the Supreme Court and lower federal courts, machinery the Constitution had named in outline or not at all. Hamilton's program: fund the war debt at full value, assume the state war debts, charter the Bank of the United States, and lay an excise tax on distilled whiskey, all resting on implied powers and the necessary and proper clause. Parties and enforcement: Jefferson and Madison answered with a strict reading, the argument moved into the newspapers and formed the Federalist and Democratic-Republican parties, and Washington enforced the excise in western Pennsylvania with a militia army of some thirteen thousand men. The gin and the Farewell: Whitney's cotton gin of 1794 made short staple cotton profitable and gave slavery a new economic engine, and Washington's Farewell Address of September 1796 warned against the spirit of party and permanent foreign alliances while setting the two term precedent.

Exit ticket 1. Fund the Revolutionary War debt at full value, assume the war debts of the states, charter a national bank, and lay an excise tax on distilled whiskey.

Exit ticket 2. Washington called out a militia army of some thirteen thousand men from four states and rode west with it part of the way, and the rebellion dissolved before it arrived, proving the new government would enforce its own laws where the government under the Articles of Confederation had been able only to watch. Two men were convicted of treason, and Washington pardoned both.

Exit ticket 3. Accept any answer that names two inventions, such as the cabinet and executive departments, the federal courts, the funded debt and national bank, the enforced excise, or the neutrality policy, and both Farewell warnings, against the spirit of party and against permanent alliances with foreign nations.
