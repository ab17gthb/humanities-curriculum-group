All images are public domain. Masters are in this folder. Document copies are in the lesson assets folder, and deck crops are in slides-build/img.

## 1. Battle of Palo Alto, 1851
- File: palo-alto-1851.jpg
- Title: Battle of Palo Alto, near Brownsville, fought on May 8, 1846 in the Mexican-American War, view from behind the United States lines toward the Mexican positions
- Creator: lithograph by Adolphe Jean-Baptiste Bayot (1810 to 1866) after a drawing by Carl Nebel (1805 to 1855), published in George Wilkins Kendall and Carl Nebel, The War between the United States and Mexico Illustrated (New York: D. Appleton; Philadelphia: George S. Appleton, 1851)
- Date: 1851 (battle depicted May 8, 1846)
- Holder: copy reproduced on the Wikimedia Commons file page for the plate
- Source URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nebel_Mexican_War_01_Battle_of_Palo_Alto.jpg
- Public domain basis: as the Wikimedia Commons file page states, the image is a faithful photographic reproduction of a two-dimensional public domain work of art, and the work of art itself, published in 1851 by artists who died in 1855 and 1866, is in the public domain in the United States.
- Use: reading figure (assets copy), deck title image (16:9 crop title-169.jpg), deck portrait crop palo-port.jpg. The print is used as evidence of how the war was pictured for American readers, captioned as such wherever it appears, not as decoration.

## Subjects for which no separate image was used
- No territorial map was used. A PD-basis period map of the Mexican cession that could be verified on its file page within this build was not secured, and the modern Commons boundary maps located are Creative Commons licensed rather than public domain, so the lesson teaches the geography in text and in the deck's stat and content slides rather than printing a map whose basis could not be recorded here.
- No image of the Alamo or of the Battle of San Jacinto was used. The later nineteenth century paintings of those subjects are imaginings made decades after 1836 and add no evidence to a lesson whose Texas record is annexation in 1845, so the lesson uses no imagined battle scene as decoration. Battle imagery in this lesson is limited to the Nebel and Bayot plates, drawn from a participant artist's wartime sketches and printed in 1851, and is captioned as evidence wherever it appears.
- No portrait of O'Sullivan, Wilmot, or Lincoln was used. The records themselves carry those stations.

## Text editions used in this lesson (recorded here as the spec directs)
- Unsigned editorial traditionally attributed to John L. O'Sullivan, "Annexation," July-August 1845. Audited TPSP bank record osullivan-annexation-1845 (jackson-reform.json); edition named in the record: United States Magazine and Democratic Review, 1845. Quoted verbatim from the record, with the record's own ellipses.
- James K. Polk, Message to Congress, May 11, 1846. Fetched during this build from the Avalon Project transcription at Yale Law School (https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/polk01.asp) and quoted verbatim from that transcription in the activity, quick check, and writing evidence bank.
- Abraham Lincoln, "Spot" Resolutions, December 22, 1847. Fetched during this build from the Papers of Abraham Lincoln transcription (document D200434b, https://papersofabrahamlincoln.org/documents/D200434b), a transcription of Lincoln's handwritten resolutions held by the National Archives (RG 233, Entry 362, as that page records). Quoted verbatim from that transcription, including its spellings teritories and teritory, which the Source line in the activity discloses.
- David Wilmot, the Wilmot Proviso, offered August 8, 1846. Text as printed in the Congressional Globe, 29th Congress, 1st session, page 1217, in the transcription consulted at Encyclopedia.com, which cites that Globe printing as its source. Quoted verbatim from that transcription.
- United States and Mexico, Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, February 2, 1848. Audited TPSP bank record treaty-guadalupe-hidalgo-1848 (jackson-reform.json); edition named in the record: Yale Avalon Project. The record carries Article I and Article VIII. The activity quotes only those articles and says on its face that the record carries them, with the record's own ellipses.
